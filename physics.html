Residual Allan Stability under Periodic Auxiliary Ramsey Sampling

Zhiqian Su

The timing of auxiliary Ramsey measurements can change the frequency stability associated with a drifting pulse shift at a fixed nominal Fisher-information rate for a constant shift. For periodic instantaneous observations of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck shift with causal prediction between updates, we derive the exact residual covariance averaged over the update cycle and the corresponding Allan variance at arbitrary averaging times. Relative to a continuous Kalman reference at the same information rate, periodic estimation increases the cycle-averaged estimation-error variance, reduces the short-time residual Allan variance, and increases its long-time value. Its covariance decays more slowly at the origin and has a larger integral. We extend the result to instantaneous sensing of the sum of finitely many independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck modes. Integrated observations preserve the short-time ordering for the scalar shift, and numerical calculations resolve the dependence of the crossover on window duration. A zero-order-hold comparison shows that the short-time ordering can change when the same estimator drives a different correction command. A three-level Ramsey model of coherent population trapping (CPT) maps the discriminator response to an effective information budget and illustrates how the dark times affect the choice of schedule.

A Nonsecular Quantum Master Equation with Detailed Balance and Second-order Error Bound

Ji-Hui Pei, Jin-Fu Chen

An effective theory of open quantum systems should preserve the detailed balance structure inherited from microscopic reversibility and remain accurate throughout relaxation. Meeting both requirements is particularly challenging in quantum many-body systems, where dense spectra can invalidate the secular approximation even at weak coupling. Here we construct a nonsecular quantum master equation that satisfies time-reversal Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (TR-KMS) detailed balance. Under suitable conditions, we prove a time-uniform $O(λ^2)$ bound on the difference between the predicted and exact reduced density matrices, where $λ$ is the system-bath coupling strength. Our construction completes the Redfield generator by imposing detailed balance while matching its dissipative and coherent coefficients at resonance. In this approach, TR-KMS detailed balance is compatible with resonance matching, whereas its counterpart without time reversal is not. In a benchmark against the exact microscopic dynamics of two coupled oscillators, our equation maintains $O(λ^2)$ accuracy across secular and nonsecular regimes, outperforming the existing detailed-balance equations tested. These results provide a systematic route from microscopic models to thermodynamically consistent descriptions of open quantum dynamics beyond the secular approximation.

Dressed-State Quantum Information Processing Using a Multilayer Ion Trap Chip with an Integrated Radio Frequency Resonator

Ivan A. Boldin, Elham Esteki, Bogdan Okhrimenko, Friederike Giebel, Konstantin Thronberens et al. (8 authors)

We present the experimental preparation of a radio frequency (RF)-dressed-state qubit, the coherent operation of a single RF-dressed qubit, and the selective readout of RF-dressed hyperfine states of $^{171}$Yb${^+}$ ions trapped using a novel microstructured ion trap chip. We demonstrate the individual addressing of RF-dressed-state qubits in a two-ion Coulomb crystal. To this end, we developed, manufactured, and characterized a trap chip specifcally designed for quantum information processing with RF-dressed-state qubits. The chip consists of multiple layers, one of which includes an integrated resonator that creates a gradient of the RF magnetic field amplitude along the axis of a linear ion crystal. This RF gradient enables the individual addressing of dressed qubit states as well as their coupling. We characterize the ion trap chip experimentally using the hyperfine states of the electronic ground state of laser cooled $^{171}$Yb${^+}$ ions and by applying a single, global, modulated RF field oscillating near 12.6 GHz that drives dressed-state resonances. We present a microfabricated multilayer ion trap chip specifically designed for quantum information processing based on dressed-state qubits. This ion trap chip consists of multiple layers, one of which includes an integrated radio frequency (RF) resonator, creating a gradient of the RF magnetic field amplitude that allows for individual qubit addressing and qubit-qubit coupling. We experimentally characterize this ion trap chip demonstrating dressed-qubit state preparation, coherent single dressed-qubit operation, and dressed-state-selective read-out using hyperfine states of the electronic ground state of $^{171}$Yb${^+}$. For this purpose, a global modulated RF driving field oscillating near 12.6 GHz is applied to the ions. In addition, we demonstrate individual addressing of dressed-state qubits in a two-ion Coulomb crystal.

Beyond hidden zeros and uniqueness in Tr$(φ^3)$: Block and rod zeros in any Tr$(φ^m)$

Laurentiu Rodina

Hidden zeros have proven powerful tools for reconstructing amplitudes in Tr$(φ^3)$, a theory of colored scalars with cubic interactions. We show that these structures extend to any Tr$(φ^m)$ theory. For odd $m$, we construct block zeros from orthogonal sums of consecutive momenta, generalizing the original cubic hidden zeros. For every $m\geq4$, including even $m$, we describe a second family of nonlinear rod zeros through a rod packing problem. In Tr$(φ^4)$ this construction reduces to a simple rank-one condition on a matrix of momentum contractions. We connect this new zero to the classic threshold nullification zeros of Voloshin, where certain amplitudes in massive $φ^4$ theory vanish when all produced particles are at rest. Finally, we demonstrate the reconstruction power of these zeros. Assuming locality, we prove that the rod zeros uniquely fix all Tr$(φ^m)$ tree amplitudes for $m\geq4$ up to normalization. We also conjecture that the block zeros enforce both locality and unitarity for any Tr$(φ^m)$ theory with odd $m$.

Efficient search for microlensed gravitational-wave signals with matched filtering and a two-image statistic

Soumen Roy, Justin Janquart

Gravitational waves from compact binary coalescences can be microlensed by compact objects along the line of sight, producing frequency-dependent waveform distortions. As standard matched-filter searches rely on unlensed templates without accounting for lensing effects, microlensed signals can lose signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) through template mismatch and suffer further losses from signal-consistency tests. We quantify both effects for the $\texttt{PyCBC}$ search using microlensed injections in simulated data. The two losses occupy different regions of lens parameter space in the geometric optics representation: template mismatch is governed by the relative magnification of the two images, while the consistency-test penalty is governed by their time delay. We show that a microlensed signal lies in the two-dimensional subspace spanned by a template and its time-shifted copy, allowing the relative magnification and Morse phase to be maximized over analytically, leaving only the time delay to search. The resulting statistic requires no extension of the template bank and reduces the median loss in re-weighted SNR from $10\%$ to $1.6\%$, and the $90^th$ percentile from $41\%$ to $9\%$. At a false-alarm rate of one per day, the relative detection volume improves by about $6\%$ across all injections, and by a factor of $1.9$ for the $10\%$ of injections most affected by lensing. In addition to increased detection capacities, we also show how the detection statistic can be used to establish the lensed nature of the trigger. Therefore, the work proposed here lays the foundation for an effective search and identification of microlensed signals in gravitational-wave data.

Residual-Algebraic Design of Symmetry-Preserving Variational Quantum Circuits

Sahinur Reja

Symmetry-preserving (SP) variational quantum circuits restrict evolution to physically relevant sectors of Hilbert space, but symmetry preservation alone does not guarantee accessibility within those sectors. We show that a restricted generator set can possess a residual dynamical Lie algebra whose commutant further decomposes the target symmetry sector into invariant blocks. The associated block populations are conserved and impose a kinematic upper bound on the fidelity with a target state. We then invert this diagnostic into a circuit-design principle: candidate SP generators can be screened before variational optimization by asking whether they break the residual commutant, which invariant blocks they couple, and what adjoint module they generate under the residual algebra. For two orthogonal residual blocks we prove that a single elementary real inter-block rotation generates the full orthogonal algebra. We further derive a complex escape criterion: if the real and imaginary parts of an inter-block seed are independent over the residual intertwiner algebra, the generated module is the full complex coupling space and the enlarged algebra becomes special unitary. A connected residual-block graph yields a natural multi-block generalization and suggests minimum-cost spanning-tree circuit design. We demonstrate this design principle in the XXZ and Hubbard models, where residual-algebra screening identifies SP circuit extensions that remove the relevant accessibility obstructions. These examples establish a unified route from residual-symmetry diagnosis and fidelity bounds to predictive quantum circuit design.

CREST: A Hardware-Aware GNN-Based Framework for High-Fidelity and Robust Superconducting Quantum Compilation

Wenjie Sun, Xiaoyu Li, Zhigang Wang, Yuchen He, Shengbin Wang et al. (6 authors)

Quantum compilation on superconducting processors requires selecting a connected execution region whose topology and calibrated properties suit the input circuit. This choice constrains subsequent routing and influences the noise encountered during execution, yet aggregate hardware-quality metrics alone may not reliably predict the execution fidelity of a given circuit. Calibration updates can also change region suitability and trigger the rebuilding of calibration-dependent resource structures. We present CREST, a graph neural network (GNN)-based framework that evaluates circuit-region compatibility before routing. It encodes gate dependencies and logical-qubit interactions alongside each candidate region's connectivity and current calibration attributes; bidirectional cross-graph attention relates the two representations, while real-device execution outcomes supervise candidate ranking. The trained scorer is reused with updated calibration attributes. Evaluations on 324 circuits from seven datasets on TianYan-176 show that CREST improves mean measurement-distribution fidelity by 19.03% over the strongest baseline and achieves the highest mean fidelity on every dataset. Furthermore, using updated calibration attributes, the same trained model maintains the highest overall mean fidelity across six measurement dates without retraining. With a unified model of circuit-region compatibility, CREST improves execution fidelity across diverse workloads and sustains that advantage as hardware calibration changes.

Quantum Simulation of First-Quantised Chemistry with a Sum-of-Squares Representation

Aleksei V. Ivanov, Christoph Sünderhauf, Jon Kunjummen, Nick S. Blunt, Róbert Izsák

Calculating molecular energies is expected to be an important application of quantum computers, but the most efficient algorithms to achieve this typically have significant space requirements. Scaling quantum processors to thousands of logical qubits is a daunting engineering challenge, so developing algorithms that run with reduced qubit requirements is of particular importance. Moreover, most algorithms are benchmarked on active spaces with about as many orbitals as electrons, missing dynamical correlation which is necessary to describe real chemistry. Capturing this correlation requires several times more orbitals than electrons, even in compact atomic-orbital basis sets. This low-filling regime favours first quantisation, where each electron is represented by a number of qubits that grows only logarithmically with the number of orbitals. Whether this approach translates into a practical advantage in orbital basis sets of realistic size, however, has been unclear. Here, we develop a first-quantised phase estimation algorithm for generic orbital basis sets, based on a non-convex sum-of-squares representation of the Hamiltonian and spectral-gap amplification. We compare it with the state-of-the-art second-quantised algorithm, compiling both with the same techniques at the logical level. For six molecules with 4 to 24 electrons in 5 to 14 times as many orbitals, our first-quantised method needs up to 16 times fewer Toffoli gates than the second-quantised algorithm in the low-qubit regime, where only a few hundred logical qubits are available. It stays cheaper up to between several hundred and about 1\,400 qubits, and its smallest qubit count is 1.4 to 2 times lower than in second quantisation. In the low-qubit and low-filling regime, our algorithm is therefore the method of choice for chemistry in orbital basis sets.

Hurst Exponent Estimation with Quantum Wavelets

Brani Vidakovic

How accurately can the Hurst exponent $H$ of fractional Brownian motion be estimated from repeated preparations of a single amplitude-encoded path, when every measurement interrogates the same realization? We extend the $ε$-decimated quantum nondecimated wavelet transform in three directions: we remove the endpoint jump of circular filtering, derive the sampling distribution of the estimator, and compare readouts at equal query budgets. A one-qubit mirror extension removes the jump and keeps every coefficient that straddles a fold of the same order as an interior coefficient, for any compactly supported filter and circular shift. The transform for any such filter is compiled from the paraunitary lattice of its filter bank, and the energy share of a level is the success probability of one multiply-controlled reflection, which is both the controlled unitary of a Hadamard test and the oracle of amplitude estimation. For filters with at least two vanishing moments we prove a root-$N$ central limit theorem for the log-energy contrasts, independent of the limiting path norm, so the studentized estimator is asymptotically standard normal for deterministic query budgets under which the success count at every level diverges. For sparse counts we give a working multinomial--lognormal likelihood, exact in its readout model for direct measurement, and check its coverage by simulation. For paths of length $512$, mirroring reduces RMSE by a factor of $3$--$10$ for $H\ge0.5$, and with exact shares the nondecimated db2 estimator is $6$--$14\%$ above the root Cramér--Rao bound. Hadamard level tests are less accurate than direct measurement of the leading qubits at every budget. At $H=0.9$ and $2^{20}$ queries, maximum-likelihood amplitude estimation attains RMSE $0.0347$, against $0.0451$ for direct measurement and $0.0295$ with exact shares.

From geometric curvature to effective backgrounds: a dictionary for Dirac fermions in one, two and three spatial dimensions

David Valenzuela, Alfredo Raya

Dirac equation on curved backgrounds can be rewritten as a flat-space equation in which geometry appears as a tensorial Fermi velocity together with a set of background fields. We derive this correspondence from a single Clifford-algebra identity and follow it through one, two, and three spatial dimensions. Once the spin connection is written in terms of Ricci rotation coefficients, only two contractions can occur. The trace produces a term with the index structure of a vector potential in every dimensionality; it enters with an imaginary factor i and is precisely the term that keeps the operator Hermitian when the Fermi velocity depends on position. The totally antisymmetric contraction produces an object that depends on the number of indices available to the Levi-Civita symbol: a trace on a line, a pseudoscalar mass on a surface, and an axial vector potential in a volume. Neither contraction is invariant under local rotations of the frame, and we give the transformation laws. On a surface, the curl of the trace term can always be removed by a frame rotation, while its divergence is proportional to the Gaussian curvature. We apply the correspondence to a Gaussian bump, where the local density of states at the center falls below its flat value and obeys a closed form at low energy; to a wire, where a nonuniform lapse binds states inside the gap and a nonuniform velocity cannot; to stationary frames on a surface, where a circulating drag shifts the Dirac mass locally with either sign; and to twisted and rotating frames in a volume, where the axial term takes the form of a chiral chemical potential produced by the frame alone, or separates Weyl nodes in a rotating sample.
