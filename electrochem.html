From picometre displacements to a three-dimensional lattice gas of polar skyrmions

Joohee Bang, Nives Strkalj, Hugo Aramberri, Stephan Rosenkranz, Arkadiy Simonov et al. (7 authors)

Polar skyrmions, the electric analogues of magnetic skyrmions, form arrays in PbTiO3/SrTiO3 superlattices that are known to be only short-range ordered, but how this disordered ensemble is organized in three dimensions, and how its microscopic structure underlies this organization, have remained largely inaccessible. Here, using three-dimensional difference pair distribution function (3D-ΔPDF) analysis of X-ray diffuse scattering, we show that the polar skyrmion bubbles form a three-dimensional lattice gas: they occupy discrete sites, but their order extends over only a few neighbours and is two to three times stronger across the SrTiO3 spacers than within a layer. This interlayer coupling is consistent with the polarization of the bubbles extending into the spacers. Within each bubble, the polarization rotates in a predominantly Néel-type manner, and Pb and Ti rotate together with comparable picometre displacements, demonstrating that both A- and B-site cations take part in the polar distortion.

Adding Temperature to the Adiabatic Connection: Hybrid Functionals for Static and Dynamic Correlation

Florian Matz, Paul G. Graf, Markus Penz, Christian Schilling

Capturing static and dynamic electron correlation simultaneously at manageable computational cost remains a key challenge in electronic structure theory. Motivated by the recurring role of effective temperature in self-consistent field methods we extend the adiabatic connection mechanism from density functional theory to include a temperature dimension. This gives rise to thermally enhanced hybrid functionals, in which static correlation is captured via fractional occupations generated by an effective "correlation temperature" within thermal Hartree-Fock, while dynamic correlation is described by established density functional approximations. The framework formally unifies information-density matrix functional theory (i-DMFT) and thermally-assisted-occupation (TAO-)DFT as special cases, clarifying their theoretical basis and the respective prescriptions for the temperature parameter. We assess the performance of several thermally enhanced hybrids for different cases where i-DMFT was previously shown to fail. Furthermore, we discuss and test different schemes for determining the correlation temperature and investigate the accuracy of different such choices for a benchmark set derived from biphenyl. The results help to clarify the achievable balance of accuracy, generality, and efficiency, establishing thermally enhanced hybrids as a systematic route toward low-cost treatment of both correlation types.

Learning the information contained in the microstructure of data distributions

Lucrezia L. Baldo, Chiara Lionello, Edward D. Donkor, Matteo Becchi, Giovanni M. Pavan

Understanding how much and where information is nested in sets of data obtained using different parameters and metrics is a typical problem in the analysis of high-dimensional datasets. Approaches based on relative data-driven metrics may be poorly quantitative and difficult to interpret, while accurate methods may be computationally expensive and impractical for large datasets. Here, we report an efficient, quantitative, and transferable method useful to this end, which we call "Micro-Information Analysis". Its foundation is based on the tight relationship that exists between the microscopic features of data distributions and the information they contain. The method performs preliminary unsupervised clustering, capturing microstructural features of data distributions such as the dominant Gaussian microdomains (microstates) present in them, their population, separation, and their residual non-Gaussian fraction. Leveraging the principle of clustering-and-minimization of Shannon entropy, this allows quantifying in bit (that is, in absolute metric) the information contained in the microstructure of the data, and how much information is nested in each Gaussian and non-Gaussian microdomain. The output of the analysis is a set of easily interpretable Information-Microstructure plots that allow to readily compare and rank data based on their information content and to graphically map the information breakdown among their various microenvironments in diagrams that are easy to interpret. We prove the efficiency of our method by analyzing and ranking data generated synthetically or from molecular simulations, demonstrating how "Micro-Information Analysis" is a physically-grounded quantitative tool useful for the study of physical systems from their data and for data science in general.

Pt-induced semimetallic transport and enhanced thermoelectric response in the half-Heusler alloy YNi_{0.5}Pt_{0.5}Bi

Vikas Chahar, Shivam Rathod, Lankun Wang, Sneh, Shubhadeep Bej et al. (14 authors)

Half-Heusler alloys provide a versatile platform for tuning electronic and thermal transport through chemical substitution. Here, we investigate the thermoelectric and magnetotransport properties of spark-plasma-sintered YNi_{0.5}Pt_{0.5}Bi. Partial substitution of Ni by Pt drives YNiBi toward semimetallic transport, characterized by hole-dominated conduction, a low Hall carrier concentration of $1.9 \times 10^{19 cm^{-3}}$, and a sizeable nonsaturating magnetoresistance of 18$\%$ at 300 K under 14 T. Compared with YNiBi, Pt alloying increases the room-temperature Seebeck coefficient from approximately 90 to 130 $μV K^{-1}$, while reducing the thermal conductivity to 3.15 $W m^{-1 K^{-1}}$. Single-parabolic-band analysis indicates an enhanced carrier effective mass, resulting in a room-temperature power factor of 13.7 $μW cm^{-1 K^{-2}}$ and a figure of merit ($zT$= 0.13). First-principles calculations further show a Pt-induced closing of the electronic band gap and a low density of states near the Fermi level, consistent with the experimentally observed semimetal-like transport. These results demonstrate that Ni/Pt alloying provides an effective route for simultaneously tuning the electronic, magnetotransport, and thermoelectric properties of YNiBi-based half-Heusler compounds.

The role of friction on heave-induced motion of offshore drilling systems: modelling, analysis, and numerical experiments

Luigi Romano, Ole Morten Aamo

This paper investigates the influence of friction dynamics on heave-induced disturbance propagation using a coupled hydraulic-structural drilling model formulated as a semilinear interconnection of ordinary and hyperbolic partial differential equations (ODE-PDEs), and capturing spatially distributed memory effects in the drillstring-wellbore interaction. First, local and global existence and uniqueness results are established, and sufficient conditions for boundedness and input-to-state stability are deduced via a small-gain analysis. Subsequently, extensive numerical investigations are carried out, including harmonic excitation studies, nonlinear distortion analysis, spatial amplification investigations, mechanism-isolation experiments, parameter sensitivity analysis, and broadband simulations under realistic offshore sea-state excitation. The simulation results indicate that, whilst the hydraulic response is governed primarily by wave propagation phenomena, compressibility, and boundary reflections, the structural dynamics display substantially greater sensitivity to distributed friction effects. Furthermore, drillstring-wellbore friction is shown to have a relatively limited influence on well-posedness but a potentially significant impact on stability margins. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for coupled hydraulic-structural dynamics and distributed friction mechanisms when analysing, monitoring, and controlling offshore drilling systems.

Simulated Solvation Dynamics of a Photoexcited Organic Molecule and the Associated Time-Resolved X-ray Scattering Signal

Simon Petry, Magnús Kristinsson, Asmus O. Dohn, Klaus B. Møller, Elli Selenius et al. (6 authors)

Solvation shell reorganization is a key component of the dynamics after photoexcitation of a solvated molecule, especially for charge-transfer excitation in polar solvents. These structural dynamics can be directly observed with time-resolved X-ray solution scattering (TR-XSS) experiments. However, disentangling the scattering signal of solvation-shell reorganization can be challenging, especially for systems containing only light atoms. Here, nonequilibrium molecular dynamics simulations of a hemithioindigo chromophore in acetonitrile are used to investigate the solvation dynamics upon photoinduced charge transfer as well as vibrational excitation and cooling, and to characterize the corresponding time-resolved X-ray scattering signals. It is found that charge transfer induces rapid reorientation of the first solvation shell (0.15-0.2 ps) followed by slower reorientational and solvent redistribution within and between shells (1-2 ps). An analysis of the volume around the solute excluded to the solvent reveals an initial contraction of the solvation shell, followed by transient rebound within the first 0.4 ps, and then slower contraction. Instantaneous vibrational excitation expands the excluded volume, reaching a maximum near 0.4 ps, followed by contraction during cooling with biexponential relaxation. Low-q features in both the calculated solute-solvent and solvent-solvent scattering signals reflect rearrangements within the first solvation shell and redistribution across shells, while higher-q solute-solvent features more closely follow local first-shell restructuring. These results show that TR-XSS can provide information on both local solvent rearrangements and redistribution across solvation shells and demonstrate that interpreting the scattering signals requires accounting for changes in solvent packing induced by vibrational heating and cooling of the solute.

Putting a Temperature on Electron Correlation

Paul G. Graf, Florian Matz, Markus Penz, Christian Schilling

It is common wisdom that a better understanding of how to quantify electron correlation should lead to more efficient treatments of the correlation problem. Here, we not only substantiate this view but turn it into a concrete, systematic framework for ground states. Since prominent correlation measures such as the nonfreeness depend only on the one-particle reduced density matrix, modeling the correlation energy as a function of such a measure naturally places the framework within reduced density matrix functional theory, while keeping the cost at mean-field level. Strikingly, the variable conjugate to the measure emerges as a ``correlation temperature'', thereby connecting correlation measures, functional theory, and effective temperatures. The framework rationalizes methods such as i-DMFT, and we illustrate its key concepts analytically for the Hubbard dimer and numerically for small molecular systems. This brings a fundamental question within reach: how much of the correlation problem can be solved at mean-field cost?

Rovibrational cross sections for the CO$_2$ $00^00$, $01^10$, $02^20$ and Fermi resonance states $10^00(1)$ and $10^00(2)$ in collisions with He atoms

Jelena Bošković, Taha Selim, Ad van der Avoird, Floris F. S. van der Tak, Gerrit C. Groenenboom

We present quantum scattering calculations of CO$_2$-He state-to-state integral cross sections for 25 transition sets, covering pure rotational transitions within and rovibrational transitions between: the ground state $00^00$, the bend fundamental $01^10$, the bending overtone $02^20$, and the Fermi doublet states $10^00(1)$ and $10^00(2)$. Exact coupled-channel (CC) and coupled-states approximation (CSA) calculations are performed using a new five-dimensional potential energy surface. The calculations cover collision energies from 20 to 5220 cm$^{-1}$, sufficient to obtain converged rate coefficients over 100-800 K, and include initial rotational states for each transition up to at least $j=35$. We present an energy-grid algorithm for rovibrational quantum scattering calculations that minimizes the number of separate calculations required. The resulting cross sections exhibit a dominant $Δj=0$ propensity and parity-dependent patterns. For the benchmark $00^00,j' \leftarrow 01^10,j$ transition, thermally averaged rate coefficients reproduce available experimental data. A comparison between the CC and CSA cross sections shows that, for vibrational quenching transitions, the CSA cross sections increasingly deviate from the CC results with increasing initial rotational state $j$. Calculated integral cross sections cover collision energies and rotational states needed to derive rate coefficients for non local thermal equilibrium radiative transfer modeling of mid-infrared CO$_2$ spectra. The computationally simpler CO$_2$-He system allows the methodology and approximations to be tested at high collision energies and rotational states, helping assess their suitability for the more complex and astrochemically important CO$_2$-H$_2$ system.

Plasma-Enabled Wideband Matching of Electrically Small Antennas

Kushagra Singhal, Abbas Semnani

This paper presents a plasma-based approach for wideband impedance matching of electrically small antennas (ESAs). By exploiting the negative permittivity of a capacitively coupled plasma (CCP), the plasma is shown to function as a wideband negative capacitor that effectively compensates for the large and frequency-dependent reactance inherent to ESAs. A VHF/UHF electrically small capped dipole antenna is used as a proof-of-concept platform to demonstrate the proposed technique. Theoretical analysis, Multiphysics simulations, and experimental measurements confirm broadband impedance matching, with -6 dB fractional bandwidth that can exceed 100\%, using only a few watts of plasma power. The matching bandwidth can be dynamically tuned by adjusting the plasma operating conditions, gas pressure, and electron density, enabling control over the trade-off between bandwidth and radiation efficiency. This plasma-enabled matching paradigm provides a physically robust and scalable solution for wideband ESAs, particularly attractive for low-frequency and high-power applications where conventional matching techniques are fundamentally limited.

Vertical ionization energies from inverse generalized Kohn-Sham calculations at fixed electron density

Vishal Subramanian, Bikash Kanungo, John P. Perdew, Vikram Gavini, Gabor I. Csonka

The exact Kohn--Sham (KS) system must have its highest occupied orbital energy equal to the negative of the lowest vertical ionization energy (VIE). No comparable theorem connects the deeper occupied levels $\varepsilon_k$ to the higher VIEs $I_k$. Chong, Gritsenko and Baerends (CGB) established empirically that $I_k + \varepsilon_k\propto\sqrt{\varepsilon_{HOMO}-\varepsilon_k}$. We ask whether a similar relation holds for the exact generalized Kohn-Sham (GKS) system, which incorporates a fraction $α$ of Hartree-Fock exchange. We invert near-exact CCSD/aug-cc-pVQZ ground-state densities of LiH, LiF, H$_2$O, HF, CO, N$_2$ and F$_2$ at $α= 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0$ and obtain the exact GKS systems at each admixture. As the density is held fixed and only the operator is varied, the resulting occupied spectra separate the effect of exact-exchange admixture on orbital energies from its effect on the density, two contributions inseparable in a self-consistent hybrid calculation. The CGB relation holds broadly across the family, with a proportionality coefficient linear in $α$, $c(α)=c(0)-sα$, where $c(0)=1.185\pm0.017$ eV$^{1/2}$ and $s=1.987\pm0.041$ eV$^{1/2}$, with $R^{2}\ge0.9996$ for H$_2$O, CO, HF and N$_2$. Using the response-potential matrix elements tabulated by Gritsenko, Braïda and Baerends, we decompose the CGB coefficient into response and spectroscopic parts, and find $s$ to agree with the homogeneous-electron-gas step-height coefficient $K_g = 1.9932$ eV$^{1/2}$, which we conjecture is its origin. The coefficient vanishes at $α^{*}=0.597\pm0.008$. At $α^{*}$ the HOMO-referenced GKS occupied levels satisfy $\varepsilon_k\approx-I_k$ across the whole occupied range. The zero crossing is fixed by the core orbitals; in the outer valence the CGB coefficient is already indistinguishable from zero at $α=0$.
