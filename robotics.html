Is Your Robot's Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies

Chubin Zhang, Bo An

Memory-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) policies are meant to remember what happened. Many of them, however, fill memory by time: they sample frames uniformly across the episode and stamp each frame with its time step. Because time passes even when nothing happens, such a memory may not tell waiting from working. We show that it does not. On the counting tasks of RoboMME, we make the robot briefly pause or fidget its wrist. Such a null event changes nothing the task depends on, yet a released memory VLA counts the interval as work done and stops early. We call this failure phantom progress. It cuts success from 47 to 16 of 50 on one task and from 44 to 10 on the other. When the same frames are withheld from memory, success returns, so the cause lies in memory rather than in the interruption itself. We trace the failure to a temporal shortcut: because demonstrations proceed at a steady pace, elapsed time predicts progress. Indeed, rescaling the time stamps alone makes the policy stop early when the clock runs fast and repeat when it runs slow. Surprisingly, removing the time encoding does not cure the failure, because the frames of a null event still occupy slots of the time-sampled memory. Across RoboMME's released designs, vulnerability follows how memory is indexed. Of the fixes the diagnosis suggests, training with injected pauses does not remove the failure and impairs counting, whereas indexing memory by events recovers half or more of the loss without retraining. A memory meant to remember what happened should count events rather than clock ticks.

TanCRAFT: Context-Reasoned Anticipatory Feedback for Assembly Tasks

Sassan Mokhtar, Lars Doorenbos, Marius Bock, Sina Mokhtar, Katharina Beckh et al. (7 authors)

Preventing human error in physical assembly tasks requires assistance systems that can intervene before mistakes are made. This is especially challenging in settings where users are free to choose the construction order and correctness depends on the evolving assembly rather than a fixed next step. We present TanCRAFT, a four-stage real-time predictive assistance system that tracks the scene state, reasons about the current partial assembly, forecasts the near-future motion of manipulated pieces, and intervenes when the predicted placement is incompatible with the target. We instantiate our framework and confirm its effectiveness in a controlled tangram assembly task through offline benchmarks and a within-subject user study. Our findings show that the predictive feedback significantly reduces mistakes relative to unguided and reactive sessions, while yielding the lowest mean perceived workload and shortest mean completion time. Overall, these results demonstrate the value of predictive feedback for error prevention and provide a step toward cost-aware assistance for automation and human-robot workcells.

UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model

Amani Kiruga, Zhiyi Li, Ruojin Cai, Hansen Lillemark, Xiaoshan Wu et al. (7 authors)

Understanding 4D scenes is a core challenge in visual intelligence, requiring models to jointly capture scene geometry, appearance, and dynamics from partial observations. However, existing approaches are subdivided into feedforward models for direct geometric prediction, and generative models for visual synthesis. We introduce UGG, a unified framework for 4D scene understanding that jointly models RGB video and geometric representations such as depth and camera pose. By assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, UGG formulates visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion within a single generative model. This flexible formulation enables the model to learn from diverse, heterogeneous datasets with different subsets of geometric and visual annotations. Furthermore, our generative formulation enables inference-time search over multiple plausible scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence extracted from observed RGB frames to improve geometric prediction without ground truth geometry. We demonstrate UGG's effectiveness over non-unified baselines across camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, including in challenging sparse observation settings. Code and visualizations are available at https://ugg-model.github.io/

Cascading Contingency Analysis of Modern Power Systems Using a Stochastic Hybrid Systems Framework

Masoud H. Nazari, Hamid Varmazyari

This paper presents a cascading contingency analysis and risk assessment framework that incorporates probabilistic mode transitions motivated by stochastic hybrid system (SHS) concepts together with power-flow-based stress evaluation. A two-stage modeling approach is adopted in which sensitivity-based analysis is first used to identify candidate cascading transitions, followed by DC power flow re-evaluation to accurately capture post-contingency stress redistribution, islanding behavior, and load shedding. A probabilistic propagation model is constructed using mode-dependent transition probabilities derived from line loading conditions. A Cascading Risk Index (CRI) is introduced to quantify the expected load shedding accumulated over cascade progression. Case studies on the IEEE 39-bus system demonstrate that the proposed framework captures diverse cascading routes under multiple initiating outages and identifies critical propagation patterns that are not revealed by N-1/N-2 screening. The results highlight the importance of SHS modeling in assessing cascading risk and providing a scalable framework for resilience evaluation in modern power systems.

One Policy for 100 Household Tasks: Our Solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge

Ilia Larchenko, Gleb Zarin, Atindra Nair, Keerti Manney, Vitaly Kleban et al. (7 authors)

We describe our solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge: 100 long-horizon household tasks in the OmniGibson simulator. We placed 9th on the final leaderboard, the only independent team in the top 10. The core of the solution is a single vision-language-action (VLA) model, one checkpoint for all 100 tasks, built on our 1st-place 2025 solution, which is based on $π_{0.5}$. On the 50 tasks shared with 2025, its Q rises from 0.260 to 0.371; across all 100 tasks it scores 0.392. Our main conclusion from 2025 was that the model was undertrained, so most of the 2026 work went into training the model for longer, optimizing some training parameters, making each training step slightly faster and reducing the gradient noise: a migration from 50 to 100 tasks (semantic stages built from the demonstrations' skill annotations, task and stage embeddings of new tasks warm-started from similar old ones, and a 21-dimensional action space matching the teleoperation controls); variance-reduced multi-sample flow matching with a simple noise-vs-compute model that picks the number of action-expert samples per observation without ablations; and a 200k-step training run, about one pass over the 211M frames of the demonstrations. A cloud evaluation infrastructure keeps a shared record with provenance, episode diagnostics and restorable simulator states, and runs six simulators on one GPU together faster than real time. On top of the learned policy, 14 tasks are routed to a GPT-6 Astra coding agent that controls the robot through observation and motion tools, starting every episode from a frozen copy of its own practice session on training instances. Across these 14 tasks, Astra's mean Q is 0.639, against 0.108 for the learned policy. The public submission included only part of the Astra results and scored 0.446 Q with 27.3% full-task success.

Analysis and Demonstration of a Current-Stiff, Isolated Capacitive Bridge Transformer

Patrick Nowakowski, Cameron Little, Mahima Gupta, Daniel C. Ludois

The traditional Voltage Source Inverter (VSI) based Dual Active Bridge (DAB) utilizes a transformer at its center to provide galvanic isolation and a turns ratio for step up/down functionality. However, the transformer is often the limiting factor of the DAB, especially as semiconductor switching speeds improve, given steel core losses, winding losses, etc. This paper presents a new DAB architecture based on Current Source Inverters (CSIs) coupled through, and isolated by, an AC capacitive bridge, yielding a system called a Capacitive Bridge Transformer (CBT). The CBT architecture eliminates all magnetic components in the AC portion of the isolated DAB circuit, reducing frequency scaling barriers normally present in DABs. The paper introduces the CBT from the perspective of duality and then develops the DC-DC power flow equations, the internal apparent power equations, and effective turns ratio. The analytical models are verified by steady state and detailed time domain measurements on a 2 kW experimental prototype.

SIGMO: Sigmoid Gated Interaction-Aware Motion Planning

Avinash Singh, Manish Saroya, Fatemeh Cheraghi Pouria, Faizan Tariq, Jovin D'sa et al. (8 authors)

Despite the widespread adoption of autonomous vehicle technologies, reliable navigation within cluttered environments remains arduous due to the necessity of interaction-aware strategies; example scenarios include: narrow passages with multiple agents, lane changing in dense traffic, and crossing unsignalized intersections. These scenarios often require highly proactive strategies, simultaneously demanding interpretability and quick adaptability, which challenge end-to-end and data-driven methods. An effective strategy requires: scalability to multiple agents, adaptability to changes in other agents' behavior, and harmony with surrounding traffic without freezing or becoming overly conservative. To meet these requirements, we present SIGMO, a sigmoid-gated interaction-aware motion planner, with two main contributions. First, we formulate polynomial sigmoid functions that analytically estimate yield scores of surrounding agents using physics-grounded interaction features that capture driving intuitions. Second, we integrate the polynomial sigmoid functions into a receding-horizon planner, which facilitates interaction-aware planning with arbitrary trajectory predictions. We validate SIGMO in interaction-intensive simulations and hardware experiments. The results show that SIGMO balances proactive and defensive behavior while remaining computationally lightweight, serving as a strong baseline in a mobility stack. The simulation and hardware experiment videos are available here: https://sites.google.com/view/sigmoid-polygate.

Uncertainty-Aware Hybrid Offline Reinforcement Learning with Online Action Selection Enabled by Sparse Gaussian-Process Critics

Marco Chan, Christos Evripidou, Liqiu Dong, Benoit Chachuat, Mehmet Mercangöz

Start-ups and grade changes remain largely operator-driven, while developing and maintaining dynamic models for model-based control requires substantial engineering effort. Hybrid Offline Learning and Online Optimisation (HOFLON) uses one-step offline reinforcement learning to select actions from historical transition data. In its original implementation, the online objective combines the predicted return from a tree-ensemble critic with an adversarial-autoencoder (AAE) penalty for unfamiliar state-action pairs. We examine the effect of this penalty and replace the critic and AAE with a sparse variational Gaussian-process (sGP) critic. Actions maximise $μ- κσ$, an uncertainty-adjusted estimate of the return from one action followed by the historical operating practice. On the HOFLON polymerisation-reactor benchmark, removing the AAE from HOFLON led to 4 failed start-ups in 15 runs over five critics. At its published weight, the variation in the AAE penalty is four orders of magnitude smaller than the variation in the tree-ensemble critic. Nevertheless, it changes actions when the predicted returns of competing candidates are nearly equal, and paired runs with and without the penalty diverged from the first move. With a Matérn-5/2 sGP critic, $κ= 0$ led to failure in 13 of 15 runs, whereas with $κ= 3$ none of 15 runs (five critics, three noise realisations) failed. The median and lowest returns were 13,464 and 13,398 (HOFLON: 13,393 and 12,411). A squared-exponential sGP critic with learned inducing inputs needed $κ\geq 3$ to remain within the empirical data support in steady state, and then settled with a temperature offset of 2.0 K.

Gaussian Process-Augmented Model Predictive Contouring Control for 6-DoF Trajectory Tracking with a Holonomic AUV

David Dörner, Giancarlo Troni, Ignacio Torroba, Ivan Stenius

Small, holonomic Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) enable close-range inspection of underwater structures from arbitrary viewpoints, requiring accurate position and attitude tracking under state and input constraints. Model Predictive Contouring Control (MPCC) naturally handles these requirements, but relies on an accurate dynamics model, which is difficult to obtain underwater. In this paper, we present a nonlinear MPCC in all six Degrees of Freedom (DoF) for a holonomic AUV and alleviate the inaccuracies in the dynamics by augmenting the nominal model employed with residual dynamics learned via Gaussian Process (GP) regression. The learned residual is embedded in the optimal control problem through a zero-order robust optimization scheme, which keeps the controller real-time capable at 25 Hz. A simulation ablation shows that residual selection is essential when designing the controller, and simulation and in-water experiments with the Multimodality, Observing, Low-cost, Agile (MOLA) AUV validate the resulting controllers against a nominal MPCC and a field proven Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller.

Reliable and explainable analysis of AVS/RS by Statistical Model Checking and Process Mining

Emanuele Guerrazzi, Andrea Vandin

Automated Vehicle Storage and Retrieval Systems (AVS/RS) are increasingly adopted to boost warehouse throughput while containing operational costs. We present an integrated analysis framework that couples discrete-event simulation with Statistical Model Checking (SMC) and Process Mining (PM) to quantify and explain AVS/RS performance. SMC, implemented through the MultiVeStA tool, delivers statistically reliable estimates of key indicators, such as cycle times, throughput, and storage utilization, across alternative stocking and picking policies with user-specified confidence levels. MultiVeStA orchestrates multiple independent simulations in a black-box manner, enabling automated verification. PM ingests the resulting event logs to uncover the behavioral patterns and bottlenecks that drive those indicators, closing the interpretability gap typical of simulation studies. We instantiate the framework on a new open-source AVS/RS simulator configured according to recent industrial guidelines and evaluate multiple control strategies. Results show that the joint use of SMC and PM enables both predictive what-if analyses and post-hoc diagnostics, providing the first combined SMC+PM assessment of AVS/RS operations and a reusable toolchain for automated warehousing research.
