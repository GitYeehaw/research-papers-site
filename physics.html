The double copy and perturbative teleparallel gravity

Georg Stettinger

In this paper we analyze the double copy between gluon and graviton amplitudes within the context of teleparallel gravity. We first set up an effective Lagrangian based on minimal assumptions which reproduces the on-shell amplitudes stemming from the double-copy procedure. By straightforwardly computing the three- and four-point amplitude, this double-copy Lagrangian can be constrained up to a few real parameters when expanding to quartic order. For a specific choice of parameters, we find an exact equivalence with perturbative teleparallel gravity. This suggests that the teleparallel formulation might be well suited to make the double copy more transparent from a Lagrangian viewpoint.

$QQ\bar Q\bar Q$ Quark System and Gauge/String Duality II: String Breaking

Oleg Andreev

This is the second of two companion papers in which we continue developing the construction of the fully heavy tetraquark system using the gauge/string duality. Here, we extend this construction to model $SU(3)$ gauge theory with two light flavors of equal mass. As an application, we analyze the two lowest Born-Oppenheimer potentials, both analytically and numerically, for rectangular and collinear geometries. Our analysis reveals several critical scales associated with string reconnection and breaking, as well as junction annihilation and pinching. In addition, we find that the string tension is universal, whereas the string breaking distance is not.

Prospects and limitations of adaptive variational quantum algorithms for lattice field theories: a case study in 2+1 dimensions

Nico Dichter, Andrea Bulgarelli, Lena Funcke, Stefan Kühn

The variational quantum algorithm ADAPT-VQE has previously been used to prepare the vacuum of lattice gauge theories in $1+1D$. In this paper, we study whether it extends to higher dimensions. We identify two general limitations of ADAPT-VQE. First, if the qubit Hamiltonian and the initial state are real, i.e., invariant under qubit-level time-reversal, all real-valued pool operators have vanishing gradients and are never selected. Second, the algorithm can converge to excited eigenstates. We illustrate both limitations, also providing examples from literature, and present a first application of ADAPT-VQE to a lattice field theory in $2+1D$, namely free Wilson fermions, where both obstacles occur. Adding charge conjugation symmetry to the operator pool improves the results, and combining different pools with suitable initial states recovers the ground and first excited states over the full mass range. These findings give practical guidelines for applying ADAPT-VQE to lattice field theories beyond $1+1D$.

A Koashi--Imoto Criterion for Holevo-Rate Visible Compression

Anthony M. Polloreno

For a finite memoryless quantum source whose encoder knows the sequence of classical labels, we determine when the Holevo rate is achievable with shared classical randomness and no shared entanglement. In the maximal Koashi--Imoto decomposition of the signal ensemble, this occurs exactly when each signal is pure on the quantum part of every sector it occupies. For qubits, this means that the signals are all pure or commute pairwise. If any occupied quantum part is mixed, every code with sufficiently small mean local error has rate bounded away from the Holevo information, uniformly in blocklength. To prove the converse, we apply Fawzi--Renner recovery to a hypothetical sequence of visible codes approaching the Holevo rate at vanishing local error, then invoke a decoupling bound of Baghali Khanian and Winter for blind compression.

Classical algorithms for sparse random Hamiltonians via free probability

Matthew Ding, Robbie King, Thomas Schuster

The search for quantum advantage in average-case problems is a pressing open question in physics and computer science. The widest conjectured example of such an advantage is in simulation of quantum many-body systems, where sign problems and entanglement growth cause the breakdown of many classical methods. Nonetheless, theoretical evidence for or against such an advantage remains scarce. Here, we investigate this in sparse random non-local Hamiltonians. We design a classical algorithm for such Hamiltonians that enables accurate computation of spectral fluctuations, thermal values, and time-ordered and out-of-time-order correlation functions. For any subexponential precision $\varepsilon$, our algorithm runs in time $(1/\varepsilon)^{2+o(1)}$, nearly identical to a quantum simulation. Crucially, this applies even at late evolution times, low temperatures, and near critical points, where existing classical methods require exponential time. Rather than directly simulating a quantum state or operator, our algorithm first computes the model's mean behavior and then a short hierarchy of instance-dependent corrections. These corrections predict long-time behavior based on short-time data using free probability theory.

Scalar perturbations in extremal Reissner-Nordström geometry: new methods at work

Giorgio Di Russo, Davide Fioravanti, Francesco Olivi, Marco Rossi

We study scalar radiation emitted by a particle moving along a circular orbit around a four-dimensional extremal Reissner-Nordström black hole. In particular, we calculate the energy fluxes carried by scalar waves to infinity and across the event horizon, as well as the scalar self-force acting on the particle, and express all these quantities as post-Newtonian expansions. Our results for the energy fluxes are in agreement with previous numerical findings in the literature. To solve the radial wave equation, we employ and test two distinct analytic approaches: the first is the so-called kink method, a novel alternative stemming from quantum integrability and the second is the Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi method. The perfect agreement of the results highlights the potential of the kink method as a complementary tool for studying wave propagation in black-hole spacetimes.

Anomalies and the Berry Phase: From $π^0\toγγ$ to the geometry of the fermion vacuum

Spenta R. Wadia

We review the chiral anomaly from a historical perspective, from the $π^0\toγγ$ puzzle and the work of Adler, Bell and Jackiw, to the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term. We then describe how the WZW term was motivated from quantum chromodynamics in 1983-85. Integrating out the non-Abelian gauge fields, that have a gapped spectrum, gives a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio type theory of quarks, and integrating out the quarks, which are gapped by chiral symmetry breaking, gives the WZW model. The fermion determinant splits into a regulator-dependent modulus, which gives the dynamics of mesons, and a universal phase, which is fixed by the anomaly and is the WZW term. Restricted to the collective coordinates of the baryon soliton, the WZW term imposes a U(1) constraint that makes the baryon wave functions monopole harmonics and fixes their spin, statistics and flavour multiplets. We then explain that this phase is a Berry phase: the Schwinger terms of current algebra are the curvature of a U(1) connection on the space of chiral field configurations, and the WZW term is its holonomy, which we call a Berry-Wilson loop. In 1+1 dimensions we write the connection explicitly in terms of Euler angles, exhibiting a Dirac monopole whose quantised charge is the level of the current algebra.

Robust growth of $α$-tantalum on sapphire at room temperature using atomically thin titanium seed layer

Anand Kamlapure, Lucas Hak, Kishore Kumar Thiyagarajan, Hozanna Miro, Marc Beekman et al. (7 authors)

Tantalum (Ta) based superconducting circuits have attracted attention for their low microwave loss. However, reproducible growth of high-quality $α$-phase Ta films remains challenging because film properties are highly sensitive to growth conditions. We present a robust, room-temperature route for $α$-Ta growth on sapphire substrates using an ultrathin Ti seed layer, which significantly widens the accessible deposition parameter space. We compare this approach to Ta films grown at high temperature without a seed layer and at room temperature with a Nb seed. We assess films using structural and surface characterization and quantify microwave loss with coplanar-waveguide (CPW) resonators measured in the quantum regime. We obtain two-level-system (TLS)-limited quality factors comparable to the state of the art for Ta-on-sapphire CPW resonators with similar geometries. In addition, temperature-dependent measurements reveal that high-temperature-grown films and films subjected to extended buffered-oxide etch treatment exhibit additional temperature activated loss, highlighting the role of growth route and post-processing in microwave performance. Our results establish ultrathin seed-layer engineering as a reliable alternative route to reproducible, low-loss $α$-Ta films and motivate closer study of the interfacial mechanisms that govern Ta phase formation.

CANON: Agent-Assisted Construction of Canonical Differential Equations for Feynman Integrals

Dong-shan Jian, Yuanche Liu, Yan-qing Ma, Bo-xuan Shi, Yu Wu et al. (7 authors)

The construction of canonical basis is an important procedure in the calculation of multi-loop Feynman integrals via the canonical differential equation methods. Currently, there are various different methods for this purpose. Among them, no single method is universally applicable. Thus, obtaining a valid basis often requires repeated attempts and verification. In this paper, we present CANON, an agent-assisted framework designed to make this search-and-verification process more convenient and more efficient. In this workflow, the agent selects methods and calls the corresponding tools to revise the basis proposal or the associated differential-equation system. Independent programs then verify whether the agent's result is valid and ask the agent to retry if verification fails. This framework contains a specific state-management tool to supports long-range basis searching works which requires multiple sub-agents. Its modular architecture enables future extensions to the framework itself. In this paper, we demonstrate this framework by applying it to some two-loop and three-loop integral families.

DEC Fill-ins, the Bartnik--Bray inner mass, and positive mass theorems with untrapped boundary

Ahmed Ellithy, Vishnu Kakkat, Stephen McCormick

Given a closed $(n-1)$-dimensional manifold equipped with prescribed spacetime Bartnik data, we ask when this can be realised as the boundary of a compact $n$-dimensional initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition (DEC). This is a natural generalisation of the problem of non-negative scalar curvature fill-ins, where the boundary metric and mean curvature are prescribed. We give conditions on the Bartnik data that suffice to prove the existence of such DEC fill-ins with inner MOTS boundary, and give some applications of these fill-ins. Our first application of these is a lower bound on the Bartnik--Bray inner mass, which we slightly reformulate here to be a genuine spacetime definition. Our other applications include proving a positive mass theorem for spin initial data with boundary that need not be trapped, and a spacetime Penrose-like inequality that gives a positive lower bound on the mass of an initial data set with boundary in terms of its boundary Bartnik data assuming the spacetime Penrose inequality holds.
