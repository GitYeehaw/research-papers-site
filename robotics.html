ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics

Bingxin Xu, Wenrui Bao, Xinxin Liu, Jiawei Zhou, Yuzhang Shang et al. (6 authors)

Agentic robotics places a large language model (LLM) agent in the robot control loop, where it composes perception, motion, and vision-language-action (VLA) tools under the guidance of a memory and thereby generalizes to new tasks without retraining. This generality comes at a steep price in efficiency. Every primitive requires a round of LLM reasoning over the full interaction history, so the cost of an episode grows with the number of primitives. We find this reasoning largely redundant: once a sub-goal is chosen, its primitives follow a routine already prescribed by memory. We present ChunkAct, which introduces action chunking to agentic robotics. Online, a single call launches a chunk of chained primitives, which executes autonomously and returns control only upon failure. Offline, ChunkAct compiles the memory into these chunks, so execution is memory-free at test time and the planner is reserved for decisions that require reasoning. LLM computation therefore scales with the number of decisions rather than the number of primitives. On LIBERO-Pro, ChunkAct improves task success by 5.0 points over the state of the art in agentic robotics while requiring 2.9x fewer LLM calls, 4.4x fewer input tokens, and 2.3x fewer output tokens.

LoopWAM: Closed-Loop Generalist Policy Improvement from Deployment Rollouts

Wenxuan Guo, Yingping Liang, Zhaoguo Wang, Ziyuan Li, Angyuan Ma et al. (14 authors)

Scaling expert demonstrations alone does not enable generalist robot policies to improve from deployment. Real-world rollouts contain successes, suboptimal behavior, and failures: imitating every recorded action can reinforce poor behavior, while filtering out failures discards evidence of physical interactions and their consequences. We introduce LoopWAM, a closed-loop world-action model that learns directly from this full quality spectrum, using rollouts from both itself and other deployed policies. Source-dependent noise sampling unifies behavior learning and action-conditioned world modeling, while quality steering and quality-weighted supervision retain full video supervision across qualities and regulate action imitation. Video and action tokens interact throughout parallel denoising, allowing learned consequences to inform action generation directly. On RoboChallenge Table30-V2, three deployment-and-training rounds raise success from 23.67\% to 43.33\%, a 19.7-percentage-point gain, with a final score of 56.80. LoopWAM also achieves 94.5\% average success on RoboTwin~2.0 and 49.5\% overall success on RoboCasa365 without additional embodied pretraining, obtaining the best overall results among the compared methods across all three benchmarks. On challenging shirt folding task, two rounds of deployment-driven improvement after single-task post-training raise success from 13.20\% to 55.56\%. These results demonstrate closed-loop improvement from mixed-quality experience under both multi-task learning and single-task post-training. \href{https://loop-wam.github.io/}{\textcolor{orange}{Project Page}}

EgoExplorer: A Vision-Motion-Touch Observatory for Human Loco-Manipulation In Situ

Yunsong Zhou, Xuekun Jiang, Haiqi Shen, Hangxu Liu, Chaojian Shi et al. (14 authors)

Learning humanoid loco-manipulation from human demonstrations rests on observing how locomotion, posture, and object interaction evolve together as tasks unfold. Scaling collection across diverse environments, in turn, favors lightweight, portable capture systems. Yet, existing datasets provide only partial views of this coordination. Thus, we introduce EgoExplorer, a 100-hour vision-motion-touch observatory of human loco-manipulation built with a wearable system. With an emphasis on professional activities, its continuous recordings align egocentric video with reconstructed whole-body motion, fine-grained finger articulation, and tactile observations, accompanied by step-level descriptions. To achieve this coverage with sparse body sensing, we develop the Calibrate-Complete-Constrain pipeline. Concretely, reusable calibration fixes participant geometry and sensor attachments before motion inference to disentangle mounting offsets from articulation. A temporal prior then completes unobserved joint motion, while layered constraints promote measurement agreement, temporal continuity, and geometric consistency, properties that motion plausibility alone does not guarantee. Against tracker-fitted references, the method reduces body-point error by 71.9% relative to the strongest baseline; matched evaluations also show gains in hand stability and contact estimation. Preliminary studies explore multimodal generation and conditioning alongside humanoid retargeting with whole-body control in simulation.

What Matters in Quality-Aware VLA Pretraining: A Benchmark and Empirical Study

Mingxuan Yan, Litian Gong, Dihong Huang, Zhixu Li, Tianyu Zhang et al. (22 authors)

How should demonstration quality be used during vision-language-action (VLA) pretraining to improve performance after target-task fine-tuning? We compare weighting, filtering, and conditioning across reward definitions and label granularities using 5,000 naturally suboptimal human demonstrations and 24 target tasks. Within each VLA, we match demonstrations and training budgets. Additional comparisons examine pretraining data size and the roles of quality tags during fine-tuning and inference. Across three VLAs, we find that approach-aware episode conditioning improves mean success over plain behavioral cloning; conditioning and filtering favor episode and chunk labels, respectively, on average, with model-dependent reversals; and constant-tag fine-tuning improves mean success after conditioned pretraining with either tagged or untagged inference. We introduce AXIS-Bench to support these controlled comparisons. Built on crowdsourced simulation demonstrations, it provides recorded object and contact states for recomputing quality labels on shared trajectories. Replayable trajectories support workspace-aligned rendering and user-defined real-world target tasks. In our real-world experiment, simulation pretraining raises success from 9.6\% to 31.7\%. Project page: https://axis-bench.github.io/.

EmbodiedEvo: Self-improving Coding Agents for Physical Intelligence

Wenyi Li, Yizhe Chi, Youjie Zheng, Chunyu Liu, Xiaoqiu Wang et al. (11 authors)

Bringing general-purpose agents into the physical world calls for an executable representation of physical intelligence. Compared with vision-language-action (VLA) models, which map observations and language instructions directly to actions, code makes control logic explicit and reusable. Code can serve not only as a policy representation but also as the workspace in which the agent solves a task. Rather than following a prescribed workflow of planning, execution, and verification, the agent can autonomously inspect the state, revise and execute code, and use feedback to choose the next step. The success of this SWE-style loop in digital environments motivates its extension to physical tasks. Yet physical execution remains prone to recurring failures. To retain and refine experience for later scenes, we introduce EmbodiedEvo, where a coding agent evolves reusable Skills and Lessons through feedback on a single Playground scene, then freezes the resulting harness for diverse new scenes. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves an average score of 44.48 and a success rate of 37.82\%, surpassing all officially verified results. Paired tests show the largest gains on memory-dependent tasks and successful reuse of coordinated procedures, while fine contact control remains difficult. Experience from stronger models also improves weaker executors: Astra's experience raises Sol and Luna's success rates by 8.86 and 5.50 percentage points over no experience. Compared with raw interaction history, Skills and Lessons achieve comparable task performance at 53.0\% lower execution cost.

AutoAgent0: An Agentic Runtime for Safe Closed-Loop Driving

Jiageng Liu, Seth Z. Zhao, Zewei Zhou, Alejandro Huerta, Aidan Bayer-Calvert et al. (12 authors)

Safe autonomous driving requires more than accurate planning at a single frame: it demands reliable closed-loop execution in dynamic environments. Although end-to-end driving policies have advanced rapidly, the accumulated closed-loop errors, especially in unseen scenarios, can push vehicles into states from which the same policy struggles to recover. We address the challenge by treating closed-loop driving safety as a test-time execution problem. We introduce AutoAgent0, an agentic runtime and harness framework that augments existing driving experts with complementary detection skills, rule-based action verification, and feedback-driven recovery. To address inaccurate scene perception, an independent detection module constructs scene context using complementary perception skills, and a rule-based verifier checks proposed trajectories against this context before execution. When the nominal trajectories are unreliable, the orchestrator of the runtime activates Recovery Planning, where an agentic loop constructs alternative action proposals from action primitives and iteratively refines them using verification feedback. This design combines independent scene assessment with feasibility checks and online recovery, addressing both perception errors and the limited recovery capabilities of individual experts. Evaluations on Fail2Drive and NavSafe-$\infty$ demonstrate improved closed-loop robustness over single-expert execution in long-tail and safety-critical scenarios. These results demonstrate that runtime supervision can improve closed-loop robustness across multiple driving experts without retraining the underlying policies.

Interaction Latents as Actions for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Yuhang Zheng, Yujie Zang, Yuchen Liu, Yupeng Zheng, Zhennan Jiang et al. (12 authors)

Contact-rich manipulation requires robots to coordinate not only where to move, but also how contact and force should evolve throughout an interaction. Yet most robot policies represent actions primarily as motion trajectories, using tactile signals as inputs or auxiliary prediction targets without explicitly encoding contact dynamics in the action representation. We propose ILA (Interaction Latents as Actions), which uses decodable interaction latents that jointly capture the evolution of motion, contact, and force application as policy action units, providing a shared representation for action generation, evaluation, and offline improvement. We learn these latents through visually conditioned joint action-tactile reconstruction with a time-conditioned implicit decoder. Using them as flow-matching generation targets supports post-training of pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs), as well as training flow-matching policies from scratch. A shared decoder maps generated latents to executable actions. For offline improvement, we learn a tactile-conditioned expert reference model from interaction latents encoded from expert demonstrations. The resulting expert-consistency rewards guide weighted learning from successful rollouts, while observation-compatible expert references guide correction of low-reward predictions from failed rollouts. Across three policy families, ILA improves success rates averaged over four real-world tasks by 21.0-36.5 percentage points relative to tactile-conditioned direct-action baselines. Ablations highlight the benefit of encoding contact evolution in action targets beyond tactile conditioning, while the same latent space supports expert-guided offline improvement. Project website: https://mrsecant.github.io/ILA/.

ForceWAM: Decoupling Future and Response Learning from Demo and Play Data for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Zijun Xu, Wendi Chen, Xinkai Wang, Jiachen Zou, Hongjin Chen et al. (19 authors)

Expert demonstrations show how to complete contact-rich tasks, but provide limited coverage of how interaction forces respond to different actions. Task-agnostic play provides broader experience of contact dynamics without specifying task goals. To fully leverage these two types of data, we introduce ForceWAM, a force world action model that decouples knowledge of task-compatible interaction outcomes from knowledge of how local actions change contact. A world model learns from successful demonstrations to jointly predict future video and six-axis force/torque trajectories. Together, these futures describe the expected evolution of motion and contact during the task. A separate inverse dynamics model (IDM) learns from both task and play, mapping current observations and recorded visual-wrench futures to their associated robot actions. Play data thus provide knowledge of force responses for goal-directed execution. At deployment, the IDM reactively translates generated futures into actions, reusing them with fresh wrench feedback between world-model updates. Across real-robot force-regulated sliding, box flipping with and without disturbances, and ring placement, ForceWAM achieves $85$-$95\%$ mean success, surpassing world-modeling and reactive baselines. Our experiments further show that adding task-agnostic play data improves success by $15$-$45$ percentage points, demonstrating its value for learning contact-rich execution. Project page and code are available at https://force-wam.github.io/.

Collaborative Planning for UAV-UGV Teams under Operational Constraints via Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion

Zihao Deng, Qianhuang Li, Maggie Wigness, John Rogers, Donghyun Kim et al. (6 authors)

Collaborative planning under operational constraints is essential for heterogeneous robot teams in large-scale missions. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provide rapid area coverage but are limited by onboard energy, while Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) can serve as mobile recharging stations yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Effective collaboration must enforce operational constraints during execution while enabling parallel, coordinated actions. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have shown promise through token unmasking but have two key limitations: entropy-guided scheduling assumes distributional alignment and reliance on differentiable constraints for diffusion-time optimization. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND), which formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAV-UGV teams. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution by reweighting the learned action distribution with a logic-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints. MEND introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predictive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. Extensive experimental results in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robot teams demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints.

Contact Trajectory Prompting: In-Context Transfer of Contact-Rich Behaviors from a Single Demonstration

Xian Nie, Yujie Zang, Yuhang Zheng, Yupeng Zheng, Songen Gu et al. (11 authors)

In-context robot learning enables robots to acquire new behaviors from demonstrations without test-time parameter updates. However, visual and kinematic specifications alone may not distinguish contact-rich behaviors that share similar motions but require different interaction intensities or patterns. We propose Contact Trajectory Prompting (CTP), a framework for one-shot transfer of contact-rich manipulation behaviors. CTP encodes motion and contact information from a single demonstration into an ordered contact trajectory prompt that captures their temporal evolution and specifies the desired physical interaction behavior. During execution, the policy uses online sensory feedback to retrieve interaction-relevant information from the prompt, conditioning closed-loop action generation on the demonstrated contact behavior. Across real-world wiping, rotation, and insertion tasks on objects excluded from training, CTP achieves 78.7% overall success, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline by 13.0 percentage points. Changing only the reference demonstration enables CTP to follow contact specifications for both intensity and direction, including an intermediate wiping force level and clockwise or counterclockwise cap rotation. These results demonstrate that contact trajectories serve as effective behavioral specifications, extending in-context robot learning beyond motion imitation to physical interaction transfer. Project website: https://ctp-robotics.github.io/project_page.
