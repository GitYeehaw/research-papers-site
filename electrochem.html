Physics-Constrained Modeling and Inference of the Equation of State for Dissociative Materials

Jolypich Pek, Ben Seiyon Lee, Jason Bernstein, Philip Myint

Equations of state (EOSs) describe how a material's internal energy and pressure change with temperature and density. They are inputs to hydrodynamic simulations in materials science and planetary science. We model the ion-thermal free-energy term for dissociative materials, where molecular bonds break apart to form new species under extreme conditions. This term is governed by two latent physical processes, a molar-mass surface and a Debye-temperature curve, neither of which is observed directly. Instead, both must be recovered from internal energy and pressure, which are derivatives of the free energy, subject to thermodynamic constraints. Existing EOS tables are typically built through material-specific hand-tuning, a process that is tedious, does not scale across materials or conditions, and offers no guarantee of thermodynamic consistency. We propose a partitioned statistical framework that infers molar mass and Debye temperature directly from EOS data, guaranteeing thermodynamic consistency by construction. Local molar-mass behavior is captured with polynomial basis functions across domain partitions, while the Debye temperature is modeled with an I-spline basis. In simulations and a carbon dioxide case study, the method accurately recovers the underlying physical quantities, improves prediction of held-out energy and pressure data, and provides uncertainty quantification while scaling with EOS table size.

A boundary integral method for computing electron repulsion integrals in heterogeneous dielectric environments

Xuanzhao Gao, Malte Rösner, Jason Kaye, Alex Barnett

We present a boundary integral method for evaluating electron repulsion integrals (ERIs) in dielectric environments of piecewise constant permittivity. This generalizes conventional bare-Coulomb ERIs by incorporating screening from structured dielectric surroundings, such as the substrates and/or encapsulating layers present in modern two-dimensional-material devices. We formulate the problem as a second-kind boundary integral equation for the induced surface charge on the dielectric interfaces, and discretize it with a Nyström method on adaptively refined panels. The incident potential generated by each smooth orbital source density is evaluated by a Fourier truncated-kernel method in or near to its source box, and by fast multipole method (FMM) accelerated quadrature farther away. The linear system is solved by a Krylov iteration also accelerated by the FMM. We focus on cuboidal device geometries, and retain efficiency at high aspect ratios via close-to-touching panel quadratures. We further develop a distributed pipeline that computes the full ERI tensor, comprising thousands of orbital pairs, embedded in a single dielectric geometry. We demonstrate the method on an effective three-layer graphene slab over a Si/SiO$_2$ heterojunction, focusing on the central graphene layer represented by $198$ $p_z$-like orbitals, retaining the $2613^2$ non-negligible entries of the $198^4$-sized ERI tensor. The model is resolved on $1.3$ million surface unknowns, achieving a relative error of $0.004\%$, at a cost of $22$ node-hours.

Vacuum-Field Squeezing by Molecules in Optical Cavities: An Exact Bound and Linear-Response Theory

Kurt R. Brorsen

The ground state of a molecule coupled to an optical cavity contains virtual photons, and squeezing of the photonic field has been found in exactly solvable models, perturbation theory, and numerical calculations. Here, two exact sum rules for the field quadratures show that, within the dipole approximation, the vector-potential fluctuations of a linearly polarized mode never exceed their vacuum value in the ground state of any molecule at any coupling strength. Consequently, the field is squeezed whenever the mode couples to a dipole-allowed transition from a nondegenerate ground state, and the cavity-induced ground-state energy shift cannot increase with the cavity frequency at a fixed coupling vector, which is an exact constraint on approximate methods. Moreover, the incoherent photon statistics, which exclude the coherent part of the field, are never sub-Poissonian. At weak coupling, an exact resolvent expression reduces the squeezing and the ratio of the anomalous to the normal photon moment to linear-response properties of the isolated molecule, with the ratio set by a mean excitation energy. Time-dependent density functional theory with CAM-B3LYP and PBE0 reproduces exact and QED coupled-cluster references for both quantities to within 5% at 45 benchmark points. A survey of 29 molecules gives mean excitation energies of 9.5-21 eV at a cavity frequency of 2 eV and shows that the most polarizable molecules squeeze the field the most.

Challenging Atomistic Foundation Models on Disordered Carbons

Joseph Hart, Samuel G. H. Brookes, Fabian L. Thiemann, Christoph Schran

Foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) promise broad transferability, yet their out-of-domain reliability is hard to establish. Here, we use pure and O/H-containing disordered carbon spanning multiple phases, competing hybridisations and porous frameworks to stress-test six popular foundation MLIPs. We first screen all six on density functional theory (DFT) energy and force errors and binding curves, then subject four to melt-quench-anneal molecular dynamics and evaluate the resulting structural observables. Binding curves and O/H-containing trajectories reveal unphysical extrapolation and dynamical failures that low DFT energy and force errors fail to reveal. Of the models tested, MACE-MH-1 is the most consistently robust, with low DFT errors and stable MD. Despite differing architectures, OMAT-based models share structural biases, highlighting the importance of training coverage. All four models generate structures of nanoporous carbon that reproduce the principal experimental and specialised carbon potential signatures. Incorporating O/H leaves short-range order largely unchanged but shifts pore-size distributions towards larger pores. These results emphasise the need for application-specific validation and extend molecular dynamics modelling of nanoporous carbon towards experimentally realistic structures.

Measurement-efficient energy estimator for quantum chemistry of photodynamic materials

Connor Lenihan, Kripa Panchagnula, Luca Erhart, Jem Guhit, Carlo A. Gaggioli et al. (17 authors)

Accurate modelling of chemical reaction rates requires precise correlated energy differences. Quantum computing offers a potential route to their determination, but extracting energies from quantum-prepared states can demand prohibitive sampling. We demonstrate measurement-efficient correlated energy estimation using an electronically challenging H$_2$DBB--Ru model derived from a Ru-functionalized hafnium metal-organic framework relevant to radiodynamic and photodynamic therapies. The mixed estimator combines a quantum-prepared target with sparse multiconfiguration references and recovers ground-state corrections using magnitude-only compute-uncompute measurements. Analytical variance estimates predict 1.6 mHa precision with a few hundred shots, about 100 times fewer than the single-determinant formulation and three orders of magnitude fewer than optimized Hamiltonian-term sampling. With approximate targets, it reaches comparable accuracy using simpler state preparation than direct expectation-value estimation. Emulator and trapped-ion experiments on Quantinuum Helios validate the approach up to (10,10), including a 50-qubit circuit combining AI-assisted state preparation with error detection, providing a measurement-efficient route to correlated energy differences for challenging chemical problems.

NIR-probe transient absorption on rubrene microcavities demonstrates no changes to singlet fission by strong coupling

Robert H. Gordon, James P. Pidgeon, Rachel C. Kilbride, Tingxiang Yang, Benjamin Stewart et al. (12 authors)

Strong light-matter coupling has been proposed to control singlet-exciton fission, yet reported effects remain controversial due to the prevalence of cavity-induced optical artifacts in transient absorption measurements. Here, we investigate singlet fission in dielectric microcavities containing rubrene by probing in the near-infrared region, outside the cavity stopband, to eliminate mode-filtering artifacts. Across contrasting cavity quality factors ($Q = 90$ and $1150$) and a range of exciton-photon detunings ($+20$ to $-110$ meV), we find that the underlying kinetics are virtually identical to uncoupled films. Although minor spectral changes occur, transfer matrix simulations reveal these stem purely from mirror interference rather than modified photophysics. Transient electron paramagnetic resonance further confirms that high-spin triplet dynamics remain unperturbed. Together, these findings establish a widely applicable experimental framework for evaluating molecular photophysics in microcavities, revealing that singlet fission remains unaltered in strongly coupled rubrene.

Terabit-scale wavelength-multiplexed transmitter enabled by thin-film lithium tantalate

Shivaprasad U. Hulyal, Dengyang Fang, Jiachen Cai, Xinru Ji, Alexander Kotz et al. (8 authors)

Artificial-intelligence systems face a growing data-movement bottleneck and require higher interconnect bandwidth without a proportional increase in electrical power consumption or the number of laser sources and fibre connections. Moving optical interfaces closer to computing chips reduces electrical transmission loss, while demanding optical engines that combine high channel capacity, broadband modulation and simple packaging. Although wavelength multiplexing relieves pressure on the fibre budget, integrating shared multi-wavelength sources with dense wavelength routing and broadband modulation in a compact format remains challenging. Here we demonstrate a monolithic transmitter that brings these functions together on a single material platform, enabled by the low birefringence and native Pockels response of thin-film lithium tantalate. The device separates, independently modulates and recombines eight wavelength channels between one optical input and output. This architecture retains a two-fibre interface as the channel count grows, directly addressing the packaging burden of parallel optical links. Measurements across the eight channels establish an aggregate line rate of 1.6 Tbit s$^{-1}$. Operation with a shared optical frequency comb achieves an estimated net data rate of 0.58 Tbit s$^{-1}$ across four channels, demonstrating compatibility with a common multi-wavelength source. Scaling this architecture into multiple wavelength-multiplexed engines offers a path towards 10 Tbit s$^{-1}$ optical interfaces with high bandwidth per unit package edge, positioning thin-film lithium tantalate for the next generation of co-packaged optical interconnects.

Vacancy and Helium-Cluster Thermomigration in Tungsten

Sanad Alturk, Enrique Martinez

Strong thermal gradients in tungsten plasma-facing components can bias the migration of irradiation-induced defects and thereby alter their redistribution, interaction, and retention. Here, we extend an atomistic jump-statistics framework previously developed for hydrogen to quantify the thermomigration of a single vacancy and interstitial He$_1$--He$_4$ clusters in body-centered-cubic tungsten. Non-equilibrium molecular-dynamics simulations performed over several thermal gradients are used to determine the directional jump ratio, while geometrically derived projected jump lengths convert the fitted directional-bias coefficient into the heat-of-transport coefficient $Q^*=αkT^2$, where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ the temperature, and $α$ a constant. All investigated defects exhibit negative values of $α$, corresponding to net migration toward the hot region. He$_4$ has the largest response, $α=-2.2457\times10^{-2}$ K$^{-1}$, followed closely by the vacancy, whereas He$_3$ exhibits the weakest response despite its intermediate cluster size. Independent constant-temperature simulations over 1000--1600 K show that He$_3$ is also the least mobile cluster and has the largest effective migration energy, $E_m^{eff}=0.271$ eV. In contrast, He$_4$ is more mobile than He$_3$ and has the smallest effective migration energy, 0.087 eV. Agreement between the He$_3$ concentration profile predicted from the jump statistics and that obtained from a long molecular-dynamics trajectory provides an independent validation of the method. These results show that thermomigration cannot be inferred from cluster size, diffusivity, or migration energy alone and provide defect-specific heat-of-transport parameters for higher-length-scale models of tungsten under fusion-relevant thermal gradients.

Resolving the Thermal Labyrinth inside 3D-IC Stacks

Xufei Guo, Yiyun He, Zirou Chen, Zifeng Huang, Runsheng Wang et al. (6 authors)

The heat pathways in 3D-ICs have long been a labyrinth lacking experimental investigations into spatial distribution of thermal properties, severely limiting study of internal thermal management inside chips. For the first time, we established a methodology for 3D thermal characterizations of advanced 3D-ICs, implemented through a systematic workflow that enables layer-resolved thermal property extraction and the construction of a location-specific thermal database. We demonstrated its application to an advanced sample featuring 2.2-μm-diameter TSVs and ~2-μm-pitch face-to-face hybrid bonding (HB). Time-domain and steady-state thermoreflectance were used to extract the thermal conductivities (TCs) of Al traces, TSV, BEOL interconnects and HB pads, indicating HB structure enables efficient thermal transport with 9 W/m-K TC. Further, we mapped and visualized the TC distribution of TSV structures and RDL Cu wires with small step sizes down to 0.2 μm. Utilizing these results, we proposed an experiment-based thermal model to reveal that co-evolution of interconnect and substrate TCs is essential for thermal design and fabrication towards effective cooling optimization.

Stabilization of Ferroelectric Domains via Interfacial Strain in HoFeO3- BaTiO3 Nanofiber Heterostructures for Enhanced Hybrid Energy Harvesting

Smita Chaturvedi, Swathi Krishna, Shivshankar Jokare, Supriya Sahoo, Vikash Kushwaha et al. (8 authors)

Piezoelectric nanogenerators (PENGs) are an attractive platform for self-powered mechanical-energy harvesting, but their output is often limited by depolarization fields, charge leakage, and inefficient stress transfer to the active dipoles, particularly in polymer-dominated composites. Here we report HoFeO3-BaTiO3 (HFO-BTO) nanofiber heterostructures prepared by alternating sol-gel electrospinning, in which orthorhombic HoFeO3 (Pbnm) and tetragonal BaTiO3 (P4mm) coexist in intimate contact. Rietveld-refined X-ray diffraction, high-resolution transmission electron microscopy, and Raman spectroscopy indicate interfacial lattice distortion. Polarization-electric field loops show that the heterostructure retains a polarization comparable to that of BaTiO3 (16.39 vs 15.80 microcoulombs per square centimeter at zero field) while its switching-current maximum moves to a higher field (about 7 vs about 3.5 kilovolts per centimeter), consistent with strain-stabilized ferroelectric domains. When the nanofibers are encapsulated in polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and excited under cyclic impact, the device produces a hybrid output: the piezoelectric response of the strained BaTiO3 is supplemented by a triboelectric contribution from contact electrification. The HFO-BTO device delivers open-circuit voltage peaks of about +6 volts and -8 volts and a power density of 24.31 microwatts per cubic centimeter at a 4.7 megaohm load. This is 2.4 times the value for an identically fabricated BaTiO3 device (10.00 microwatts per cubic centimeter) and 8.7 times that of the HoFeO3 device (2.78 microwatts per cubic centimeter). These results link interface-driven lattice distortion to improved electromechanical output and identify all-oxide nanofiber heterostructures as a promising route to lead-free energy harvesting.
