Joint Electricity Price and Load Forecasting for Data Center Battery Dispatch Using Polynomial Diffusion Models

Matthew Van Ham, Yiping Liu, Xiaozhe Wang

The rapid growth of large-scale data centers (DCs) is increasing electricity demand, market volatility, and operating costs. Battery energy storage systems (BESSs) can mitigate these challenges by shifting electricity purchases from high-price to low-price periods. This paper proposes a real-time BESS dispatch framework using polynomial diffusion models (PDMs) to forecast both electricity prices and DC loads. Leveraging the closed-form conditional expectations of PDMs, the forecasts are integrated into a dynamic programming (DP) framework to minimize electricity procurement costs. Across the evaluated BESS configurations, the proposed approach achieves 2.63 times the cost savings of a rule-based (RB) strategy.

OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions

Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, Chen Xu, Haruki Nishimura et al. (7 authors)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. Teaching them to recover from these failures requires substantial corrective data: existing approaches learn to recover from failures by directly associating corrective actions with the observations in which they were demonstrated. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. We hypothesize that semantics can capture the underlying reasons for failures and their corrections, enabling policies to learn from only a handful of corrective demonstrations and generalize those corrections to novel objects, and scene variations. We propose OopsieRecover, a framework that uses abduction to infer the intent behind a small set of corrective demonstrations and encodes the inferred knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. Please refer to https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover for videos.

Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation

Yixiao Wang, Peiyi Chen, Mustafa Mukadam, Shreekant Gayaka, Masayoshi Tomizuka et al. (6 authors)

Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging because policies must acquire both perceptual grounding and precise control, while reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation can acquire rich control behaviors from large-scale interaction with privileged information. We introduce Latent RL Prior, a two-stage framework that reuses this control knowledge for data-efficient visual policy learning. During privileged pretraining, we distill large-scale RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context. During visual adaptation, we freeze the decoder and learn a visual interface that maps images to its latent context using action supervision alone, without privileged labels or latent targets. The same decoder can further be shared across arms by learning coordinated visual contexts for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated tasks, our method outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime; on door lifting and bimanual assembly, it surpasses full-data baselines using $10\times$ and $5\times$ fewer demonstrations, respectively. Further analysis shows that direct visual-to-latent conditioning improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing visual predictions through the pretrained state encoder. Robustness to unseen mass variation and greater linear accessibility of contact-force information in decoder representations further provide evidence of retained RL-acquired control knowledge. Project website: https://yixiaowang7.github.io/latent-rl-prior-page/.

VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

Yu-Cheng Chou, Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, Chi-Pin Huang, Chiao-An Yang et al. (19 authors)

Models for physical AI tasks, such as robotics and autonomous driving, need not reason solely in language. They can use visual plans--points, bounding boxes, and trajectories--to represent spatial goals and anticipate motion. However, autoregressive vision-language models generate these plans one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly. We introduce VFlash, a method for fast, accurate visual planning through Flash Primitive Decoding. VFlash exploits the known structure of visual primitives to predict their coordinates jointly, using a lightweight drafter that generates an entire primitive in one forward pass. The layout adapts to the primitive's type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long, variable-length trajectories. Crucially, spatial accuracy need not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash therefore trains on annotated primitives and directly accepts structurally valid predictions without target-token agreement. Across the evaluated 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks, VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding. Preliminary single-rollout measurements indicate approximately 25x faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.

Dynamics Aware Past-Future Information Bottleneck

Wooyoung Chung, Stas Tiomkin

The simplification of complex dynamical systems is essential for efficient estimation and control. A principled approach to this task is through the lens of information-theoretic compression. The Past-Future Information Bottleneck (PF-IB) provides a theoretical framework for compressing linear dynamical systems. However, the standard formulation often lacks structural consistency, yielding representations that are not realizable as dynamical systems. By constraining the objective to a specific manifold of block Hankel matrices, which we prove is a Lie group, we bridge the gap between PF-IB and realization theory. Our approach ensures that the resulting compressed dynamics are realizable, thereby enabling its direct application to estimation and control. We demonstrate our approach in numerical simulations.

Failure Prediction and Classification in Vision-Language-Action Models Via Calibrated Latent Probing

Michal P. Podolinsky, Yunhao Yang, Daniel Little, Hong Lu, Rohan Siva et al. (9 authors)

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Under shift to unseen tasks, the bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates remain valid in up to 86% of runs, versus at most 21% for baselines. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen et al. (8 authors)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

Social Humanoid: Socially Responsive Human-Humanoid Interaction

Vongani H. Maluleke, James Ni, Kie Horiuchi, Lea Wilken, Jiahui Lei et al. (10 authors)

Humanoid robots are typically trained as solitary agents, leaving them unable to coordinate physically with human partners who naturally anticipate and adjust to one another. To bridge this gap, we present Social Humanoid, a hierarchical system that generates and executes socially responsive whole-body motion on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 conditioned on a human partner's motion. Its high-level policy performs flexible sequence modeling of human and humanoid motion through a shared tokenized representation, generating reactive humanoid motion and anticipating the partner's motion when they are occluded, while a low-level whole-body tracking controller executes the generated motion on the robot. To train the system, we introduce a two-phase adaptive retargeting procedure that retargets one interaction partner to the robot while morphing the other partner to preserve contact dynamics, reachability, and relative body scale. We apply this procedure across five motion-capture datasets to produce a new Human-Humanoid Interaction (HHI) benchmark of 260 hours of human-humanoid interactions. Our results demonstrate that Social Humanoid produces coordinated, human-aligned behavior across diverse interaction types in simulation and the real world. Project page: https://von31.github.io/social-humanoid/

A Multimodal Haptic Interface for Simultaneous Softness and Thermal Modulation

Joshua Brown, Fernando Bello, Lynette Jones

Haptic interactions, such as touching an object in virtual reality, involve detecting a plethora of sensations through the skin. Whilst tactile displays exist to simulate and manipulate all of these cues individually, little progress has been made constructing devices that can control multiple cues simultaneously. This paper presents a tactile display technology that offers both softness and temperature change. A prototype display, based on particle jamming and the thermal transfer of heated and cooled water, is evaluated in terms of its ability to change both stiffness and temperature. Mechanical evaluations demonstrate controllable stiffness increase of 300%, whilst thermal characterisation showed strong surface uniformity and a range of achievable temperatures from 10°C up to 50°C, with closed-loop control maintaining a 0.2°C error from a setpoint. This work advances the design of multimodal tactile displays with applications to perceptual research, medical simulation, and affective haptics.

SCALE: Real-Time Latent Communication for Cyber-Physical Agents

Shanghao Shi, Jian Xiao, Ching-Hsiang Chan, Sanjoy Baruah, Sastry Kompella et al. (8 authors)

Latent communication enables agents to collaborate efficiently by directly exchanging intermediate model representations rather than raw sensor data or text messages. Existing approaches, however, are primarily designed for LLM agents and do not consider the bandwidth constraints and heterogeneous task deadlines faced by cyber-physical agents. In this work, we propose SCALE, a real-time latent communication method tailored for cyber-physical agents. Motivated by the spatio-temporal continuity of the physical world, SCALE matches tokens across consecutive latent messages using similarity scores to identify reusable information and avoid redundant transmission. It then profiles the semantic gain and communication cost of transmitting different amounts of latent information. Based on these profiles, SCALE employs a real-time scheduler that jointly determines which pending messages to transmit and how many latent tokens to send, subject to available bandwidth and task deadlines. We evaluate SCALE across three cyber-physical benchmarks and a physical spider robot case study under diverse network conditions and heterogeneous task deadlines. Our results show that SCALE significantly improves both task performance and on-time delivery in bandwidth-constrained networks. Our code is available at: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/SCALE-4E80/.
