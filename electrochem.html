Landau quantization of $C_3$ high-fold fermions in honeycomb lattices

Luisa Madail, Joaquín Fernández-Rossier

We present the theory of the Landau levels of $C_3$ high-fold fermions in honeycomb lattices. These fermions arise as a generalization of the tight-binding model in a honeycomb lattice with one orbital per site, known to describe graphene, to a family of models with several orbitals per site transforming according to representations of $C_3$, relevant for triangulene two-dimensional crystals. The models are defined by the number of orbitals $n_a$ and $n_b$ at the $a$ and $b$ sites of the unit cell. They feature energy bands that combine flat bands and Dirac cones, that are isomorphic to those of graphene, the Lieb, $p_{xy}$ honeycomb, and the Kagome tight-binding models. Combining tight-binding and $k\cdot p$ methods, here we compute the Landau levels of the $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$, $(2,2)$ and $(3,3)$ models, we compare them to those of the reference models, and we explore the similarities and differences with the Landau levels in graphene. Our findings reveal that the nature of $C_3$ high-fold fermions combine Dirac-type Landau quantization, originating from the linear crossings and associated relativistic band dispersion, with anomalous Landau quantization rooted in the quantum geometry of the singular flat bands.

Exchange Interactions and Magnon Spectra in bcc Fe(001) Thin Films: A Computational Study

Joanna Marciniak, Wojciech Marciniak, Jorge Luis Briseño-Gómez, Jan Rusz

We combined density functional theory with atomistic spin dynamics to understand how surfaces influence magnon dispersion. For this purpose, we investigate thin layers of body-centered cubic iron and compare them with the bulk material. We present how the broken symmetry affects exchange interactions and magnon behavior. Our results reveal enhanced magnetic moments and exchange interactions for surface atoms compared to bulk Fe. The increase in exchange interaction integrals is mostly visible for first- and second-nearest neighbors. We also find a significant spread in exchange values within each coordination shell. Magnon dispersion analysis shows, for the layered system, pronounced band blurring, a significant reduction of maximum frequencies relative to the previously reported bulk values, and the emergence of a low-frequency band outside the \g{}-point. Our study shows that relying on bulk exchange parameters introduces errors in the magnon spectra of ultrathin systems, underscoring the need for a detailed, system-specific approach.

Growth-induced trade-off between polarity, electrical isolation, and structural quality in AlN grown on Si(111) by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy

Hossein Yazdani, Nhu Tran, Xin Du, Vit Hornik, Roman Gröger et al. (6 authors)

We investigate the effects of substrate temperature and Al flux, at a fixed N flux, on the polarity, electrical properties, and structural quality of AlN films grown on high-resistivity Si(111) by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy. N-rich conditions result in relatively highly resistive Al-polar AlN/Si, whereas Al-rich conditions promote N-polarity and improved structural quality but simultaneously lead to increased p-type conductivity of AlN/Si stacks, consistent with unintentional Al doping of the Si substrate during AlN growth. Increasing the substrate temperature further improves the structural quality while enhancing the unwanted conductivity, revealing a growth-induced trade-off among polarity, electrical isolation, and structural quality. By reducing the substrate temperature to 575°C while maintaining slightly Al-rich conditions, an N-polar AlN/Si stack with a sheet resistance of 276 kΩ/sq is achieved. These results provide guidelines for optimizing AlN/Si templates for various nitride device heterostructures.

Effect of deep-trap density of states for a-Si:H layers on HIT cell performances: Modeling Framework

Tewfik Souier, Adel Gougam

In this work, an aSi and cSi heterojunction solar cell with an intrinsic layer is simulated using AFORS-HET software. The study focuses on the role of the electronic density of states DOS of the amorphous emitter, p type aSiH, in governing the performance of the HIT cell and the electronic transfer across the heterointerface. It is found that the DOS of the p type aSiH emitter can cause an unfavorable recombination and trapping effect, which becomes more severe and difficult to avoid when the bulk dangling-bond density in the a-Si emitter approaches or exceeds the doping concentration. An increase in deep trap density beyond a critical value causes a drop in the built in voltage and shifts the depletion layer into p type aSi. This situation leads to a sharp decline in HIT cell performance, namely the open circuit voltage and the fill factor, and to the appearance of an S shaped IV characteristic. The simulation results suggest that the emitter activation energy needs to be optimized to achieve high efficiency. Under optimal conditions, holes accumulate near the heterojunction interface, leading to the formation of an inversion layer. This paper presents a physical mechanism for recombination processes within the emitter and concludes with the optimized parameters required to achieve a HIT cell efficiency comparable to Sanyo record of 23 percent on a thin silicon wafer.

Structural identification in general crystalline systems via Voronoi cell topology

Emanuel A. Lazar, Chris H. Rycroft

Widely used methods for identifying crystal structure in atomistic simulations are limited in practice to a handful of simple structures, leaving many important crystalline systems without automated classification techniques. Voronoi topology analysis offers a route to classifying arbitrary crystalline and defect environments, but realizing this potential first requires enumerating the Voronoi cell topologies that can arise under thermal perturbation of a structure of interest. We present an algorithm that deterministically enumerates all Voronoi cell topologies arising from generic infinitesimal perturbations of an arbitrary crystal structure, producing structural classifiers that extend automated identification to general crystalline systems. We demonstrate the approach on several structures of general interest.

Cartesian and Curvilinear Anharmonic Frequencies and Intensities: Perturb-then-Diagonalize Polyad Transport and Adiabatic Discrete-Variable Representation

Vincenzo Barone

Anharmonic intensities reveal vibrational coupling that band positions alone cannot determine. We derive explicit second-order infrared transition moments in curvilinear normal coordinates, including kinetic metric derivatives and transport through variational resonance polyads. The vibrational perturbation-to-second-order-then-diagonalization (VP2TD) treatment covers fundamentals, overtones, three-quantum transitions, and hot bands. A general computational platform combines Extended Symmetry-Adapted Local Coordinates (ESALCs), using Cartesian (ESALC-C) or internal (ESALC-I) primitives, with interchangeable quantum-chemical programs and parallel finite differences. Independent oscillator matrices verify the intensity equations; a molecular water check reproduces second-order amplitudes within $8.4\times10^{-8}$ atomic units. Comparisons with established tools reproduce a formaldehyde Fermi polyad within 0.163 cm$^{-1}$ and 0.061 km mol$^{-1}$ and water Raman activities within $10^{-7}$ Angstrom$^6$. For 29 matched formaldehyde rotational line pairs, the predicted hot-to-fundamental intensity ratio, using the measured lower-state energy, is consistent with the nuclear-spin-corrected median: the differences are 3.9% (ESALC-I) and 3.8% (ESALC-C), compared with a 15% uncertainty estimate for individual measured line intensities. Common analytic reference derivatives ensure identical harmonic observables, while internal coordinates reduce and localize intermode anharmonic couplings. Rotation-vibration constants extend the same tensor treatment. An integrated adiabatic discrete-variable representation treats large-amplitude motions, and finite-level partition functions provide anharmonic thermochemistry.

Modeling electrodeposition of thin films with compact, dendritic, and random morphologies

Fábio D. A. Aarão Reis, Dung di Caprio, Sidiney G. Alves, Ismael S. S. Carrasco

We summarize previously published computer simulation studies of a metal electrodeposition model that considers diffusive cation flux, a fast reaction in contact with the electrode or with the growing deposit, and adsorbed atom diffusion in finite time windows. Simulations with various parameters show compact, fractal, dendritic deposits, and dynamic transitions between different morphologies during the growth of individual samples. The dendritic shapes are characterized by hierarchical organizations in which branches of a given order reproduce the shape and crystalline orientation of the branches of the previous order, in contrast to the random branching of the fractal deposits formed by DLA and related models. The simulations show dendrites with maple leaf shape in SC lattices, pine tree shape in FCC lattices, and flower-like shape in HCP lattices, with surfaces predominantly oriented in the lowest-energy configurations of each lattice. Simulations with nucleation restricted to a nanoscale seed show the initial formation of faceted particles followed by dendrite formation, in agreement with silver electrodeposition on substrates patterned with gold nanoparticles. A quantitative application to faceted and dendritic silver obtained in the same experiment provides values of the model parameters which are consistent with the characteristic widths of these structures being of the same order of magnitude of the diffusion lengths of atoms on (111) and (001) surfaces. Using an extended model for iron electrodeposition on Si(100), the simulations qualitatively reproduce the initial formation of multilayer islands, small oscillations of the roughness of a polycrystalline compact film after island coalescence, and instability development. Possible directions for applying this modeling to the electrodeposition of other materials are suggested.

Bulk superconductivity in quintuple-layer square-planar nickelate La6Ni5O12

Mengzhu Shi, Mei Du, Hongyu Li, Zhiwei Wang, Zhengjie Wang et al. (8 authors)

Nickelate superconductors, sharing a similar electronic environment with cuprate superconductors, offer a promising platform for unraveling the mechanism of high-temperature superconductivity. However, the realization of superconductivity in these systems has so far been restricted to thin films or bulk samples under high-pressure. This limits the applicability of various experimental probes to study the mechanism of superconductivity, and hinders the potential application of the nickelate superconductors. Exploring bulk superconductivity at ambient pressure is therefore essential to understand its origin and enable broader experimental research. Here, we report the realization of bulk superconductivity at ambient-pressure in quintuple-layer square-planar (SP) nickelate La6Ni5O12. The SP phase is obtained by performing topotactic reaction on the quintuple-layer Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) compound La6Ni5O16. Optimized reduction condition endows La6Ni5O12 with a superconducting state that exhibits onset transition temperature of ~ 18 K, as verified by both zero resistance and diamagnetic shielding effect. Pronounced hysteresis loops revealed by magnetization and torque magnetometry demonstrate the occurrence of vortex pinning. Specific heat measurements on a polycrystalline pellet of La6Ni5O12 reveal a clear superconducting transition at ~18 K and a superconducting volume fraction of ~ 25%, which strongly supports the presence of bulk superconductivity. Our results establish an unprecedented bulk sample platform for investigating superconductivity in nickelates at ambient pressure, which paves the way for further experimental studies into the superconducting mechanism.

A Topological\-Geometrical Descriptor (TGD) for Scalable and Interpretable Modeling of Two\-Dimensional Carbon Networks

Rômulo Uriel Liberal de Oliveira, Raphael Matozo Tromer

We introduce a compact Topological-Geometrical Descriptor (TGD) for planar two-dimensional carbon networks. Atomic coordinates are mapped to a periodic planar graph and represented through ring populations, topological-charge statistics, projected ring geometry, and dual-graph defect adjacency. The resulting modeling vector contains 19 physically interpretable features. We evaluate TGD on 120 published planar carbon allotropes using the DFT relative energy per atom with respect to graphene as the target. Repeated five-fold cross-validation with gradient boosting yields a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0826 +/- 0.0138 eV/atom and R2 = 0.803 +/- 0.074. Under the same validation protocol, TGD gives a mean MAE of 0.0828 eV/atom, compared with 0.0929 eV/atom for a 203-feature Matminer representation. We further analyze 360 vacancy-defected graphene configurations spanning 12 vacancy concentrations from 2 to 24 percent. When entire vacancy concentrations are held out, TGD gives a pooled MAE of 0.269 eV/vacancy and R2 = 0.861, outperforming simple baselines based on vacancy concentration alone or non-hexagonal-ring fraction alone. Sensitivity tests show that the inferred edge sets are stable to moderate changes in the adaptive bond factor. Finally, the mean absolute topological charge is examined as a one-variable empirical surrogate for the allotrope dataset and is treated explicitly as dataset-specific rather than universal. These results show that TGD combines compact prediction with direct structural interpretation while making clear the present restriction to single-layer planar carbon networks.

High-Frequency Mass Transfer Enabling Over-Limiting Currents in Porous Electrodes

Md Abdul Hamid, Kyle C. Smith

Classical film theory predicts a finite diffusion-limited current at porous electrodes. However, over-limiting currents (OLCs), which exceed this limit, are often observed and have been attributed to surface charge, confinement, or electrokinetic effects. Here, we use a frequency-domain framework to identify a different mechanism governed by the time scale of concentration variation. For perfectly reactive interfaces, the spectral Sherwood number, a frequency-dependent dimensionless measure of interfacial mass transfer relative to molecular diffusion, grows as $\Shnd\simω^{1/2}$ at high frequency, while its phase approaches $π/4$. This response corresponds to the semi-infinite Warburg limit of the electrochemical impedance. A scaling analysis shows that the exponent and prefactor are determined by diffusion within a thin layer adjacent to the interface and are therefore independent of the global pore geometry. Numerical results for parallel plates, circular tubes, and twelve porous microstructures confirm this scaling. Geometry, flow strength, and flow direction affect the low-frequency response and crossover frequency, but not the asymptotic scaling in the high-frequency limit. This temporal route to OLCs requires neither surface charge nor confinement and could inform the design of systems for pulsed electrodeposition, mineral recovery and renewable-energy storage.
