Evolutionary constraints on protein fold diversity

Harutyun Sahakyan, Eugene V. Koonin

Arrangements of secondary structures in proteins yield a wide variety of possible topologies, yet the protein folds observed in nature are limited in their diversity. Is the number of protein folds limited by biophysical constraints or by evolutionary contingency? In recent years, the development of new methods for protein structure prediction and comparison, molecular design, and the rapid progress of cryo-electron microscopy revealed new insights into the protein fold space. Here, we present recent findings demonstrating that natural proteins adopt only a small fraction of all possible folds and discuss how evolutionary processes limit the diversity of evolving protein folds.

Bayesian Learning of Spatially Heterogeneous Causal Structure in Spatial Transcriptomics

Kyungwook Back, Junsouk Choi

Spatial transcriptomics preserves the location of each expression profile, making it possible to study how gene regulatory networks differ within a tissue. Across regions of a tissue, regulatory relationships may vary and even reverse direction, so identifying the causal relationships in a specific pathological region can provide insight into therapeutic targets. We propose SpaDAG, a Bayesian method for learning spatially varying causal directed acyclic graphs. By defining the graphs on a triangulation of the tissue, SpaDAG allows each edge to have its own spatial pattern and even to reverse direction across regions. We show that the graphs nonetheless remain fully identifiable, and develop an MCMC sampler that explores them efficiently by reversing an edge over an entire connected region in a single move. SpaDAG outperforms competing methods in simulations with and without direction reversals. In a section spanning a renal cell carcinoma and the adjacent kidney, SpaDAG finds largely different relationships in the two tissues, with those in the tumor concentrated on genes associated with therapeutic targets. Between two of these genes, it identifies a relationship whose direction reverses across the tumor boundary, generating hypotheses about their region-specific coupling. Supplementary materials and code for this article are available online.

Normative and neural network models of multisensory integration

Marcus Ghosh, John S. Butler

Animals continuously detect scents, sights and sounds, which their brains combine into diverse multisensory streams. Multisensory integration accelerates learning, increases reaction speeds and helps to resolve sensory ambiguities. In studying this process, the field has developed a continuum of models; from concise mathematical equations, with a handful of variables, to artificial neural networks, with billions of parameters. In this review, we divide this spectrum into two classes: normative models start from a hypothesis about how multisensory signals should be integrated, while neural network models aim to emulate how circuits could integrate them. Together, these models demonstrate that observers can integrate cues in a statistically optimal fashion, and help to explain experimental findings, including the diversity of multisensory neuron response profiles and network architectures. In this review, we provide a didactic overview of multisensory models, complete with accessible equations and Python code, and compare their assumptions and explanatory power. We detail what has been learned from these models. Finally, we discuss how the ideas and insights from classical theoretical models can be reconciled with those from artificial neural networks, and how this convergence should shape future research.

EEG-World: Context-Conditioned Dynamics of the Watching Brain

Junxiang Zhang, Jiayu Zuo, Wenhao Ye, Junfeng Xia, Xiang Chen et al. (7 authors)

Brain encoding models predict neural responses from audiovisual input, but typically overlook how ongoing brain activity shapes an individual's response. We introduce EEG-World, a recurrent world model that forecasts 10 seconds of EEG band-power dynamics from 6 seconds of preceding EEG and the subsequent audiovisual stimulus. Pretrained on continuous EEG, EEG-World learns shared neural dynamics and adapts to unseen individuals through lightweight subject-specific readouts. Using 2,584 hours of EEG from 78 participants, we show that nonlinear recurrent forecasting surpasses strong history-aware linear baselines at scale. EEG-World achieves a forecasting correlation of 0.261 versus 0.233 for the strongest history-and-video linear model, with a 3.3-second forecasting-horizon advantage. Its learned dynamics transfer to unseen participants, outperforming the linear baseline in 23 of 24 individuals after calibration. While ongoing EEG history accounts for most predictive performance, controlled stimulus analyses reveal an additional video-dependent component. This component also enables evaluation of visual representations, with V-JEPA 2 improving clip-locked prediction by 35% over combined CLIP and DINOv2 features. Our results demonstrate the value of transferable neural dynamics for individual EEG forecasting and highlight the importance of separating state-driven from stimulus-driven predictability.

Positive recurrence of weakly reversible stochastic reaction networks with mass-action kinetics

Daniele Cappelletti, Giulio Cuniberti

We study finite stochastic reaction networks with mass-action kinetics. A network is weakly reversible if every reaction lies on a directed cycle of complexes. We prove that, for arbitrary positive rate constants, the Markov chain of every such network is non-explosive and positive recurrent on each communicating class. This settles the long-standing chemical recurrence conjecture. The proof is by construction of a Lyapunov function. For a fixed choice of weights on the species, the function is the exponential of a weighted count of the molecules, adjusted by a bounded quantity which is defined through the paths of reactions leading back to a reference population, and which accounts for the species that are absent or scarce. Before the adjusted count can decrease, several preparatory steps may be needed. We count faster and slower steps separately, giving priority to paths that require fewer slow steps. Weak reversibility controls how competing reactions can change these counts. Finitely many choices of weights are then combined, and a minimization over a scale parameter gives a single function on each communicating class.

Accuracy of Noise Ceiling Estimators for Brain Scores

Aude Maier, Lucas Gruaz, Martin Schrimpf, Johanni Brea

The internal activations of modern machine learning models align surprisingly well with the activities recorded in real brains. In the last five years, the alignment between encoding models and brain activities, often called brain scores, has steadily increased. Because brain recordings are noisy, there exists an upper bound to the brain scores that any model can achieve. This bound, called the noise ceiling, is crucial for two reasons: it determines whether further improvements in model performance are possible, and it enables meaningful comparisons of brain scores across benchmarks. The field has developed multiple approaches to estimate noise ceilings, but the statistical properties of existing estimators remain poorly understood, leaving it unclear in which experimental settings they provide reliable estimates. Here, we introduce a unified mathematical framework for brain recordings, use it to derive closed-form expressions for four commonly used noise ceiling estimators, and provide theoretical predictions for the behavior of each estimator. We validate the theoretical predictions with simulations that we compare to noise ceiling estimates in existing fMRI and EEG datasets. We find that the existing methods underestimate the true noise ceiling in different settings. We conclude with recommendations for best practice in different situations.

Cancer multi-omics joint dimension reduction leads to reliable and interpretable survival predictions

Vincent Le Goff, Vincent Guillemot, Cathy Philippe, Wiam Ahdidou, Gwendoline Mendes et al. (8 authors)

Multi-omics studies are becoming more and more frequent, and are especially suited to decrypt the complexity of dysregulation happening in cancer. In this context, improving prognostication through interpretable methods could provide better patient stratification and help identify new prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Multi-omics data are however very often high-dimensional (few patients/high number of variables). A possible solution to high-dimensionality is Joint Dimension Reduction (JDR) methods, which compute a low-dimensional latent space from multi-omics data. In this work, we benchmark 12 JDR methods on 18 different TCGA cancer datasets. Survival prediction is achieved through a two-step process: the estimation of a low-dimensional multi-omics latent space followed by survival prediction with or without additional clinical data. We had to equip 4 methods with the ability to predict the survival of new patients. Results reveal that JDR methods can improve survival prediction with clinical data, and match the predictive performance of clinical data alone, with a high variability between different cancers. Among JDR methods, JIVE and RGCCA show superior performance. In a subsequent bootstrap analysis, JDR methods identify reliable prognostic biomarkers across cancers. We propose a clinically relevant interpretation of such markers on Low-Grade Glioma. Reproducible codes are available on https://github.com/vlegoff/MultiOBenchmark.

A Description-Derived Drug-Drug Interaction Benchmark Reveals Split-Dependent Molecular Representation Rankings

Manel Gil-Sorribes, Isaac Filella-Merce, Alexis Molina

Drug-drug interaction (DDI) prediction is clinically important and poses a distinct challenge for molecular representation learning. Unlike single-molecule property prediction, DDI requires a predictor to compose two molecular representations into a relational outcome. DrugBank provides curator-authored DDI descriptions rather than ready-made categorical labels, so a categorical prediction target must first be derived from the text. We construct a dataset of DDI pairs by mapping processed descriptions to six text-derived categories and define a benchmark with four holdout regimes that restrict train-test chemical overlap from random pairs to scaffold-disjoint evaluation. We then use this benchmark to compare molecular fingerprints, pretrained sequence representations, and graph-derived features across five seeds and multiple classifier heads. Molecular fingerprints lead the high- overlap random split, whereas graph-derived representations lead the drug- and scaffold-holdout regimes, with the gap increasing as molecular overlap is restricted. Changing the classifier head affects absolute performance but not the relative representation ranking. These results show that representation rankings change with test-set novelty under a fixed description-derived label space.

Beyond Hard Tokens: Predictive Memory for Multimodal Protein Generation

Xiaoliang Shi, Zichen Wang, Zhijian Wei, Zhanpeng Shi, Zhenghong Zhou et al. (7 authors)

Multimodal protein language models unify sequence and discrete structural tokens via iterative masked decoding, offering a promising paradigm for joint protein design. However, standard decoders rely on a memoryless mask reset at each iteration: only a sparse subset of newly committed hard tokens is carried forward, while all remaining unresolved sites are reset to an identical [MASK] embedding. In open-ended co-generation where neither modality provides a fixed external anchor, this stateless transition limits single-step refinement depth and deprives both tracks of stable cross-step soft anchors, leading to slow convergence or degenerative repetitive collapse. Observing that uncommitted predictions evolve with pronounced temporal coherence rather than erratic noise, we introduce Predictive Memory (PredMem), a training-free self-conditioning recurrence that carries these soft beliefs across steps. At each step, PredMem summarizes raw logits into a probability-weighted Top-$k$ expectation embedding at every residue and affinely interpolates it into eligible masked inputs on the next step at zero extra denoiser cost. Across DPLM-2, DPLM-2.1, and ESM3, PredMem raises unconditional sequence-structure co-generation success by 3.6, 17.7, and 48.7 percentage points, respectively, while improving the quality-diversity trade-off and suppressing repetitive mode collapse. Controlled diagnostics show that these gains stem primarily from early trajectory stabilization in unanchored cross-modal co-evolution rather than extra compute or simple entropy reduction, establishing PredMem as an efficient inference mechanism for multimodal discrete generation.

Network topology imposes spectral constraints on collective nonequilibrium responses

Dingjie Wu, Yaozu Wang, Rong Wang, Ying Wu

Collective nonequilibrium dynamics in Langevin systems depend on both network topology and stochastic driving. Understanding the role of topology in responses to stochastic driving and in the emergence of nonequilibrium is essential. Starting from the principle of microscopic reversibility and linear Langevin dynamics on networks, we derive a mode decomposition for both the asymmetry of short-time cross-correlations and heat currents at steady state. This decomposition reveals two sources of nonequilibrium---fluctuations of individual modes and correlated fluctuations between distinct modes. For reciprocal networks with independent local driving, we derive two nonequilibrium response operators that yield explicit expressions for the global asymmetry intensity and the entropy production rate. These operators are weighted Laplacians independent of stochastic driving. Their spectra determine attainable upper and lower bounds on nonequilibrium at fixed driving heterogeneity. These bounds are exact for asymmetry intensity at arbitrary deviations from equilibrium and hold to leading order near equilibrium for entropy production rate. Finally, we find that the hierarchical modular organization of human structural connectomes broadens the brain's nonequilibrium operating range, with resting-state brain activity supporting this topological advantage for sustaining a lower nonequilibrium level. Our study provides a basis for understanding biological network organization and designing networks with targeted nonequilibrium operating points.
