Double quantum dots as dynamical impurities in one-dimensional transport

Yang-Zhi Chou

We study single-electron scattering in a tight-binding chain capacitively coupled to double quantum dots, which act as dynamical two-level-system impurities with electrically tunable level splittings. For a single impurity, we solve the scattering problem exactly and show Feshbach-like perfect elastic reflection, whose resonant condition distinguishes the initial two-level state. For several impurities in a strongly reflecting chain, transmission peaks at an intermediate mean level splitting. At small splittings, energy exchange with the impurities can weaken constructive interference between backscattered paths, consistent with the initial rise in transmission. As excitation channels close, virtual transitions can enhance reflection and drive transmission below its purely elastic value $T_0$. At larger splittings, these virtual processes weaken and transmission approaches $T_0$. We discuss how gate-controlled transmission measurements in quantum-dot arrays could test these predictions.

The Shape of the Strongly Coupled MQM Ground State

Alexander Frenkel

We study a surprisingly efficient family of variational ansatze for the ground state of bosonic matrix quantum mechanics at large $N$. These are ansatze whose norm-squared probability distributions are themselves matrix integrals with single-trace actions, and so have their own independent `t Hooft expansions. They are efficient in the sense that optimizing over a small number of parameters produces many digits of accuracy for the energy, single-trace moments, and connected correlators. For infinite-$N$ planar data our results rapidly converge to within published bootstrap intervals and stabilize to a few digits of precision beyond them. We also demonstrate that we can efficiently capture $1/N$ corrections by computing connected two-point functions. These are matched against resummed quantum perturbation theory and Monte Carlo simulations.

A separation criterion for chiral systems and its application to Plebański--Demiański perturbations

Hideo Furugori, Daisuke Yoshida

We give a constructive criterion for local separation of first-order chiral systems coupling two complex doublets. It combines a Gram decomposition of the coupling sources with separate coordinate dependence and a differential transport test. For recoverable, nondegenerate source families, the criterion is necessary and sufficient within the specified class of mode-independent complex matrix transformations with unit determinant. For a fixed differential operator and admissible Gram decomposition, we identify conditions under which the sources uniquely determine the Hermitian coefficient matrix required for separation. As an application, we construct an explicit algebraic transformation and derive separated radial and angular equations for coupled radiative Einstein--Maxwell perturbations of rotating, charged Plebański--Demiański backgrounds with acceleration, NUT charge, and a cosmological constant, on specified local patches. Its Gram decomposition is unique under additional generic assumptions. We also derive angular endpoint indices for Bonnor and Misner identifications with conical deficits or excesses. Under the stated regularity assumptions, each specified self-adjoint angular realization at fixed real mode parameters has a discrete spectrum and a complete orthonormal eigenbasis.

Separation of gravitational perturbations of Myers-Perry black holes

Jun-Kun Zhao, Hong-Da Lyu

We study linearized gravitational perturbations of five-dimensional asymptotically flat Myers--Perry black holes with two unequal spins. We construct separated solutions for the five coupled gauge-invariant polarizations for arbitrary azimuthal mode numbers. The key observation is that the Killing--Yano symmetry induces a first-order angular operator that, together with a commuting second-order operator, determines the angular profiles of all five gravitational polarizations. The Carter interchange relates the scalar angular and radial equations, and the five tidal components of each separated solution share a single radial amplitude. We prove completeness of the angular harmonics at real frequencies and show that the resulting physical perturbations admit local metric reconstruction. In the singly spinning limit, we recover Santos's angular operators and radial equation for perturbations with $m_ψ=0$ and even parity under $ψ\to-ψ$. These results provide a framework for studying quasinormal modes and gravitational stability of five-dimensional rotating black holes with two independent spins.

Exceptional cubics on 7-manifolds

Sergey Grigorian

A $G_2$-structure $\varphi$ on a $7$-manifold $\mathcal M$ determines a two-dimensional space of invariant totally symmetric cubic forms on each fiber of the rank-$27$ bundle $S^2_0T^*\mathcal M$ of traceless symmetric $2$-tensors. This space is spanned by the trace cubic $C_0$ and a cubic $C_\varphi$ that, up to a constant factor, is the pointwise integrand of the Yukawa coupling on the $G_2$ moduli space. We determine the exact infinitesimal stabilizers over $\mathbb C$ of all nonzero cubics in this family. For $C_0+λC_\varphi$, the stabilizer has dimension $78$ precisely at the two non-real values $λ_\pm=(1\pm i\sqrt7)/8$. Its dimension is $21$ for $C_0$ and $14$ in all other cases. At $λ_\pm$, the stabilizer is simple of type $E_6$, and the corresponding cubic is linearly equivalent to the complex Albert algebra cubic norm. The two cubics at $λ_\pm$, which we call critical, define complex-conjugate $E_6$-reductions of the complexified bundle. At any element with nonzero critical norm, the corresponding cubic determines a complex Albert algebra with basepoint stabilizer $F_4$; a line subbundle of the tangent bundle selects a smooth bundle of these algebras with tensorial operations and a Euclidean real form. The induced Levi-Civita connection preserves either critical cubic exactly when the $G_2$-structure is torsion-free. Its orthogonal projections onto the two exceptional reductions coincide with the connection induced by the minimal $G_2$-connection.

Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction

Alec Eickbusch, Nick Noll, Michael Newman, Matt McEwen, Leigh S. Martin et al. (362 authors)

Scaling fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) requires suppressing errors in both space and time, yet standard logical memory experiments primarily probe spatial faults. Here, we use a 105-qubit Willow superconducting quantum processor to realize and scale the surface code stability experiment, which is alternatively sensitive to temporal error chains. Scaling from patch sizes of $3 \times 3$ to $7 \times 7$, we realize a timelike block suppression factor of $μ_T = 1.47 \pm 0.25$, demonstrating the ability to maintain temporal error suppression as the number of qubits increases. By extending the stability experiment for many cycles, we uncover a sum-of-exponentials behavior in error suppression caused by a temporally correlated error: data-qubit heating to the third excited state $|3\rangle$. We fully capture this effect with a parameter-free, device-level leakage model incorporating higher-state transport during syndrome extraction cycles. To suppress this error, we develop and realize a walking stability circuit, reaching a logical error probability floor of $\le 3.20\times 10^{-8}$ at 60 cycles---two orders of magnitude below the standard construction---and limited by ionizing radiation error bursts. Finally, by adaptively terminating syndrome extraction based on decoder confidence in an offline analysis, we reduce spacetime logical error by 38% and time overhead by 34% compared to the optimal fixed-cycle strategy.

Peak state transfer in trees and the cost of distance

Andrew Osborne, Andrew A. Houck

There is peak state transfer between vertices $u$ and $v$ in a graph $G$ if there exists a time $t$ for which $|(e^{\mathrm i t M})_{u,v}|$ saturates the triangle inequality imposed by its spectral decomposition and $M$ is either the adjacency or Laplacian matrix. We show that peak state transfer at distance $d$ requires $\frac{d^3}{24}(1 + o(1))$ edges in the adjacency case, $Ω(d^2)$ edges in the Laplacian case, and that the leading coefficient in the former is tight. We find a family of graphs with Laplacian peak state transfer at distance $d$ and $Θ(d^5)$ edges, showing that peak state transfer is achievable in a polynomial graph size. In contrast, Laplacian perfect state transfer costs at least $Ω(2^{d/2})$ edges when the distance partition of vertices around one vertex is almost equitable. Finally, we show that Laplacian peak state transfer in trees requires $Ω(d!)$ edges, and we characterize all trees of diameter $\leq 4$ admitting Laplacian peak state transfer, thus answering a pair of questions posed by Coutinho, Guo, and Schmeits.

Optimal Quantum Sensing Algorithms for Discrete-Frequency Spectroscopy

Yu-Jie Liu, Richard R. Allen, Ruohan Shen, Bingtian Ye, Timothy T. Chang et al. (7 authors)

Spectroscopy is a versatile tool that drives scientific discovery and underpins modern technologies. Quantum sensing offers promising opportunities to advance spectroscopy through enhanced sensitivity and spectral and spatial resolution. However, existing approaches largely focus on improving general measurement capabilities, often overlooking the structure of specific sensing tasks and leaving potential performance gains untapped. Here, we develop an algorithmic approach to quantum sensing tailored to various spectroscopic tasks, providing new avenues for improvement. Specifically, we consider general time-varying signals supported on a known set of $N$ candidate frequencies, a setting we call discrete-frequency spectroscopy. We formulate three types of practically motivated sensing tasks---Detection, Search, and Learning---that require progressively more spectral information. For all tasks, we design optimal quantum sensing algorithms achieving a $\sqrt{N}$ speedup over conventional frequency-scanning approaches. Optimal Detection and Search need only a single-qubit sensor under $π$-pulse control, whereas optimal Learning provably requires coherent processing with a quantum computer. End-to-end numerical simulations incorporating various experimental imperfections predict up to two-orders-of-magnitude speedup in Detection. As an example, we analyze the feasibility of our Detection and Search algorithms for real-world molecular screening based on state-of-the-art nitrogen-vacancy centers. We also show how quantum-computing-enhanced Learning enables wireless communication in a weak-signal regime where conventional methods fail. Our results establish a practical route toward realizing quantum advantages via designing task-specific sensing algorithms.

On the Asymptotic Expansion of Massive Fields in Spherically Symmetric Systems

Robin Fynn Diedrichs, Iris van Gemeren

The behavior of massive real scalar fields, when sourced by a central object, is usually assumed to follow a Yukawa falloff towards radial infinity. In this paper, we show that this is generically not the case, even for minimally coupled fields. Further, while the point-particle action provides a coordinate-invariant definition of the central object's scalar charge, we show that the coefficient one usually extracts at asymptotically large radii in the case of massive scalar fields is neither coordinate invariant nor identical to the scalar charge appearing in the point-particle action. We resolve this issue perturbatively by constructing matched asymptotic expansions and investigate the non-perturbative structure using numerical methods.

Benchmarking memory and nonlinear processing capacity of quantum recurrent neural networks for time series prediction

José Daniel Viqueira, Andrés Gómez, David Mera

Quantum machine learning has emerged as a promising approach to time series prediction. While quantum reservoir computing has been widely explored for this setting, variational quantum models tailored to sequential data remain comparatively understudied, largely because they are difficult to train. In this work, we present a rigorous benchmark of variational quantum recurrent neural networks (QRNNs) on time series prediction tasks, focusing on two key capabilities: memory and nonlinear processing. We introduce a task-driven methodology that quantifies model capacity, enabling an intuitive and fair comparison across architectures, including classical recurrent baselines. We evaluate the proposed framework on two generic benchmark tasks and on a synthetic dataset describing a chaotic system. Overall, the quantum models achieve performance comparable to their classical counterparts, but classical models scale better with the memory size within the range studied. Notably, one QRNN variant shows higher average capacity on the task designed to jointly probe memory and nonlinear capacity. Moreover, our results show that quantum models offer better capacity when we are able to find a good parameter initialization. However, the increasing sensitivity to initialization leads to a lower probability of finding good solutions as the model size increases. These results provide a principled starting point for designing richer quantum recurrent architectures and for assessing where they may offer practical benefits.
