Dynamics of random recurrent neural networks: colored noise and sublinear activations

German Mato, Facundo Rigatuso, Gonzalo Torroba

We study the dynamics of recurrent neural networks with random couplings, sublinear odd activation functions, and temporally correlated noise. Using large N methods, we determine stationary correlations and distinguish the loss of local linear stability from the onset of chaos, characterized by a positive largest Lyapunov exponent. These thresholds delimit an intermediate regime in which local instability coexists with asymptotic stability under a common noise history. Their separation depends on both the noise correlation time and the asymptotic growth of the activation function. We show that internal noise with any finite correlation time cuts off the divergence of the network's intrinsic correlation time tau_corr at the edge of chaos; it exhibits a critical scaling tau_{corr,max} propto 1/sqrt{D_0}, where D_0 is the integrated noise strength. Static random input instead produces coincident thresholds and a divergent correlation time. We also calculate the frequency-dependent participation dimension from a four-point correlation function. Its zero-frequency value is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the network correlation time, predicting a smooth minimum at the weak-noise chaos threshold. Numerical simulations support the large-network predictions and reveal appreciable finite-size effects.

Benchmarking Recent Single-Cell Synthetic Data Generation Methods on the OneK1K Dataset

Buse Giledereli, Basar Temiz, Amirreza Sattarzadeh Khanehbargh

Synthetic single-cell RNA-seq data generation offers a promising route for privacy-aware biomedical data sharing, but donor-linked datasets require careful balancing of biological utility and privacy risk. In this work, we benchmark synthetic generation methods for the CAMDA 2026 ELSA Health Privacy Challenge using the donor-split OneK1K dataset, comparing scGFT, scLDM, and Cell2Sentence across statistical fidelity, downstream biological preservation, and membership inference vulnerability. Because full-transcriptome generation is computationally demanding, we focus generative modeling on highly variable genes and evaluate alternative strategies for reconstructing non-HVG expression. Our results show that non-HVG handling is a central determinant of privacy: directly copying non-HVG values leads to severe leakage, whereas Poisson-based reconstruction substantially reduces membership inference risk. We further find that scGFT and scLDM better preserve biological structure than an independent Poisson baseline, while maintaining near-random privacy profiles under appropriate configurations. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating synthetic single-cell data beyond marginal similarity alone, emphasizing robustness, interpretability, and privacy-conscious design choices throughout preprocessing, generation, and post-processing.

Generalized modeling reveals stabilizing mechanisms in cross-feeding microbial communities

Tom Clegg, Thilo Gross

Understanding what makes microbial communities stable and able to resist perturbation is a fundamental question in microbial ecology. Theoretical approaches to this problem typically require making strict assumptions about the structure, parameters and functional forms used in models, hindering general insight. Here, we address this problem by developing and analyzing a generalized model of microbial community dynamics, capturing resource-mediated competitive and cross-feeding interactions between populations. The generalized modeling approach allows us to assess the stability of microbial systems across large ensembles of models with variable size, structure and non-linear dynamics. Our analysis shows how community stability is constrained by system size and identifies its key metabolic and dynamical drivers, highlighting the central roles of self-limitation, metabolic efficiency and plasticity. Furthermore, we show how the structure of communities mediates their stability, allowing communities to counteract the destabilizing cross-feeding loops through organization into hierarchical trophic structures. Together, these results provide new insight into the stability of microbial communities and offer a flexible analytical framework for future work to explore the responses of communities to perturbation.

Music Between Two Minds: Individual Signatures, Shared States, and the Emergence of Creative Interaction

Tatsuya Daikoku, Risa Kobayashi, Rina Tabuchi, Yoshia Abe, Yasuo Kuniyoshi

Some of the most valued forms of human creativity arise between people-in conversation, collaboration, and improvised music-yet how two minds jointly regulate the flow of novelty in real time remains unknown. Here we examined the information dynamics of interpersonal improvisation using 986 professional jazz improvisations. Pitch-based fluctuations of uncertainty and surprise behaved as signatures of individual identity: the two partners differed within 90% of takes, performers were identifiable from single solos and the separation between partners persisted undiminished across turns and takes. Rhythm-based fluctuations showed the reverse organization: partners were statistically indistinguishable within a take yet shifted together from take to take, and this shared state drifted gradually as each performance progressed, with each performer moving away from their own past at the same rate as from their partner's. Beyond joint drift, solo hand-offs revealed turn-by-turn complementarity: when one performer exceeded their own average surprise in the predictive dynamics of improvisation, the partner's next solo fell below their own, in both pitch and rhythm and in all duos. Improvising pairs thus neither fuse into one system nor remain two independent ones: they preserve individual melodic voices while sharing a rhythmic state, within which moment-to-moment push-pull continually rebalances their interaction. Individuality and togetherness thus coexist, carried by different musical dimensions, a measurable organization of creativity that emerges between minds.

From Turing Patterns to Spike Self-Replication and Nucleation in a Growing-Domain Tulip Breaking Virus Model

Fanya Xia, Chunyi Gai

A recently proposed reaction-diffusion model for the tulip breaking virus (TBV) has been shown to generate striped pigmentation patterns on growing tulip petals. In this paper, we investigate the formation of localized spike patterns in this model in the asymptotic limit of small viral diffusivity. It is known that two distinct bifurcation mechanisms can occur which generate new spikes as the domain half-length $L$ slowly increases: spike self-replication and spike nucleation. Self-replication occurs through a saddle-node bifurcation of the inner spike core problem, whereas nucleation is associated with a fold of the nonlinear outer problem. Using matched asymptotic expansions, we derive critical conditions for these mechanisms and construct phase diagrams that predict which mechanism occurs first as $L$ increases, or whether nucleation is absent. We further combine these results with a Turing instability analysis of the homogeneous steady state to obtain a phase diagram of pattern-forming mechanisms. An interesting finding is that, for the TBV model, the Turing-unstable region lies entirely within the parameter regime where spike nucleation is possible. These results provide further insight into the mechanisms underlying the formation and evolution of pigmentation patterns in virus-infected tulips.

Global Convergence of Detailed Balanced Mass Action Systems

Haoran Lv, Chengjin Ni, Manqin Wang, Shenli He

We give a self-contained proof that, for a finite detailed balanced mass-action system with positive rate constants, each positive stoichiometric compatibility class contains exactly one positive equilibrium and every positive trajectory converges to it, together with a Lean 4 formalization of the complete argument from persistence to convergence. Boundedness and persistence come from the barrier argument of OpenAI (2026) for weakly reversible systems: an arbitrary-tolerance affine approximation yields a finite-minimum barrier whose superlevel sets confine trajectories and give one logarithmic bound for all solutions from a given initial state. The Horn-Jackson dissipation identity identifies the positive equilibria as an exponential family with parameters in the orthogonal complement of the stoichiometric subspace, which gives uniqueness in each class. A bounded-speed descent argument replaces LaSalle's invariance principle; it uses neither uniqueness of solutions nor continuous dependence on initial data, and it places every cluster point in that family. The route given here is direct and elementary, and its main theorem is checked by the Lean kernel against explicit definitions. Report the tolerance, not the count: exact phylogenetic distance deconvolution with incomplete references

Haotian Tang

Phylogenetic distance deconvolution represents mixture-to-reference distances by weighted sources placed on a reference tree. We study the minimum number of strictly interior sources that explains a distance profile, and what incomplete references cost. On a positive-rational reduced tree we give an exact algorithm with 2^O(K) poly(B) bit complexity for a free positive mean pendant and any nonempty set of observed leaves. With arbitrary missing references and a prescribed pendant the problem is W[1]-hard in the source budget K, even on positive binary trees supplied with a strict explanation using at most K+1 sources. We then use the algorithm as an instrument. Removing references can only lower the minimum source count, never raise it. The direction is a theorem at every error tolerance and held without exception in 23,545 draws, so which references are missing matters more than how many. Two adversarially chosen references suffice, at the median, to destroy uniqueness of the minimum explanation, and that number does not grow with panel size (Spearman 0.203, p = 0.15, over 20 to 353 references) where random loss scales linearly. Discarding 7,931 of 10,575 references from a real 16S panel at random costs 1.8% of the tolerance at which one source suffices. Discarding comparably many as whole clades costs 90%. That tolerance needs neither ground truth nor an error model, which makes the model measurable on real data. Published distance estimates exceed it in 296 of 296 cases. The mixture inferred by the published heuristic reproduces real observations better than the configuration that generated them in 282 of 296. The phenomenon is not unobservable in practice. It is unobservable through a point estimate of the count. Methods for this problem should report the tolerance at which an explanation of a given size exists.

RiboBridge: protein-conditioned de novo RNA binder structure design

Zhou Zhang, Hanqun Cao, Minghao Sun, Odin Zhang, Yang Zhang et al. (8 authors)

Designing RNA molecules that bind to target protein structures requires generating both an RNA conformation and its bound complex geometry, without assuming a predefined binding site. Existing approaches generally model these coupled processes within a unified representation, entangling RNA conformation generation with protein-relative placement. In this work, we introduce RiboBridge, a structure-first framework that separates RNA conformational generation from protein--RNA assembly through cross-frame interaction geometry. RiboBridge first proposes multiple candidate RNA-binding interfaces on the protein surface, generates canonical RNA conformations within a pretrained RNA structural latent space (RiboSphere), and then predicts protein--RNA interaction constraints to bridge the independently defined RNA and protein coordinate frames. The resulting interaction geometry is converted into a geometrically consistent protein-relative pose through rigid placement. We evaluate RiboBridge on a temporally split, sequence-filtered, and redundancy-controlled benchmark of 124 protein--RNA complexes. Under post-hoc interface-correspondence evaluation, where native information is used only after generation to select among already generated predicted-pocket candidates, RiboBridge achieves the highest fNAT and DockQ and the lowest interface and ligand RMSDs among compared methods. Component ablations and stage-wise diagnostics further characterize the contributions of RNA conformational generation, interaction prediction, and rigid placement, identifying the main sources of complex-recovery error.

Supercoiling Drives DNA-Protein Co-Condensation

Sihan Liu, Hongbo Zhao, Andrej Košmrlj

The co-condensation of DNA and proteins plays an important role in genome compaction, including heterochromatin formation and chromosome segregation. Meanwhile, transcription and replication continuously generate DNA supercoiling, yet how the resulting torsional stress affects co-condensation remains poorly understood. Here, by coupling DNA elasticity to a protein concentration field, we show that supercoiling acts as a topological catalyst for co-condensation through a mutually reinforcing interplay with protein wetting. Specifically, protein wetting facilitates twist-to-writhe conversion, generating highly writhed DNA conformations that in turn promote capillary collapse into a protein-rich droplet. In the absence of supercoiling, DNA remains kinetically trapped in metastable extended states within our model, whereas sufficiently strong supercoiling removes the nucleation barrier and enables spontaneous co-condensation. These results establish topology as a distinct control parameter for biomolecular condensation and provide a mechanical framework for how active genomic processes can dynamically regulate large-scale genome organization.

Modelling Toxin-Induced Intraspecific Personality Variation in Predator-Prey Dynamics

Jingyi Jin, Tian Xu Wang, Hao Wang

Personality traits, such as boldness and shyness, can affect interspecific interactions through differences in individual behaviour. Environmental toxins may also alter these traits. However, few predator-prey models have considered animal personality. In this study, we extend a toxin-dependent predator-prey model by incorporating personality variation in both prey and predators. Prey personality affects birth and growth, whereas predator personality affects the predator functional response. The model is parameterized for rainbow trout and aquatic insect prey exposed to methylmercury. Numerical results show that prey and predator personality have different effects on population dynamics. Prey personality tends to reduce biomass fluctuations and promote stable coexistence. In contrast, predator personality can increase biomass fluctuations and lead to persistent population oscillations. These effects are mainly observed at low and intermediate toxin concentrations that are environmentally relevant to freshwater ecosystems. Using a quasi-steady-state approximation, we reduce the model to a two-dimensional system and study its positivity, boundedness, and the existence of equilibria. The equilibrium structure depends jointly on the toxin level and the prey and predator personality contrasts. Based on this existence classification, we numerically investigate the local stability of all feasible equilibria and identify parameter regions corresponding to locally stable extinction, prey-only persistence, coexistence, and bistability. Overall, toxin-induced personality variation can qualitatively change predator-prey dynamics.
