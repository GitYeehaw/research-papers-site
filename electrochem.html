Antiferromagnetic phase of MnTe2: spin-order-induced renormalization of phonon Raman spectrum

Taras Palasyuk, Ashutosh S. Wadge, Eduard E. Zubov, Igor Antoniazzi, Gayatri et al. (13 authors)

Recently, two modes of spin wave excitations (magnons) were detected in Raman scattering spectra of MnTe2. It is an unconventional magnet that features a pyrite-type crystal structure hosting a non-collinear, non-coplanar antiferromagnetic (AFM) spin order below the Néel temperature. In this brief communication, the focus is primarily on the effect of spin ordering on lattice dynamics (phonons) that has been established through temperature (5 - 300 K), linear polarization angle (0 deg - 360 deg) and external magnetic field (up to 16 T) dependent measurements. It has been found that below the magnetic transition temperature, the frequencies of four out of five Raman-active lattice vibrations (i.e., Eg and three Tg phonon modes) revealed significant departure from the mechanism of the three-phonon anharmonic decay described by Klemens model. Here, we propose a new model considering spin-phonon coupling to explain frequency renormalization of the phonon part of the Raman spectrum upon the para-/antiferro- (PM/AFM) magnetic phase transition change in MnTe2. Through employing Green's function formalism, we show that a simple two-sublattice collinear antiferromagnet approach follows well both spin ordering induced hardening and softening of phonon modes observed in AFM phase of MnTe2 upon cooling. Additionally, the detailed temperature and field dependences of the energy for both magnon modes are presented. In addition to unexpected twofold anisotropy of the higher energy magnon mode (approx. 3.5 meV) unveiled in crossed configuration, our magneto-optical measurements revealed a subtle, yet definite softening trend for the lower energy mode (approx. 2.6 meV). Although the precise reasons for these effects remain unclear, we put forward a symmetry reduction in the local atomic surroundings as a possible mechanism for discussion.

Topological insulators on site-eliminated, projected, and patterned fractal lattices

Aiden Mains, Danile Salib, Christopher Leong, Bitan Roy

Identifying fractal lattices of fractional (Hausdorff) dimension as geometric descendants of parent crystals, we showcase the successful realization of canonical topological insulators in one-, two-, and three-dimensional crystals on their self-similar descents using three complementary approaches, starting from lattice-regularized models in integer dimensions. (a) In the first approach, we construct the fractal Hamiltonian by removing all parent-lattice sites that do not belong to such a network (method of site elimination). (b) In the second one, we integrate or project out the same set of sites to obtain an effective Hamiltonian for the fractal lattice (method of projection or renormalization). (c) In the third approach, we retain the parent-crystal lattice intact but spatially pattern the on-site mass parameter, which controls the topology of the parent insulating system, such that it assumes two topologically distinct values on the fractal network and its complement (patterned fractal lattices). By numerically diagonalizing the resulting Hamiltonians and computing appropriate topological invariants or local markers, corroborating the bulk-boundary correspondence through gapless modes at outer or inner boundaries, we establish Su-Schrieffer-Heeger topology on the Cantor set using all three approaches, Haldane topology on the hexaflake [using (a) and (b)] and Qi-Wu-Zhang topology on the Sierpiński carpet [using (c)], and strong $Z_2$ topological insulating phases on the tiled Menger sponge from methods (a), (b), and (c). These findings complement and substantially extend the previously reported realization of Qi-Wu-Zhang topology on the Sierpiński carpet using methods (a) and (b) [Phys. Rev. B 110, L241302 (2024)].

Compositionally Tunable Synthesis of High-Entropy Transition Metal Dichalcogenides Exhibiting Above-Room-Temperature Magnetism

Chen Zhang, Xiahua Zhong, Dejia Kong, Wenhu Wang, Christopher T. Nelson et al. (18 authors)

As silicon-based electronics approach their physical limits, low-dimensional magnetic materials have attracted increasing attention for next-generation electronic and information technologies. Realizing these opportunities requires two-dimensional (2D) materials with robust and tunable room-temperature magnetism. Here, we report a two-step synthesis strategy for fabricating 2D high-entropy transition metal dichalcogenides (HE-TMDs) incorporating magnetic elements beyond conventional groups 4-6. High-entropy layered double hydroxides (HE-LDHs) are first synthesized via hydrothermal coprecipitation, enabling atomic-level mixing of diverse transition metals across groups 4-12. Subsequent chalcogenization yields 2D HE-TMDs while preserving homogeneous elemental distribution. This approach circumvents the constraints of solid-state reaction, chemical vapor transport methods, enabling the synthesis of compositionally complex and structurally controlled 2D magnets. Representative (VCrFeCoNi)S2-x exhibits robust magnetism above room temperature, demonstrating the potential of this high-entropy design strategy for expanding the compositional and functional diversity of 2D magnetic materials.

Robust growth of $α$-tantalum on sapphire at room temperature using atomically thin titanium seed layer

Anand Kamlapure, Lucas Hak, Kishore Kumar Thiyagarajan, Hozanna Miro, Marc Beekman et al. (7 authors)

Tantalum (Ta) based superconducting circuits have attracted attention for their low microwave loss. However, reproducible growth of high-quality $α$-phase Ta films remains challenging because film properties are highly sensitive to growth conditions. We present a robust, room-temperature route for $α$-Ta growth on sapphire substrates using an ultrathin Ti seed layer, which significantly widens the accessible deposition parameter space. We compare this approach to Ta films grown at high temperature without a seed layer and at room temperature with a Nb seed. We assess films using structural and surface characterization and quantify microwave loss with coplanar-waveguide (CPW) resonators measured in the quantum regime. We obtain two-level-system (TLS)-limited quality factors comparable to the state of the art for Ta-on-sapphire CPW resonators with similar geometries. In addition, temperature-dependent measurements reveal that high-temperature-grown films and films subjected to extended buffered-oxide etch treatment exhibit additional temperature activated loss, highlighting the role of growth route and post-processing in microwave performance. Our results establish ultrathin seed-layer engineering as a reliable alternative route to reproducible, low-loss $α$-Ta films and motivate closer study of the interfacial mechanisms that govern Ta phase formation.

A model colloidal gel former for the measurement of inter-particle forces

Rui Cheng, Kristian Thijssen, Abraham Mauleon Amieva, Tanniemola B. Liverpool, Malcolm A. Faers et al. (7 authors)

Tracking of particle coordinates in colloidal model systems is an established technique. Recently, an emulsion system has been introduced where not only the coordinates, but also contacts and compressive forces between the droplets can be determined [Dong et al. J. Chem. Phys. 156 214907 (2022). Here we develop this system to control both the density mismatch and refractive index contrast between the emulsion droplets and the solvent. The former is important in minimizing sedimentation due to gravity and the latter is necessary for 3d imaging with confocal microscopy. By mapping our system carefully to computer simulation, we quantify interdroplet forces and packing geometries in depletion gels and probe the relationship between local structure and compressive stress. The simulations also provide ensemble measurements of the attractive part of the force between droplets and related quantities such as the stress tensor. Finally, we implement an improved measure of local stress in our experiments, which can be directly compared with the corresponding quantity in simulations. We find that locally rigid minimum energy clusters are associated with regions of higher stress in the gel network.

Anharmonic enhancement of thermal conductivity in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$: A consistent picture from molecular dynamics and Wigner transport

Luiz Felipe C. Pereira, Kamil Iwanowski, Michele Simoncelli, Omar Abou El Kheir, Marco Bernasconi et al. (7 authors)

Anharmonicity limits heat conduction in crystals, but its role in amorphous solids, where heat is carried mainly by tunneling between quasi-degenerate vibrations, remains unsettled. Here we disentangle the contributions of structural disorder, quantum statistics, and anharmonicity to the thermal conductivity of amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ (aGST), the prototypical phase-change memory material. Using a single machine-learned potential trained on first-principles data, we compare classical equilibrium and nonequilibrium molecular dynamics (MD), which include anharmonicity to all orders, with harmonic Allen-Feldman theory and the anharmonic Wigner transport equation, both of which include quantum statistics. Four MD approaches agree on a conductivity of 0.171-0.181 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ at room temperature, about 16% above the harmonic Allen-Feldman value for the same model, and the Wigner formulation bridges the two: with classical statistics, it agrees with MD to within 5%. We trace this anharmonic enhancement to vibrational couplings that grow with the frequency difference between modes, so that anharmonic broadening opens additional channels for heat conduction. A comparison with silica, alumina, and disordered carbons reveals three regimes, in which anharmonicity reduces, barely affects, or enhances the conductivity. The predicted glass-like increase of the conductivity with temperature is consistent with thermoreflectance measurements. These results show that anharmonicity has no universal effect in disordered solids, and can promote rather than limit heat transport in strongly disordered materials.

Native vacancy energetics and association in TiN and CrN: chemical potential dependence, clustering, and surface effects

Barsha Bhattacharjee, Emilia Olsson

Native vacancy populations in transition-metal nitride coatings reflect vacancy formation, association, and depth-dependent stability. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compare these effects in rocksalt TiN and CrN in bulk and at (001) surfaces, within phase-stability-constrained chemical potential ranges. In TiN, $V_N$ is favoured under N-poor and $V_Ti$ under N-rich conditions; in CrN, $V_N$ remains preferred throughout its stability range. Association energies are referenced to corresponding isolated vacancies where positive values mean association lowers the energy and negative values favour separation. Mean bulk association energies for mixed metal-nitrogen pairs are $+0.132$ eV in TiN and $+1.053$ eV in CrN. At (001) surfaces, $V_N$ in TiN prefers the surface and $V_Ti$ the first subsurface layer. In CrN, $V_N$ has similar formation energies at the surface and first subsurface layer, with a slight preference for the latter; $V_Cr$ slightly prefers the second subsurface layer. Surface cluster energies are referenced to separated vacancies at their preferred depths. The compact $2V_Ti+2V_N$ cluster in TiN has favourable association in bulk ($+0.217$ eV per vacancy) but unfavourable association at the surface ($-0.346$ eV per vacancy). By contrast, $2V_Cr+2V_N$ in CrN remains stabilised, with its association energy decreasing from $+1.188$ to $+0.431$ eV per vacancy. CrN's stronger association occurs without larger structural distortions and is accompanied by distinct Cr-centred magnetic responses.

Nanoparticles with a BANG: Detonation-Driven Synthesis of Metals and Metal Oxides

Jan Maurycy Uszko, Robert Matyáš, Naoto Shirahata, Stephen J. Eichhorn, Avinash J. Patil et al. (6 authors)

Bristol Accelerated Nanoparticle Generation (BANG) method is a solvent- and capping-agent-free nanoparticle synthesis. Previous work was limited mostly to gold, silver and copper particles. Here, the method is extended to ten further elements, including Pb, Bi, Tl, Mn, Fe, Zn, Pt, Ni, Cd and Pd. Detonation in air favoured metal formation for less oxophilic metals, whereas detonation of silver azide under nitrogen suppressed detectable silver oxide formation while retaining locally identified hexagonal close-packed (HCP) Ag alongside face-centred cubic (FCC) Ag. The outcome of the synthesis can be modified by changing the detonation environment, while the highly non-equilibrium conditions provide access to phases that are difficult to obtain using conventional synthetic routes. BANG is a broadly applicable, highly non-equilibrium nanoparticle synthesis method whose products are governed by precursor chemistry and atmosphere, with potential access to kinetically trapped phases and direct supported-particle deposition.

Quantum-Inspired Tensor Train Spectral Solver for High-Resolution PDEs with Non-Periodic Boundary Conditions

Sascha H. Hauck, Nis-Luca van Hülst, Matthias Kabel, Dieter Jaksch, Nicolas R. Gauger

Tensor train (TT) methods enable high-resolution computational mechanics simulations by exploiting low-rank structure in discretized fields and operators. While the superfast Fourier transform (SFFT) has enabled efficient TT-based spectral solvers for periodic problems, corresponding representations for non-periodic boundary conditions remain underexplored. We introduce the superfast cosine and sine transforms (SFCT and SFST), extending the TT spectral framework to homogeneous Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions, respectively. Building on a quantum-circuit construction of the discrete cosine and sine transforms, we derive both transforms from a shared TT operator representation and prove that their TT ranks are bounded by $6r_S$, independently of the discretization order, where $r_S$ is the rank of the SFFT at the prescribed accuracy. The measured ranks plateau near 32 for grid sizes up to $2^{26}$ points. For Rayleigh-Bénard convection, spectral pressure projection achieves up to a 7-fold speedup in the Poisson solve relative to a preconditioned physical-space TT solver while maintaining comparable accuracy. Three-dimensional computational homogenization achieves a 2-6-fold speedup and a 4-7-fold reduction in memory consumption compared with the existing TT-based approach. The results establish an efficient TT framework for spectral PDE solvers with non-periodic boundary conditions.

Fast and Accurate Boys Function Evaluation Using Nonuniform Gridded Taylor Expansions

Longxiang Yan, Wei Wu, Peifeng Su

Efficient Boys function evaluation is important for Gaussian integral calculations on both central processing units (CPUs) and graphics processing units (GPUs). Gridded Taylor expansion provides a simple evaluation procedure, but a uniformly spaced reference table requires substantial storage over a wide argument range. We address this limitation using a nonuniform grid whose spacing increases with the argument. For large arguments, we introduce a normalized auxiliary function whose scaling and derivative properties allow it to be evaluated using the same Taylor procedure as the main-domain Boys functions. The main-domain and large-argument reference tables are constructed jointly, with polynomial degrees and grid parameters selected under accuracy and storage constraints. Low-part corrections to the reference values are included to improve the accuracy of the Taylor evaluation. The resulting method is implemented in the Fast and Accurate BOys (FABO) library and evaluated in QUICK and NWChem-Simint. FABO gives the smallest maximum and mean errors among the tested implementations for all three reported error measures, with the largest sampled error being approximately 1.067 units in the last place (ULPs). In application benchmarks, FABO reduces the Fock-build time relative to the original Boys evaluators in both applications and is competitive with the other tested implementations.
