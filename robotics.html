Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots

Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao et al. (11 authors)

Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

Modeling the Frictional Contact of Continuum Robots with Environments through Linear Complementarity Problem

Jia Shen, Junhyoung Ha, Yue Chen

Accurate friction modeling in continuum robots is essential for precise motion control and path planning. However, current approaches make predictions only for the mechanism's internal friction that is limited by the boundary forces of the Coulomb friction cone, which fail to characterize the static friction and motion hysteresis. In this paper, we propose a novel friction model based on complementarity constraints under the Coulomb friction assumption. This model predicts various friction effects caused by environmental interactions, including both static and sliding frictions and hysteresis behavior, as well as the corresponding robot deformation induced by these interactions, which are crucial for the practical motion planning and control of continuum robots. Additionally, real-time implementation of this model can be achieved, meeting the demand for computational efficiency in robotic applications. Experimental validations demonstrated that this model is able to reduce the maximum tip position error from 45.4 mm in the frictionless model to 5.85 mm in our friction-included model with a 287.9 mm-long tendon-driven continuum robot.

Impact of Grid-Forming Inverter Placement and Penetration on Local and Aggregate Frequency

Fatma Alfailkawi, Marena Trujillo, Bri-Mathias Hodge

Modern power systems are undergoing a huge shift in generation mix, including inverter-based resources that are fundamentally changing the stability dynamics of the grid. Thus, synchronous Generators (SG) are gradually being displaced by Grid-forming inverters (GFM) that are proposed as a solution to continue supporting grid stability. However, the effect of GFM placement in the network on frequency performance and how well aggregate-frequency assessment captures placement sensitivity observed in local frequency metrics remains unclear. This paper evaluates all SG and droop-controlled GFM assignments in the IEEE 39-bus system. A reduced-order model is used for the exhaustive search with load disturbances applied separately at every eligible load bus while considering nadir and Rate of Change of Frequency (RoCoF) derived from both device rating weighted aggregate frequency and local bus frequencies. Results reveal that at fixed GFM penetration, placement produced up to 104 mHz variation in worst-case local nadir and 0.21 Hz/s in maximum local RoCoF. Aggregate-frequency assessment reduced the apparent across placements by 89\% for nadir and 88\% for RoCoF at 50\% GFM penetration. These results indicate that aggregate-frequency assessment alone may obscure favorable or adverse placement effects on local frequency performance.

Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments

Kaleb Ben Naveed, Haejoon Lee, Anouck Girard, Dimitra Panagou

Autonomous robots are widely used for persistent monitoring of spatiotemporal environments, where quantities of interest (QoI) change across space and time and require repeated measurements. In practice, robot motion can be affected by external disturbances that vary across the environment. When such external disturbances are not considered in trajectory optimization, it can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot motion and, consequently, the information acquired about the QoI during execution. However, estimating disturbances throughout the environment can be inefficient, as disturbances in regions with little overlap with monitoring trajectories have limited impact on monitoring performance. We therefore propose a two-stage informative planning framework for unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization. First, we identify where QoI measurements are needed and generate alternative paths to these locations using the environment geometry. We then prioritize disturbance measurements based on their expected reduction of disturbance uncertainty along these paths. Second, rather than optimizing information acquisition based on nominal robot motion, we incorporate the learned disturbance belief into trajectory optimization to account for its effects on robot motion and information acquisition. Across four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces disturbance-learning time by 25.0--52.3 percent compared with environment-wide estimation and identifies paths that appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable when disturbances are considered.

EPIGRIDS: Documentation and Validation of Seven Open Synthetic Transmission Grid Cases

Jonathan M. Snodgrass, Scott Greene, Thomas J. Overbye, Christopher L. DeMarco, Bernard C. Lesieutre

This paper documents and checks EPIGRIDS, seven open synthetic transmission grid cases from four independently built networks, made with an optimization-based construction method instead of the graph-statistics method behind most large public synthetic grids. The cases range from 250 to 78,484 buses, with geolocated substations, voltages up to 765 kV, and line parameters taken from conductor data. Each ships under an open license in three common power flow formats that agree at the solved operating point, and the six larger cases have a companion hourly, bus-level load series for one year. The Texas, Florida, and Michigan, Ohio, and Indiana cases are sub-networks of the Eastern case. Every case solves an alternating-current power flow from its stored operating point. With parallel circuits merged, the Eastern and Western cases are 9 and 16% denser than the real interconnections, while graph-statistics cases on the same footprints lie within 6% and meet more published realism criteria. A full single-outage contingency analysis completes on every case, and in an optimal power flow benchmark every case except the New England base case and one of its 12 operating points solves in polar form.

In Situ Monitoring and Predictive Modeling of Harmful Algal Blooms with Autonomous Surface Vehicles

Nenyi Kweku Nkensen Dadson, Jean Paul Gonzalez, Daniel P. Arabie, Ryan J. Cedel, Dorin Boldor et al. (6 authors)

Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) pose significant environmental, economic, and public-health challenges in coastal and inland water bodies, creating a need for scalable methods to collect spatially distributed water-quality data. This paper presents an autonomous surface vessel-based sensing system for in-situ water-quality monitoring related to harmful algal bloom detection. The system integrates a BlueBoat Autonomous Surface Vessel (ASV), a motorized winch for depth-controlled sonde deployment, a multiparameter water-quality sonde, side-scan sonar, and an event-camera payload. Autonomous waypoint missions were used to collect measurements across selected sampling regions and multiple depths, enabling both horizontal and vertical characterization of environmental conditions. Field deployments were conducted in Lake Pontchartrain, Louisiana, with additional measurements collected in False River, Louisiana to increase dataset diversity. The collected measurements included algal pigment indicators, nutrient concentration, turbidity, temperature, and other water-quality parameters relevant to bloom formation. In addition, complementary sonar and event-camera data were collected during vehicle operation to provide contextual environmental information. A machine-learning classification approach was also evaluated using the collected water-quality measurements to support HAB and non-HAB classification. The results demonstrate the feasibility of using a modular ASV platform for repeatable, multi-modal water-quality data collection and support future development of autonomous monitoring systems.

Toward Resilient Self-Driving Laboratories through Telerobotic Interventions

Ziheng Wu, Adrian Vecina Tercero, Andre A. Fatehi, Abhik Sarkar, Brian Giera et al. (9 authors)

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) accelerate scientific research by integrating robotics, automated experimentation, and machine learning to execute experiments autonomously. Biology and chemistry have adopted these systems. However, increasing SDL complexity heightens susceptibility to unexpected faults in automated workflows, highlighting the need for efficient recovery mechanisms. In this study, we investigate telerobotic interventions to enhance SDL resilience against unexpected failures. We developed a teleoperation interface enabling human operators to resolve disruptions during automated tasks. The interface enables precise control of a six-degree-of-freedom (DOF) robotic arm (UR3e) using a 3D mouse (3Dconnexion SpaceMouse Pro). Two velocity-based controller strategies were evaluated: (i) a coupled mode mapping all six DOFs to the 3D mouse's central knob and (ii) a decoupled mode separating translational and rotational motions. To evaluate the framework, teleoperators intervened across three SDL fault scenarios. In the first study, comparative trials showed that the decoupled controller was associated with shorter completion times and reduced rotational motion. In a second study, teleoperators performed an aiming task with and without reference-path guidance. Users were more target-driven and risk-tolerant without guidance but showed behavioral differences under different guidance strategies. These findings suggest that controller configuration and reference-path design influence operator performance during SDL fault recovery, informing teleoperation interface optimization. This work demonstrates the potential feasibility of teleoperation as a human-in-the-loop recovery mechanism for SDL workflows, combining robotic precision with human adaptability and problem-solving. These findings also provide a foundation for future studies in shared autonomy and semi-autonomous intervention.

When Will You Hand It Over? A Hidden Start Delay Makes Self-Play Handover Robust to Unfamiliar Partners

Mohammad Khoshnazar, Mike Degany, Deyuan Qu, Qing Yang, Matyas Szanto

Cooperative self-play can teach a humanoid to receive an object without any human data, but the partner it meets after training is never the one it trained with; we show that a fluent self-play handover policy breaks with unfamiliar partners, identify the main cause, and repair it. In a physics-based parcel handover between a Unitree G1 and a learned giver, both trained by alternating PPO self-play, the receiver completes 95% and 90% of handovers with its own partner (two training seeds) but only 31% with scripted partners it has never met. Paired interventions isolate the cause: under a shared reward the learned giver always starts its offer at once, so the receiver learns a single rendezvous, and delaying its own partner by one second cuts success from 95% to 6% (87% to 34% in the second seed). We introduce hidden start-delay randomisation (HSD), a one-line rule on the learned partner that the receiver never observes. After 300 further updates, success with unseen scripted partners rises from 31% to 80% in both seeds, against 35--41% for a budget-matched control. The gain holds on a fresh scenario seed, on eleven held-out partner conditions and on partners that start later than any training delay; it cuts high-force contact three- to five-fold and needs no retraining of the partner.

From AI-Enabled Remote Patient Monitoring Toward Agentic Robotics: Embodiment, Governance, and Care Integration

Ivana Ragusa, Lorenzo Guiducci, Filippo Cavallo

AI-enabled remote patient monitoring is evolving beyond passive data collection toward agentic AI systems that interpret patient states, coordinate care, and initiate action across domestic and clinical settings. This structured scoping review finds that mature agentic care has not yet emerged: Most systems remain human led, while stronger decision-action coupling and embodied AI remain largely confined to prototypes and pilots. Healthcare robotics makes the transition from monitoring to action especially visible by translating AI-generated recommendations and decisions into speech, movement, prompting, physical assistance, escalation, and care coordination, making embodiment, clinical integration, human oversight, and governance fundamental design requirements. Further progress requires safe and interoperable care architectures in which intelligent components operate through clinically validated and accountable workflows. We propose agentic robotics as a future direction for supervised systems embedded in the care ecosystem, connecting AI agents and robots with clinicians, caregivers, and patients through auditable workflows that can be interrupted and remain subject to human oversight and explicit AI governance.

Policy-Conditioned Koopman Representations for Parameterized Closed-Loop Systems

Kiran G. Krishnan, Shankar A. Deka

Predicting the response of a nonlinear system across different feedback controllers requires a model that captures how the closed-loop dynamics change with the controller parameters. In a finite-dimensional Koopman model, these changes may affect both the latent dynamics and the observable coordinates used to represent them. In this paper, we propose a policy-conditioned Koopman representation framework to address this problem. This framework allows a single learned model to predict a family of closed-loop trajectories without retraining for each controller. We test the framework on four nonlinear prediction benchmarks, where the model stays below a 15% error threshold on every system, while each baseline exceeds it on at least one. As an example application, the resulting surrogate supports one-shot feedback-parameter selection: an optimization at the initial state selects a controller that then operates with fixed parameters and ongoing state feedback. On nonlinear control benchmarks including MuJoCo, the selected controllers stabilize every system with 3 to 97 times less online computation than nonlinear model predictive control. These results demonstrate how policy-dependent coordinates can support both prediction across closed-loop systems and controller selection without repeated online trajectory optimization.
