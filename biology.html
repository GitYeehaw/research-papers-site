Connectivity from activity in spiking neural networks

Albert J. Wakhloo, Sebastian Mizera

We show that a wide range of recurrent neural network models obey a simple set of equations tying synaptic connectivity and single-cell response properties to neural correlations. Our modeling framework considers networks in a stationary, asynchronous state and contains a broad class of synaptic filters, spiking nonlinearities, and random-plus-structured connectivity. The resulting equations naturally decompose into contributions from monosynaptic, disynaptic, and higher order paths through the network. We leverage this decomposition to express the effective monosynaptic connection between two neurons in terms of experimentally measurable statistics of the activity, and in particular, their high-frequency correlations. In this way, we show that it is possible to recover network connectivity from neural activity.

Anchoring Motion, Refining Trajectories: Generalizable Continuous Neural Decoding

Fanqi Shen, Junru Chen, Surui Cheng, Jiahe Li, ZhiZhang Yuan et al. (9 authors)

Brain activity encodes motor information across multiple temporal scales, enabling continuous neural control beyond discrete action recognition. However, existing methods are typically tailored to specific subjects and tasks, with direct neural-to-kinematic mappings that can entangle subject-specific neural variability with task-dependent motion patterns, limiting generalization. To address these limitations, we introduce AMRT, a shared neural decoding framework for heterogeneous electrode configurations and movement paradigms. Motivated by cortical encoding of movement direction and speed, AMRT adopts a two-stage design that anchors local motion through velocity estimation and refines the resulting trajectories over longer horizons, encouraging reuse of local motion tendencies across subjects and tasks. Across three datasets, AMRT generalizes to unseen trials and subjects and demonstrates transfer potential across neural recordings and movement paradigms, highlighting the potential of shared neural representations and anchor-and-refine decoding for generalizable continuous neural control.

Open-fMIND: A Large-Scale Open-Access fMRI Corpus for Pretraining Brain Foundation Models

Carlo Alberto Barbano, Giovanni Marraffini, Demian Wassermann

Self-supervised pretraining has transformed language and vision, but its application to functional MRI (fMRI) is constrained by data access. The largest standardized fMRI resources are governed by data use agreements and dominated by a small number of acquisition protocols, while the thousands of openly licensed datasets on OpenNeuro are individually small, heterogeneously organized, and unprocessed. We present the first release of Open-fMIND, a uniformly preprocessed, openly accessible corpus of parcellated fMRI timeseries from 322 OpenNeuro studies, comprising 11,379 subjects and 45,731 functional runs. Each run is preprocessed with fMRIPrep and parcellated under 52 atlases spanning 14 families, yielding a massive 2.4 million timeseries. Timeseries are released either denoised with our default pipeline (preproc) or as the fMRIPrep output with its confound files (quasiraw), with functional connectivity matrices, per-run provenance and motion metadata. Open-fMIND is intended as a pretraining resource for self-supervised brain representation learning. Pretraining on it yields downstream performance comparable to that obtained with UK Biobank.

Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation modulates auditory-evoked responses and conflict adaptation in humans

Issey Kamatani, Yasuo Kabe, Shinichi Kumagai, Yoshiyuki Onuki, Hexin Xu et al. (9 authors)

Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) engages ascending neuromodulatory systems, and animal studies have motivated the hypothesis that it shifts sensory cortical processing toward feedforward (FF) relative to feedback (FB) transmission. Whether transcutaneous auricular VNS (taVNS) produces a corresponding pattern in humans remains unclear. In a single-blind, sham-controlled crossover study, 11 healthy adults received 120 min of taVNS (25 Hz, left tragus) and sham stimulation (left earlobe) in separate sessions. Click-evoked EEG was recorded before, twice during, and immediately after stimulation. Sensory discrimination thresholds and conflict adaptation in a Simon task were assessed before and during stimulation. Relative to Sham, taVNS attenuated the decline in the early P0 response across the session and enhanced the Pa-Nb response early during stimulation. The later P2 response did not show the increase observed under Sham. After stimulation, taVNS reduced click-locked theta (5-8 Hz) inter-trial phase coherence relative to Sham, with no significant between-condition difference in the change in theta power. Conflict adaptation increased under taVNS, whereas sensory thresholds and the overall Simon effect did not differ significantly between conditions. In exploratory analysis, greater decreases in theta phase coherence were associated with greater increases in conflict adaptation. These findings indicate that taVNS modulates auditory responses across processing stages and conflict-related behavior. The pattern is compatible with altered FF/FB weighting, but the direction of information flow and the underlying neuromodulatory mechanisms were not measured directly.

From antidepressants to psychedelics: revisiting the pharmacology of so-called serotoninergic treatments

Marc Gielen, Daniela Duarte Domingues, Eric Burgui{è}re, Kevin Swierkosz- Lenart, Luc Mallet

Recent years have seen a resurgence of interest in serotoninergic psychedelics as potential treatment for diverse psychiatric disorders. Here, we will delve into the molecular mechanisms of drugs used to treat depression and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), with implications on the mechanism of action of so-called ''serotoninergic'' psychedelics as well as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs). In particular, we argue that the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of SSRIs suggest that the inhibition of the serotonin transporter (SERT) alone does not provide a comprehensive account of their clinical effects. On the contrary, the SSRI posology required to treat depression and OCD is consistent with their major molecular target(s) displaying an SSRI affinity much lower than SERT. Such is the case for the tyrosine receptor kinase B (TrkB), recently described as a putative target for drugs with antidepressant effects. TrkB might thus provide a unifying framework for understanding the therapeutic effects of antidepressant drugs. We finally discuss the pharmacological implications of this hypothesis on the therapeutical potential of psychedelics.

iEEG Can Help EEG: Improving Clinical Scalp EEG Analysis through Intracranial Priors

Xianglong Bao, Yi Ding, Zengxiang Li, Nan Liu, Kewen Wang et al. (7 authors)

Scalp electroencephalography (EEG) is a cornerstone of clinical brain monitoring, making the quality of its signal fundamental to downstream tasks and models. However, that signal is heavily contaminated by non-neural interference and blurred by volume conduction. Existing methods operate almost exclusively within the scalp modality, so they inherit the contamination they would need to overcome. In practice, intracranial EEG (iEEG) observes the neural activity with far higher fidelity, yet it is invasive and therefore scarce, and the two modalities are rarely recorded as pairs at scale, share no common channel space, and offer no clean ground truth to directly supervise a mapping. Motivated by the neuronal synchronization mechanism shared by the two modalities, we propose a reprogramming approach that fits a signal-to-signal model on iEEG alone and freezes it as a cross-modal prior. We introduce ReBrain, a framework to transfer an iEEG-learned prior to scalp EEG, which re-expresses any scalp signal through that prior and can be inserted upstream of any downstream model with no pairing or fine-tuning. ReBrain consistently improves host models across recordings from multiple hospitals and across architecture families, and our analysis traces this gain to genuine neural structure carried by iEEG rather than to the architecture itself. This work provides evidence that a scarce clean modality can serve as a transferable prior for an abundant noisy one, offering a general recipe for cross-modal transfer among biosignals. Our code is available at https://github.com/Xianglong-Bao/ReBrain.

Deep Sequence Model for Genome Wide Discovery of Coding and Regulatory Element Signatures

Rayhaneh Shabani Nia, Ali Karkehabadi

Interpreting deep neural networks for genomic sequence classification remains challenging despite strong predictive performance. We develop a lightweight CNN with post-training gradient-based analysis to identify which sequence positions drive coding versus intergenomic classification. Applied to the standardized demo_coding_vs_intergenomic_seqs dataset, our model achieves 91.87% validation accuracy while revealing interpretable patterns: nine hot spots with consistently high importance, clear class-specific separation at positions 20-100 (coding) and 150-190 (intergenomic), and a strong mean-variance correlation (r = 0.530) indicating robust discriminative features. Gradient-based importance analysis shows that the model implicitly learns biologically meaningful sequence distinctions without explicit annotations. This work demonstrates that neural network interpretability and accuracy can coexist, providing a framework for understanding genomic sequence classification and enabling biology-driven hypothesis generation.

From the possible to the optimal: differentiable optimization of gene regulatory networks

Leah Friedman, Gašper Tkačik, Thomas Gregor, Paul François

Understanding the space of ``the actual and the possible'' regulatory architectures capable of realizing biological functions is one of the major challenges in biology. Optimization-based approaches can rationalize regulatory mechanisms from functional objectives and constraints, but their computational cost has limited them to finding individual solutions. Here, we introduce \texttt{jaxgrn}, a framework that combines differentiable programming with stochastic simulations of gene regulatory networks (GRNs) to optimize thousands of networks end-to-end on GPUs. Using morphogen-driven embryonic patterning as a case study, we optimize networks for domain formation and positional information, while varying constraints on input noise, boundary precision and scaling with system size. While recovering known archetypes for scaling, we find that varying the constraints organizes optimal solutions into qualitatively distinct regimes. When optimizing domain formation, we observe phenotypic convergence across a continuum of regulatory architectures, ranging from input-driven to feedback-driven networks. When optimizing positional information, solutions are initially phenotypically diverse despite moderate variation in regulatory architecture; imposing scaling drives both phenotypic and regulatory convergence of the optimized networks onto a few recurring regulatory architectures. Our systematic approach opens the door for variational systems biology in which changing functional constraints reshape the landscape of possible phenotypes and regulatory architectures.

An Exploration of Physical and Chemical Representation in Coarse-Grained, Machine-Learned Protein Models

Aldo S. Pasos-Trejo, Luca Sagresti, Cecilia Clementi

Machine-learned coarse-graining has emerged as a reliable framework for building protein models that accurately and efficiently sample their relevant conformational landscape. Existing studies assume a predefined map from full- to coarse-grained resolution. However, the choice of map strongly affects the quality of the models. Here, we study the dependence of system-specific machine-learned coarse-grained (MLCG) models across 7 mapping choices, spanning different resolutions and embedding types for coarse-grained beads. We show that resolution and chemical diversity do not always correlate with accuracy, and that accurate protein-specific models can be generated using minimal chemical diversity if the latent space representation of the system has high enough dimensionality.

Indication of demographic and epidemiological data on early-stage risks and long-term epidemic burden

Sicong Zhang, Mingyuan Wu, Jian Yang, Jianpeng Xiao, Fan Bai

This study investigates the relationship between age-specific cumulative infection burden and the probability of triggering a major outbreak in heterogeneous populations. We develop two modeling frameworks that incorporate empirical age-stratified contact matrices: a deterministic framework for deriving the final size relation and a stochastic framework based on a Continuous-time Markov chain epidemic model, for which a multi-type branching-process approximation is used to characterize early-stage outbreak risk. The framework is illustrated using empirical social contact data from Guangzhou, Foshan, and Zhuhai, China, under two disease-motivated scenarios representing COVID-19 and hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD). The illustrative analyses indicate that the relationship between cumulative infection burden and early-stage outbreak risk depends on both epidemiological characteristics and population contact patterns. In particular, age groups with the highest final attack rates do not necessarily have the highest probability of triggering a major outbreak. These findings highlight the complementary nature of the two measures and the value of considering both when assessing age-specific epidemic risks in practice.
