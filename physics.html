KEBBABS: Kilometric Enhanced Balloon-Baseline for Axion Birefringence Search

Tommaso Feri, Silvia Gasparotto

We propose a broadband search for ultralight axion dark matter through the rotation of laser polarization induced by the axion--photon coupling. Two stratospheric balloons flying at altitudes of 30--40 km form a free-space optical link. One balloon sends a linearly polarized beam to the other, which reflects it back to a polarimeter on the first balloon. The long physical baseline provides an alternative to the repeated reflections used in resonant optical cavities. We target axion masses in the range $4\times10^{-15}\,eV\lesssim m_a\lesssim10^{-11}\,eV$. The lower edge corresponds to a frequency of about $1\,Hz$, below which gondola roll about the line of sight can mimic a polarization signal. At $m_a=10^{-14}\,eV$, a low-cost Pathfinder using flight-demonstrated technology could probe couplings as small as $g_{aγ}\simeq2.8\times10^{-11}\,GeV^{-1}$, about a factor of two below the CAST bound. At the same mass, the Feasible today configuration could probe couplings just below the astrophysical bounds considered here, while the Futuristic configuration could reach $g_{aγ}\simeq6.4\times10^{-15}\,GeV^{-1}$, roughly two orders of magnitude below those bounds.

An algebraic quantization of gravity from the null cone

Venkatesa Chandrasekaran

We propose a background independent quantization of vacuum general relativity on four dimensional Lorentzian spacetimes. Starting from first order gravity, we solve the gravitational constraints on intersecting null cones of a bulk subregion and obtain the reduced phase space at finite $G_N$. We promote its canonical Poisson brackets to commutation relations and use deformations of the reduced action to define an algebra of observables. Equipped with this, we use Gaussian wavefunctions of canonical pairs to construct a nonperturbative Hilbert space representation, for which this algebra generates a type I factor. By composing amplitudes between states prepared on null cones, we then obtain a Lorentzian path integral in which each sum over intermediate states converges absolutely. As a check, we compute the amplitude for graviton pair creation and annihilation on a null cone nonperturbatively in $G_N$ and find a finite closed form expression, even though expanding first in $G_N$ yields a logarithmic divergence. We next use diffeomorphism invariance of the path integral to show that a macroscopic causal diamond with a given maximum size has a finite dimensional Hilbert space. From the Lorentzian trace we obtain the Bekenstein Hawking entropy for marginally trapped surfaces and calculate its logarithmic correction for large Schwarzschild black holes, recovering at one loop the coefficient Sen obtained through Euclidean methods. We end by deriving the semiclassical Einstein equations to one loop as the low energy limit of this theory.

Vacuum correlation measures as CFT partition functions

Hewei Frederic Jia

We show that in any $d$-dimensional CFT, the vacuum sandwiched Rényi mutual information (SRMI) of integer order $n$ between two disjoint balls maps to a CFT partition function on a manifold of topology $\#^{n-1}(S^1 \times S^{d-1})$, with moduli fixed by the ball configuration and single-ball modular flow. At $n=2$, it is given by the thermal partition function on $S^{d-1}$, upper-bounding the von Neumann mutual information. For $n>2$, SRMI diverges at finite separation due to a hot-spot limit of the partition function. Explicit CFT evaluations agree well with density matrix computations.

Bound Entanglement with Negative Partial Transpose Exists

Islam Faisal

We show that the Werner state $ρ= (23 I - 3\,SWAP)/1448$ on $\mathbb{C}^8 \otimes \mathbb{C}^8$ has a negative partial transpose and is bound entangled: no LOCC protocol can distill entanglement from any number of copies of $ρ$, and even with separable operations the fidelity of a two-qubit output with a Bell state never exceeds $(1+\sqrt{2})/4 \approx 0.604$. It follows that distillable entanglement is neither additive nor convex.

Complementary mechanisms of critical quantum metrology

Nikita Leppenen, Shaked Levy, Ephraim Shahmoon

Critical metrology conventionally exploits enhanced susceptibility to a system parameter near a phase transition. Here we identify a complementary mechanism for critically enhanced estimation of intrinsic system parameters via suppressed quantum noise rather than enhanced response. We demonstrate both mechanisms in coherently radiating spin states (CRSS)-correlated states of $N$ spin-$1/2$ particles defined as asymptotic eigenstates of the collective-spin lowering operator with complex eigenvalue $r e^{-i\varphi}$. At the critical value $r=1$, CRSS exhibit a second-order transition. We show that near criticality, estimation of $r$ benefits from enhanced susceptibility despite increased noise, whereas estimation of $\varphi$ benefits from suppressed quantum noise without enhanced response. Remarkably, both yield the same estimation error $\sim N^{-2/3}$, improving over the standard quantum limit $N^{-1/2}$. For $\varphi$, this establishes quantum-enhanced metrology of phases intrinsic to the critical system, rather than phases externally encoded by interferometry. We demonstrate these results for CRSS realizations in both dissipative (superradiant) and Hamiltonian (Ising) systems relevant to cavity QED and trapped ions, where fast preparation of CRSS is shown possible thanks to gap opening. Applications to metrology of coupling rates and phases or optical lengths are discussed.

The thermodynamic budget of objectivity

Wonyong Chung

An event becomes a fact when enough independent records of it exist such that no operation is possible that leaves all observers ignorant of it again. Records are physical, and creating, keeping, and destroying them have thermodynamic costs. We compute these costs exactly in a minimal broadcast model with a driven central qubit writing records into $N$ initially thermal registers, with work sources and baths at temperature $T$. The basic arguments for the operations are outlined here, with full treatment in the text. Creation of one event record at redundancy $R$ and information deficit $δ$ requires work $W \ge R\, k_BT \ln 2\, (1-δ) H(\varepsilon)$, linear in redundancy at the Landauer coefficient. Recording in a basis misaligned by $θ$ with the einselecting interaction fails at a critical angle, $θ_c = 2\arcsin\sqrt{H_2^{-1}(δ)}$, beyond which objectivity is unreachable at any work, and below $θ_c$ the optimal work is a closed form in the pointer basis that minimizes the work of objectivity. Reducing objectivity is impossible at any work with access to fewer than $R - R_{\min} + 1$ records, while work protects nothing if given access. Inter-record redundancy is extractable free energy and erasing the accessed records yields a net gain of work beyond the first copy. The second law constrains the destruction of the correlation: erasing a fact from a sector an adversary controls dissipates at least the mutual information erased, but a consolidation dissipates nothing. Records in re-thermalizing media are nonequilibrium steady states, and the minimal power to maintain a record at $(R,δ)$ is the known two-state driving bound evaluated at the record-quality boundary.

Schrödinger's Legacy: The Wave Function and Quantum Reality

Davide Romano, Massimiliano Badino

The article discusses how Schrödinger arrived at the formulation of wave mechanics and his legacy on the philosophy of quantum mechanics, focusing in particular on the metaphysics of the wave function. Schrödinger's interpretation of the wave function as electron charge density is generally dismissed or presented only as a curious historical example in the literature. On a closer look, however, while this interpretation was eventually abandoned in favor of Born's statistical interpretation, Schrödinger's idea survived in modern realist approaches to the wave function, such as the mass-density approach and the multi-field interpretation. It also proved to be of great utility in modern techniques and approximations in quantum chemistry to compute the shape of atoms and molecules. Overall, Schrödinger's interpretation of the wave function and his realist understanding of quantum mechanics paved the way to modern attempts to define a clear ontology of the microscopic world, a tradition that survived the Copenhagen interpretation of Bohr and Heisenberg and that is still present today.

Massive Triangle in Cosmological Correlator beyond Conformal Coupling

Taushif Ahmed, Syed Mehedi Hasan

A massive scalar coupled in pairs to the inflaton generates a one-loop primordial bispectrum whose mass corrections at generic momenta have lacked closed analytic expressions beyond conformal coupling. We obtain two leading mass corrections at arbitrary external momenta in terms of multiple polylogarithms of uniform weights three and four. Auxiliary-energy dressings preserve rational time integrals, revealing unexpected simplicity despite elliptic intermediate geometry. Independent full-loop evaluations, including blinded predictions, validate the closed forms. We also determine a subleading light-field singularity and recover the heavy-mass effective theory.

Neural Network Decoders For Qubit Loss

J. Wilson Staples, Winston Fu, Yihe Dong, Jeff D. Thompson

Qubit loss is a significant source of error in many quantum hardware platforms, requiring circuits that periodically replace or reset qubits to maintain fault-tolerance. When the reset process includes loss-resolving measurements, this information can improve decoding and substantially reduce overhead compared to the case where this information is not available. Prior work has demonstrated loss-tolerant circuits for the surface code that show significantly improved thresholds and subthreshold scaling by incorporating loss information. However, the computational complexity of loss simulation and decoding, which falls outside of the usual stabilizer framework, has limited extending these studies to high-rate qLDPC codes, which are already challenging to decode in the absence of loss. In this work, we present a loss-aware convolutional neural-network (CNN) decoder framework that circumvents this challenge. Training is enabled by a novel sampler, \texttt{slim}, which achieves a throughput for circuits with Pauli noise and loss that is comparable to the state-of-the-art for pure Pauli noise. On the surface code with loss, the decoder matches the accuracy of computationally expensive most-likely error (MLE) decoders at much lower latency. For the $[[144,12,12]]$ bivariate bicycle (Gross) code, we study a walking syndrome extraction circuit with Pauli and loss errors. For physical error rates $p<1\%$, we observe a more than $10^3$-fold lower logical error rate for a pure loss model, relative to a pure Pauli error model at the same physical error rate, along with significantly enhanced sub-threshold error rate scaling. This demonstrates that loss-dominated error models can also significantly improve performance in a high-rate qLDPC code, and provides a simulation and decoder training platform that can be extended to other codes and algorithmic gadgets.

The Price of Quantum Advantage in Computational Chemistry

Haejung Koh, Katerina Gratsea, Hao Zhang, Matthew Otten

As the fault-tolerant era approaches, the question shifts from ``is it possible'' to ``is it worthwhile.'' To assess practical quantum advantage against reliable classical baselines, we examine 26 benchmark instances including FeMoco, iron-sulfur clusters, cytochrome P450, and ruthenium catalysts using two selected configuration interaction solvers, SHCI and TrimCI. Comparing classical workloads with fault-tolerant resource estimates, we calculate the break-even whole-QPU hourly price. Even at an optimistic price of US\$7{,}000 per whole-QPU hour, and with mid-range CPU clusters, classical computation remains cheaper for six of seven systems. FeMoco alone remains sensitive to the classical method and extrapolation strategy. Wavefunction analysis further reveals how orbital choice affects the determinant support required for target-state overlap in quantum phase estimation (QPE). Our results highlight the importance of reliable classical references and trial-state characterization, and quantify the economic conditions under which quantum computing can become competitive with classical alternatives.
