Learning Force-Reactive Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Sihang Li, Xiaofeng Guo, Jinkun Cao, Charlie Cheng, Ruihan Yang et al. (8 authors)

Many everyday tasks require controlling contact force as well as motion. On a humanoid, this force must often be coordinated across both hands and the whole body, and cannot be specified by kinematic trajectories alone. Therefore, we present FLORE, a framework for learning force-reactive humanoid loco-manipulation. We collect robot-free demonstrations using two force-sensing UMI-style handheld grippers for hand motion and contact wrenches, and wearable trackers for body motion. A force-reactive diffusion policy takes vision, proprioception, and measured wrenches as input, then predicts actions and per-hand force commands. A hybrid compliance whole-body controller with force tracking executes these commands while maintaining balance: an integral of the force error measured by real force/torque (F/T) sensors lets each hand track force accurately along the commanded direction and remain compliant along the others. In simulation, the controller tracks per-hand force commands of varied magnitude and direction, with little loss in whole-body motion accuracy. Across three contact-rich tasks on the robot, the force-reactive policy succeeds in 80% of rollouts, compared with 26.7% for a position-only policy trained on the same demonstrations and executed by the same controller. For more details, please visit our website: https://louis-leee.github.io/FLoRe/.

Differentiable Contact Dynamics for Legged Locomotion: Policy Learning and Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization

Gijeong Kim, Seungbin Oh, Dongyun Kang, Hae-Won Park

This paper proposes a smooth analytic gradient for frictional contact and applies it to policy learning and contact-implicit trajectory optimization for legged locomotion. The exact contact sensitivity is available in closed form but describes changes within the current contact mode, providing no first-order information toward neighboring contact modes. The proposed gradient replaces this sensitivity only in the backward pass, leaving the hard-contact forward solve unchanged and the realized rollout feasible. A central-path relaxation of the de Saxcé-corrected second-order-cone complementarity problem for Coulomb friction smooths the conditions defining the separating, clamping, and sliding modes. The relaxed sensitivity is evaluated at a point on the central path obtained by a Newton iteration initialized from the hard-contact solution. The sensitivity remains continuous through contact-mode transitions and converges to the exact sensitivity as the relaxation vanishes. Using 100,000 samples and less than 2 min of training on a desktop PC, policy learning with the proposed gradient produces a policy that transfers to hardware without fine-tuning. On hardware, the robot under a trotting policy withstands pushes and traverses debris and slippery ground. In trajectory optimization, the proposed gradient reaches contact-rich solutions missed by exact and Signorini-relaxed gradients. Project page: https://diffcontact.github.io

Nonprehensile Robotic Catching Using Virtual Constraints

Aakash Khandelwal, Ranjan Mukherjee

The problem of nonprehensile robotic catching is addressed using virtual nonholonomic constraints (VNHCs); the motion of the robot end-effector is designed and controlled directly in terms of the states of the object before it is caught on the surface of the end-effector. In contrast to contemporary approaches, our approach avoids the need for object trajectory prediction, and consequent time-varying robot trajectory generation. With a focus on the planar case, we demonstrate that our VNHC approach can be used to catch an object on an end-effector at a desired height, with minimal relative normal velocity at the contact instant. Moreover, the VNHC function depends only on the desired catching height, and the same VNHC can be used for a range of initial conditions. We use model predictive control (MPC) to bring the object and end-effector to rest at a desired location after contact is made. The approach is validated in simulation.

OSWAM: An Effective World Action Model with One-Step Action Generation

Guowen Zhang, Liyi Chen, Xu Hu, Pengfei Wang, Jingfan Chen et al. (7 authors)

Direct-policy World Action Models (WAM) have demonstrated the benefits of decoupling action generation from explicit future visual prediction for efficient inference in autonomous driving and embodied control. However, they still require tens of denoising steps to achieve competitive action quality, incurring substantial latency and hindering real-time deployment. Simply reducing the number of denoising steps will collapse the nonlinear multi-step denoising trajectory into coarse updates, substantially degrading action quality. To reduce the denoising steps while enabling real-time action generation, we present an effective world action model with one-step action generation, namely OSWAM, for autonomous driving and embodied control. We argue that it is the absence of structured action priors in random noise initialization that forces direct-policy WAMs to recover coherent action sequences through iterative denoising. Hence, we construct imagination tokens to initialize the diffusion process, providing physical priors and action-conditioned representations. We further introduce privileged information distillation with future conditioning to enhance future-aware action generation. As a result, OSWAM can efficiently and effectively generate high quality actions in just one diffusion step. Our experiments demonstrate that OSWAM achieves comparable or even better results in both autonomous driving and embodied control than direct-policy WAM and Joint-WAM that require tens of steps. It obtains approximately $3\times$ speedup over direct-policy WAM baselines and supports real-time compiled inference with latency as low as 40ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. The project page is available at https://gwenzhang.github.io/OSWAM/

Profiling a Frontier Agent with EBench: From Direct Control to Harness with Policy

Yifei Yao, Yude Zou, Guanbo Jiao, Xing Gao, Ning Gao et al. (15 authors)

Frontier multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can control robots without robot-specific training, but how their capabilities compare and interact with post-trained vision-language-action (VLA) and world action model (WAM) policies across manipulation demands remains to be thoroughly evaluated. In this report, we evaluate GPT-6-Astra on EBench as the core of an agent that controls a robot through an agent harness under the same visual and proprioceptive observation protocol as post-trained policies. We study two agent configurations: direct control, in which the agent generates waypoint commands directly, and harness with policy, in which it can also invoke a post-trained VLA or WAM policy for action execution. Under direct control, the agent achieves competitive overall performance relative to post-trained policies. It leads on short-horizon tasks and exhibits the least performance variation across generalization dimensions, whereas it underperforms post-trained policies on long-horizon tasks, tabletop manipulation, and high-precision contact-rich tasks. In the harness with policy, invoking a post-trained VLA or WAM combines the complementary strengths of both components, raising overall task success to the level of the strongest compared post-trained policies while substantially reducing token cost. However, performance remains below the per-task upper bound of the standalone components due to suboptimal policy invocation, fixed-instruction execution without subgoals, and grasp disruption when the agent resumes control. Overall, our study characterizes the capability boundary of a frontier MLLM agent and suggests directions for improving future agent harness design. Project page: https://internrobotics.github.io/EBench-Astra-Tech-Report/

Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization in a Sim-Trained Robot Agent

Benjamin Pikus, Daniel Saunders, Mircea Mironenco, Salman Mohammadi, Mathew Mathew et al. (14 authors)

A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without any real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. We demonstrate that ZAP also generalizes to completely new objects, goals, strategies, and language by seating a test tube in a rack (and choosing a different hole in the rack when prompted), handing a test tube between arms mid-air, drawing an X on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess. These goals and types of manipulation don't appear in our training data. Furthermore, ZAP also transfers to an unseen embodiment. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

Security Region Compression via Hybrid Vertex Pruning for Enhanced Computational Efficiency

Sicheng Gong, Zeyu Liu, Danyang Xu, Hongjie Jia

The security region (SR) specifies a joint feasible region in the operational space involving various installations within the power system. It enables dynamic allocation of capacity among these energy units, thereby unlocking additional grid potential for power delivery and mitigating congestion. By constructing a convex hull from a set of vertices to form a polytope, the SR can be effectively represented with an acceptable accuracy loss. However, the SR computation inevitably generates many low-quality vertices, which contribute little to the overall hypervolume and can be discarded. By introducing SR compression, the SR data size can be reduced, which in turn facilitates the memory-efficient computation of SR and time-efficient solution of SR-related optimization problems. Accordingly, this paper proposes a vertex pruning approach that mixes local and backward search policies to achieve effective SR compression. In particular, the backward search strategy allows previously pruned vertices to be recycled when they are re-identified as critical. Subsequently, we incorporate this approach to enhance computational efficiency in the aforementioned applications. Numerical validations are conducted on two realistic medium-voltage Dutch grid cases. The results demonstrate at most 1.65% hypervolume loss with over 96.0\% SR data size reduction after applying the proposed compression approach. Empowered by this compression, the 8-dimensional (8D) SR becomes computationally tractable. Moreover, in relevant grid-aware computation challenges, the solving time can be reduced by at least 52.9% with at most an 1.36% optimality loss. All above validate both the efficacy and values of the proposed SR compression approach.

World Model for Energy Efficiency Maximization in Dynamic Metasurface Antennas

Abdolrasoul Sakhaei Gharagezlou, Mehdi Monemi, Matti Latva-aho, Mehdi Rasti

Dynamic metasurface antennas (DMAs) have emerged as a promising technology for realizing large-scale antenna arrays in next-generation wireless networks owing to their ability to enable dense and energy-efficient antenna architectures. However, jointly optimizing digital beamforming and DMA element weights under time-varying channel conditions remains challenging due to their coupled effects on the received signal and interference powers. We formulate this problem to maximize time-averaged energy efficiency (EE) under time-varying channel conditions, subject to transmit power constraints, Lorentzian phase limitations, and minimum rate requirements. To enable long-term planning and improve sample efficiency compared with model-free reinforcement learning, we develop a world model framework that learns latent system dynamics from interactions with the environment and jointly optimizes digital beamforming and DMA element weights through imagined trajectories. Furthermore, we incorporate feasibility-preserving action mappings and safety cost signals to ensure constraint satisfaction during policy learning. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework improves the time-averaged EE by approximately 20% compared with the deep Q-network baseline, while achieving higher sample efficiency and more reliable constraint satisfaction.

Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning Using Routed Ensembles and Trajectory Attribution

Zhenshuo Zhang, Ruoxuan Xiong, Hongyang R. Zhang

Multi-objective reinforcement learning is a fundamental problem for modern AI, with applications spanning post-training, reasoning models, and robotic control. A central challenge is to learn policies that balance competing objectives, such as helpfulness versus honesty in safety and alignment, or speed versus energy in robotics. The optimal policy can vary sharply with objective-weighting combinations, and the extent of this variation depends crucially on the degree of conflict among objectives, making per-preference policy learning sample-inefficient. In this paper, we introduce objective-conflict coefficients and derive near-optimal regret bounds for online settings using this measure, thereby quantifying the impact of misalignment on learning efficiency. Then, we design routed ensemble policy learning, an algorithm that maintains a small set of basis policies and dynamically routes trajectories to the policy with the highest potential reward. Finally, we consider trajectory selection as another approach to mitigate objective conflict, and design a kernel surrogate trajectory attribution algorithm that estimates how feedback signals from a set of trajectories influence policy learning on other trajectories. The kernel can capture nonlinear interactions such as synergy and antagonism between trajectories. We evaluate our approach across diverse environments and benchmarks: routed ensembles improve average performance by 4.3% over baselines for online environments from Meta-World and MO-Gymnasium, and kernel surrogate attribution improves correlation with leave-one-out analysis over existing baselines by 50%. When applied to trajectory selection, kernel-based attribution yields a 27% improvement in reward over baseline attribution methods.

SPECOLA: A Tool for Specification Driven Robot Evaluation

Immanuel Ampomah Mensah, Hadas Kress-Gazit

Simulation benchmarks are a useful way to measure progress in robotics; typically they provide success rate as the dominant measure of performance. However, success rate reports only whether a rollout completed the task, hiding by what margin it succeeded, how sensitive the success is to goal parameter changes, requirements it may have violated, and whether success persisted. Signal Temporal Logic (STL) provides a formal language for capturing specifications; it can be used to define requirements, and measure how close a robot behavior is to satisfying or violating them. Using STL monitors in a simulator, however, requires exposing simulator state and mapping it to specification-relevant signals. We present Specola, a tool for evaluating robot behaviors in simulation with respect to STL specifications. Specola provides an interface for writing specifications over simulator signals, and creates and manages the relevant monitors while a policy executes. We provide a Python API and command-line interface for integrating monitoring into existing policy execution loops. We provide an adapter interface for connecting new simulators, and provide adapters for nine simulation environments and benchmarks, including LIBERO, RoboCasa, Isaac Lab, and RoboDojo. We provide case studies that illustrate the nuanced evaluation that Specola enables. Code and adapters are available at https://github.com/specola-eval/specola.
