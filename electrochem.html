Pressure and Magnetic Field Control of Topology in Antiferromagnetic Bilayers

Narsimha Rao Elaprolu, Marco Gibertini

Controlling band topology in magnetic semiconductors requires strategies that couple electronic structure to experimentally accessible degrees of freedom. Here we propose a cooperative approach based on pressure and magnetic reorientation in A-type antiferromagnetic bilayers. Antiparallel layer magnetizations suppress hybridization between exchange-polarized band-edge states, limiting their response to interlayer compression. A magnetic field that aligns the layer moments removes this spin mismatch, enabling pressure-enhanced hybridization to drive a band inversion. The magnetization direction then controls the symmetries of the inverted bands and whether spin--orbit coupling produces a Chern-insulating gap or symmetry-protected nodal crossings. First-principles calculations for bilayer CrSBr illustrate this mechanism within a compressed orthorhombic structural model. Out-of-plane magnetization yields a Chern insulator with a chiral edge state, whereas in-plane magnetization supports a two-dimensional Weyl semimetal with node-connecting edge states. Continuous spin canting further demonstrates how magnetic reorientation can drive the transition and stabilize the Chern phase before full magnetic saturation. Although material-specific transition conditions depend on electronic correlations and structural stability under compression, the underlying strategy identifies interlayer hybridization as a link between magnetic order and band topology. These results provide a design principle for externally tunable topological phases in layered antiferromagnetic semiconductors.

Solution of the Qubit Marginal Problem from Fermionic Representability

David A. Mazziotti

Whether local reduced states are compatible with a global quantum state---the quantum marginal problem---is central to quantum information and simulation. Here we present a solution to the ensemble marginal problem for qubits based on the representability theory of fermions. Encoding each qubit as one fermion in two orbitals, we prove that the representable qubit marginals form an exposed face of the set of $N$-representable fermionic reduced density matrices, selected by the vanishing of every site-occupation variance. Complete hierarchies of fermionic representability conditions therefore transfer exactly to qubits. Applications to frustrated Heisenberg and transverse-field Ising rings show that the $(2,3)$-positivity conditions are exact at the Majumdar--Ghosh point and accurate to about 0.5% at criticality. Because the conditions hold for any quantum state, they also provide a framework for mitigating measurement errors on qubit-based quantum devices.

Blind in-memory arithmetic with confined skyrmions: doubling and successor functions on a skyrmion byte

Joan Reverté, Nuria Del-Valle, Jaume Cunill-Subiranas, Natanael Bort-Soldevila, Carles Navau

We present a skyrmionic-based computation platform, able to perform fully in-memory computing by collectively manipulating the skyrmions that serve as bits of the binary memory. The computation protocols are implemented through driving currents and adequately located anisotropy defects that modify the dynamics of the skyrmions along a magnetic track. Simulations demonstrating the feasibility of such an in-memory computing system are shown for several basic and simple mathematical operations with natural numbers, such as doubling and adding one (the successor function). We also discuss how further optimization of the geometry, barrier profiles, current amplitudes, and timing parameters could improve both the robustness and the overall practical feasibility of the system.

Fine-tuned atomistic foundation model for accurate sodium-carbon chemistry in Na-ion batteries

Andres Felipe Usuga, Ihor Radchenko, Yuxin Tang, Oleksandr I. Malyi

Atomistic foundation models have emerged as a promising route to DFT-level accuracy for materials simulation with complex chemical environments. However, their reliability across competing bonding regimes and, more importantly, how complex bonding affects predictions of material properties remain unclear. Among materials with competing bonding regimes, sodium storage in hard carbon poses a substantial challenge. Hard carbon features diverse local structural motifs, including defective and curved graphitic regions, amorphous regions, and closed/open pores. Motivated by its importance for Na-ion batteries, we systematically evaluate existing pretrained foundation models based on the MACE architecture across different local Na/C motifs. We find that the pretrained models fail to accurately capture Na-Na and Na-C interactions for metallic Na clusters on graphene. To recover a DFT-level energetic description, we employ targeted fine-tuning, showing that it can describe the energetic stability of local motifs and achieve sufficient transferability to unseen Na/C environments. With the developed fine-tuned model, we establish a correlation between closed-pore radius in hard carbon and plateau voltage, showing that the small pores contribute at larger voltages than large ones, suggesting thus how the developed fine-tuned foundation model can link atomistic information with materials design for complex bonding environments.

A charge-dependent electron emission yield from electron irradiated dielectrics

M. Belhaj, A. M. Cavalca de Almeida, N. Fil

The total electron emission yield (TEEY) of dielectric surfaces may vary during electron irradiation owing to charge accumulation within the material. In the present work, the generation, transport, and escape formulation of Dionne is extended by introducing an additional attenuation of secondary electrons that depends on trapped positive charge. The resulting emission law is coupled to a charge-balance equation, allowing the TEEY to be calculated as a function of incident energy and injected charge. The model is examined using measurements on polycrystalline Al2O3, from which effective cross sections describing interactions between trapped charge and secondary electrons are determined. Their mean value is then used to predict the evolution of the TEEY curves without further adjustment. Finally, an analytical approximation of the nonlinear charging law is derived to facilitate the implementation in electron-irradiation simulations.

Machine learning of strength surfaces enables high regularization length phase-field fracture

S Chockalingam, Aditya Kumar, Adrian Buganza Tepole

Classical phase-field models of brittle fracture capture toughness-controlled crack growth but not the material's strength surface, which governs crack nucleation. The theory of Kumar et al. (2020) incorporates the strength surface through a nucleation driving force, and Chockalingam (2025) derived this driving force for arbitrary strength surfaces described by $n$ material parameters, with which the phase-field strength surface (PFSS) converges to the material strength surface as the regularization length $\varepsilon \to 0$ and matches it exactly at $n$ chosen states for any $\varepsilon$. In practice, however, the strength surface must be supplied as an analytic form chosen and calibrated case by case, and for the widely used analytic surfaces ($n=2$) accurate strength reproduction requires $\varepsilon$ commensurate with the Irwin characteristic length $l_{ch}$, imposing prohibitive mesh requirements for large structures. We introduce a neural-network (NN) strength function that is learned directly from strength data, is physics-constrained by construction, and provides far more calibration parameters than analytic surfaces; the calibration is generalized to any number of strength states and to enforce openness in chosen directions. A single architecture captures synthetic data from the Mohr-Coulomb, Drucker-Prager, Mogi-Coulomb and 3D Hoek-Brown surfaces and experimental data for graphite, natural rubber and styrene-butadiene rubber. The resulting PFSS is accurate up to $\varepsilon = 10\,l_{ch}$, whereas that of the exact analytic surfaces performs comparatively poorly even at $\varepsilon = 0.5\,l_{ch}$. Finite-element simulations validate the theory across fracture regimes. By decoupling strength fidelity from the regularization length, the approach substantially reduces the mesh resolution required for phase-field fracture simulations of large structures.

High Entropy Oxides as Room Temperature Anderson Insulators with Colossal Soft Gaps

Elena Salagre, Heather Hare, Eli Rotenberg, Jonathan D. Denlinger, Joshua D. Sugar et al. (7 authors)

The Anderson effect, arising from destructive quantum interference of electron wave packets in disordered potentials, is usually observed at cryogenic temperatures and typically arises from extrinsic defects in conventional materials such as phosphorus dopants in silicon or oxygen vacancies in transition metal oxides. Here we demonstrate that a multication lattice of transition metals in a high-entropy oxide (HEO) natively establishes an interacting Anderson insulator that extends up to $500\,K$. By controlling the population of transition metal cations in $La_{0.7}Sr_{0.3}Mn_{1-x}(Cr,Fe,Co,Ni)_{x/4}O_3$ (LS5B), we tune the system from the prototypical correlated metal $La_{0.7}Sr_{0.3}MnO_3$ at $x=0.00$ to a localized insulator at $x=0.80$, where the disorder length contracts to near the unit cell (3.85 $\mathring{A}$) and drives the electron mean free path to the Ioffe-Regel limit. Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) reveals the systematic localization of itinerant $d$-quasiparticle states at the Fermi level, consistent with the emergence of a soft Coulomb gap, while the underlying band structure and symmetry are preserved. Transport reveals Efros-Shklovskii variable-range hopping governed by a colossal soft gap with characteristic temperature $T_{ES}>50{,}000\,K$ and screened Coulomb interaction strengths as high as 1.6 eV. Together, multiple lines of evidence indicate that interacting Anderson behavior is a native characteristic that survives to temperatures as high as $500\,K$ in high-entropy materials.

Validating Graph Neural Network Feature Attribution Through Local Perturbations: A Study on CoMoFeNiCu Decomposition Enthalpy

Miguel Tenorio, James Chapman

Graph neural networks (GNNs) show promise for predicting materials properties, but interpretability remains a challenge, particularly understanding whether attributed feature importance reflects physical reality or statistical artifacts. Here, we present a methods validation study that deliberately overfits a physics-aware GNN ensemble to probe the robustness of feature-ranking approaches. Using the CoMoFeNiCu high-entropy alloy system with three stoichiometries, we train GNNs to predict decomposition enthalpies from DFT-calculated structures while explicitly constraining output weights to encode feature importance. By design, our dataset is limited (approximately 900 binary and 4,500 ternary decomposition pathways) to control compositional complexity and enable precise interrogation of local chemistry effects. We validate that feature rankings capture meaningful local-environment information through a series of atomic swap perturbations: perturbing individual atomic sites and re-ranking features reveals spatial decay consistent with the GNN's interaction cutoff, confirming that weights encode neighbor-shell effects rather than global stoichiometric artifacts alone. Results demonstrate that feature attribution methods can remain physically interpretable even under overfitting, provided validation is rigorous. This work establishes a foundation for trustworthy interpretability in materials GNNs and sets the stage for scaling to multiple chemistries and testing generalization across compositional spaces.

Tunable Hybridization of Whispering Gallery Modes in High-Index Photonic Molecules

Ayusmin Panda, Birabar Ranjit Kumar Nanda

Photonic molecules formed by coupled dielectric microresonators provide a platform for controlling collective optical modes, yet the interplay between molecular topology, resonator detuning, and dielectric environment in determining their spectral response remains insufficiently quantified. Here, we systematically quantify the evolution of Whispering Gallery Mode (WGM) hybridization in high-index TiO$_2$ and perovskite coated TiO$_2$ microresonators with molecular topology, and establish the corresponding effective inter-cavity coupling using a combined numerical and analytical analysis. We further show that perovskite-shell incorporation provides a diameter and thickness-dependent modification of the effective coupling, producing a non-monotonic response rather than a uniform suppression of coupling. Despite the resulting closely spaced hybridized resonances in the high-index perovskite environment, the supermodes remain spectrally resolvable, with $Q$-factor of order \(10^3\) for representative modes. The observed spectral splitting is quantitatively related to the effective inter-cavity coupling strengths, revealing a non-monotonic coupling response to perovskite-shell incorporation that depends on resonator size and shell thickness. These results establish molecular topology and dielectric environment as distinct parameters for controlling WGM hybridization, inter-cavity coupling, and spectral response, providing a basis for the design of coupled resonator systems for applications in optical sensing, filtering, switching, and other resonant photonic devices.

Graph diffusion transformer for interpretable generation and inverse design of disordered atomic structures

Jiani Hu, Chenyan Wang, Yongkun Yang, Guanyu Hou, Jihaochen Zhang et al. (7 authors)

Atomic disorder offers a potent degree of freedom to tailor material properties, yet the resulting vast configuration space obscures structure-property relations and hinders rational inverse design. Here we introduce Graph Amorphous Diffusion Transformer (GaDiT), a generative model that combines dynamic periodic graphs, masked conditioning and an interval-weighted training-noise distribution. GaDiT generates high-quality disordered structures while accelerating sampling by two orders of magnitude compared with existing corrector-based approaches, as demonstrated across two-dimensional amorphous carbon and three-dimensional amorphous silicon and silica. Moreover, GaDiT supports conditional generation under single or combined structural features, physical properties and preparation conditions in a unified model, and enables inverse design that expands accessible structure-property ranges beyond the training distribution. To interpret these structures and the trained network, we propose a conditioned atomic response mapping analysis, revealing both the global structural descriptors and the local atomic environments that govern target properties. Overall, GaDiT establishes a general framework for interpretable generation, inverse design and structure-property discovery for disordered atomic structures.
