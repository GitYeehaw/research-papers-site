Trapping and Fracture of Particles in Three-Sphere Ball-Milling Collisions

Ziyi Cui, Qiong Zhang, Suwei Liu, Richard M. Lueptow

We numerically investigate the collision and fracture processes of ball milling based on a minimal model of a three-sphere geometry using discrete element method (DEM) simulations combined with Christensen-type and Weibull-type normalized fracture criteria. The outcome of the brittle target particle (ejected versus trapped, fractured versus intact) depends on the impact geometry and velocity. We develop a reduced three-sphere collision description in which trapping is governed by impact geometry and friction, while fracture inside the trapping regime is evaluated from DEM output using stress-based and contact-force-based normalized criteria. The trapping regime follows a closed-form geometric condition, while the critical conditions for fracture vary with impact angle, obliquity angle, impact velocity, and particle size ratio. Head-on collisions with larger size ratios require lower critical impact velocities for fracture.

Spin supersolid in triangular lattice easy-axis spin-1/2 antiferromagnet: a fermionic parton perspective

Meng Zeng, Roderich Moessner, Shu Zhang

We study the supersolid order of the spin-1/2 easy-axis XXZ antiferromagnet on the triangular lattice using a fermionic parton mean-field theory built on the $π$-flux Dirac spin liquid, with the mean-field order generated by an on-site spinon interaction term and nearest-neighbor (anisotropic) spin couplings. The collective modes are obtained using the random-phase approximation (RPA). In a finite parameter region, we find the Y state as a self-consistent solution, together with the competing Y' state, which is a $90^\circ$-rotated Y state inside the order plane. The mean-field energy favors Y', even though the two states are exactly degenerate in the classical limit. However, the Y state can become lower in energy when higher-order corrections due to the fluctuations of the collective modes are considered. The Y state can also be stabilized on the mean-field level by applying small external fields, which we do in two different ways: moment-aligned local field and uniform field along the easy axis. In both cases, the RPA calculation for the stabilized Y state gives a roton minimum at the $M$ point in the excitation spectrum. A gapped pseudo-Goldstone mode above the gapless Goldstone mode is obtained as well, which has also been observed experimentally. Furthermore, we investigate the effects of tuning the spinon interaction strength and the anisotropy in spin couplings in our phenomenological model. Comparison with the recent numerical and experimental results is also discussed.

Finite-element reconstruction of molten-zone dynamics and chemical segregation in optical floating-zone grown Ni--Mn--Ga

Denys Musiienko, Taras Kovaliuk, Petr Harcuba, Oleg Heczko, Ross H. Colman

Homogeneous single-crystal growth of Ni--Mn--Ga magnetic shape-memory alloys is limited by incongruent melting, Mn reactivity, and longitudinal composition segregation. We report a modified optical floating-zone method in which growth is performed inside a transparent sapphire crucible, stabilising the molten zone while preserving optical heating and visual monitoring. The resulting crystals show improved growth reproducibility and controlled macroscopic geometry. To interpret the measured segregation profiles, a transient finite-element heat-transfer model in COMSOL Multiphysics was coupled to an effective molten-zone mass-balance calculation. The model reveals that the local solidification velocity is not fixed by the imposed translation speed but varies strongly as the molten-zone geometry evolves. The calculated solidification-front geometry is independently supported by EBSD analysis of a quenched growth interface, while the coupled mass-balance calculation reproduces the dominant features of the measured composition profiles. These results show that deviations from ideal Scheil--Pfann behaviour arise from the evolving molten-zone geometry and solidification conditions. The experimentally constrained model therefore provides a practical means of reconstructing otherwise inaccessible growth conditions and a basis for optimisation of sapphire-confined floating-zone growth of incongruently melting alloys.

Experimental Evidence for Type-I Superconductivity in $OsB_2$

Yogesh Singh

Since the discovery of superconductivity in $MgB_2$, metal diborides have attracted significant attention. Among these, osmium diboride ($OsB_2$), crystallizing in an orthorhombic structure (space group $Pmmn$) with puckered boron layers, has been classified as a low-$κ$ Type-II superconductor ($T_c \approx 2.1 K$). Here, we report experimental evidence establishing that $OsB_2$ is in fact a rare nonelemental Type-I superconductor. Our identification is supported by three primary experimental hallmarks: (i) a pronounced Differential Paramagnetic Effect (DPE) peak in AC susceptibility ($χ_{ac}$) measurements under static DC magnetic fields, signaling the intermediate state; (ii) a field-induced crossover to a divergent first-order phase transition in specific heat ($C/T$); and (iii) pronounced magnetic hysteresis in field-ramped transitions indicative of supercooling.

Machine learning quantum data to accelerate quantum Monte Carlo

Tong Jiang, Andrew Zhao, Shashwat Kumar, Jigyasa Nigam, Eliott Rosenberg et al. (9 authors)

Simulating strongly correlated electrons in molecules and materials remains a central goal for quantum computing. A promising strategy uses quantum processors to prepare trial wavefunctions that guide classical quantum Monte Carlo (QMC). However, the enormous computational cost of repeatedly processing quantum measurements has severely limited the practical scalability of this approach. Here, we overcome this bottleneck by compressing quantum measurement data into machine-learning models that efficiently provide the wavefunction overlaps needed by QMC. This accelerates quantum-classical QMC by orders of magnitude, enabling chemically accurate simulations on up to 40 qubits, more than doubling the scale of previous hardware demonstrations with comparable accuracy. We demonstrate our approach on hydrogen chains and strongly correlated iron-sulfur clusters using measurements from Google's Willow processor. By enabling efficient reuse of quantum-state information on the classical coprocessor, our framework addresses a fundamental bottleneck in hybrid quantum algorithms that will persist even into the fault-tolerant era.

The polar discontinuity picture in nickelate superlattices: comparison of the insulating and metallic phases

Edith Simmen, Jonathan Amara, Philippe Ghosez, Nicola A. Spaldin

We present a first-principles study of the formal-charge polar discontinuity in superlattices of BaTiO$_3$ interfaced with SmNiO$_3$ or BiNiO$_3$, comparing the results for the insulating and metallic nickelate phases. Polar discontinuities arising from differently charged layers in perovskites are well-studied in heterostructures such as LaAlO$_3$/SrTiO$_3$, and recently, signatures consistent with such a polar discontinuity have been observed in systems where the formally charged layers would reside in a metal. We first identify signatures of the polar discontinuity in the structure and the density of states of superlattices with BaTiO$_3$ and insulating BiNiO$_3$, and find partial screening by modification of the BiNiO$_3$ charge disproportionation pattern analogous to our previous study on BaTiO$_3$/SmNiO$_3$. We then examine superlattices constructed from the corresponding metallic nickelate phases and find that, despite the presence of screening charges, the qualitative interfacial response is remarkably similar to that of the insulating counterparts. We show that the metallic screening charges have little influence on the apparent formal-charge polar discontinuity and discuss practical limitations of the polar-discontinuity picture when interpreting and quantifying screening at metallic nickelate/ferroelectric interfaces.

Key role of orbital exchange in CoO magnetism

Leonid V. Pourovskii, Yuriy Mokrousov

Co ions in the antiferromagnetic phase of CoO are known to possess a substantial orbital moment, making this material promising for orbitronic applications. However, intersite exchange interactions involving these orbital degrees of freedom remain poorly quantified, and their role in CoO magnetism is unclear. Here, we derive a full low-energy effective Hamiltonian for CoO, including orbital exchange terms, using an ab initio force-theorem approach based on DFT+dynamical mean-field theory. We find sizable orbital interactions, with quadrupolar orbital exchange emerging as the dominant nearest-neighbor term. The collinear antiferromagnetic order obtained from the effective Hamiltonian features magnetic moments tilted away from the pseudocubic <001> direction and an orbital contribution amounting to about one-third of the total moment, in agreement with experimental estimates. We show that the moment tilting originates from the combined effect of orbital and spin-orbital exchange interactions. The same interactions also produce a prominent splitting of the lowest excitation band. By directly modeling the inelastic neutron scattering (INS) intensity, we further show that the characteristic column-like structures previously observed in CoO INS provide a clear fingerprint of orbital exchange. We also discuss possible impact of our findings for further advances in the field of complex collective excitations, their transport properties and applications in the realm of orbitronics.

Ferroelectric control of magnetic coupling in a Cr dimer adsorbed on $α$-In$_2$Se$_3$

J. F. Ospina, S. Di Napoli, M. A. Barral

Ferroelectric substrates offer a route to controlling magnetic interactions in low-dimensional systems through polarization-dependent changes in local bonding and electronic structure. Here, using first-principles calculations, we investigate the adsorption of 3$d$ transition-metal atoms and dimers on monolayer $α$-In$_2$Se$_3$ and identify Cr as a stable dimer configuration on both polar surfaces. Reversing the ferroelectric polarization drives a pronounced change in its magnetic coupling: the AFM state is favored for one polarization, whereas the FM and AFM configurations become nearly degenerate for the opposite one. Analysis of the spin density, projected electronic states, and Wannier Hamiltonians reveals concomitant changes in the direct Cr-Cr hopping and in the interaction with the bridging Se atom. In particular, enhanced Cr-Se hybridization and Se spin polarization, together with a strong reduction of the direct two-center Cr-Cr hopping, point to a polarization-dependent balance between direct and substrate-mediated exchange contributions. These results show how ferroelectric polarization can tune magnetic interactions in an atomically defined 2D hybrid system.

Microscopic Origin and Josephson Signatures of $d$-Wave Superconductivity in Monolayer FeSe

Jiong Mei, Lili Wang, Yu-Jie Sun, Kun Jiang

Monolayer FeSe on SrTiO$_3$ exhibits high-temperature superconductivity with an electron-only Fermi surface, raising fundamental questions about its unconventional pairing mechanism. Motivated by recent Josephson scanning tunneling microscopy evidence for nodeless $d$-wave superconductivity, we investigate the microscopic role of the Fe $d_{xy}$ orbital using an experimentally constrained multiorbital theory. A tight-binding model fitted to quasiparticle interference and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy reveals substantial $d_{xy}$ character near the Fermi level and dominant nearest-neighbor hopping, in contrast to the conventional kinetic-frustration picture of iron-based superconductors. Both itinerant spin-fluctuation calculations and strong-coupling exchange approaches favor a robust $d$-wave pairing state, whose interband pairing components allow a fully gapped quasiparticle spectrum. We further establish a symmetry-based selection rule for spatial Josephson tunneling, revealing characteristic bond-centered maxima and mirror-enforced interference zeros that distinguish $d$-wave pairing from competing $s$-wave states. These symmetry-enforced nodal lines survive near Fe-site impurities, while a pronounced contrast between the $d$- and $s$-wave Josephson patterns persists around vortices despite the loss of mirror protection. Our results connect the unconventional $d_{xy}$ electronic structure to nodeless $d$-wave superconductivity and demonstrate the power of spatial Josephson interference for resolving pairing symmetry in multiorbital superconductors.

Heat Transport Mechanisms in MOF-5

Zezhu Zeng, Xia Liang, Hengtai Zheng, Aron Walsh, David E. Manolopoulos

We find that MOF-5 supports both long-lived phonons and linker vibrations whose split spectra signal a breakdown of the phonon quasiparticle approximation. Combining mode-projected molecular dynamics spectra with spectral Wigner heat transport, we resolve two contrasting transport channels: low-frequency particle-like phonon propagation decreases strongly from 200 to 500 K, whereas wave-like phonon coherence remains nearly constant and supplies about half the intrinsic conductivity at 500 K. A phenomenological extrinsic scattering model provides a plausible explanation for the discrepancy between calculated and measured conductivities in terms of both its magnitude and temperature dependence. We also identify signatures of phonon quasiparticle breakdown in the linker vibrations of ZIF-8 and HKUST-1, indicating that this feature extends to other metal-organic frameworks.
