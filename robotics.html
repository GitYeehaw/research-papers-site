Pruning Autonomous Driving VLAs: What to Preserve for Reasoning and Action

Seongchan Kim, Soowon Son, Sangoh Kim, Hyunah Ko, Eunbeen Hong et al. (12 authors)

Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) models for autonomous driving use chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to guide action generation, but incur substantial memory and computational costs. We study structured pruning for these models, focusing on preserving both scene understanding and trajectory prediction. Our analysis of Taylor importance in Alpamayo-1.5-10B shows that reasoning and trajectory objectives assign different importance to the same vision-language model (VLM) structures. In the action expert, MLP channels exhibit more stable importance rankings across denoising steps than query heads, and a small fraction of channels accounts for most of the measured importance. Based on these findings, we propose MoOP, which combines importance ranks from both objectives for VLM pruning and aggregates stepwise importance to select a fixed subset of expert MLP channels. At 24.0% total parameter reduction, VLM-only pruning with MoOP performs comparably to or slightly better than the strongest baselines in driving, while retaining 94.0% of the dense model's LingoQA score, far above all other methods. At a higher reduction of 30.0%, MoOP degrades much less than every other method, as it is the only one whose reasoning does not collapse. Removing 93.75% of expert MLP channels increases total parameter reduction to 39.4%, with nearly unchanged open-loop errors relative to VLM-only pruning and competitive closed-loop performance. The resulting model achieves a 1.37x end-to-end inference speedup and 36.6% lower peak memory than the dense model, without post-pruning fine-tuning.

ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

Zexin He, Rui Li, Yushi Lan, Chengkai Jin, Zhi Hou et al. (6 authors)

Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

Tropical Geometry-based ReLU Lyapunov Function and Controller Synthesis

Jesse Wayment, Brian Yarbrough, Jingbo Wang, Shreyas Sundaram, Philip E. Paré

We investigate the joint synthesis of neural network Lyapunov functions and stabilizing controllers for systems modeled as neural ordinary differential equations (neural ODEs) with ReLU activations. Our approach leverages tools from tropical geometry, an emerging analytical framework for characterizing properties of ReLU neural networks. To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first to introduce tropical geometry-based methods into control theory. We represent candidate Lyapunov functions as tropical quotients of tropical signomials, which allows us to enforce the equilibrium condition at the origin and formulate constraints ensuring positive definiteness over a prescribed region of interest. We construct a total Lie surface representing the Lie derivative of the candidate Lyapunov function along the neural ODE dynamics and formulate an optimization problem to find Lyapunov parameters that ensure its negativity throughout the region of interest. This enables the systematic search for Lyapunov functions satisfying the desired stability conditions. Finally, we extend this framework to jointly synthesize a ReLU neural network stabilizing controller and a neural Lyapunov function, providing a unified approach to stability analysis and controller synthesis. We validate our proposed method on several nonlinear systems approximated as neural ODEs, demonstrating applications to Lyapunov function synthesis as well as joint Lyapunov function and controller synthesis.

Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness

Xiao-Ming Wu, Kang Liao, Yuexiao Ma, Taolin Zhang, Mingqian Zhou et al. (15 authors)

Frontier multimodal models exhibit extraordinary generalization in generating robot actions from observations and instructions, enabling a new paradigm in which they serve directly as policies within closed-loop robotic harnesses. However, the design principles of such harnesses, and the capabilities underlying their strong performance, remain unclear. We present Embo-Harness, a framework for systematically studying frontier-model-as-policy harnesses. We investigate four components of the robotic agentic loop, namely, prompting, planning, action execution, and tool use, and distill six empirical findings into a practical recipe. This recipe prioritizes interfaces that exploit the model's native visual reasoning and tool-use capabilities over handcrafted plans, skills, or action priors, and identifies effective choices for action representation, chunking, reasoning effort, visual observations, short multi-action sequences, and policy refinement. On RoboLab-120, Embo-Harness achieves an 83.3\% success rate, outperforming the strongest reported baseline by 26.2 percentage points. It also succeeds in 15 of 20 zero-shot trials across four real-world manipulation tasks. Beyond performance, controlled interventions indicate that the studied model depends more strongly on accurate spatial target estimation than on explicit low-level joint or trajectory generation. These results provide both a practical recipe for building strong frontier-model-as-policy harnesses and a clearer account of the capabilities that drive their performance.

VITA-WAM: Manifold-Aligned World-Action Modeling for Real-Time Robot Control

Boqi Zhao, Dechen Gao, Hanchu Zhou, Hang Wang, Junshan Zhang

World action model (WAM) is a promising paradigm for visuomotor control by jointly modeling world dynamics and actions. Notably, existing WAMs often rely on large generative models and iterative sampling which incur substantial latency for action generation, limiting real-time deployment. In particular, there are two root causes of inefficiency in conventional flow matching or diffusion based action models in WAMs: 1) transporting uninformative Gaussian noise into precise actions necessitating multiple ODE steps, and 2) repeatedly injecting task context through expensive cross-attention modules. Our key insight is that jointly learning and aligning the source and action manifolds streamlines the transport between them, substantially accelerating action generation. Building on this insight, we introduce VITA-WAM, a WAM architecture with manifold-aligned flow-matching (VITA flow matching) that directly transports latent observation representations containing visual, language, and proprioceptive information into latent actions. More specifically, a flow latent alignment (FLA) mechanism is employed to facilitate manifold alignment between latent observation and action representations, enabling accurate generation with few ODE steps. The resulting architecture also eliminates costly attention for observation conditioning during repeated denoising steps. Notably, with a single ODE step, VITA-WAM outperforms 10-step FastWAM by 21.47 pp on LIBERO-Plus and by 1.65 pp on LIBERO, achieving these gains through the FLA objective without distillation, while inferring at 55.67 ms latency, a $2.10\times$ speedup compared to FastWAM.

EmboEagle: An Efficient Agentic Embodied Framework for Long-horizon Robotic Manipulation

Fan Xu, Zhongtao Wang, Hao Wu, Yitong Zhu, Wenzhuo Ma et al. (12 authors)

Long-horizon robotic manipulation demands reliable execution across interdependent stages, yet standalone World-Action Models (WAMs) suffer from compounding physical errors, while stateless Vision-Language Model (VLM) supervision incurs prohibitive chunkwise latency and lacks 3D spatial persistence. We present EmboEagle, a closed-loop agentic framework that unifies subtask-conditioned WAM control, latent-space adaptive supervision, and memory-guided geometric recovery. First, we adapt sequential WAMs via decoupled two-stage flow matching that preserves cross-stage visual history while calibrating truncated action supervision at subtask boundaries. Second, without an auxiliary vision encoder, we distill teacher intervention decisions into a lightweight routing gate driven solely by the WAM Action Expert's pre- and post-chunk latent transitions and subtask embeddings. Third, upon invocation, the VLM decouples diagnostic status from discrete routing actions and executes parameterized recovery primitives grounded in structured memory (initial references, active grasp bindings, and tool traces), followed by mandatory post-repair verification. Across RMBench and LIBERO-Mem, EmboEagle achieves 91.3\% and 90.6\% average success, surpassing the strongest baselines by 8.0 and 9.8 percentage points, while boosting real-world success over VoLoAgent under identical executor weights. Moreover, adaptive gating cuts VLM calls by 74.2\% versus chunkwise monitoring (91.3\% vs. 93.7\% success).

Towards a Formalised Framework for Analog Electronics and Circuit Theory

Yaohua Zhang, Zhi Kai Pong

Circuit theory provides the mathematical foundation for the analysis and design of electronic circuits. However, conventional circuit derivations often rely on assumptions and mathematical relationships that are not explicitly recorded, making their verification and reuse challenging. In this paper, we present the first step towards a formalised framework for circuit theory and analog electronics using the Lean 4 interactive theorem prover. As a proof of concept, we formalise an ideal RC low-pass filter, establishing reusable definitions for component impedance and circuit connections, and machine-checked theorems for voltage division, the transfer function, and low-pass behaviour. Building upon this demonstration, we outline a broader vision for an extensible library of circuit models, definitions, and verified results. Our long-term aim is to digitise, standardise, and verify circuit theory, enabling established knowledge to be systematically checked, shared, and reused across different circuit analyses.

Topological Fairness in Continuous-State Systems

Mahdi Nazeri, Alessandro Abate, Anne-Kathrin Schmuck, Sadegh Soudjani

Since the early days of probabilistic programs, fairness has been used to abstract randomness in software. Intuitively, a fair scheduler reproduces the almost-sure behavior of a random choice based solely on the set of possible outcomes, independent of their (often unknown) distribution. As this abstraction attaches fairness to individual transitions, it becomes ineffective over uncountable state spaces, where every single successor has probability zero. We overcome this limitation by imposing fairness on open sets instead. This leads to the novel notion of topological fairness: every basic open set---with respect to a countable basis of the state-space topology---that lies in the set of possible successors infinitely often must be visited infinitely often. We show that topological fairness carries the intuition from program abstraction to continuous state spaces. For systems with additive stochastic disturbances, almost every trajectory is topologically fair under every control policy. Reasoning over all topologically fair trajectories is therefore a sound proof principle for almost-sure properties of the original system. Unlike in the finite-state case, however, it is not complete: topological fairness cannot capture almost-sure behavior that hinges on the relative likelihoods of disturbance outcomes. Finally, we show that topological fairness supports synthesis directly on the continuous state space. For reach-avoid objectives, we give a nested fixed point over subsets of the state space which, for a broad class of systems, computes the fair winning domain exactly together with a memoryless winning strategy. Our case studies show that this winning domain is substantially larger than the one obtained with currently available techniques, which detour through a finite abstraction and certify a winning domain that depends on the discretization in a non-monotone way.

Data-Driven Identification of Uncertainty Sets with Conformance Guarantees

Laura Lützow, Mykel J. Kochenderfer, Matthias Althoff

Formal verification and controller synthesis require accurate models. Conformance relations specify the required accuracy of a model such that properties established for the model carry over to the target system. Existing methods for establishing them from measured data are typically restricted to a single relation, a specific model class, or a downstream verification algorithm. We present ConfID, a unified framework that establishes reachset, trace, and simulation conformance with respect to measured traces by identifying uncertainty sets for a given, possibly nonlinear nominal model. Unmodeled effects are thus captured by nondeterminism rather than by refining the nominal dynamics. For all three conformance relations, the identification is a linear program that minimizes the size of the reachable sets; only its constraints depend on the relation. We further derive distribution-free bounds on the probability that the identified models are conformant for unseen behaviors. Experiments on simulated and real-world data evaluate the models identified for the three relations, confirm the guarantees, and show that these models cover unseen behaviors more reliably than baselines, with comparable or smaller sets.

WeaveVLA: 3D Relational Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Models

Zien Wang, Yuhao Pan, Yunfeng Fan, Nan Cheng, Xin Xia et al. (6 authors)

Vision-language-action (VLA) models leverage pretrained vision-language representations for instruction-conditioned robot control, yet their understanding of task-relevant 3D relations and how these relations evolve during manipulation remains largely implicit. To address this challenge, we introduce WeaveVLA, a framework that enhances VLA action generation by explicitly modeling task-relevant relative 3D relations and maintaining relational context across observations. WeaveVLA employs a task-conditioned sparse graph neural network to encode relative 3D relations among objects, the gripper, and nearby references. Pair-specific recurrent edge states integrate geometric evidence across observations to maintain evolving spatial context. A learned-query readout summarizes the updated graph representations into compact relational tokens, which condition the action expert alongside the original vision-language context. An auxiliary geometry reconstruction objective further encourages spatial structure preservation. We instantiate WeaveVLA on StarVLA-GR00T and $π_{0.5}$, achieving over 98\% average success on LIBERO with both backbones. On LIBERO-Plus, WeaveVLA improves success from 74.9\% to 82.0\% and from 85.7\% to 87.8\%, respectively, with additional improvements on CALVIN D$\rightarrow$D. On three real-world tasks, the StarVLA-GR00T-based variant improves average success from 55.6\% to 70.0\% under standard conditions and from 33.3\% to 53.3\% under OOD perturbations, surpassing the $π_{0.5}$ baseline in both settings.
