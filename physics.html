Entropy Products and the Extremal Limit That Never Comes

Kevin Goldstein, Vishnu Jejjala, Suresh Nampuri

Two regular black hole horizons need not have an entropy product fixed by the charges and couplings. In Einstein--Maxwell--scalar theory, we construct asymptotically flat branches with products above or below the squared extremal entropy. Regularity selects their horizon scalar values, and different branches can share the charges, couplings, and asymptotic scalar. Each product stays constant as mass and asymptotic scalar vary. The horizon areas stay apart and no extremal limit is reached: the temperature vanishes only where the mass diverges. We prove local existence.

High-temperature Gibbs states are uniformly Markovian and trivial

Andrew D. Kim, Tai Hsuan Yang, Jong Yeon Lee

We prove that, above a constant temperature threshold, Gibbs states of local noncommuting qudit Hamiltonians exhibit exponential decay of mutual information and conditional mutual information (CMI). Specifically, we prove exponential decay of the CMI for arbitrary spatial tripartitions $A \sqcup B \sqcup C$ which need not comprise the entire system, thereby establishing a uniform Markov property for high-temperature Gibbs states. A central obstruction to proving such bounds is that conventional cluster expansions for the logarithm of reduced Gibbs states can fail to converge due to noncommutativity. Our main insight is to construct a classical representation of the Gibbs state using an overcomplete basis. Although such representations generally involve negative quasiprobability weights, we show that a decomposition based on projective 2-designs admits a convergent cluster expansion with strictly positive weights at sufficiently high temperatures, allowing us to circumvent the obstruction. The separability of high-temperature Gibbs states is a natural byproduct of this decomposition. Finally, in the context of mixed-state phases of matter, our results imply that high-temperature Gibbs states belong to the same mixed-state phase as the maximally mixed state.

Little Red Dots from Primordial Vacuum Bubbles

Miguel Barroso Varela, Katherine Freese, Matthew Kleban, Cameron Norton, Evangelos I. Sfakianakis

Quantum tunneling transverse to the inflaton trajectory can produce $10^6$--$10^8M_\odot$ primordial black holes at the abundance of JWST's little red dots, without the enhanced curvature perturbations excluded by limits on CMB spectral distortions. Rare vacuum bubbles nucleate during a brief interval of inflation and later form black holes, with adjustable mass and abundance and a narrow mass spectrum of percent-level width. The leading constraint, from accretion, is satisfied for a population matching the bright little red dots even if all the emitted radiation escapes as hard X-rays.

Field theory and the biquadratic spin-1 chain

Luisa Eck, Paul Fendley

The antiferromagnetic biquadratic spin-1 chain is gapped, $SU(3)$ symmetric, and integrable. Standard arguments suggest that its low-energy physics is described by the $\mathbb{CP}^2$ nonlinear sigma model at $θ=π$. Using the Bethe ansatz, we show that its low-energy excitations can be interpreted as deconfined domain walls, just as in the sigma model. However, the lattice model is integrable, whereas the field theory is generally believed not to be. We propose a solution to this mystery by finding an integrable field theory with the same low-energy limit but which is not Lorentz invariant. The integrability-preserving scaling limit is obtained by adding a higher conserved charge and tuning the chain to criticality. The gap closes quadratically, so that the limit must be described by some nonrelativistic field theory rather than the relativistic sigma model. We describe the two-particle scattering matrix of this field theory.

Local velocimetry for atomic superfluids: from Riemann rarefaction waves to quantized vortices

Yansheng Zhang, Yi Jiang, Feiyang Wang, Louis Depaepe, J. Q. Matthew Chia et al. (8 authors)

We demonstrate local velocimetry for atomic superfluids in two dimensions (2D). Using matter-wave interferometry based on Raman--Bragg beam splitters, we map the local gradient of the phase of the superfluid's wavefunction onto displacement of the interference fringes and reconstruct the 2D velocity field. We illustrate the capabilities of this technique by applying it to two canonical problems. First, we directly observe the self-similar evolution of the velocity field in 1D Riemann rarefaction flows. Second, we map out the full 2D velocity field around quantized vortices, explicitly demonstrating the quantization of velocity circulation.

Query-Optimal Quantum Minimum Finding from Numerical Estimators

Egil Bae

We propose a quantum minimum-finding algorithm for objectives accessed via numerical estimators that avoids the logarithmic query overhead of amplifying the confidence of estimates before the search. A coherent estimator that is $\varepsilon$-accurate with probability at least $2/3$ on each of $N$ labels suffices to find a $2\varepsilon$-optimal label using $O(\sqrt N)$ base-estimator queries at constant confidence. The query bound and the additive-error constant are separately worst-case optimal. More generally, suppose a preparation assigns probability mass at least a known $p$ to objective values at most $a$. Without knowing $a$ or the prepared distribution, our algorithm returns a candidate and an estimate $v$ such that, jointly with constant probability, $v$ is $\varepsilon$-accurate for the candidate and $v\le a+\varepsilon$. The algorithm uses $O(p^{-1/2})$ queries to each of the preparation and estimator oracles in the worst case, and the selected candidate can be retained as a quantum state. For preparation width $s_P$ and estimator workspace width $w$, it requires $O(s_P+w+\log\log(4/p))$ qubits, independently of the requested confidence. Applications give worst-case optimal $Θ(\sqrt N/η)$ queries for selecting an $η$-optimal bounded expectation, and guided ground-energy estimation using $O(ω^{-1})$ preparations and $O((ωη)^{-1})$ elementary controlled-evolution queries at constant confidence, where $ω$ is a lower bound on the guiding state's ground-space amplitude overlap.

Fast, in-place, and ancilla-free quantum algorithms for integer multiplication and inversion

David Halpenny, Adithya Sireesh, Petros Wallden

Arithmetic operations are a fundamental building block in the design of quantum algorithms, with their implementation cost expected to have a significant impact on the viability of a wide variety of quantum applications. In this work, we present novel algorithms for a number of in-place quantum arithmetic operations that are highly efficient in terms of their space and gate count overheads compared to the current state-of-the-art. We show that in-place modular multiplication of integers, modulo a power of two, can be performed with either a classical or quantum odd multiplier using $O(n^{1+ε})$ gates and no ancilla qubits, for any $ε>0$. We then show that standard in-place integer multiplication can be performed with a non-zero classical or quantum multiplier using $O(n^{1+ε})$ gates for any $ε>0$, with no ancilla qubits required for the classical multiplier and a logarithmic number of ancilla qubits for the quantum. Using our quantum modular multiplication algorithm, we also show that in-place modular inversion, modulo a power of two, can be performed using $O(n^{1+ε})$ gates and no ancilla qubits, for any $ε>0$. To our knowledge, these are the first quantum algorithms achieving both subquadratic gate counts and sublinear ancilla counts for each of the considered in-place operations. All but one of these fast operations are optimal in terms of memory, only requiring enough space to store the output of the operation, which overwrites the input directly. We present our multiplication algorithms as consequences of a single unified theorem, which may find further application within quantum arithmetic.

Scalable Simulation of Strongly Correlated Fermions with Neural Gaussian Tensor Networks

Runze Chi, Ao Chen, Garnet Kin-Lic Chan

Accurate simulations of strongly correlated electrons remain a central challenge in computational physics. We introduce neural-network Gaussian projected entangled-pair states (NN-GPEPS), which exploit the locally-entangled subspace of Gaussian states to give an architecture more compatible with modern neural networks and an exact contraction cost of $O(N^{3/2})$ on two-dimensional lattices. For the Hubbard model at $U/t=8$ and $1/8$ hole doping, we obtain state-of-the-art variational energies in large lattices of up to 1024 sites. These results demonstrate an accurate approach to strongly correlated fermions by connecting neural networks with Gaussian tensor networks.

Equivariant iterated Eisenstein integrals for congruence subgroups of level 2

Emiel Claasen, Mehregan Doroudiani, Claude Duhr, Axel Kleinschmidt, Franca Lippert et al. (7 authors)

The modular properties of holomorphic Eisenstein series for $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and its congruence subgroups do not carry over to their primitives. Instead, the transformation of iterated Eisenstein integrals under congruence subgroups of level $N$ introduces inhomogeneous terms comprising (among others) cyclotomic multiple zeta values of level $N$. We combine iterated Eisenstein integrals for the congruence subgroups of level $N=2$ of arbitrary iteration depth with their complex conjugates and (single-valued) level-2 cyclotomic multiple zeta values such that they define non-holomorphic modular forms. Our construction of these so-called equivariant iterated Eisenstein integrals for $Γ(2)$ and $Γ_0(2)$ proceeds at the level of generating series that take values in an infinite-dimensional (pro-unipotent) Lie group and closely follow Brown's analogous construction in the $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ case. The required insertions of cyclotomic multiple zeta values are controlled by zeta generators for level 2 that normalise the corresponding (pro-nilpotent) Lie algebra of the generating series and whose Lie-bracket relations we determine systematically. Upon restriction to Eisenstein series of modular weight~2, we recover single-valued multiple polylogarithms on the genus 0 modular curve $Γ(2)\backslash \mathbb{H}$.

UV Bounds on Inflationary Parity Violation

Alexander Cassem, Mark P. Hertzberg

A question of fundamental interest is whether parity is violated on large scales in the Universe. This possibility has been motivated by recent analyses of the four-point matter correlation function. It has also been suggested that detectable parity violation could be generated during inflation by the dynamical Chern-Simons operator, a leading effective field theory correction to general relativity. In this work, we examine this possibility in realistic UV completions, including fermionic and string-theoretic examples. We show that existing bounds on the tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum strongly constrain the possibility of generating a detectable parity-odd trispectrum. For tensor-to-scalar ratios near current observational limits, detectability is incompatible with, or pushes against, the regime of validity of these UV completions. A detectable signal can remain possible for a sufficiently small tensor-to-scalar ratio, but only at the cost of an extremely large enhancement of the TSS vertex. Thus, while a future search for large scale parity violation is worth pursuing, generating a detectable signal within a consistent effective theory is highly nontrivial.
