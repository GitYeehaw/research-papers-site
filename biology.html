A Bayesian Optimized Biquad Filter-Burg Model for Real-Time EEG Phase Estimation

Ivan Martinez-Kay, Tatiana Kardashina, ShiNung Ching, Eric C. Leuthardt, Benjamin T. Acland

Real-time phase estimation on stimulus-aligned transient EEG is critical for closed-loop neuromodulation systems targeting the theta-band, yet most real-time phase estimation methods have been developed and validated on resting-state, high-fidelity signals in the alpha band. Recent work has pursued deep learning architectures, while individualized classical bandpass filter tuning remains underexplored. This study characterizes the spectral and temporal properties of non-anticipative bandpass filtering and demonstrates the performance gains achieved through individualized tuning. We present a Bayesian-Optimized Biquad Filter-Burg (BOB) model evaluated on EEG recorded during peripheral vibrotactile stimulation of the left median nerve (theta, N=20 healthy subjects) and on resting-state recordings from the Max Planck Institute's LEMON dataset (alpha, N=20 healthy subjects). BOB was benchmarked against the Deep Dual-Branch Complex Neural Network (DBCNN), EEG Phase Prediction Network (EPN), Phastimate, and Phase Estimation with Autoregressive Padding (PEAP) under matched testing conditions: channel selection, individualized optimization, evaluation time points, and an offline reference phase obtained through zero-phase filtering and the Hilbert transform. Under these testing conditions, BOB achieved matched or better performance than these models at a fraction of the calibration time.

iGC: Antibody Optimization via Reinforcement Learning over a Learned Somatic Hypermutation Prior

Thao Nguyen, Robert G. Alberstein, Ewa Nowara, Homa MohammadiPeyhani, Holly R. Steach et al. (13 authors)

Antibody affinity maturation is a natural optimization process in which B cells acquire mutations through somatic hypermutation (SHM), undergo selection for improved antigen binding, and iteratively evolve toward higher affinity. Computationally modeling this process requires capturing both biologically plausible mutations and antigen-specific selection. However, existing mutation models do not directly optimize binding, while affinity optimization often neglects realistic mutation trajectories. We introduce iGC (in silico Germinal Centers), a two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework for antigen-specific antibody optimization. Stage 1 learns an SHM mutation prior from parent-child pairs in B-cell lineage trees and adapts it to a target affinity-maturation dataset. Stage 2 initializes an RL policy from this prior and optimizes antigen binding with Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization toward the frozen prior. To prevent reward exploitation, including repetitive high-reward substitutions and structurally tolerated framework mutations, we introduce a Reward-Tax Meta-Gradient objective. RewardNet smooths the affinity landscape, while TaxNet penalizes reward overexploitation, jointly defining a learned meta-reward for policy optimization. We evaluate iGC on mutation prediction, SHM consistency, affinity optimization, and mutation diversity. The mutation prior more than doubles the strongest baseline in mutation-site prediction. Following RL optimization, iGC maintains SHM sequence structure. It also achieves a strong exploration-exploitation balance, yielding competitive reward-diversity products across candidate-selection cutoffs. These results demonstrate that iGC integrates biologically plausible mutation generation with antigen-specific affinity selection, providing an in silico framework for iterative antibody affinity maturation.

Empirical Bayes Flow Matching for Continuous Cryo-EM Heterogeneity

Daniel Aibinder, Azmi Haider, Dan Rosenbaum

We address the problem of learning generative priors over high-dimensional latent variables from indirect, noisy observations, with a focus on continuous heterogeneity in cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM). In this setting, 3D atomic structures $x \in \mathbb{R}^{3N_a}$ are never directly observed and must be inferred from 2D projection images $y$ generated by a complex, non-linear forward operator. We propose Empirical Bayes Flow Matching (EB-FM), a method that learns a flow matching prior $p_θ(x)$ directly from observations using a Monte Carlo stochastic approximation Expectation-Maximization framework. EB-FM alternates between an E-step that performs approximate posterior sampling from $p_θ(x \mid y)$ via guided flow-based generation while maintaining persistent latent estimates through stochastic approximation, and an M-step that updates the flow model using these stabilized latent variables as training targets. This approach eliminates the need for pretraining on clean data and naturally accommodates non-linear forward models. We validate our method on controlled inverse problems using two-moons and MNIST data, and demonstrate its effectiveness on simulated cryo-EM data with known poses, showing that EB-FM can recover continuous distributions of protein conformations directly in atomic-coordinate space.

Ablating Design Choices for Protein Ensemble Generators Across Structural Regimes

Eoin Quinn, Oliver Bent

Generative models of protein conformational ensembles inherit much of their design from structure prediction for folded proteins. Ordered and disordered proteins, however, occupy distinct structural regimes, and we investigate how design choices hold up across them. Using a minimal C$α$ flow-matching model, we ablate choices of architecture, sequence conditioning, rigid-body alignment and training source, and evaluate each variant against experimental SAXS and NMR chemical-shift data partitioned into ordered, disordered, multi-domain and transitional proteins. Mixture-of-experts routing, a static pair representation and a disorder probe have no detectable effect, a smaller protein language model performs worse on ordered proteins but not on disordered ones, and alignment in the loss degrades agreement with SAXS. Replacing coarse-grained simulations of disordered proteins with fragment-assembled ensembles improves agreement with chemical shifts at a cost to SAXS, while an equal mixture of the two keeps most of the gain at a smaller cost. This points to the training data, rather than the architecture, as the main constraint.

Basic Reproductive Numbers in Host-Parasite Systems with Demographic Bistability

Carli Peterson, Erika Gallo, Christopher Kribs

The basic reproductive number, $\mathcal{R}_0$, is widely used to determine whether an infectious disease can persist in a population. However, its interpretation may become unclear when the underlying disease-free demographic dynamics exhibit multiple types of stable attractors. In this study, we investigate how demographic bistability between fixed points and periodic cycles affects the calculation and interpretation of $\mathcal{R}_0$. We consider two discrete-time host-parasite models with infection: a tick-host model motivated by Borrelia burgdorferi transmission and a Nicholson-Bailey host-parasitoid model with intraspecific competition and infection. For each model, we calculate reproductive numbers associated with different disease-free attractors using the next-generation operator method and compare their invasion thresholds. In the tick-host model, the reproduction number calculated on the stable demographic cycle differs from that calculated at the positive coexistence fixed point, resulting in parameter regions where infection can invade at one demographic attractor but not the other. In contrast, in the host-parasitoid model, the host-driven invasion threshold is necessarily the same at the positive coexistence fixed point and on the host-only periodic cycles. These contrasting results demonstrate that demographic bistability alone does not necessarily produce attractor-dependent invasion thresholds. Rather, the effect of demographic dynamics on invasion depends on the structure of pathogen transmission. In particular, a complete host--vector--host transmission pathway can allow differences between demographic attractors to alter the invasion threshold. Our results emphasize the importance of considering both demographic dynamics and transmission structure when interpreting $\mathcal{R}_0$ in populations with multiple stable disease-free states.

Curriculum unlearning in humans and neural networks

Jirko Rubruck, Alice Zhang, Andrew Saxe, Christopher Summerfield

During learning, humans often benefit from curricula that teach simple rules before exceptions. However, we lack a computational explanation for this phenomenon. Here, using a canonical behavioural task and modelling with neural networks, we develop and test a theory for why simple-to-complex curricula succeed. We show that during learning, both humans and neural networks show simplicity biases, a tendency to learn simple rules that are underfit to the data. In neural networks this bias is accompanied by compression of neural representations. Simple-to-complex curricula help both types of learners overcome this compression, developing representations (and policy) with appropriate dimensionality for the task. We test and confirm a further prediction from this theory: learners that have been pre-exposed to simple tasks and with more compressed initial representations benefit more from curricula. These findings suggest that curricula help with ``unlearning'' of simple rules.

Predicting protein structures at any folding stage from sequence

Lisheng Zhang, Qingsong Li, Zinan Xie, Yanting Yang, Qikui Yang et al. (10 authors)

Protein structure predictors reliably identify native conformations, but do not describe the continuum connecting unfolded ensembles to folded states. Here we present FoldScape, a sequence-conditioned deep learning framework that generates C$α$ conformational ensembles at a specified folding fraction $Q$. FoldScape reconstructs three-dimensional chains through hierarchical, sliding-window regressions of one-dimensional inter-residue distances, followed by chain assembly and sampling with a rectified-flow decoder. It is trained on multi-scale coarse-grained folding trajectories and all-atom mechanical-unfolding trajectories. Across 20 human proteins (36-857 residues), at matched sample size, FoldScape covers about an order of magnitude more of the folding coordinate than seed-varied AlphaFold 3 ensembles, robustly across proteins, order parameters and binning choices. AlphaFold 3 better anchors the immediate native vicinity, which FoldScape does not reach, making the approaches complementary. FoldScape reconstruction accuracy remains approximately constant along $Q$, from collapsed coil to near-native globule, rather than deteriorating away from the native state. A stride-one crop-and-superpose scheme extends inference to chains several times longer than a single chain-level forward pass. We also show that training trajectories resolve mechanical unfolding at residue resolution, that elongation-conditioned ensembles exhibit reproducible continuous observables across pulling velocities, and that partially unfolded chains form geometrically coherent segments. FoldScape thus combines broad coverage of folding stages with stable reconstruction accuracy, without per-protein simulation.

Evolving view on phylogenetic networks

Claudia Solis-Lemus

Reticulate processes such as hybridization, introgression, and horizontal gene transfer cannot be fully represented by a bifurcating tree. Enter phylogenetic networks: first as split graphs to visualize tree discordance, then as explicit probabilistic models that capture biological phenomena. Here, we describe the broad taxonomy of network representations, distinguishing the principal classes of explicit networks, their biological interpretability and our ability to accurately estimate them from empirical data. We also trace the evolution of the main network inferential methods from hybrid detection tests, distance- and subgraph-based amalgamation methods, probabilistic approaches under the multispecies network coalescent, composite-likelihood and divide-and-conquer frameworks, while highlighting the selective pressures of statistical identifiability and computational scalability that have shaped this evolution. As we move towards a network thinking paradigm, previously isolated methodological lineages from population genetics, phylogenomics, and mathematical network theory are now introgressing, uniting diverse network models into a shared framework that can integrate sequence- and species-level reticulate processes, increase robustness to systematic errors, and refine algorithms for genome-scale data, expanding the tree of life into a richer, more entangled yet clearer picture of evolution.

GINsim 3.1: Qualitative modelling and dynamical analysis of logical regulatory networks

Laurent Tichit, Dominique Benielli, Olivier Chabrol, Denis Thieffry, Claudine Chaouiya et al. (6 authors)

GINsim is a software for the definition and dynamical analysis of qualitative (Boolean and multi-valued) models of biological regulatory networks. Building on its modular architecture, based on the MDDlib decision-diagram and bioLQM model-manipulation libraries, GINsim version 3.1 introduces new functionalities aimed at the analysis of large logical models: the symbolic computation of trap-spaces, the Booleanisation of multilevel models and the reversal of model dynamics. GINsim 3.1 also extends the estimation of attractor reachability (Monte Carlo and Avatar algorithms), the computation of stable states, as well as model reduction functionalities. Analyses can be applied to perturbed models, and scripted, in particular from the CoLoMoTo interactive notebook. We illustrate the main functionalities of GINsim on a reference multilevel model of regulatory network that control the mammalian cell cycle, including the computation of its stable states and cyclic attractor, the assessment of their reachability, and the identification of the regulatory circuits driving multistability and oscillations. GINsim is freely available under GPL-3.0 at https://ginsim.github.io/, runs on any platform supporting Java, and is interoperable with the other tools of the CoLoMoTo community. Its website provides installation instructions and reference documentation, as well as a repository of Boolean and multilevel logical models.

When Model Complexity Matters in Cross-Subject Naturalistic fMRI Prediction

Junhao Zhao, Jacob Kang, Colin Kurniawan, David Simberg, Nan Xu

Increasingly complex neural models predict blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) responses to naturalistic stimuli, but the predictive value of this added complexity remains unclear. We ask when pretrained stimulus representations help and how much downstream complexity is needed, with and without recent measured BOLD history. We compare linear diagnostic references and nonlinear predictors across HCP movie fMRI (154 subjects), BOLD Moments (10 subjects), and Algonauts 2025 (4 subjects). Under a shared offline protocol, models are fitted on training subjects and evaluated on held-out subjects viewing largely familiar stimuli, without refitting. Pretrained visual features generally improve prediction over raw pixels; for linear autoregression, this advantage becomes more pronounced over longer rollouts (recursive predictions without additional measured BOLD). Measured BOLD history provides substantial gains that generally diminish {as rollouts lengthen}. Nonlinear stimulus-only predictors can improve on linear readouts, whereas linear autoregression provides a strong history-conditioned baseline. Controls examine context length, regularization, fitting procedures, and model capacity. Regional response consistency across subjects is associated with held-out prediction accuracy, while less consistent regions tend to gain more from measured history. In paired simulations with fixed linear neural dynamics, slower hemodynamic responses generally sustain high temporal prediction correlations over longer rollouts, illustrating how the observation process can contribute to predictive persistence. Together, these findings support evaluating stimulus representations and downstream complexity separately, using history-conditioned linear prediction as a diagnostic reference under matched inputs and measurement availability.
