Novikov-Magnus Master Equation for Driven Quantum Systems under Classical Correlated Noise

Nada Khogeer, Jerison Parra, Caroline Desgranges, Hugo Ribeiro

We introduce a modular framework for deriving time-local quantum master equations for driven systems subject to temporally correlated classical noise. The construction combines the Donsker-Furutsu-Novikov relation with the Magnus expansion: Functional response closes the noise average, while nested commutators retain the noncommuting chronological action of noise-induced perturbations along the controlled quantum trajectory. For zero-mean Gaussian noise, we derive the resulting Novikov-Magnus quantum master equation through fourth order in the noise strength. The same response hierarchy suggests a natural extension beyond Gaussian noise, with connected higher-order noise correlations weighting higher functional derivatives of the stochastic map. For a driven qubit under Ornstein-Uhlenbeck and band-limited $1/f$ noise, the second- and fourth-order maps remain CPTP throughout the perturbative regime and accurately reproduce stochastic simulations. Their diamond-norm errors are comparable to those of the cumulant-based pseudo-Lindblad master equation, with a modest fourth-order improvement for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise.

Trajectory-Dependent Decoherence and Leakage During Shuttling of Multi-Spin Qubits

Yu-Ning Zhang, Aleksandr S. Mokeev, Maximilian Rimbach-Russ, David P. DiVincenzo, Viatcheslav V. Dobrovitski

Logical qubits encoded in collective states of multiple spins are promising building blocks for large-scale semiconductor quantum processors. As novel fault-tolerant architectures increasingly rely on qubit shuttling, understanding decoherence of the shuttled multi-spin qubits becomes crucial. We analyze fidelity and leakage of the logical qubits dephased by the spatio-temporally correlated $1/f$ noise, considering two-, three-, and four-spin encodings. We study different shuttling protocols and establish that for all encodings generally, parallel shuttling provides the best protection against decoherence at short distances, whereas sequential shuttling outperforms others for long-range transport (although the exact boundaries depend on the specific device parameters). We explain this common behavior in terms of the dephasing factors of pairs of the shuttled spins; we provide explicit expressions for fidelities and leakage, thus enabling experimentalists to predict performance of the multi-spin qubits from only several independently measured pairwise dephasing factors. Our results establish a connection between shuttling trajectories, noise correlations, and qubit decoherence, and provide guidance for achieving noise-resilient shuttling in scalable semiconductor quantum processors.

Existence of extremal solutions in the 1d conformal bootstrap

Filip Herček, Nat Levine

Extremal solutions and functionals play an important role in the conformal bootstrap. These are the solutions that saturate unitarity bounds, and the dual functionals that encode the optimal bounds. While they have fundamental theoretical implications, their existence has not been rigorously established. This is particularly subtle in the setting of the full infinite-dimensional bootstrap, i.e. the limit of zero numerical truncation. In this article, we prove that extremal solutions exist for OPE maximisation problems in 1d CFT. These are a large class of generically interacting, unitary, exact solutions of the crossing equation that maximise an OPE coefficient. We also prove that these problems have 'zero duality gap', meaning there exist functionals arbitrarily close to extremality. Our approach is to formulate the bootstrap as a convex optimisation problem in a Hilbert space and apply the Fenchel-Rockafellar theorem.

Emergence and polymer physics of the center-vortex network: Confronting SU(4) lattice Yang-Mills theory

Luis E. Oxman, Jackson A. Mickley, Derek B. Leinweber

The geometric organization and statistical properties of center-vortex networks provide a window into the infrared dynamics underlying confinement in Yang-Mills theory. Motivated by this connection, we investigate new consequences of a previously proposed confinement mechanism based on physically equivalent elementary Cartan fluxes. We show that, in the percolating phase, these fluxes dynamically reorganize into center-charge sectors whose dominant vortex tensions satisfy a Casimir scaling law, similar to that observed for confining string tensions. We then confront this result with the first nontrivial Yang-Mills case: the doubly charged vortex chains recently observed in SU(4) lattice simulations. To extract the parameters needed to connect the lattice ensemble with the effective description, we characterize how center-vortex chains grow and bend in SU(3) and SU(4) using polymer-inspired techniques not previously applied to center vortices. We find good quantitative agreement for the SU(4) doubly charged sector, providing further evidence for the proposed mechanism.

A Quantum Mechanical Description of Active Matter

Ghansham Chandel, Siddhartha Das

The natural environments habitable to life are inseparable from dissipative and entropic forces, which mandate active matter to operate on constant fluxes of energy and momentum to survive and propagate through spacetime. In contrast to fundamental particles which are characterized by energy and momentum, we postulate that energy expenditure rate and momentum generation rate are the fundamental dynamical quantities driving this evolution of swimming active matter and accordingly construct the corresponding Lagrangian and Hamiltonian of motion of the active matter. Furthermore, living systems broadly employ periodic, non-repeating motion for both locomotion and various biological functions, with meaningful information stored in the periods of these functions. Consequently, analyzing these dynamics in the Fourier space lands us at the familiar tools of the operator based, quantum mechanics. This is accomplished by introducing a complex field to describe the evolving probability distribution of a single unit, as well as the population density of mutually non-interacting active particles under the constraint of scarce resources. The resulting single-unit wavefunction evolves analogously to the Dirac equation with minimal coupling, wherein gauge symmetry reflects the generality of the governing equations across arbitrary resource landscapes. Additionally, we present a Schrödinger-like equation where the effective potential term represents the environmentally mandated, non-motile base metabolic rate. Finally, we formalize the bidirectional coupling between active swimmers and the surrounding fluid via a multipole expansion of the active force with an emphasis on force monopoles and dipoles, where each pole corresponds to a distinct mode of active motility.

Particle production from noncommutative-inspired Reissner-Nordström black hole

Alberto Maya, Marco Maceda

By considering a massless Klein-Gordon field and the moving mirror model, we calculate particle production for a noncommutative-inspired Reissner-Nordström black hole sourced by smeared distributions of mass and charge. We show that noncommutative truncated trajectories diminish the emission spectrum at large frequencies, due to noncommutative effects on the external surface gravity of the black hole, in sharp contrast with the spectrum of a classical Reissner-Nordström black hole.

Raman anyon-Hubbard model in a 1D Bose lattice gas

Josep Cabedo, Claudio Iacovelli, Leticia Tarruell, Alessio Celi

The anyon-Hubbard model is the paradigmatic lattice model of one-dimensional (1D) anyons, in which chiral interactions make bosons equivalent to particles with fractional statistics. Its continuum limit, the chiral BF theory, emerges as the low-energy description of a Raman-coupled Bose gas with imbalanced interactions. Here, we show that loading such a gas into a 1D optical lattice realizes the lattice counterpart of this theory, which we name the Raman anyon-Hubbard model. The Raman-dressed bosons acquire density-dependent Peierls phases when tunneling through occupied sites. For a small statistical phase, the model reduces to the anyon-Hubbard model, while beyond this limit it defines a distinct lattice anyon model with unbounded rather than periodic statistical interactions. Numerically, we show that the dressed gas displays the defining properties of 1D anyons, namely a density-dependent asymmetry of the ground-state momentum distribution, interaction-induced asymmetric transport and chiral bound states. Remarkably, these features are readily accessible with Raman-coupled $^{39}$K atoms, opening up the experimental exploration of 1D anyons beyond the few-particle regime.

Classical control of quantum-state relaxation in confined molecular hydrogen

Nathan McLane, Richard C. Remsing, Leah G. Dodson

Quantum-state conversion between the ortho and para nuclear-spin states of H$_2$ is forbidden in the isolated molecule but these states can become connected through interactions with a surrounding environment. How that same environment governs the dynamics of the newly connected states remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate this problem using H$_2$ confined in crystalline molecular solids, where the host environment selectively enables conversion between a pair of otherwise disconnected ortho-H$_2$ and para-H$_2$ states. Rotationally resolved infrared spectroscopy directly determines the state-specific conversion dynamics in crystalline CO$_2$ and N$_2$O at 10 K, revealing that increasing H$_2$ concentration suppresses nuclear-spin conversion by nearly an order of magnitude, reaching complete suppression by 20% H$_2$ loading. Molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations identify two complementary origins of this concentration dependence: changes in the thermally populated phonon modes available to mediate relaxation, and strain-driven H$_2$ clustering that progressively removes molecules from the host crystal field responsible for enabling conversion. The resulting picture connects a classical collective response of the host to microscopic quantum-state dynamics: H$_2$ loading generates strain that reorganizes the local molecular environment, thereby modifying both the phonon bath and the molecular coupling to that bath. These results establish a multiscale mechanism by which condensed-phase environments can govern the connectivity and dynamics of an effective two-level system.

Gibbsian Entropy for General Wigner Distributions

Gabriel Rabelo-Soares, Yevgeniy Kovchegov, Giorgio Torrieri

We propose a definition of non-equilibrium Gibbsian entropy applicable to general quantum systems, computable from Wigner functions, for both Gaussian and non-Gaussian states. We show that while the Wigner function is a quasi-probability distribution, fundamental results linking observables in repeated observations to the function, such as the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem and, crucially, the relation between the entropy and the cumulant generating function continue to apply. This ideas are the core of the large deviation theory, which allow to formulate a positive-definite definition of entropy based on our ignorance of the underlying state structure after repeated measurements of cumulants. This new definition is a parallel to the Gibbs entropy formulation, but it can also be used in out-of-equilibrium regimes. Some important properties of this entropy are discussed. In addition we derive some information theory quantities such as relative entropy, conditional entropy, mutual information and Rényi entropy. We then compare the results with Wehrl entropy for the 1D harmonic oscillator, for pure and mixed states. We conclude the the work discussing potential applications of this new formalism, ranging from quantum correlations beyond entanglement to thermalization in closed quantum systems.

99.9...% of the SU(2) BFSS ground state

Alexander Michel, Jaroslav Scheinpflug, Xi Yin

We solve for the ground-state wavefunction of the SU(2) BFSS matrix model by applying the Rayleigh-Ritz method to an auxiliary operator $H_{aux}$. We show that this operator agrees with the Hamiltonian on the ground-state subspace and has a spectral gap. For SU(2) specifically, the representation theory of the singlet sector gives an astronomical reduction in the number of degrees of freedom. This allows us to construct an efficient variational basis whose inner products we compute exactly. We demonstrate the method by computing a state that satisfies rigorous bounds on its energy $E\,g_{YM}^{-2/3}\leq 4.18\times10^{-8}$ and ground-subspace overlap $\|P_{\ker H}ψ\|^2\geq 0.999997$. We compute ground-state moments with high precision, finding values consistent with the bounds from our matrix bootstrap. Additionally, the state accurately reproduces both the exponent and coefficient of the asymptotic radial probability density predicted by the three-graviton amplitude.
