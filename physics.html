Reconstruction of Dynamical Dark Energy and Gravity from latest cosmological data

Maria Luisa Gallavotti, Alessandra Silvestri

Recent cosmological observations have renewed interest in non-standard late-time evolution. We investigate whether departures from the standard expansion history are accompanied by modifications of gravitational dynamics through a joint, non-parametric reconstruction of the effective dark energy density, $Ω_{\rm X}(a)$, and the gravitational response functions $μ(a)$ and $Σ(a)$. Combining CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+, and large scale structure observations, including DES Year 3 $6\times2$pt data, we perform reconstructions with and without a theory motivated correlation prior derived from stable Horndeski models. We find a persistent maximum in $Ω_{\rm X}$ at $z\simeq0.5$--$0.7$, corresponding to a crossing of the effective phantom divide. This feature is already present in the CMB+BAO combination and remains robust when modifications to clustering and lensing are allowed. The reconstructed gravitational response exhibits suggestive enhancements in $μ$ and $Σ$ over approximately the same redshift range, although these departures are less significant. Including LSS information instead favours a reduced lensing response, $Σ<1$, while leaving the background feature largely unchanged. An interpretation within Generalized Brans-Dicke theories illustrates how modifications to the effective gravitational coupling and a scalar-mediated fifth force may contribute to these trends. Our results indicate that current departures from $Λ$CDM are most significant in the expansion history, while leaving open the possibility of correlated modifications to gravitational dynamics that future Stage IV surveys can test.

Does Dark Energy Evolve $and$ Cluster?

Maria Luisa Gallavotti, Alessandra Silvestri

Recent cosmological observations have renewed interest in dynamical dark energy, and naturally raise the question of whether departures from the standard expansion history are accompanied by modifications of gravitational dynamics. We address this question through a joint, non-parametric reconstruction of the effective dark-energy contribution to the expansion and the gravitational responses governing clustering and lensing, complemented by a combined covariance-mode analysis, without imposing model-specific relations between the sectors. Combining CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ supernova observations, and subsequently adding large scale structure observations including DES Year 3 $6\times2$pt data and other surveys, we recover a broad maximum in the effective dark-energy density near $z\simeq0.6$--$0.7$. The gravitational responses exhibit suggestive low-redshift enhancements over their GR values, approximately coincident with the background feature. Adding large scale structure information preserves the background feature but favours a mildly reduced lensing response. The combined covariance-mode analysis identifies the combinations of background and gravitational responses most tightly constrained by current observations and quantifies their departures from $Λ$CDM. The leading modes are decisively background dominated, while the gravitational sector remains less precisely constrained. Thus, present data cannot yet determine whether the suggestive low-redshift features in the two sectors share a common physical origin, a question that ongoing Stage-IV surveys will be able to address

From pure spinor modules to superspace superfields

Ondrej Hulik, Raphael Senghaas

We relate the pure spinor formalism to the superspace description of constrained superfields and gauge multiplets, mainly for abelian two-dimensional N=(2,2) supersymmetry, with three- and four-dimensional N=1 examples for comparison. For each multiplet we give an explicit module over the supertranslation Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra and match its reconstruction with the known superfields. First-order constraints come from modules supported on linear subspaces of the nilpotence variety, and higher-order ones, such as the complex-linear constraint, from Yoneda products of their Ext classes. Requiring a connection to act on given matter and its conjugate restricts its curvature, which yields the ordinary, twisted and semichiral vector multiplets with their field strengths. The module of each vector multiplet is the fiber product over the origin of the coordinate rings of the matter supports: its potentials are differences of partial potentials, and its gauge parameters are the matter superfields. For the large vector multiplet this fiber product is not a quotient of the pure spinor ring; its field strengths are four odd semichiral superfields, and partial regluing gives its constrained versions.

Real-time simulation of scattering in $φ^4$ theory: localized excitation, scalable wave packet preparation, and peak-generating artifacts

Boyu Gao, Natalie Klco

Probing real-time dynamics is essential for understanding non-perturbative scattering and interactions in quantum field theory. A common challenge in real-time simulation is preparing accurate asymptotic incoming states. Through efficiently solvable eigenvalue problems, we jointly optimize a superposition of single particle momentum eigenstates and a single site interpolating operator to maximize preparation fidelity from the interacting vacuum. This leads to a localized excitation in the interacting theory analogous to a position eigenstate. Weighted sums of the resulting interpolating operator over lattice sites prepare wave packets of arbitrary spatial extent. To ensure that this construction produces the correct continuum limit, relative phases among single particle momentum eigenstates in this superposition are calibrated using a relation derived from Lorentz symmetry. Analyzing single particle wave packet propagation through the position space profile reveals oscillatory Airy distortions, which originate from interference between saddle point contributions at different momenta that share the same lattice group velocity. These oscillations systematically weaken when approaching the continuum limit, and reliable regimes are identified as a function of simulation time and wave packet momentum profile. Two-particle scattering simulations in interacting scalar field theory are also implemented, revealing artificial inelasticity arising from coarse local field digitization or deviation from the Nyquist-Shannon sampling point. With these artifacts under control, present simulations provide emerging evidence for physical inelastic processes in $φ^4$ theory. The constructions introduced in this work may provide new understanding of state preparation for the quantum simulation of scattering.

Geometric Quantization of the Giant Graviton Master Moduli Space

Kasia Budzik, Davide Gaiotto

We describe a remarkable reorganization of the space of gauge invariant operators of free or twisted $U(N)$ gauge theories as combinations of single-trace and determinant operators modulo certain "open loop equations". The reorganization has a transparent holographic interpretation, describing how states at finite $N$ can be reproduced by imposing certain $N$-independent gauge constraints on the combination of the closed strings Fock space and a geometric quantization of the moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes, with quantization parameter $N^{-1}$. The moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes is reproduced from tachyon condensation of auxiliary D-brane/anti-D-brane pairs which are "Koszul dual" to the D-brane system which supports the original gauge theory. We describe how to impose the open loop equations via certain BRST complexes and conjecture that their homology recovers the finite $N$ BPS Hilbert space in the limit of large auxiliary rank. The BRST construction conjecturally categorifies Murthy's Giant Graviton Expansion of indices counting $U(N)$-invariant BPS operators.

The Spectral Anatomy of Complex-Time Singularities

Samuel Valach, Mile Vrbica

The properties of the bulk spacetime in a holographic theory may be probed by the analytic structure of the boundary correlator $G(t,x)$. In this letter, we analyse the singularities of $G(t,x)$ by deriving them from Fourier space. We show that the quasinormal modes are characterised by two distinct momentum scaling regimes. The first regime is defined by a finite $k/ω^γ$ for some characteristic exponent $γ$ which is extracted from the singularity, and probes $G(t,0)$, reproducing the bouncing geodesic analysis. The second regime is defined by a finite $k/ω$, and probes the complexified bulk away from the singularity, which allows us to study the singularities of $G(t,x\neq 0)$. We extract coefficients and exponents of the singularities for general spacetime dimensions, and allow for more exotic interiors. The heavy reliance of our computation on quasinormal modes opens the doors to a similar analysis being done on spacetimes that are accessible only numerically.

Parity conservation of the isotropic galaxy 4PCF in redshift space

Chanasorn Kongprachaya, Nicola Bartolo, Daniele Bertacca, Sabino Matarrese

Recent observational investigation of cosmological Parity Violation (PV) through the galaxy Four-point Correlation Function (4PCF) has motivated a careful theoretical examination of potential systematic contributions. In this work, we demonstrate that parity-odd contributions to the isotropic 4PCF estimator induced by Redshift Space Distortion (RSD), up to the leading order $\mathcal{H}/k$ of general relativistic corrections, vanish upon averaging over all the Line-of-Sight (LOS). We analyse the perturbative relativistic galaxy number density, combining the biased tracer expansion and the dark matter gravitational dynamics within the framework of Standard Eulerian Perturbation Theory (EPT) to third order in the density perturbation. Building on prior studies of the parity-odd mode of the trispectrum (the Fourier-space counterpart of the 4PCF), we extend existing results in three directions: incorporating the wide-angle effect by adopting a local LOS definition for each galaxy in the 4PCF, accounting for tidal bias in the galaxy population, and generalising the assessment to an arbitrary cosmological background. Expanding the 4PCF in spherical harmonics, we show that angular momentum selection rules, encoded in the Wigner 3-j symbols, enforce that only parity-even contributions survive the integration over all LOS orientations, both in the plane-parallel approximation and in the full wide-angle case. This result is crucial for large-scale statistical analyses using isotropic estimators. It supports interpreting possible PV detections as a signal not due to a systematic RSD artefact. We conclude with a discussion of the caveats of our results and identify potential sources of PV.

Complex fluid flow dynamics by bright scattershot boson sampling

Robert Schade, Shailendra K. Rathor, Tim J. Bartley, Benjamin Brecht, Christian Plessl et al. (9 authors)

Boson sampling machines have become a fruitful tool for establishing new concepts, which connect fundamental physics with computer science applications. Here, we exploit the intrinsically high-dimensional correlations generated by the measurement outcomes of a multiphoton boson sampling machine and demonstrate how a reconfigurable architecture can be programmed to autonomously model the time evolution of nonlinear dynamical systems that describe complex fluid flows. By extending on the original scattershot boson sampling (SBS) scheme to include higher photon number components -- a scheme which we label bright scattershot boson sampling (BSBS) --- this scheme overcomes scaling issues inherent to SBS-based schemes by utilizing all randomly generated input photon number distributions. We illustrate the capability of this scheme for two complex fluid-flow models: an eight-dimensional Lorenz-type model of two-dimensional thermally driven convection and a 16-dimensional model of global atmospheric circulation. Using a digital twin of the BSBS architecture based on multiphoton statistics, we show that the photonic approach accurately reproduces the temporal dynamics of both systems while being resilient to photon losses of up to 50 per cent. We also verified that the BSBS case outperforms a comparable run with coherent light. Moreover, its prediction horizon exceeds that of a classical reservoir-computing model with the same query complexity. These results highlight the potential of near-term photonic quantum computing for addressing relevant real-world tasks on noisy intermediate-scale devices.

Agentic AI for Discovering Fault-Tolerant Quantum Circuits

Aritra Lahiri, Florian Marquardt

Scaling quantum computing toward practical quantum advantage is currently limited by the efficiency of circuits that operate on information encoded by quantum error-correcting codes. The full stack of quantum computing encompasses several tasks: preparation of logical code states, implementation of logical gates, logical routines on encoded qubits, etc. Yet, these circuits are largely designed by hand for individual cases, with some recent works incorporating machine learning for certain aspects. We formulate this problem as agentic evolutionary program synthesis, wherein a large language model, leveraging its pretraining knowledge and tool-use proficiency, and guided by a circuit verifier, evolves a program that defines a Clifford circuit for a wide range of curated tasks such as logical state preparation, logical gate design, and logical routines such as code-switching, code growth, etc. Our approach consistently finds fault-tolerant circuits for all of these tasks, each verified by an exhaustive check over faults.

Solution of the Qubit Marginal Problem from Fermionic Representability

David A. Mazziotti

Whether local reduced states are compatible with a global quantum state---the quantum marginal problem---is central to quantum information and simulation. Here we present a solution to the ensemble marginal problem for qubits based on the representability theory of fermions. Encoding each qubit as one fermion in two orbitals, we prove that the representable qubit marginals form an exposed face of the set of $N$-representable fermionic reduced density matrices, selected by the vanishing of every site-occupation variance. Complete hierarchies of fermionic representability conditions therefore transfer exactly to qubits. Applications to frustrated Heisenberg and transverse-field Ising rings show that the $(2,3)$-positivity conditions are exact at the Majumdar--Ghosh point and accurate to about 0.5% at criticality. Because the conditions hold for any quantum state, they also provide a framework for mitigating measurement errors on qubit-based quantum devices.
