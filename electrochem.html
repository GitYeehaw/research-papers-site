Energy- and momentum-resolved Cooper-pair emission from a cuprate superconductor

Henry Amir, Jack Zwettler, Ka Ho Wong, Genda Gu, Rafael M. Fernandes et al. (8 authors)

Superconductivity arises from Cooper pairing, but conventional photoemission probes electrons individually rather than as pairs. Here we report the observation of Cooper-pair emission from an optimally doped cuprate superconductor using two-electron angle-resolved photoemission (2e-ARPES). Below the superconducting transition, we observe a distinct two-electron emission peak at zero pair binding energy and zero pair momentum that disappears in the normal state, revealing the characteristic fingerprint of a Cooper pair. Unexpectedly, the joint energy distribution broadens markedly along the relative-energy axis as temperature decreases, while the total energy width remains nearly unchanged. This anomalous behavior cannot be explained by instrumental resolution, static disorder or thermal broadening, and instead points to scattering processes involving collective excitations of the superconductor. Our results establish 2e-ARPES as a probe of both Cooper-pair emission and the many-body dynamics of the superconducting state.

Classical control of quantum-state relaxation in confined molecular hydrogen

Nathan McLane, Richard C. Remsing, Leah G. Dodson

Quantum-state conversion between the ortho and para nuclear-spin states of H$_2$ is forbidden in the isolated molecule but these states can become connected through interactions with a surrounding environment. How that same environment governs the dynamics of the newly connected states remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate this problem using H$_2$ confined in crystalline molecular solids, where the host environment selectively enables conversion between a pair of otherwise disconnected ortho-H$_2$ and para-H$_2$ states. Rotationally resolved infrared spectroscopy directly determines the state-specific conversion dynamics in crystalline CO$_2$ and N$_2$O at 10 K, revealing that increasing H$_2$ concentration suppresses nuclear-spin conversion by nearly an order of magnitude, reaching complete suppression by 20% H$_2$ loading. Molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations identify two complementary origins of this concentration dependence: changes in the thermally populated phonon modes available to mediate relaxation, and strain-driven H$_2$ clustering that progressively removes molecules from the host crystal field responsible for enabling conversion. The resulting picture connects a classical collective response of the host to microscopic quantum-state dynamics: H$_2$ loading generates strain that reorganizes the local molecular environment, thereby modifying both the phonon bath and the molecular coupling to that bath. These results establish a multiscale mechanism by which condensed-phase environments can govern the connectivity and dynamics of an effective two-level system.

Reversible Interchain Associations Dominate Plateau Elasticity in Conjugated Polymers

Juncheng Zheng, Xiaobing Zuo, Xiaodan Gu, Shiwang Cheng

The modulus plateau is widely regarded as a hallmark of topological entanglement in homopolymer melts and has been used to identify entanglement in semiflexible conjugated polymers. Using regiorandom poly(3-alkylthiophenes) as a model system, we show that their plateau elasticity relaxes through an Arrhenius process decoupled from segmental and terminal dynamics, reorganizes persistently under mechanical perturbation, and varies systematically with side-chain length. These behaviors are hard to reconcile with a purely topological-entanglement picture and instead support a transient network dominated by reversible interchain associations, consistent with π-π interactions between conjugated backbone segments. The results thus demonstrate that plateau elasticity alone does not uniquely identify topological entanglement in conjugated polymers. More broadly, they identify the strength and lifetime of interchain association as molecular design parameters linking backbone packing to stress relaxation, viscoelastic properties, and processability in conjugated polymers, complementing their established role in electronic structure and charge transport.

Exciton design via topological spin textures

Karim Rezouali, Selcuk Sözeri, Samir Lounis

Excitons carry energy and information through a semiconductor without carrying charge. This makes them attractive for optoelectronics, but also hard to guide. Magnetic skyrmions, on the other hand, are moved by small electrical currents. Here we develop a theory of excitons interacting with a skyrmion in which the electron and the hole are treated as distinct constituents, each with its own spin--orbit coupling. We find that the topology of the skyrmion is transferred to the exciton only when the exchange coupling exceeds the relevant spin--orbit splitting. The exciton then experiences a full flux quantum, with opposite signs in the two valleys. The quantum metric of the spin texture creates a ring-shaped barrier that traps excitons, whereas the topological charge fixes the angular momentum of the trapped states and hence their optical selection rules. Because the trap moves with the skyrmion, excitons can be transported by current pulses, and small skyrmions accommodate a single exciton, which opens the door for potential hybrid applications in future information technology intertwining topological magnetic bits with optical excitations.

Time-gated optical-parametric-amplification and field-resolved detection of mid-infrared free-induction decay signals

Sergio Revuelta, Gerard Faustino, Ka Fai Mak, Ferenc Krausz, Nicholas Karpowicz

Infrared spectroscopy is a key tool to study complex structural dynamics in molecular systems. However, resolving weak or broad vibrational modes is challenging due to their rapid decay. Here, we demonstrate a technique to significantly extend the detectable lifetime - and thereby spectral resolution - of molecular signatures by employing synchronized time-gated optical parametric amplification of mid-infrared free-induction decay signals. Utilizing a Cr:ZnS laser system and a nonlinear ZnGeP2 crystal, we achieve a 10-fold increase in the electric field strength of the infrared molecular signal, yielding a two-orders of magnitude enhancement in dynamic range and a 2-fold increase in the signal-to-noise ratio. Using isopropyl alcohol as a model system, we demonstrate that OPA-enhanced detection extends the observable window of vibrational modes up to 72 %. This approach allows for the reconstruction of high-fidelity molecular fingerprints that resolve complex vibrational relaxation pathways and weak structural dynamics, previously hidden within the background and noise, providing a pathway for new high-sensitivity and time-resolved studies of complex chemical structures.

Theory of Optical and Magneto-Optical Properties of [111]-Oriented Site-Controlled Quantum Dots

N. D. O'Connor, G. Juska, E. Pelucchi, S. Schulz

Site-controlled [111]-oriented quantum dots (QDs) are promising sources of non-classical light, yet their optical spectra exhibit apparently conflicting symmetry signatures. At zero magnetic field, excitonic spectra are commonly interpreted as $C_{2v}$-like, whereas measurements in Faraday geometry display selection rules characteristic of $C_{3v}$-like symmetry. Here, we show that both regimes can be described consistently without invoking any change in the underlying structural symmetry. Combining 8-band $\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}$ theory, a self-consistent Hartree-informed configuration interaction scheme, and group theoretical analysis, we introduce a controlled symmetry reducing perturbation that lowers the QD symmetry to $C_s$. At zero field, the optical response remains predominantly $C_{2v}$-like because the additional low-symmetry transitions carry only weak oscillator strengths. In Faraday geometry, the same reduced-symmetry structure produces four dominant, nearly circularly polarised trion transitions, reproducing the experimentally observed $C_{3v}$-like response. By contrast, an exact $C_{2v}$ description inferred from the zero-field spectrum permits only two such trion transitions and therefore fails at finite field. Our results thus provide a unified description of the zero and finite magnetic field optical response without any restoration or enhancement of the underlying structural symmetry. More generally, we demonstrate that a higher-symmetry-like optical spectrum does not uniquely identify the exact symmetry of a QD, therefore also providing useful guidance for other QD and nanostructure systems that lie beyond the ones studied here.

Spin to Orbital Angular Momentum Conversion in the Neutron Nucleus Weak Interaction

Niels Geerits, Stephan Sponar, Hartmut Lemmel, Andrea Piovano, Kyle Steffen et al. (6 authors)

The neutron nucleus weak interaction generates a small parity non conserving rotation of the neutron spin as the neutron moves through matter. This parity-odd neutron rotary power can reach up to 2.2e-3 rad * cm-1 for slow neutrons in 139La, which possesses a p-wave resonance at 0.7 eV where the parity-odd asymmetry is greatly amplified. This spin rotation must conserve total angular momentum. Here we show that this conservation occurs through neutron orbital angular momentum generation. We experimentally demonstrate that a neutron prepared initially in a longitudinal spin-orbit state experiences no change in total angular momentum.

Experimental Evaluation of High-Throughput Structure Predictions

Gabriele D. Di Gianluca, James Grossmann, Jackson Niedel, Juan Esquivel, Molly Crossman et al. (16 authors)

High throughput computational databases such as Materials Project, the Open Quantum Materials Database, and Alexandria now each list on the order of 10^5 predicted stable compounds. However, the overall reliability of these predictions in terms of actual experimental synthesizability remains poorly understood. In this work we attempt to synthesize, via arc melting, 34 different predicted ternary compounds. The majority of these materials did not form in the predicted structure, instead resulting in disordered variants or multiphase mixtures. Four formed ordered structures not reported before, one of them at a composition reported earlier without a structure. We discuss both the failures and the successes, and identify simple filtering criteria that can help researchers avoid hypothetical materials that are unlikely to form.

Inversion symmetry breaking by interlayer hybridization in a tetralayer van der Waals heterostructure

Juan C. Arias-Muñoz, Cuiju Yu, Nianze Shang, Christopher P. Edwards, Zixuan Ning et al. (13 authors)

Inversion symmetry breaking unlocks a broad range of physical phenomena fundamental for modern technologies, motivating efforts to engineer noncentrosymmetric materials. In this context, monolayer transition metal dichalcogenides, which naturally lack inversion symmetry, have emerged as a versatile platform for valley physics, strong excitonic effects, and nonlinear optics. However, in their hexagonal stacking phase, inversion symmetry is restored in even-layer stacks and bulk form, suppressing these effects and limiting applications. In this work, inversion symmetry breaking is demonstrated in a vertically stacked tetralayer heterostructure assembled from two intrinsically centrosymmetric bilayer flakes, as evidenced by second harmonic generation measurements with an intensity comparable to that of their constituent monolayer. Spectroscopic measurements and theoretical estimations indicate that effective symmetry breaking in the tetralayer heterostructure is driven by interlayer hybridization, giving rise to pronounced electronic and phononic signatures. This symmetry breaking further induces polar anisotropy, with an intensity ratio reaching 2.36. We propose that asymmetric contributions from excitons to the second harmonic optical dipole may influence this behavior. These findings establish interlayer coupling as a critical mechanism for introducing inversion symmetry breaking and tuning the optical response of van der Waals heterostructures, providing a powerful strategy for photonics and optoelectronics in two-dimensional materials.

Cavity-QED Molecular Dynamics: An eT-i-PI Interface for Ab Initio Cavity Born-Oppenheimer Dynamics and Joint Nuclear-Photon Path-Integral Sampling

Giuseppe Cassone

Simulating molecules in quantum electrodynamic (QED) cavities requires a consistent description of electronic response, nuclear motion, and electromagnetic-field fluctuations. Here, I present Cavity-QED Molecular Dynamics, an interface connecting eT and i-PI for atomistic simulations of finite molecular systems coupled to a single photon mode. The code evaluates a self-consistent cavity Born-Oppenheimer Hartree-Fock (CBO-HF) energy surface and supports classical-nuclei dynamics under canonical (NVT) and microcanonical (NVE) ensembles, as well as canonical joint nuclear-photon path-integral sampling including nuclear quantum effects (NQEs). A fixed-displacement gradient correction provides nuclear and photon forces consistent with the same energy surface as the photon coordinate evolves independently. OpenMP threading, concurrent bead evaluations, and analysis utilities support practical simulations. Water-monomer benchmarks assess both dynamical propagation and equilibrium sampling. Energy-derivative checks and NVE timestep tests demonstrate consistent force evaluation and integration, while path integral molecular dynamics (PIMD) simulations with PILE-L thermostatting reproduce analytic finite-bead photon second moments, including their centroid and internal contributions, within sampling uncertainties. At the tested parameters, cavity coupling produces a small but statistically resolved increase in photon fluctuations, while changes in mean molecular geometry remain within sampling uncertainty. The resulting Cavity-QED Molecular Dynamics framework enables controlled investigations of how coupled quantum fluctuations of matter and the electromagnetic field influence molecular equilibrium properties within the CBO-HF approximation, providing a foundation for future studies of cavity-modified hydrogen bonding, vibrational spectra, and molecular polaritonic phenomena.
