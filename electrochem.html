Ionic switching between supermagnetic states

Sumaiyatul Ahsan, Yin-Ju Yen, Andrew H. Comstock, Tanya A. Balandin, Jason D. Azoulay et al. (8 authors)

Supermagnetic states are typically set during synthesis. Changing and retaining these states requires sustained external stimulus. Without resynthesis or continued stimulus, we show, for the first time, a reversible magneto-ionic switching between superferromagnetic (SFM) and superspin-glass (SSG) states using room-temperature Li-ion transport. We utilize the incomplete lithiation of NiO into nanoscale Ni and Li2O to switch between the states. Lithiation generates a fragmented nano-Ni network that strengthens dipolar interactions and promotes SFM order with stronger memory and higher room-temperature moments. Delithiation partially regenerates NiO and drives the system into an SSG state with higher low-temperature coercivity and sharper temperature-selective memory. The interconversion between SFM and SSG persists at all measured subsequent lithiation-delithiation cycles, creating a general framework for supermagnetic switching.

Orbital Angular Momentum Currents and Textures in a Multiorbital Double Helix

Danny Cordova, Edwin Vásconez, Ernesto Medina

The microscopic origin of chirality induced spin selectivity (CISS) in biomolecular structures has traditionally been discussed in terms of spin orbit coupling and spin angular momentum. However, orbital angular momentum (OAM) is increasingly recognized as a more direct electronic response to molecular chirality. A helical hopping network built only from s orbitals can support an itinerant orbital moment and an orbital Edelstein response, but such a basis has no local atomic OAM and therefore omits the complementary contribution arising from coherence among active p orbitals. Here we construct a six-band multiorbital double helix model parameterized by characteristic B form DNA geometry and hopping scales. The model incorporates major minor groove asymmetry, an axial base pair offset representing stagger, and compositional inequivalence between the two strands, providing structural control parameters with which the orbital response can be tuned. Inter strand hybridization generates a full three component atomic OAM texture in the corotating local helical frame, including a radial component absent in the corresponding single helix model. We separately evaluate the itinerant OAM using the Bloch wave packet formulation of orbital magnetization and identify an additional inter-strand circulation channel unique to the double-helix connectivity. The resulting atomic and itinerant responses have distinct microscopic origins, magnitudes, and temperature dependences, establishing a multiorbital framework for geometry controlled angular momentum generation and transport in chiral molecular conductors.

Active Learning for Interatomic Potentials: A Review of Uncertainty-Driven Data Efficiency

Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander V. Shapeev, Alexander G. Kvashnin

Developing a machine learning interatomic potential (MLIP) that is accurate for the specific conditions under which it will be applied, rather than simply for the predefined configurations chosen by the model developer, is a significant challenge in atomistic simulation. Active learning (AL) addresses this challenge by closing the loop between candidate generation, data selection, reference calculation, and model updating. Here, we survey more than seventy studies, with an emphasis on work from 2020 to 2026, to understand how active learning has evolved from a niche, on-the-fly training technique for query-by-committee and Gaussian process force fields into a diverse family of data-efficient strategies. he reviewed examples include on-the-fly molecular dynamics, active data selection for fine-tuning pretrained models, kernel- and representation-based acquisition using frozen pretrained models, and reaction-aware sampling for chemical systems far from equilibrium. We explore the use of active learning in elemental and alloy mechanics, hydrogen and ion diffusion, ferroelectrics and effective Hamiltonians of functional materials, reactive and photochemical dynamics, high-entropy and compositionally disordered materials, as well as generative and autonomous material discovery pipelines. Drawing on a growing body of critical, methodologically self-aware literature, we also show that this achievement carries systematic caveats. Uncertainty estimates are not interchangeable across model architectures and are frequently the least reliable exactly where they matter most. We close by discussing the outlook for standardized, uncertainty-calibrated, and observable-targeted active-learning workflows in the foundation-model era.

Engineering nonrelativistic spin splitting in semiconductors by altermagnetic proximity

Sing Lam Wong, Marcel Franz

Altermagnets combine vanishing net magnetization with momentum-dependent, nonrelativistic spin splitting, offering functionalities attractive for spintronics and quantum devices. Their practical use, however, is constrained by the limited availability of materials that simultaneously possess the required altermagnetic symmetry, semiconducting electronic structure, and weak spin-orbit coupling. Here we show that these requirements can instead be separated between constituent materials through proximity engineering. We develop an analytical theory for a conventional semiconductor coupled to a gapped $d$-wave altermagnet and demonstrate that virtual tunneling transfers the altermagnetic spin splitting to the semiconductor. Crucially, the induced nonrelativistic and relativistic spin splittings exhibit qualitatively different dependences on band alignment. Virtual processes involving the valence and conduction bands of the altermagnet can reinforce the altermagnetic splitting while contributing with opposite signs to the interfacial Rashba coupling. Consequently, electrostatic tuning can access a regime in which the Rashba splitting vanishes while a sizable $d$-wave spin splitting persists. Exact diagonalization of the full heterostructure model confirms the analytical theory and establishes the robustness of this mechanism beyond the perturbative regime. Our results identify altermagnetic proximity as a route for engineering nonrelativistic spin-split electronic states in established semiconductor platforms, providing a general strategy for disentangling magnetic and relativistic spin interactions in quantum heterostructures.

Temperature and Thickness Dependent Spin Current Transmission through Epitaxial Antiferromagnetic α-Fe2O3

Koushik Prabhakara, Rajeev Nepal, Subhashree Chatterjee, Rabindra Basnet, Ezana Negusse et al. (6 authors)

Antiferromagnetic insulator α-Fe2O3 is a potential medium for coherent motion of spin waves over relatively large distances, allowing non-dissipative transfer of information between circuit elements. However, the structural defects and temperature dependent spin reorientations in this oxide impact the propagation. Understanding how the magnetic state of an antiferromagnetic insulator affects spin-current transmission is therefore important for elucidating the mechanisms governing spin transport. Here, we report spin-current transmission through epitaxial α-Fe2O3 thin films sandwiched between Permalloy and Pt. Using spin pumping and inverse spin Hall effect, we observe a finite symmetric dc voltage across the Pt layer over the temperature range 1.6 to 300 K for alpha-Fe2O3 thicknesses of 0, 30, 40, and 100 nm. The symmetric voltage coefficient Ks exhibits two approximately linear temperature regimes, separated by a characteristic temperature T* whose value depends on the thickness of the α-Fe2O3 layer. Further, the dc magnetization measurements on some of the samples suggest that the T* is correlated with the spin reorientation transition (Morin transition) in α-Fe2O3. Interestingly, a significantly larger magnitude of dKs/dT is noted below the characteristic temperature T*. The observed thickness and temperature dependent spin-current transmission indicates a sensitivity to the magnetic state of the insulating hematite layer.

Limits from nonlinear loss of spin-orbit-torque control of magnon transport

Lerato Takana, Katya Mikhailova, Charles Zheng, Sauviz Alaei, Xiangcheng Liu et al. (10 authors)

Controlling magnon currents is central to the development of magnonic devices, which exploit spin excitations for information transport without charge flow. Here, we use the spin-orbit torque (SOT) from a Pt gate to control the effective magnon damping in three-terminal devices fabricated from low-damping lithium aluminum ferrite (LAFO) thin films and identify the nonlinear damping that ultimately limits the gate response. With increasing gate current, the nonlocal magnon signal initially increases, but saturates and subsequently decreases above a critical gate current density. Brillouin light scattering microscopy reveals that this turnover coincides with the emergence of an SOT-driven auto-oscillation mode localized beneath the gate, whose frequency continuously redshifts with increasing gate current, characteristic of large-amplitude nonlinear dynamics, and with a suppression of the propagating magnon population away from the gate. Micromagnetic simulations reproduce the observed nonmonotonic gate response only when an amplitude-dependent effective damping is included, indicating that nonlinear losses, rather than saturation of spin injection, limit the degree of magnon modulation. Our results show that SOT compensation of magnon damping is ultimately limited by nonlinear damping attributed to auto-oscillations, providing a framework for understanding the limits of magnon current modulation in magnetic insulators.

Analysis of Transient Microscopy Data in Reciprocal Space

Thomas J. Sheehan, William A. Tisdale

Transient microscopy has evolved to include a family of well-established techniques for studying the spatiotemporal dynamics of excited states in a variety of materials systems. In these methods, a laser pulse is focused to locally excite a material, and the subsequent evolution of the excited species is visualized to extract transport parameters such as diffusivities. Typically, transport coefficients are extracted from experimental datasets using curve-fitting methods to analyze the change in the mean squared displacement (MSD) of the spatial profiles over time. However, this approach can lead to inaccurate or misleading diffusivity values under many common experimental conditions, and is only formally accurate for the case of a single diffusing species. In this work, we introduce a new approach to the analysis of transient microscopy data based on the evolution of profile shapes in reciprocal space. We use simulated datasets to quantitatively analyze the performance of our proposed methods and compare them to current state-of-the-art analysis techniques. We demonstrate that reciprocal-space-based analysis methods have the highest overall performance in terms of accuracy and precision for normal diffusive transport over a range of realistic initial conditions and signal-to-noise ratios. Furthermore, we highlight the limitations of using MSD alone to characterize anomalous transport in materials, and we show how analysis of spatial frequencies can yield a more comprehensive understanding of complex excited-state dynamics and their underlying physical mechanisms. Our results show that reciprocal-space-based analysis methods are powerful tools for characterizing and quantifying many different types of transport behaviors.

Learning the self-energy through equivariant neural networks

Wudong Shen, Yang Zhong, Min-Ye Zhang, Xinyang Dong, Hongjun Xiang et al. (6 authors)

The electronic self-energy determines the single-particle Green's function and, through it, quasiparticle energies and spectral functions, but computing it from first principles is expensive and it is a difficult machine-learning target: complex-valued, matrix-valued in a local-orbital basis, and dependent on a continuous frequency. We predict the dynamical correlation part of the imaginary-frequency G_0W_0 self-energy matrix of molecules from atomic species and coordinates with an E(3)-equivariant message-passing network, the rotational covariance of the orbital-space matrix being imposed by the architecture itself and the static exchange part taken from the density-functional reference. Frequency enters either as an explicit network input or through frequency-specific output heads, and the predicted matrices are continued to the real axis in a representation in which causality and a non-negative spectral density hold by construction. The frequency-conditioned model reproduces matrix elements to below 1 meV on the imaginary axis for the systems studied here, and that accuracy survives the continuation: for water clusters, including a tetramer absent from the training set, and for chemically diverse molecules from QM9, the reconstructed spectral functions follow the G_0W_0 reference peak by peak and the frontier quasiparticle levels are recovered to a few tens of meV. Learning the self-energy on the imaginary axis is therefore a practical route to real-frequency spectra: once the model is trained, and given the density-functional reference it is built on, a new geometry costs no explicit G_0W_0 calculation.

Scalable long-range machine learning interatomic potentials with electrical response for atomistic material modeling

Yicheng Chen, Xiaoyun Yu, Hyejeong Song, Xintian Wang, Zihui Li et al. (8 authors)

Accurate atomistic simulations of materials under electric fields require long-range electrostatics and electrical-response modeling at length scales beyond the reach of conventional first-principles methods. Yet these capabilities remain difficult to reconcile with the scalability of machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs). Strictly local models enable efficient spatial parallelization but neglect long-range interactions, whereas existing electrostatic extensions introduce global dependencies that hinder distributed computation. Here, we overcome this limitation by analytically separating the long-range electrostatic forces and Born effective charges (BECs) into a global electrostatics calculation and strictly local derivatives of learned latent charges. This formulation preserves the spatial decomposability of equivariant MLIPs while enabling distributed evaluation of energies, forces, stresses, and BECs across different boundary conditions. Implemented as DPA4C-LR on the \texttt{nvalchemi} platform, the framework scales to 5.2 million atoms on four GPUs at approximately twice the computational cost of its short-range backbone. Trained on energies and forces, DPA4C-LR accurately infers BECs and reproduces the field-dependent infrared spectra of water. We further demonstrate its capabilities by recovering the characteristic slim polarization hysteresis of relaxor \ce{Pb(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O3}, which is obscured by finite-size effects in smaller simulations; predicting a room-temperature field-driven ionic conductivity of $6.8 \pm 0.7$ mS/cm in amorphous \ce{Li_{0.9}NbO_{0.9}Cl_{4.1}}; and modeling interfacial thermal transport across Si/diamond interfaces with slab-corrected electrostatics. By combining long-range physics, electrical-response inference, and distributed computation, this framework extends predictive atomistic modeling toward device-relevant length scales.

Grain boundary strength and corrosion cracking susceptibility in TiN coatings under fission product segregation

Jia-Hong Ke, Rongjie Song

During fuel-cladding chemical interaction (FCCI), lanthanide fission products generated in the fuel can migrate into cladding grain boundaries (GBs), where segregation may reduce GB strength and promote corrosion cracking. We use first-principles segregation, decohesion, and bonding calculations to quantify Nd and Ce effects on six coincident-site lattice GBs in bcc Fe and TiN. For each material, segregation energies, work-of-separation values for complete interfacial cleavage, and traction-separation curves measuring ideal resistance to crack opening were evaluated within a consistent computational framework. Nd and Ce segregate favorably to both materials, and the median segregation energies are more negative in TiN, showing that TiN does not simply exclude these species from internal interfaces. The mechanical responses of Fe and TiN differ sharply. In Fe, Nd and Ce consistently reduce work of separation and peak traction, with median work-of-separation retentions of 0.72 and 0.65 and median peak-traction retentions of 0.47 and 0.43. In TiN, most decorated GBs preserve much of the clean response, with median work-of-separation retentions of 1.00 and 0.97 and median peak-traction retention of 0.85 for both fission products. Bonding analysis shows weak, occupied antibonding X-Fe replacement bonds in Fe, whereas the surrounding Ti-N network remains largely preserved after Nd/Ce segregation in TiN. These results provide atomistic support for the use of TiN and related nitride coatings to reduce FCCI damage by retaining GB strength if fission products segregate to coating internal interfaces. This retention of GB strength also provides a mechanistic descriptor for assessing coating susceptibility to FCCI-assisted corrosion cracking and environmentally assisted intergranular fracture.
