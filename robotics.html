World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation

Heng Fang, Zehui Guo, Yuehan Zhang, Mingyang Du, Dingkang Liang et al. (6 authors)

Trained action policies write manipulation skill into network weights, tying what is learned to the distribution it is learned from. In-context learning (ICL) avoids this coupling but has not matched trained policies, because a demonstration recorded in another scene carries no feedback from the world the robot currently faces. We observe that what an agent learns in context is semantic, recording task steps and object roles rather than visual appearance, so experience gathered in one world transfers to another. Based on this insight, we propose World In-Context Learning (WICL), a paradigm in which a frozen model starts from a demonstration, rehearses the task in a world such as a simulator, and writes the feedback into its context rather than its weights. We build RoboScript, a modular infrastructure that implements WICL for agentic manipulation. From a single demonstration, RoboScript learns each task through rehearsal in simulation, freezes the learned knowledge as a Task Memory, and deploys the frozen memory on new scenes with data isolation that ensures every deployment input is available on a real robot. On all 50 tasks of RoboTwin 2.0, RoboScript reaches 83.4% mean success across clean and randomized settings with one demonstration per task and no gradient updates, exceeding the strongest trained action policy by over 20 points. The method generalizes across visual distributions, improves through reflection, scales with the foundation model, and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot through cross-embodiment memory adaptation. With a world in the loop, a robot can learn a manipulation task in its context as well as it learns one in its weights. All code is available at https://github.com/ArcheOne-AI/RoboScript

SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment

Yu Zhou, Xinyuan Xia, Xuankun Yang, Yinghao Yang, Wendi Chen et al. (11 authors)

World action models jointly predict video and actions conditioned on language. Existing studies focus primarily on architectures and training mechanisms, typically treating language-video alignment acquired through video pretraining as an established foundation. However, introducing action learning may impair this alignment. Principled guidance on data and supervision design for maintaining language-video alignment during action learning remains limited. To investigate this, we curate a pool of approximately 47,000 hours of general videos, egocentric videos, UMI demonstrations, and robot demonstrations. Building on this, we systematically study how language-video supervision, language-video-action supervision, and the joint use of these two forms affect WAM performance. In particular, we examine how language annotation quality affects pretrained language-video capabilities, and how action supervision from UMI and robot demonstrations extends these capabilities to language-video-action modeling. To better scale the model, we further conduct detailed studies on joint training recipe and model architecture. Under a fixed training budget, mixing egocentric videos and UMI demonstrations improves control generalization. Model architecture affects how effectively these experiences are used, while scaling pretraining under the selected configuration brings further gains. The resulting recipe, post-trained on RoboTwin Clean demonstrations only, achieves success rates of 88.10% and 61.56% under Easy (Clean-to-Clean) and Hard (Clean-to-Random) conditions, respectively. Our model achieves 40.00% SR across three real-world tasks, compared with 23.33% for the strongest baseline, indicating better generalization under unseen conditions. We release the curated data, training code, and model weights.

Distributed Global Pose Synchronization for Heterogeneous Multiple Rigid Body Systems on SE(3)

Fengyu Lin, Miaomiao Wang, Housheng Su, Zhigang Zeng, Abdelhamid Tayebi

This paper addresses the problem of global pose synchronization for heterogeneous multiple rigid-body systems evolving on the matrix Lie group $\SE(3)$. We first construct a new potential function on $\SE(3) \times \mathbb{R}$ with a generalized non-diagonal weighting matrix, together with a set of switching variables governed by continuous-discrete hybrid dynamics. Based on this new potential function and these switching variables, we propose a distributed hybrid feedback control scheme that uses relative pose on $\SE(3)$ and group velocity measurements, endowed with global asymptotic stability guarantees. We then further propose a distributed velocity-free hybrid feedback control scheme that uses relative and absolute pose measurements, without requiring group velocity measurements, while preserving global asymptotic stability. Numerical simulations are presented to demonstrate the performance of the proposed distributed hybrid control schemes.

DreamLander: A 6-DOF World Model based Reinforcement Learning Planetary Lander

George Nehma, Patrick Quinn, Madhur Tiwari, Camilo Riano-Rios

Soft powered descent landings are becoming evermore important in the field of space exploration and scientific advancement, because of their ability to deliver mission critical payloads, both highly accurately and safely. The high efficiency restriction on planetary landings due to the severely limited fuel supply results in the need for optimal guidance trajectories and control. Model based reinforcement learning is proving to be an important tool in simplifying the highly complex process of generating optimal guidance trajectories and control. In this work, we present DreamLander, a state of the art model based reinforcement learning algorithm based on DreamerV3, to generate optimal guidance and control for a realistic 6 degree of freedom entry descent landing problem. The model based approach creates a sample efficient and highly generalizable model that can be applied to a variety of scenarios whilst maintaining a high degree of accuracy, landing within 2 meters of the designated landing zone, and with velocity norm less than 0.3 meters per second. DreamLander makes use of state sensor observations, making progress toward End-to-End solutions of the Entry Descent Landing problem.

Safe Performance-aware Online Input Design

Diyou Liu, Mohammad Khosravi

In this paper, we propose an input design framework for the safe online excitation and identification of uncertain linear systems without requiring informative initial data. The objective is to actively excite the system and collect sufficiently informative input-output data while maintaining safe operation and satisfactory control performance. Achieving this objective is challenging due to the potential conflict between the excitation required for learning and the safety and performance requirements, particularly when limited prior knowledge of the system is available. Inspired by the dual control principle, the proposed scheme balances exploitation for control performance with exploration for model improvement. A Bayesian learning approach is employed to update the model uncertainty online, starting from a conservative initial uncertainty set. As informative data are collected, the uncertainty set contracts, enabling progressively less conservative operation. To reduce the online computational burden, the resulting program is reformulated into a computationally tractable form. Furthermore, an online ellipsoidal terminal set with non-increasing conservatism is designed to guarantee the recursive feasibility of the proposed scheme. Numerical experiments are conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, particularly in terms of uncertainty reduction and control performance.

Simulating Vein Compressibility for Ultrasound-Guided Femoral Access: Speed, Accuracy, and Phantom Validation

Amit Manicka, Laurent Coulon, Darwin Ang, Michael Loushin, Timothy M. Kowalewski

In ultrasound-guided femoral access, probe pressure deforms the tissue and changes the vascular anatomy a robot observes. A simulator must reproduce these mechanics fast enough for robotic interaction, yet the computational effort needed for a clinically meaningful signal like vein collapse before artery is unclear. We test the feasibility of a physics-based simulation in NVIDIA's Isaac Sim for Healthcare, with Stable Neo-Hookean tissue solved by Vertex Block Descent on one GPU. A simulation of a low-fidelity cylindrical benchtop phantom with known geometry and mechanical properties, pressed by a robot, predicted its artery within 5 percentage points at every depth. It matched the vein within 3 points through 11 mm of a 25.4 mm press and followed the collapse behavior of the low-fidelity physical phantom. Across element size, time step, and solver iterations, wall clock time per frame scaled linearly with solver work, but accuracy did not improve with more work. The chosen setting reduced solver time per frame by a factor of 976, from 29,700 ms to 30.4 ms, while remaining within 1.03 points of the finest reference setting. The physics solver alone took 30.4 ms per frame, while the full stack, including ultrasound and graphics rendering, took 56.4 ms per frame. Finally, when seeded with realistic literature-based mechanical properties, the digital leg model compressed the vein far more than the artery at every setting, reproducing the expected clinical signal of vein collapse before artery, which is used to distinguish them. In total, these results suggest that the NVIDIA Isaac Sim platform may be suitable for augmenting reinforcement learning training or online simulation for autonomous femoral access with ultrasound.

Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation

Bing Han, Yichuan Peng, Haoyu Dong, Yuanmeng Hu, Yongyi Jia et al. (19 authors)

Tactile sensing is an indispensable modality for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, a capability that hints at the ultimate frontiers of embodied intelligence. However, commercially available tactile sensors are still at a relatively early stage of development, with each type of sensor offering irreplaceable strengths alongside its own drawbacks. A key challenge is thus to free tactile understanding from sensor-specific physics, so that embodied models can effectively leverage heterogeneous tactile data. To this end, we develop LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model characterized by the following properties. (i) Our model yields a unified representation across different tactile sensors (i.e., piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile sensors), and is easily extensible to new sensor types. (ii) The resulting tactile tokens are plug-and-play: they can be seamlessly integrated into policy models across paradigms, from diffusion policy to vision-language-action (VLA) models such as $π_{0.5}$ and LingBot-VLA, consistently enhancing manipulation dexterity. (iii) Extensive experiments, spanning simulation and real-robot deployments across multiple hardware platforms, demonstrate that our tactile representation substantially benefits complex manipulation tasks while maintaining strong computational efficiency. We believe the findings in this work demonstrate the potential of leveraging cross-sensor data to scale tactile learning, and offer encouraging insights for the design of future hardware and embodied models.

Benchmarking Real-World Robotic Manipulation through Stochastic Challenges

Elias Gläßer, Gülhan Şikaroğlu, Pavlo Kupyn, Marc Pollefeys, Zuria Bauer et al. (6 authors)

A challenging bottleneck in manipulation research is reproducibility and comparability of methods in real-world environments. Since manipulation is an interactive task, it cannot be evaluated on a static dataset of observations. Benchmarking in simulation is a common remedy, but cannot cover the whole complexity of real-world tests. This paper proposes a new paradigm of stochastic challenges. Like the well-known YCB objects, it is based on reproducible real-world scenes and therefore allows system-level innovation and comparability between different platforms. Due to their stochastic nature, our challenges however, mimic the rigor and surprise of hidden test sets. To this end, we show how different robots in different labs can be compared based on their success rate over different random task samples, if these samples come from the same distribution. We propose stochastic challenges as a general paradigm for robot benchmarking and implement this paradigm in a novel benchmark for mobile manipulation, on which we compare two robots tested in two different labs.

Certified Zonotopic Reachability Analysis for Trajectory Tracking on Lie Groups

Yanliang Huang, Peng Xie, Zhen Zhang, Zhuoqi Zeng, Amr Alanwar

Autonomous vehicles track planned trajectories close to obstacles. Unknown disturbances make them deviate from these trajectories. With a bound on the tracking error that disturbances can cause, a planned trajectory can be certified as safe before execution. Existing set-based methods bound the tracking error by one invariant ellipsoid for the whole trajectory or by intervals, which are both conservative. For aerial and space vehicles, the tracking error evolves on a matrix Lie group with nonlinear dynamics. However, for group-affine systems, the error dynamics are linear in Lie algebra coordinates when the vehicle receives the inputs of the reference. The control input and the disturbance enter through the inverse right Jacobian, a matrix that depends on the error. We propagate the linear part of the error dynamics with zonotopes and enclose the effect of the inverse right Jacobian on the disturbance in closed form. The resulting tubes are tight and provably contain all reachable tracking errors in continuous time. When some error components have no input, the inverse right Jacobian leaves unmatched nonlinear terms in the closed loop. None of the tested methods certifies such a closed loop. Hybrid coordinates make these terms matched, so that our controller cancels every nonlinear term that does not contain the disturbance. Our tubes give tighter bounds than the ellipsoid method in simulations of a planar aircraft and a fully actuated multirotor and in real flights of a quadrotor, where the tube contains every measured attitude error. Under larger disturbances and the same feedback gain, the ellipsoid method yields no valid ellipsoid, whereas our tubes remain valid.

Succinct Infinite-Horizon Lyapunov and LQR Value Problems Are BQP-Complete

Mohamed Serry, Jun Liu

Fundamental tasks in linear systems analysis and control require evaluating infinite-horizon output energies for autonomous systems and optimal value functions for controlled systems. These quantities are characterized by Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati equations, respectively. In this work, we study promise problems for their evaluation when the system matrices are sparse and represented succinctly by Boolean circuits specifying their nonzero entries, with input length polynomial in the logarithm of the system dimension. For dissipative autonomous systems with coordinate-vector initial states, we define the succinct Lyapunov value problem (SLV). Its extension to linear-quadratic optimal control is the succinct LQR value problem (SLQRV). We prove that SLV is $\BQP$-hard: every problem in bounded-error quantum polynomial time ($\BQP$) reduces to it in polynomial time. We then establish that the more general SLQRV problem admits a quantum algorithm running in time polynomial in the succinct input length. Since SLV is a special case of SLQRV, both problems are $\BQP$-complete. Consequently, unless bounded-error probabilistic polynomial time coincides with $\BQP$, no classical randomized algorithm can solve either problem on all promised instances in time polynomial in the succinct input length.
