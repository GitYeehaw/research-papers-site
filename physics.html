Heavy-Heavy-Light-Light correlators from holography on the worldsheet

Matías Colaneri, Diego H. Correa, Víctor I. Giraldo-Rivera, Nicolás A. Ivanovich

We study correlation functions of light local operators and defect changing operators on the conformal line set by the 1/2 BPS Wilson line of $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang-Mills. We consider the defect changing operators associated with geometrical and internal cusp angles, which at strong coupling have a large scaling dimension and back-react on the dual $AdS_2$ world-sheet. We set up a holographic computation of correlation functions, in which the light operators are described by fluctuations around the cusped classical string solution, so that correlators of two defect changing operators and an arbitrary number of light protected operators are obtained from the on-shell action of these fluctuations. We implement this prescription in detail for the case of certain 4-point functions, with two light and two defect changing operators. Finally, since for small cusp angles the defect changing operators become light, we manage to derive relations satisfied by integrated correlators of light operators, valid at any coupling. We verify these relations at strong coupling for both internal and geometrical cusps.

Constraining the Progenitor Orbits of Sequential Black-Hole Mergers in Hierarchical Triples: Peters-Based Estimates

Felix M. Heinze, Zhoujian Cao

Two black-hole merger events may originate from sequential mergers in a pre-existing hierarchical black-hole triple if their spatial localizations overlap and the remnant mass of the first merger is compatible with a component mass of the second. In this scenario, the inner binary merges first, leaving a remnant that remains bound to the tertiary and subsequently undergoes a second merger. We present a method to constrain the progenitor triple's outer orbit by modeling the gravitational-wave-driven evolution of the remnant-tertiary binary using Peters' equations. Given an assumed upper bound on the recoil velocity of the first merger, the method requires only the black-hole masses, the time delay between the mergers, and the eccentricity at a reference frequency - quantities that can be inferred from the merger events themselves. We investigate the potential constraining power by constructing a constraint atlas spanning a range of system properties and identify the conditions under which the method places tight and reliable bounds on the progenitor triple's outer orbital parameters. By imposing an upper bound on the detector-band eccentricity, we also obtain meaningful constraints for candidate hierarchical mergers proposed in the literature, including GW190514-GW190521. We provide numerical tools for applying the method to user-specified systems and evaluating the reliability of the resulting Peters-based constraints. This framework offers a computationally inexpensive first step toward reconstructing hierarchical-merger progenitors and provides a foundation for extensions incorporating more sophisticated relativistic treatments.

Logical CCZ state cultivation

Yuta Hirasaki, Jong Yeon Lee

Logical CCZ states enable Toffoli gates, a central building block of fault-tolerant quantum arithmetic. We introduce an end-to-end protocol for direct CCZ-state preparation on bivariate tricycle and bicycle (BT/BB) codes. Our factory combines the cup-product CCZ gates native to BT codes with two novel ingredients, both enabled by engineering the logical coupling between BT and BB codes through projection-preserving lifts. Index-2 lifted BB ancillas enable selective $Z$-basis initialization, allowing the native cup-product gates to directly prepare two disjoint logical CCZ states. The prepared CCZ states are faithfully transferred to index-3 lifted BB memories with high code distance. We use decoder-based postselection to obtain high-fidelity output states with low overhead. We perform exact simulations of our factory under a circuit-level noise model motivated by neutral-atom hardware, obtaining joint infidelities of $1.84\times10^{-5}$ at $21.94\%$ acceptance and $4.10\times10^{-7}$ at $8.89\%$ acceptance for single-qubit, CZ, and CCZ error rates of $0.01\%$, $0.1\%$, and $0.5\%$, respectively. At the output-infidelity target of our smallest factory, its estimated spacetime cost per CCZ state is lower than that of $T$-state cultivation followed by CCZ conversion, providing an efficient route to logical CCZ states for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Post-Newtonian theory in de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spacetimes

R. Loganayagam, Avi Wadhwa

We develop the post-Newtonian framework for the near-zone dynamics of a binary in de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spacetime. We consider a binary interacting via a free scalar field in a fixed (A)dS background in $D+1$ spacetime dimensions, with $D\ge3$ and odd. Using multipole expansion and re-summation techniques, we solve for the field degrees of freedom to obtain an effective particle description in the form of a Fokker action. We derive the equations of motion for the binary up to second post-Newtonian (2PN) order and obtain the corresponding 2PN Hamiltonian and conserved charges in phase space. We show that the conformal algebra, corresponding to the isometry algebra of the (A)dS background, is realised in phase space as a curvature-dependent deformation of the Poincare algebra at each PN order. Our results provide a systematic extension of post-Newtonian effective dynamics to finite-curvature backgrounds. This could serve as a useful framework for studying bound-state dynamics in (A)dS as well as in the corresponding holographic CFT.

Binary-lens amplification of starlight: from magnification flares to quasi-periodic tracking of orbital evolution

Hanxi Wang, Bence Kocsis, Miguel Zumalacárregui

Supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHB) are expected at the center of galaxies as a result of galaxy mergers. The dynamics and evolution of SMBHBs shed light on the evolution and formation of galaxies, the gravitational wave background and the nature of gravity. Many methods have been proposed to identify sub-parsec SMBHBs via the detection of gravitational waves or the modulation of the emission from the surrounding gaseous environment. In a previous study, we proposed a new method of identifying SMBHBs in non-active galactic nuclei via gravitational lensing. The caustics, i.e. the source positions with a divergent magnification, exhibit a complex extended geometry which rotates with the binary period. Each caustic-crossing produces an extreme magnification by up to a factor of $10^{6}$. In this work, we generalize the previous study and describe the rich phenomenology of binary-lens amplification of starlight (BLAST). We explore the electromagnetic transients produced when accounting for the relative proper motion of the source, either a single or a binary star, the eccentricity and precession of the SMBHB, and the relative inclination. Systems where a star rapidly drifts across the caustic produce a single- or double-peaked flare. In the opposite limit, a star with no relative proper motion produces quasi-periodic lensed starlight (QPLS), i.e., repeated peaks that track the system's orbital evolution. We determine the expected magnification and peak duration in different circumstances, and calculate the magnification curves and their Fourier transforms. This study may help construct search pipelines to identify SMBHBs in electromagnetic time-domain surveys.

Cubic Quasinormal Modes in the Black-Hole Ringdown

Neev Khera, Huan Yang

We formulate and numerically solve the nonlinear Teukolsky equations through third order, obtaining a first-principles calculation of cubic quasinormal-mode amplitudes for Kerr black holes. Our approach combines lower-order metric reconstruction in split light-cone gauges with a hyperboloidal, compactified formulation solved on complex radial contours. Spin-dependent complex coupling coefficients fix the cubic amplitudes from products of their linear parent amplitudes. We apply these predictions to a nonprecessing numerical-relativity waveform, including cubic products of the $(2,2)$ fundamental mode, its first overtone, and their conjugates. The predicted cubic terms improve agreement over selected early-ringdown fitting windows and shift inferred linear-overtone amplitudes without adding fitting parameters. $3M$ after the peak of $|h_{22}|$, the summed cubic contribution reaches approximately $38\%$ of the total $h_{22}$ signal; $M$ is the initial total mass. These results identify an appreciable cubic response in early ringdown.

Quantum-Aware Key Rank for Side-Channel Attacks evaluation

Yevgen Kotukh

Side-channel attacks on block ciphers rarely recover the key outright: they leave a residual key space ordered by subkey posteriors, and evaluation laboratories report the key rank of the true key against thresholds near 2^48 and 2^60. No evaluation framework models an adversary who collects traces classically and finishes the search on a quantum computer, although quantum search with advice provides exactly that. We give such an adversary a cost model and a metric. For a device whose key has rank r, any quantum algorithm with classical access to the posteriors needs Omega(sqrt(r)) cipher evaluations, and block search achieves O(sqrt(r)); the per-instance advantage is exactly quadratic, and the super-quadratic exponents reported for search with advice are averages over the prior that apply to no single device. Under the NIST MAXDEPTH budget D and a Grover iteration of g gates and depth d, the quadratic advantage holds only for ranks up to (4D/(pi d))^2 and collapses to a constant factor beyond; with the best published AES-128 oracle and D = 2^40 the window closes near rank 2^58.6. We define the quantum-aware key rank, the classical rank of equal cost, computable from the rank estimators laboratories already run, and derive the classical rank a device must retain to keep a given threshold against each depth budget. Block search and amplitude amplification from the product-form posterior state are within a factor of two to four of each other on Hamming-weight posteriors, so the cost of coherent indexing decides between them. Experiments on simulated leakage and a toy cipher confirm the predicted window: at SNR 0.125 a depth-limited quantum adversary is worth about 22 traces to a classical one, however large its gate budget.

Intermediate memory costs of quantum channel synthesis with a protected subsystem

Kai Liu, Deguang Han

A quantum memory can preserve a subsystem perfectly while discarding information needed by a later operation. We study the total intermediate dimension required for channel synthesis when the first stage protects a $b$-dimensional subsystem and has a prescribed noncommutative graph imposing full confusability on an $a$-dimensional control register. For a finite family of classically controlled branches, exact synthesis is possible if and only if the target graph contains the prescribed graph of the first stage. For every feasible family, we construct a single processor that implements all branches exactly. The processor uses pairwise nonorthogonal pure program states in a register of dimension at most $a$. A target graph may be realizable with a carrier of dimension $b$, while synthesizing a channel with that graph can require dimension $ab$. We also characterize the closure of the attainable channels, distinguishing exact synthesis from arbitrarily accurate approximation. Finally, for a family built from the trine communication task, a four-dimensional carrier permits exact synthesis with quantum programs under output depolarization. At the same total carrier dimension and without free shared randomness, classical programs have optimal normalized diamond error $(1-η)/4$, where $0\leqη\leq1$ is the depolarization strength. For $η<1$, the gap persists under sufficiently small perturbations of the target branches.

Thermal AdS two-point functions and loop induced effective one-point vertices

Astha Kakkar

We present a study of thermal two-point functions from Witten diagrams in thermal AdS$_{d+1}$, defined with the conformal boundary $S_β^1 \times \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$, with an aim to establish a connection between thermal CFT data and perturbative bulk dynamics. We analyze one-loop tadpole and bench diagrams arising from the cubic $φφχ$ interaction in the bulk, segregating the different relative thermal winding sectors for the bench, and also extend the analysis to include quartic scalar contact diagrams with and without derivative interactions. We also analyze the spinning exchange thermal two-point function. Utilizing the split representation of bulk propagators and the method of images we reorganize the thermal Witten diagrams into the standard AdS integrals and obtain spectral and image-sum representations that make KMS periodicity of the correlators manifest. Additionally, we show possible position dependent effective one-point vertices of scalar and spinning fields in the bulk arising from the reduction of the coincident- point thermal bulk to bulk propagator loop, allowing tadpole-type diagrams to be reconstructed from effective local bulk interactions. The bench diagram however retains an intrinsically bi-local structure. We further investigate the thermal one-point coefficients and perturbative OPE data encoded in these thermal correlators, including contributions associated with anomalous dimensions. Finally, we examine their relation to thermal dispersion relations and the role of contributions that are not determined by discontinuity data alone. Our results provide a set of explicit bulk constructions for exploring the analytic structure of thermal CFT correlators and their connection to the thermal conformal bootstrap.

Quantum Work Statistics from Entanglement-Assisted Invariance

Akira Sone, Akram Touil, Emery Doucet, Sebastian Deffner

We demonstrate that the one-time measurement scheme is more consistent than the standard two-time measurement scheme with respect to the fundamental symmetry of entanglement, namely entanglement-assisted invariance (envariance). In particular, the work statistics obtained from the one-time measurement scheme arise naturally as a direct consequence of envariance for any driving unitary, without involving an explicit measurement postulate, whereas the analogous identification for the two-time measurement scheme is possible only for a Haar-measure-zero set of unitaries. This envariance-based formulation eliminates the ambiguity in work statistics related to the energetic cost of measurement.
