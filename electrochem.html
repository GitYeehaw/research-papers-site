Spin Migration in the Argon Dimer

Stefanos Carlström, Serguei Patchkovskii, Misha Yu Ivanov

We introduce the concept of spin migration, i.e. time-dependent spin density, and the absolute smallest example of molecular spintronics. We illustrate the concept by ab initio calculations of the coupled vibronic motion of the electrons and the nuclei in the argon dimer cation, in which the vibrational level spacing is similar to the spin--orbit splitting of the atomic argon cations. We find that the spin density varies on multiple time scales, given by the natural time scales of the electronic and nuclear wavepackets.

Accuracy of Vibrational Properties of Machine-Learning Force Fields for Molecular Crystal Transformations

Natalia Goncharova, Oskar Schweighofer, Johannes Hoja

Machine-learning force fields (MLFFs) enable the calculation of transformation pathways in molecular crystals at a fraction of the computational cost of density functional theory (DFT). However, a good agreement in energies and structures does not necessarily imply an accurate description of vibrational properties, which are essential for characterizing transition states and determining free energies at finite temperatures. Herein, we assess the accuracy of system-specific SO3krates MLFFs trained on PBE+MBD reference data for describing vibrational properties of molecular crystal transformations. As examples, we consider a polymorph transition of 1,4-diiodobenzene and a single-crystal-to-single-crystal Diels-Alder reaction. The transition state structures obtained from solid-state nudged-elastic-band (NEB) calculations with the MLFFs exhibit a single imaginary $Γ$-point frequency associated with the respective transformation. Compared with PBE+MBD, the mean absolute errors of $Γ$-point vibrational frequencies and relative harmonic free energies at 300 K are about 3 cm$^{-1}$ and 1 kJ/mol, respectively. Furthermore, we compare for the Diels-Alder reaction a collective transformation pathway with a sequential one, in which individual diene-dienophile pairs react successively, and demonstrate that PBE0+MBD single-point corrections can significantly affect the predicted reaction energetics. These results demonstrate that system-specific MLFFs can reproduce not only the energies and forces but also the local curvature of the potential-energy surface with high accuracy, enabling reliable calculations of harmonic vibrational free energies, providing a basis for extending the studies of molecular crystal transformations to larger supercells.

Necessary conditions for benchmarking next-generation AI in materials discovery

Daniel E Widdowson, Tatiana V Kurlina, Olga D Anosova, Vitaliy A Kurlin

Current AI predicts materials by speeding up energy minimisation and training on large databases. However, scaling data generation prevents manual analysis by domain experts. If novelty is evaluated by chemical compositions or traditional representations in Crystallographic Information Files, or standard structure matchers based on manually chosen thresholds, one can easily generate millions of ``new'' compositions or files without discovering truly novel materials. This paper uses a hierarchy of crystal invariants to continuously trace and quantify structural novelty based on the new mathematical definition of a crystal structure and asymptotic guarantees for invariant descriptors. The invariant-based analysis through cascade computations on a modest computer exposes the unexpected scale of duplication in the world's largest materials databases, shown here in the high-profile case of GNoME datasets. To prevent current ``embarrassment of over-prediction'', we propose necessary conditions for next-generation AI that should be automatically enforced before claiming any new materials.

Doubly Metastable Synthesis for Accelerated Quantum Materials Discovery

Kyuho Lee, Berit H. Goodge, Soonhyun Kwon, Yash Anand, Theo Manev et al. (10 authors)

New physics emerges from new materials, which in turn rely on the development of new synthesis techniques. Identifying the enlargement of metastable synthesis window as enabling for realizing novel materials, we develop diffusion-activated epitaxy, a technique which combines two metastable synthesis methods of diffusion and epitaxy. We observe that within a narrow window of investigated synthesis space we can experimentally identify the stabilization of four distinct phases, three of which show X-ray diffraction and scanning transmission electron microscopy characterization data that are incompatible with the crystal structures registered in experimental and computational inorganic structure databases. These results suggest an enlarged metastable synthesis window is enabled by this synthesis approach, identifying doubly metastable synthesis as a promising method for exploration of quantum materials.

Phononic Frequency Combs in Twisted Bilayer Transition Metal Dichalcogenide Heterostructures from First Principles

Naman Arora, Adarsh Ganesan

Phononic frequency combs are equidistant spectral ladders generated when a driven vibrational mode transfers energy to an autoparametrically excited subharmonic mode through anharmonic intermode coupling. In previous studies, the coupling strength governing this energy transfer has typically been treated as a free parameter, extracted from a fabricated device, or adopted from an earlier calculation for a specific mode pair. As a result, whether a given crystal can support a phononic frequency comb, and the drive strength required to generate it, has not been predictable a priori. Here we obtain the complete parameter set from density functional theory. Twisted bilayers of \MoSSe{} and \MoSMoSe{} at the commensurate angle $21.787^{\circ}$ adopt space group $P3$ in a 42-atom \rootseven{} moiré supercell. The second-order force constants provide the mode frequencies; the third-order constants provide the linewidths through the Boltzmann transport equation and hence the quality factors; and the same third-order tensor projected onto the phonon eigenvectors of a chosen mode pair provides the anharmonic couplings $\kt$ and $\kss$. Both bilayers constitute a mode pair within $0.8\%$ of the $2\!:\!1$ resonance with $ω_1/ω_2 = 1.9923$ in \MoSSe{} and $1.9943$ in \MoSMoSe{}. By integrating the resulting two-mode equations, both modes generate combs through a discontinuous transition: at $701\,\fth$ in \MoSSe{} the envelope variance rises from $2.1\times10^{3}$ to $1.37\times10^{4}$ and the tooth count of mode 1 from 7 to 16 within a single $5\,\fth$ increment, identifying the onset as subcritical. The spectral entropy peaks before this jump, at $341\,\fth$. The two compounds carry comparable coupling, differing by a factor of $1.2$. However, the comb excitation threshold is set by the quality-factors of signal modes: $Q_2 = 672$ for \MoSSe{} and $Q_2 = 3844$ for \MoSMoSe{}.

Improved screening of doped ZrO$_2$ catalysts for CO$_2$ hydrogenation from graph-based machine learning

Karsten Walz Jensen, Mie Andersen, Raffaele Cheula

Dopants can tune the activity of zirconia-based CO$_2$ hydrogenation catalysts through changes in adsorbate and transition-state (TS) energetics. Computing these quantities with density functional theory (DFT) becomes expensive when several dopants, reaction intermediates, and TSs must be screened. Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO$_2$(101) surfaces doped with Al$^{3+}$, Cd$^{2+}$, Ga$^{3+}$, In$^{3+}$, and Zn$^{2+}$. Adsorbate-surface structures are represented as attributed graphs that combine bonds connectivity and features. We benchmark WWL-GPR against thermodynamic scaling relations (TSR) for reaction intermediates and Bronsted-Evans-Polanyi relations (BEP) for TSs, and then propagate the predicted energies to microkinetic CH$_3$OH-yield estimates. WWL-GPR consistently improves the prediction of reaction intermediate energetics relative to linear scaling approaches and provides TS estimates that are competitive with BEP-based models. These improvements become especially relevant when the predicted energetics are propagated through the reaction network. Microkinetic calculations show that WWL-GPR-derived energies give CH$_3$OH yields closer to DFT-derived yields than the linear-relation workflow. Degree-of-rate-control analysis links the activity errors to dopant-dependent rate-controlling steps, including HCOO* formation, H$_2$COOH* dissociation, and H$_3$CO* formation. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.

Dislocation mediated twin boundary faceting in $β$-Sn

John Israel, Ian Chesser, Khanh Dang, Vinamra Agrawal

Grain boundaries (GBs), including twin boundaries (TBs), play a critical role in plastic deformation by interacting with dislocations. The deformation and strengthening mechanisms in $β$-Sn are of significant interest because Sn-based alloys are widely used as solder materials. Recent experiments have shown deformation-induced faceting in $β$-Sn arising from twin-dislocation interactions. Using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations informed by a recently developed machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP), we demonstrate a $\langle c\rangle$ dislocation-absorption mediated faceting reaction along $(301)$ TBs in $β$-Sn in which facet length grows with the number of absorbed dislocations. We show that the predicted facet length is consistent with isotropic elasticity theory only when the disclination character of the asymmetric twin facet is taken into account. We discuss analogies to faceting reactions in other low symmetry metals and hypothesize that the ubiquity of $(301)$ twins in $β$-Sn stems from their ability to grow rapidly through a forest of dislocation obstacles.

Electron-Density-Informed Molecular Property Prediction Using Surrogate Functionals

Manuel V. Klockow, Simon Wagner, Roman Remme, Dominic Plein, Fred A. Hamprecht

Machine learning methods are increasingly used to predict chemical properties directly from the molecular structure to bypass the often prohibitive cost of quantum mechanical calculations. Recent work has shown that including the ground-state electron density as input to the machine learning model can improve accuracy in this setting. This work employs the recent surrogate functional framework originally developed for orbital-free density functional theory and extends it to predicting molecular properties. We show that passing the ground-state density as input to the model, while simultaneously enforcing it to be the minimum of the surrogate functional, surpasses state-of-the-art performance for six out of seven extensive properties of the QM9 dataset. Additionally, the approach shows improved generalization to larger molecules taken from the QM40 dataset. On all extensive properties of both datasets, our approach outperforms models that are informed by the ground-state density but do not enforce the surrogate functional constraints.

Ferroelectric Domain-Wall Motion, Stability, and Frequency Responses under Different Screening-Charge Conservation Laws

Sergei V. Kalinin

We analyze ferroelectric domain wall motion, shape, and internal structure arising from coupling between polarization and screening charge. Universal cases are organized by electrical boundary conditions, charge conservation and dynamics, and polarization free energy. We consider exposed surfaces, interfaces beneath a dielectric layer and top electrode, and interfaces with a blocking mixed ionic-electronic conductor. Screening occurs through local exchange, exchange with lateral transport, or strictly conserved redistribution. Analytical derivations and finite-depth calculations establish screening drag, velocity branches, and conditions for spatial instability. For an isolated wall joining differently compensated domains, strict surface conservation and zero far-field current exclude steady translation of the screening profile, while allowing transient displacement, finite-sample arrest, and charge-balanced structural changes. Conservation also constrains long-distance transport around a moving junction in an adjoining conductor. Large-field simulations in the top-electrode geometry reveal nonlinear corrugation, transfer toward longer wavelengths, and coupled polarization rotation. First-order polarization energetics allow two interphase fronts enclosing a paraelectric core; multiaxial polarization permits wall rotation and competing intermediate ferroelectric domains. A driven structural-screening reduction supports stable periodic behavior. Reduced-variable diagrams summarize motion and structural regimes, while BaTiO3 and BiFeO3 examples connect them to material thermodynamics. Small-signal analysis distinguishes displacement, polarization, and electrostatic response functions. These results provide a framework for wall dynamics and structural transitions under different driving and screening conditions.

Giant geometric nonlinear transport in inhomogeneously strained Bi$_2$Se$_3$ single crystalline slabs

Igor Veremchuk, Pavlo Makushko, Anita Guarino, Muhammad Waqee Ur Rehman, Rosalba Tatiana Fittipaldi et al. (16 authors)

Electrical transport in non-centrosymmetric materials exhibits a nonlinear conductivity tensor component that generates second-harmonic voltages in response to applied electric fields. Harnessing this effect is essential for developing zero-threshold electromagnetic energy harvesters and broadband frequency upconverters, both of which require maximizing the nonlinear responsivity. Here, we demonstrate that inhomogeneously strained macroscopic slabs of the topological insulator Bi$_2$Se$_3$ achieve a record-high nonlinear responsivity of $10^4\,μ$m/V: a six orders of magnitude boost compared to its thin-film counterparts. We attribute this giant enhancement to a classical geometric nonlinear transport effect, which is driven by complex strain gradients within the bulk material. Notably, a similar geometric mechanism known as geometric nonlinear Hall was previously observed only in microscale-patterned structures. Our findings establish macroscopic strain engineering as a powerful route to enhance nonlinear transport, with immediate relevance to a broad class of layered systems, including transition metal dichalcogenides and moiré heterostructures.
