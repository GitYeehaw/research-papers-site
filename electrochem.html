High Pressure Material Phase Identification with Gaussian Kernel Neural Networks

Cameron Ryan, Yasantha Pasindu Hetti Kankanamalage, Shuai Zhang

Phase identification is a computational task involving the classification of material structure using the positions of atoms. Using data from Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, our task is to identify the material phase of a set of atoms, based on how the simulations predict their positioning relative to each other. By leveraging existing research on mechanistic neural network theory, particularly Neural Tangent Kernels (NTK), we develop a new and efficient method for classifying simulated material samples. Our method involves defining a set of "atom detectors" that are given positions around the origin in cartesian coordinates. The sum of Gaussians centered at each atom is calculated at each atom detector, and is used as the input to a neural network. We present theoretical and experimental evidence to show that this method is highly effective and efficient at the task. Our method obtains better or similar accuracy scores to commonly used methods while having a major computational speedup.

Finite temperature and pressure structural relaxations including zero-point motion and anharmonicity

Henricus R. A. ten Eikelder, Florian Knoop, Florian Trybel

At finite temperature and pressure, the equilibrium structure of a crystal minimizes the Gibbs free energy rather than the static Born-Oppenheimer energy. We present WARP (Wyckoff-constrained Anharmonic Relaxations at Pressure and temperature), a symmetry-constrained, variable-cell relaxation based on the stochastic temperature-dependent effective potential method, including anharmonicity and zero-point motion. Every position update is projected onto Wyckoff positions and every strain onto symmetric strains, so a relaxation can raise the symmetry but never lower it. For aluminum, the thermal expansion follows measurements at cryogenic temperatures, including the quantum plateau below 50 K. For lanthanum hydrides at megabar pressures, equations of state agree with phonon-based and path-integral calculations, and LaH$_{10}$ relaxes from its rhombohedral static minimum to the observed cubic phase, also with a machine-learned potential fine-tuned on 150 supercells. For brucite, Mg(OD)$_2$, lattice parameters, bond distances and thermal expansion agree with neutron diffraction, and the O-D isotope effect is captured.

Catalysis-induced phase separation of enzymes via diffusivity contrast

Marie Jardat, Jacques Fries, Vincent Dahirel, Jaime Agudo-Canalejo, Pierre Illien

Catalysis can drive phase separation by coupling local chemical composition to molecular transport, even in the absence of attractive interactions. Here, we demonstrate such catalysis-induced phase separation in a microscopic particle-based model of enzymes catalyzing the interconversion of substrate and product molecules with different diffusivities. We derive an analytical instability criterion in the dilute limit, showing that a homogeneous mixture becomes unstable when the nonequilibrium chemical drive and the diffusivity contrast are sufficiently large, and the substrate and product concentration exceeds a threshold set by the enzyme volume. Brownian-dynamics simulations confirm the predicted instability and reveal macroscopic coexistence of enzyme-rich and enzyme-poor phases, including in regimes where equilibrium depletion interactions cannot account for demixing. Finally, we show that, upon eliminating the fast chemical degrees of freedom, the reactive ternary mixture maps onto an effective binary mixture nonreciprocal interactions. Our results establish an explicit microscopic mechanism for catalysis-induced phase separation and connect chemically driven phase separation to the physics of motility-regulated and nonreciprocal matter.

Out-of-plane interference effects in the angle-resolved photoemission of epitaxial graphene

E. Favorite, P. Krüger, Ph. Rosenzweig, R. Joshi, S. Williams et al. (10 authors)

The epitaxial growth of monolayer graphene by Si sublimation of 6H-SiC(0001) is one of the most effective ways of producing large-scale monocrystalline graphene samples of high quality. However, graphene is left interacting with a reconstructed carbon buffer layer known as zerolayer graphene (ZLG) and the SiC substrate underneath. Beyond the mere creation of Dirac cone replicas, the effect of this interaction on photoemission measurements is poorly understood. We utilized synchrotron radiation for photon-energy-dependent angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) to investigate the complex final-state interactions of photoelectrons within the heterostructure of monolayer graphene, ZLG, and SiC. We identify multiple resonances in the photoemission intensity of the Dirac cone as a function of photon energy that originate from interlayer interference, confirmed with a multiple scattering model. We also demonstrate for the first time that the relative intensity of the replica bands induced by the $(6\sqrt{3} \times 6\sqrt{3})R30^\circ$ reconstruction scales with the electron kinetic energy. Finally, we resolved four nondispersing electronic states, which originate from the ZLG. Our findings show that the three-dimensional (3D) structure of SiC and ZLG manifests itself in the photoemission from two-dimensional (2D) epitaxial graphene. By revealing how 3D substrate architecture dictates the photoemission of a 2D layer, this work establishes a crucial framework for investigating the electronic structure of complex heterostructures via ARPES.

On the Method of Projection onto Localized States for Describing Reactions and Clustering

V. D. Efros

The method of projection onto localized states intended for describing reactions and clustering is examined. In particular, the method offers a simple extension of the no-core shell model to continuum- and clusterized bound states. A critical analysis of a previous realization of the method is provided. A way to minimize the number of basis functions required for convergence is given. An easy way to obtain exactly unitary and symmetric $S$ matrix from calculations is pointed out. The method is tested in one- and two-channel $np$ scattering problems with the Daejeon $NN$ interaction. It is shown that excellent convergence can be reached even when only oscillator states with low number of excitation quanta are retained.

The Weight Is in the Reading: Hidden States in Memristors, Memlumors, and Electrochemical Transistors

Gonzalo Rivera-Sierra, Heyi Zhang, Daiki Nishioka, Takashi Tsuchiya, Juan Bisquert

Physical synapses are routinely described by a single number, the weight, that is identified with a conductance, a current, or a luminescence yield. This identification conflates two different objects: the internal state that the device stores, and the signal obtained when that state is read. Building on Chua's formulation of memristive systems, we separate the state variable, which evolves with the excitation history, from the observation map that converts it into a measured signal, and we define the weight as the outcome of a specified read protocol. Three platforms show the consequences: a memristor, in which the same voltage writes and reads; a memlumor, in which light both modifies the state and produces the photoluminescence that reports it; and an organic electrochemical transistor (OECT), in which the gate writes and the channel reads. In the transistor, two states prepared by different histories give the same channel current at a specific drain bias and become distinguishable again when the bias is changed, while the stored charge persists and the gate current continues to separate them. Published experiments provide precedents: a drain-bias-dependent cancellation of the OECT transient, and trapped charges in perovskite memlumors that are invisible to conventional time-resolved photoluminescence but revealed by a read-write-read sequence. A synaptic weight is therefore not a property of the material alone but of the material together with its readout, and reports of synaptic behavior should specify what is stored, what is measured, and how one is inferred from the other.

Damped polarizability and first hyperpolarizability of large systems with hybrid functionals: self-consistent and Lanczos solvers

A. García-Blázquez, A. Erba, A. Boccuni, J. K. Desmarais

We present a damped coupled-perturbed Kohn-Sham method to compute the dynamical polarizability and first hyperpolarizability of finite systems, implemented in the CRYSTAL code for semi-local, global and range-separated hybrid functionals. The approach is based on the adiabatic time-dependent density functional theory for the complex-valued (hyper)polarizabilities, describing optical phenomena such as absorption, and the nonlinear second-harmonic generation, rectification and Pockels effect. These are obtained either at individual frequencies with a self-consistent solver based on Pulay extrapolation, or at an arbitrary grid of frequency and damping factors with a Lanczos solver, from which the first-order amplitudes required in the hyperpolarizability can be reconstructed. With hybrid functionals, the self-consistent solver tends to become impractical at some point beyond the absorption onset, where there are many excitations for which the external frequency lies between their energy and those of their contributing single-particle transitions, and its convergence eventually stalls for large systems. In contrast, the convergence of the Lanczos method is essentially independent of size, hence the cost of the dynamical response calculation scales with system size as the hybrid ground-state calculation. The implementation is validated in a small molecule and, as an application, we study the first and second-order optical response of a series of hexagonal boron nitride flakes of up to 324 atoms. The use of range-separated hybrid functionals allows to capture the strongly-bound first excitation of the monolayer, and evidences the failure of the self-consistent method to converge excitations with strong electron-hole attraction.

Neutronic Field-Effect Transistor

Dusan Sarenac, Charles W. Clark, Owen Lailey, Muhammad Arif, Wangchun Chen et al. (9 authors)

Field-effect electronic devices are in widespread use in communication, computing, data storage, and sensing applications. We demonstrate a neutronic field-effect transistor (NFET) that is analogous to the Datta-Das electron spin transistor and atomtronic devices. The NFET is based on a perfect-crystal Mach-Zehnder neutron interferometer with phase shifts applied to the spin and path degrees of freedom.

Analytic $GW$-Bethe--Salpeter Gradients with Quasiparticle Response in the Resolution of the Identity

Christof Holzer

Analytic nuclear gradients for excited states from the Bethe$-$Salpeter equation (BSE) in the static screening approximation are presented within this work. The response of the screened interaction to nuclear displacements is treated within the resolution-of-the-identity (RI) approximation, in close analogy to RI random phase approximation gradients, and is folded into the Lagrangian formalism of time-dependent density functional theory gradients. Quasiparticle gradients enter via the full analytic derivative of the corrections for RI contour-deformation $G_0W_0$ and ev$GW$, also taking into account that incomplete quasiparticle spectra are typically supplied. Comparisons to earlier approaches using a geometry-independent fixed quasiparticle correction are made. For the latter, the orbital-rotation terms are derived exactly, too. The implemented gradient scales as $\mathcal{O}(N^4)$, and the three-index integrals are processed in batches with a memory demand of $\mathcal{O}(N_aux^2 + N_occ^2 N_aux)$. Excited-state geometries of a set of small molecules are compared to EOM-CCSD references and to TD-DFT. It is shown that the full quasiparticle gradient removes most of the systematic underestimation of the bond lengths in singlet excited states. A comparison along the C$-$C stretch of acetylene shows that the neglected derivative of the quasiparticle corrections is modest, but becomes erratic for $G_0W_0$ near poles of the self-energy, which ev$GW$ avoids.

GibbScope: interactive calculation and visualization of phase diagrams in the browser

Ramya Ramachandran, Zhuocheng Huang, Jiayang Wang, Marat I. Latypov

Phase diagrams are a constant work in progress as Gibbs-energy models are fitted, revised, and disputed in the light of an endless stream of experimental and computational data. Yet, phase diagrams are still largely communicated as static figures, in much the same way as a century ago. We present GibbScope, a web-based framework for interactive calculation and visualization of binary phase diagrams. Written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly, the new CALPHAD engine recomputes a complete phase diagram in the browser in a fraction of a second with zero installation on the user or server side. The high speed allows new ways of interacting with phase diagrams and underlying assessments. Validation against well-established CALPHAD software shows agreement to within 0.01 K for invariant temperatures and within 1e-6 for tie-line compositions for most databases. GibbScope offers interactive CALPHAD parameter adjustments, overlays of different assessments and point-by-point misfit to experimental data, which makes the diagram a live, working object. In addition, GibbScope provides convenient access to 2745 thermodynamic databases from the SGTE collection (Hallstedt, 2025) and their live adjustment. For over 1200 of these databases, all phases are modeled in full with the current implementation.
