When optimized geometries are not optimal: Implications for inverted singlet-triplet gap predictions

Atreyee Majumdar, Elamathi Selvan Samynathan, Raghunathan Ramakrishnan

Pseudo-Jahn-Teller distortions can substantially alter singlet-triplet gaps, potentially reversing the predicted excited-state ordering in molecules proposed to exhibit inverted singlet-triplet (INVEST) gaps. In this study, we benchmark geometry optimization methods for 13 azaphenalenes against CCSD(T)-quality reference geometries, assessing their ability to reproduce symmetry lowering and its consequences for singlet-triplet gaps. Among the methods examined, $ω$B97M-D4 reliably describes the distorted geometries and corresponding gaps, whereas the commonly used methods B3LYP and MP2 retain high-symmetry structures, potentially leading to false-positive INVEST assignments. We further assess $ω$B97M-D4 geometries across 337 molecules comprising azaphenalenes, boron/nitrogen-substituted polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and non-alternant hydrocarbons. Subsequent L-CC2/aug-cc-pVDZ calculations predict negative singlet-triplet gaps for 85 molecules and identify discrepancies with previously reported INVEST assignments. These findings demonstrate that the choice of geometry optimization method is crucial for reliable INVEST screening, particularly in systems susceptible to symmetry-breaking distortions.

Longitudinal and Transverse Thermoelectric Properties in Topological Quantum Materials

Jyoti Duhan, Anil Kumar B. M., Satya N. Guin, Koushik Pal

Thermoelectric materials can be topologically trivial or non-trivial, enabling reversible interconversion of heat and electricity using a solid-state technology. Incidentally, few of the first experimentally discovered 3D topological materials, namely, Bi, Sb, Sn and Pb-based chalcogenides, were long known as good thermoelectric materials. In these compounds, the heavy atoms that give rise to strong spin-orbit coupling often become essential for band inversion and non-trivial band topology. They lead to low lattice thermal conductivity, linearly dispersing Dirac-like bands induce high electrical mobility, and a band-inverted electronic structure with multiple band extrema gives rise to high Seebeck coefficients. In materials, where the applied temperature gradient induces electrical voltage (Seebeck effect) in the same direction, they are termed as longitudinal thermoelectrics. On the other hand, when the generated electrical voltage and applied thermal current remain orthogonal to each other, the materials are termed as transverse thermoelectrics. The transverse response can be driven by an additional external magnetic field (ordinary Nernst effect), intrinsic remanent magnetisation, or non-zero Berry curvature of a material, which arises from their non-trivial topology (anomalous Nernst effect). We discuss another class of quantum materials, known as goniopolar materials, that exhibit axis-dependent conduction polarity and hence transverse thermoelectric effects, which are based on mechanisms of longitudinal thermoelectric properties. In this review, we cover both types of thermoelectricity, focusing on materials that possess non-trivial topological order. We make connections between their structures, topology and transport properties and suggest tunability of thermoelectric properties going beyond conventional mechanisms such as electron-phonon interactions.

Thermally Conditioned Time-Series Forecasting of Grain Growth in Polycrystalline Materials

Eliane Younes, Elie Hachem, Marc Bernacki

Grain growth during heat treatment strongly affects the microstructure of polycrystalline materials and, consequently, their mechanical properties. Although numerical methods such as front-tracking or front-capturing simulations can accurately describe grain growth, their high computational cost limits their use for long or non-isothermal thermal cycles. In previous work, grain growth was successfully predicted under isothermal conditions by treating grain size distributions as time series and forecasting their evolution using recurrent neural networks, allowing complete grain growth trajectories to be generated in seconds rather than through costly PDE-based simulations. In the present work, this forecasting framework is reformulated to handle non-isothermal conditions by explicitly incorporating thermal information into the model architecture. Grain size distributions are encoded using a convolutional neural network, thermal descriptors are embedded through a dedicated multilayer perceptron, and temporal evolution is predicted using a recurrent network. Instantaneous temperature and thermal rate are provided at each time step, allowing the model to adapt its predictions to changing thermal conditions. The framework is trained and evaluated on datasets generated from validated front-tracking simulations across a wide range of non-isothermal thermal paths. The results show that the thermally conditioned model accurately reproduces grain growth dynamics under complex thermal histories, achieving, in k-fold cross-validation over all simulations, mean relative errors of 2.99% (one-hour cycles) and 4.51% (four-hour cycles) with coefficients of determination exceeding 0.99, and mean relative errors between 1.91% and 14.66% on ten simulations following four unseen thermal histories of increasing complexity.

Evolutionary Design of Interpretable Spatial Descriptors for Structure-Property Relationship Discovery

Aditya Raghavan, Sergei V Kalinin

Structure property prediction is a classical task in machine learning, ranging from molecular activity and materials properties to local functional responses measured in microscopy. For image-based regression, two choices define the information available to the predictor: the size of the image neighborhood and the spatial resolution retained within it. A small neighborhood can omit relevant surrounding structure, whereas a large, finely sampled neighborhood can introduce redundant or irrelevant information. Here, we develop a framework for constructing spatial descriptors for prediction of local spectra and hysteresis loops. We benchmark regression models, use SHAP maps to examine their spatial attribution, and explore the effect of the patch size and compression to establish the relationship between predictive accuracy and descriptor dimensionality. We then use a genetic algorithm to optimize spatial partitions and the assignment of mean, minimum, and maximum pooling operations. Each retained region contributes an explicit measurement of the image, allowing the search to balance prediction error against the number of descriptor values. Cartesian and polar constructions provide complementary ways to organize this information. The resulting descriptors make it possible to examine where spatial detail is needed, where regional summaries are sufficient, and how these choices depend on the predictor and the measured response. The same approach can be used for molecules and solids once the genetic algorithm rules for these are established.

Open-shell Configuration State Functions as trial states for phaseless auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo

Don Danilov, Hugh G. A. Burton, James Shee

The accurate description of open-shell states in molecular systems poses challenges for approximate electronic structure methods. One emerging approach is to variationally optimize the orbitals of a single user-selected Configuration State Function (CSF), i.e., a linear combination of Slater determinants that is constructed as an eigenstate of the total spin operator. In this work we explore the use of these orbital-optimized single CSFs as trial wavefunctions in phaseless auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (ph-AFQMC), a stochastic method that recovers electron correlation by evolving an initial state in imaginary time, subject to a constraint imposed by the trial state. We investigate the symmetric stretch of H-He-H, a series of small $p$-block compounds with diradicaloid singlet states, twisted ethene conformers, and a bimetallic iron-sulfur complex. We find that accurate energetics are obtained for states dominated by open-shell character, and that for certain atoms and diatomics the accuracy can be significantly improved when the trial is made to also preserve the relevant angular momentum symmetries. Furthermore, our study reveals that the open-shell CSF trial is suboptimal when the target electronic state reflects a significant mixture of ionic and covalent character, e.g., at shorter bond lengths in H-He-H and for the superexchange-stabilized ground state of [2Fe-2S]$^{2+}$. Our findings suggest that ph-AFQMC using CSF trial states is a promising method for many (but not all) strongly correlated molecular states, especially given its relatively low computational cost, and that it provides a reasonable non-perturbative way to recover some of the dynamic correlation that is otherwise missing in the CSF.

Machine Learning the Complete Active-Space Wavefunction Reconstruction

Aniruddha Seal, Bhavnesh Jangid, Helen S. Clifford, Andrew L. Ferguson, Laura Gagliardi

Machine-learned interatomic potentials enable multireference-quality reaction dynamics but do not retain the electronic structure underlying the predicted energies and forces. Recovering this information by independently performing complete active space self-consistent field (CASSCF) calculations is both expensive and potentially inconsistent. We introduce the Complete Active-Space Wave Function Reconstruction (CAS-WR) model, which learns the geometry-dependent electronic structure from CASSCF data and reconstructs the CAS wave function without repeated orbital optimization. Combined with uncertainty-based active learning, CAS-WR is demonstrated for the Diels--Alder reaction and \ce{TiC+}-catalyzed C--H activation. The approach enables trajectory-resolved analysis of multiconfigurational electronic structure across reactive ensembles. Preliminary applications to excited-state proton transfer and transition-metal photocatalysis suggest that CAS-WR can provide a general route toward recovering electronic structure along complex ground- and excited-state reactive dynamics.

Millikelvin magnetometry up to 30 T using a multi-cross micro-Hall sensor

Bingbing Tong, Yinina Ma, Zhenhua Zhao, Shuyu Zheng, Li Lu et al. (7 authors)

We report a multi-cross micro-Hall sensor fabricated from an MBE-grown GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure for high-sensitivity magnetometry. The device enables dc magnetization measurements at temperatures down to $\sim$15 mK and in dc magnetic fields up to 30 T. Bulk samples with dimensions ranging from 100~$μ$m to 1 mm can be measured with a resolution better than 1$~μ$T with a 1~$μ$A excitation at 13.77 Hz. Test-of-performance measurements were carried out by probing the field-dependent magnetization plateaus of the triangular-lattice quantum magnet K$_{2}$Co$_{2}$(SeO$_{3}$)$_{3}$ at 20 mK, the antiferromagnetic transition of K${_3}$GdTe${_2}$O${_9}$ near 220 mK via temperature-dependent magnetization, and the quantum oscillations in a ruthenium single crystal. A simple and reliable sample-mounting setup that ensures reproducible magnetic measurements is provided.

First-principles study of the formation of Group-IV--vacancy color centers in diamond

Xiangru Han, Adam Gali

$XV$ ($X$ is Si, Ge, Sn, or Pb, and $V$ is the carbon vacancy) color centers are promising platforms for quantum computing and quantum information science due to their favorable optical and spin properties. They are commonly fabricated by ion implantation, which inevitably introduces vacancies and carbon self-interstitials (C$_i$) into the lattice. However, the microscopic formation mechanism remains unclear, limiting the yield and stability of $XV$ centers. Here, first-principles calculations are conducted to systematically investigate the roles of $V$ and C$_i$ in $XV$ formation. We find that $XV$ formation is energetically favorable, with large binding energies and low diffusion barriers. Nevertheless, $XV$ readily captures C$_i$ to form substitutional defect $X_s$, or captures an additional $V$ to form the complex $XV_2$. Charge transition levels show that oppositely charged reactants never meet in these reactions, and that Coulomb repulsion blocks them only when both reactants carry charges of the same sign, which for the capture of C$_i$ requires strongly n- or p-type conditions. These competing defect reactions destabilize the split-vacancy configuration and could account for the low formation yield of $XV$ centers, so that they appear at concentrations well below those expected from the implanted dose. Our results elucidate the microscopic origin of this intrinsic instability and suggest that Fermi-level control may suppress defect conversions and stabilize the split-vacancy configuration.

Anomalous thermal transport in isotope-enriched germanium carbide

Wenjiang Zhou, Bai Song

Advances in modern electronics demand the dissipation of heat fluxes approaching 1000 W cm$^{-2}$, which drives an urgent need for materials with thermal conductivity ($κ$) exceeding 1000 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$. Here, by solving the Boltzmann transport equation considering both three- and four-phonon interactions, we observe that isotopically pure cubic germanium carbide (GeC) exhibits a remarkable $κ$ of 1018 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ at room temperature, which is further corroborated by state-of-the-art machine-learning molecular dynamics simulations. Two unique mechanisms underlie this anomalously high $κ$: a remarkably strong isotopic enhancement effect (289%) and a surprisingly modest suppression due to four-phonon scattering (22%)-despite the presence of a wide acoustic-optical phonon gap. Moreover, we find that GeC defies the conventional Leibfried-Schlomann formula and exhibits a non-monotonic variation of $κ$ with external hydrostatic pressure at elevated temperatures. Our findings establish isotope-enriched GeC as a material of ultrahigh $κ$ and an ideal platform for exploring exotic phonon physics.

Explicit pore pre-conditioning and inverse design of amorphous nanoporous carbon

Matteo Alberghini, Giacomo Pierotti, Maryam Amini, Mauro Sgroi, Alessio Tommasi

Porous carbons store energy, capture gases and purify water, and their performance depends strongly on the geometry of their pores. Atomistic models mostly simulate their synthesis by molecular dynamics, with results that depend on the interatomic potential, and return the pore network as an output rather than as a specification. Here we show that the pore network can be an input: pores drawn from a target size distribution fill a prescribed volume fraction, carbon is grown around them by stochastic ring placement, and a particle swarm recovers the construction parameters from a structural fingerprint. The search reproduces the density, surface area and pore volume of a carbon produced by quenched molecular dynamics within about 5 %, together with its pore size distribution. As a stress test, it also recovers a bimodal distribution, and the search history reveals which parameters the targets constrain and flags a failed search. A short anneal completes the network, recovering the coordination of the reference while the pore size distribution keeps its shape. Molecular dynamics still supplies the local chemistry, but the pore geometry becomes a design variable for a given application.
