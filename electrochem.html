Vacancy-enhanced Li solid-solution anodes for solid-state batteries: computational screening enabling high critical current density

Arseniy S. Burov, Nadezhda A. Ashikhmina, Nurlan Zhanabayev, Anton O. Boev, Aleksandra D. Radina et al. (8 authors)

Sluggish vacancy-mediated self-diffusion in lithium metal limits the critical current density (CCD) of Li anodes in solid-state batteries by promoting interfacial void formation during stripping. To enhance the effective vacancy concentration while preserving the bcc Li host, we screen 50 substitutional solutes using density functional theory (DFT) within a funnel based on automatic phase diagram construction, solution energies, solute-vacancy binding energies, and experimental solubility limits. Using an adaptation of the Lomer model for the dilute limit, we show that only Ag, Ge, and Cd solid solutions are predicted to reach $1 mA cm^{-2}$ within their solubility limits. The thermodynamically estimated CCD of Ag at $\sim 9$ at.% reaches $\sim 4 mA cm^{-2}$ at a theoretical capacity of $1522 mAh g^{-1}$. Migration barrier calculations indicate that the enhancement may be partially offset by vacancy trapping, necessitating further validation via molecular dynamics simulations. Notably, Hume-Rothery rules alone prove insufficient as prefilters, whereas machine-learning models trained on the DFT data can eliminate most unsuitable solutes early in the funnel and extend the workflow to related solid-solution searches.

Scaling the Wavelength to Optimize Ultrafast Laser Processing of Bandgap Materials

Maxime Chambonneau, Mario Garcia-Lechuga, David Grojo, Stefan Nolte

The spectral characteristics of ultrashort laser pulses offer unique possibilities to control interactions in space and time as well as the subsequent material responses. In particular, selecting the wavelength predefines the fundamental mechanisms governing laser-matter interaction, and thus, how energy is deposited. This Chapter provides an overview on how the essential physical effects in ultrafast laser-matter interaction scale with the wavelength, and the consequences for bandgap material processing applications. First, we recall the main spectral properties of bandgap materials and discuss the important question of the expected processing resolutions depending on wavelength. Then, recent studies on the impact of the wavelength in ultrafast laser-dielectric interaction are presented. Finally, we present how the latest developments in infrared ultrafast laser sources can be advantageously utilized to internally process narrow-gap materials despite challenges related to increased nonlinearities.

CO2-Induced Shifts in the Confined Phase Transformation of CH4 in Metal-Organic Frameworks

Anthony Dorhauer, Bartosz Mazur, Bogdan Kuchta, Carlos Wexler

A sharp increase in gas uptake over a narrow pressure range can occur for fluids confined in nanoporous materials such as metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), reflecting a transformation between distinct adsorption states, often accompanied by the existence of metastable states. Understanding how co-adsorbed species modify this behavior is important for predicting and optimizing gas storage and separation. Here, we investigate the effect of CO2 on the adsorption transformation of confined CH4 in rigid IRMOF-1 and IRMOF-8. Transition-Matrix Monte Carlo (TMMC) simulations were performed for systems containing 0-75 and 0-150 CO2 molecules in IRMOF-1 and IRMOF-8, respectively. While the addition of CO2 decreases the maximum CH4 uptake, consistent with competition for pore space, remarkably, the presence of CO2 significantly shifts the discontinuity in the CH4 adsorption isotherm to substantially lower pressures relative to the pure CH4 system. This promotion of the high-density CH4 phase at lower pressures demonstrates that CO2 substantially alters the thermodynamic landscape governing confined CH4 through changes in both fluid-fluid and fluid-framework interactions. These results establish CO2 as a thermodynamic modifier of confined CH4 behavior and suggest that controlled co-adsorption provides a means of tuning adsorption transformations in nanoporous materials.

Combining Simulations and Spectroscopy to Design Protective Coatings on Sulfide Solid Electrolytes

Zhiyi Wang, Leonie Braks, Michele Ceriotti, Ali Coskun, Hanna Türk et al. (6 authors)

Sulfide solid electrolytes are attractive for all-solid-state batteries because of their high Li-ion conductivity and favorable processability, but their practical use is limited by electrochemical instability and moisture-induced degradation. Molecular surface coatings offer a scalable strategy to improve humidity tolerance and interfacial stability, yet there is limited understanding of the molecular-level factors governing surface interactions. By combining large-scale atomistic simulations enabled by a machine-learning potential with targeted experiments, we present a comprehensive framework for designing molecular coatings on Li$_6$PS$_5$Cl (LPSCl). Using a PET-MAD interatomic potential fine-tuned on an r$^2$SCAN reference dataset covering LPSCl surfaces, molecular adsorbates, and their interactions, we model surface adsorption of carboxylic (CA), sulfonic (SA), and phosphonic (PA) acids with alkyl chains of varying lengths and the interaction of these coated surfaces with water at finite temperature. The simulations show that lithiated head groups generally exhibit more favorable adsorption energies. Impedance spectroscopy shows that the lithiated coatings maintain 40--55\% of the initial ionic conductivity after powder processing. While adsorption strength is largely controlled by head-group chemistry and lithiation state, coating morphology depends primarily on alkyl-chain length and surface coverage. In particular, long-chain lithiated carboxylates form dense, brush-like hydrophobic layers that reduce water access to reactive Li surface sites, with protection setting in at a coating thickness of approximately 0.8 nm. Consistent with these predictions, longer carboxylate chains suppress H$_2$S evolution more effectively. Because thicker coatings also hinder Li-ion transport, intermediate chain lengths offer the best balance between moisture protection and ionic conductivity.

Single-molecule cold chemistry: Isotopic branching ratios of the $H_2O^+ + HD$ reaction at cryogenic temperatures

Nanditha Sunil Kumar, Prerna Paliwal, Mikhail Popov, Xueyi Li, Anyang Li et al. (7 authors)

Isotopic branching in ion-molecule reactions influences interstellar deuterium fractionation, yet its dynamics remain poorly understood for many important systems at astrophysically relevant temperatures. Here, we investigate H- and D-atom transfer in the $H_2O^+ + HD$ reaction using single sympathetically cooled water ions in a cryogenic radiofrequency trap. Nondestructive identification of the products from 441 individual reaction events at a mean collision energy of 7 K reveals an $H_3O^+/H_2DO^+$ product branching ratio of $1.50 \pm 0.15$ demonstrating a preferential H-atom transfer. The experiments are rationalized using quasi-classical-trajectory calculations which indicate a transition from direct dynamics at higher energies to a complex-mediated reaction under the present experimental conditions and point to an important role of intramolecular vibrational redistribution affecting the isotopic branching in this regime. The present results extend measurements into an astrochemically relevant energy regime and establish cryogenic single-ion chemistry as a new platform for resolving competing reaction pathways.

Rationalizing charge transfer: a single-molecule case study across three distinct surfaces

Benjamin Lowe, Jan Berger, Martin Ondráček, Oleksandr Stetsovych, Shayan Louie et al. (18 authors)

Organic molecules are versatile building blocks for the formation of complex low-dimensional nanostructures on surfaces. Recently, interest has grown in translating on-surface synthetic protocols from noble metal surfaces to insulating surfaces, which offer technological relevance, and to magnetic or superconducting surfaces, which offer new routes to correlated phases. It is important, therefore, to understand how these alternative surfaces may alter the properties of adsorbates through processes like charge transfer and screening. Evidence for charge transfer is often obtained experimentally via scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and spectroscopy (STS), and theoretically via density functional theory (DFT). In this work, we systematically compare the properties of isolated macrocyclic emeraldine (Me$_6$) molecules on three different surfaces: metallic Au(111), a semiconducting ferromagnetic monolayer of FeCl$_2$, and a cleaved superconducting surface of bulk 2H-NbSe$_2$. We show that STS and DFT alone are insufficient to quantify charge transfer for these cases. Instead, we complement these methods with Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) measurements and model Hamiltonian calculations to demonstrate that on Au(111) and FeCl$_2$, Me$_6$ molecules donate a single electron to the substrate, whereas on NbSe$_2$ two electrons are transferred from molecule to substrate. Our work provides insight into the interplay of charge transfer, screening, and molecule-substrate coupling, and develops experimental and theoretical methods for comparing the properties of adsorbates across different surfaces for future studies.

A classical trajectory method for diatom-atom-atom ternary association reactions

Daniel Julian, Jesus Perez-Rios

We introduce a classical trajectory method for calculating direct diatom-atom-atom ternary association re- action rates that incorporates the rotational degrees of freedom of the diatomic reactant. By treating the diatomic molecule as a rigid rotor, we include the effects of the anisotropy of the molecule-atom interaction and the dynamical effects of the rotational motion. We apply this approach to a system of experimental interest, CD+ + He + He, along with the isotopic variant CH+ + He + He -> CHHe+ + He, and examine the role of the internal, rotational degrees of freedom of the diatomic molecule. In particular, we find that in the regime where the collision energy is much smaller than the rotational energy of the entrance channel, the rotational motion leads to deviation from the classical threshold law by violating the assumptions of long-range capture theory models.

Unconventional charge density wave order in ErTe$_{3}$: symmetry breakings and fluctuations probed by Raman scattering

Théotime Freitas, Mattia Udina, Alexandr Alekhin, Niloufar Nilforoushan, Sarah Houver et al. (10 authors)

Recent experiments have revealed unexpected mirror-symmetry breakings in the charge density-wave (CDW) state of rare-earth tritellurides, suggesting a putative ferro-axial CDW state. Here we report polarization-resolved Raman scattering measurements tracking the point-group symmetry breakings across the CDW phase transition in the rare-earth tritelluride ErTe$_3$. We show that the mirror-symmetry breakings follow closely the emergence of the CDW order, suggesting a single component CDW order parameter. Furthermore, Raman measurements in the $A_{2g}$ symmetry channel above the CDW transition temperature indicate the absence of significant ferro-axial fluctuations. We discuss the broad implications of these findings with respect to the nature of the CDW state in the tritellurides.

A Compact Single-Beam Optically Pumped Magnetometer Based on Absorption Detection

Jia-You Liou, Yi-Hsuan Chen, Hsiang-An Chuang, Chih-Hsuan Huang, Zong-Hsin Liu et al. (8 authors)

We demonstrate a compact single-beam optically pumped magnetometer (OPM) employing a pure 87Rb microfabricated vapor cell with 500 Torr buffer gas. The residual magnetic field is actively minimized to below 400 pT through a combination of mu-metal magnetic shielding and internal compensation coils. We systematically characterize the OPM response as a function of laser detuning and optical intensity, and derive the magnetic-field sensitivity from the signal slope and noise amplitude spectral density. A best sensitivity of 11 fT/Hz^1/2 is achieved near the resonance transition at an optimal optical intensity, using a small cell volume of only 0.027 cm^3. Furthermore, a miniaturized OPM prototype was developed using a folded flexible printed circuit board (FPCB) to integrate the vapor cell, a compact heater, and magnetic-field coils into a cubic sensing head. Two coil configurations patterned directly on the FPCB were investigated. Under optimized operating conditions, the OPM prototype achieved a sensitivity of 66 fT/Hz^1/2. These results demonstrate that high-buffer-pressure single-beam OPMs can achieve excellent sensitivity, establishing a pathway toward compact and scalable arrays.

Ginzburg-Landau Theory with Elastic Incompatibility Describes the Liquid-G-phase-Crystal State Diagram

Alessio Zaccone, Konrad Samwer

We derive a mechanically consistent Ginzburg-Landau theory for the metastable heterogeneous G state reported in deeply undercooled metallic liquids. The central result is that type-II-like mixed-state physics can emerge structurally with the random internal stress field playing the role of the magnetic field. Frozen eigenstrain due to elastic incompatibility between ordered cores and the amorphous matrix generates incompatible stress, while the non-affine internal strain in the liquid-like matrix in between the ordered cores relaxes that stress over a finite screening length $λ_σ$ (analogous to the London length in the superconducting problem). Structural order heals over a distinct mechanically-dressed length $ξ_G$; their ratio $κ_G=λ_σ/ξ_G$ provides the structural counterpart of the Ginzburg-Landau parameter. Because the matrix is transiently rigid yet locally relaxational only over an intermediate time window, the same mechanism selects crystal for slow protocols, a heterogeneous G state at intermediate rates, and a homogeneous L-like glass under rapid arrest. Residual incompatibility frustrates coarsening, allowing finite ordered cores and screened, orientation-dependent core interactions. The resulting texture is an inverted Abrikosov analogue: structural order resides in the cores, whereas incompatible-stress screening is carried by the surrounding disordered matrix. The theory yields direct simulation and experimental tests through stress-decay, structural-healing, non-affine-susceptibility, and kinetic measurements.
