When to retire: the cultural evolution of post-reproductive oblique teaching

Hirotaka Goto, Joshua B. Plotkin

Teaching is ubiquitous across human societies. Instruction from parents to offspring is the most fundamental form of teaching, explained primarily through kin selection and inclusive fitness. Instruction to non-kin, known as oblique teaching, also plays a dominant role in modern societies, where teaching is often institutionalized. Nonetheless, it remains puzzling how or when oblique teaching can evolve. Here we address the evolution of oblique teaching by developing a mathematical model of stage-structured populations. Younger individuals engage in a useful activity, called hunting, and reproduce. At some point in life, they cease contributing directly to hunting and reproduction, and instead serve exclusively as teachers who increase the hunting expertise of the young. We refer to this transition in life stage as retirement, and we analyze the evolution of the retirement rate, and the resulting frequency of post-reproductive teachers, in a population subject to a global carrying capacity. We find that retirement and teaching can evolve without kinship or inclusive fitness, but only in populations above a threshold size. Unlike in classical adaptive dynamics, the evolutionarily stable retirement rates depend on the absolute population size of a mutant type upon introduction. In many regimes, evolution produces fewer teachers than would maximize the population's per-capita hunting returns or intrinsic birth rate. A mutant that differs only slightly from the resident type generally cannot invade from a minority, so teaching cannot emerge through gradual evolution. The mechanism we describe is therefore available to cultural evolution, where a distinct norm for retirement and elder care may be adopted by a sub-population simultaneously. Our account is thus tied closely to theories of cultural evolution, including the "information grandmother hypothesis" of Henrich and Henrich.

Unlocking the Full Potential of Taylor Dispersion Analysis Through Advanced Detection

Rami Laoufi, Agnès Hagège

Today, pharmaceutical sciences rely on a wide range of innovative therapeutics of varying sizes: nanoparticles, proteins, antibodies, and nanocarriers for drug delivery. Measuring their size is one of the crucial parameters in their characterization, to ensure drug safety, stability, and effectiveness. Over the past two decades, Taylor Dispersion Analysis has emerged as an important sizing method. Its scope of application is perfectly suited to the sizes of these therapeutics. By design, it can be coupled with a wide range of detectors, and extensive research has been conducted in this field. In this review, we will present an overview of the studies conducted, and discuss how the application drives the choice of one detector or another (size measurement, aggregation studies, interaction studies, and other analyses, performed in both simple biological buffers and in more complex media, particularly serum) and makes Taylor Dispersion Analysis a highly versatile tool.

Bayesian Comparison of Covid Origins Hypotheses

Michael B. Weissman

Applying standard Bayesian methods to the most reliably known unclassified facts about the Covid epidemic allows crude estimation of the odds for research-related vs. purely zoonotic origins. Here a broadly inclusive zoonotic hypothesis is compared with a rather specific subset of the research-related hypothesis defined by known prior work and grant proposals. The important observations are the type of virus, the city of the first outbreak, the lack of known prior hosts, the anomalous early pattern of evolution, and two specific features of the viral sequence. The resulting odds strongly favor research. The odds remain fairly strong under inclusion of uncertainties in prior odds.

Bio-Inspired Auditory Processing: Coupling Parallel Resonate-and-Fire Dynamics with Ternary Recurrent Networks for Speech Recognition

Pavlos Poulos, Katelyn Sadorf, Themos Stafylakis, Costas A. Anastassiou

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) promise energy-efficient inference through sparse, event-driven computation, yet remain constrained on continuous, context-dependent tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR). A key bottleneck is the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron, whose membrane decay erases information across pauses and phonetic boundaries while it must both extract features and retain long-range context. Inspired by cortical separation of nonlinear filtering and somatic integration, we introduce the Spiking Ternary GRU (stGRU): a ternary-weight gated recurrent unit performs temporal filtering, while downstream Resonate-and-Fire (RF) neurons generate the binary spike stream driving the next block's event pathway. This makes binary event transmission a native training constraint rather than a separate deployment-time activation quantization step; together with ternary weights, learned projections avoid dense floating-point matrix MACs. Trained end-to-end on LibriSpeech-960, Small, Medium, and Large stGRUs reach 8.09%, 7.04%, and $6.44 \pm 0.17\%$ WER on test-clean without an external language model. To our knowledge, these are the first matrix-MAC-free spiking networks without a learned ANN sequence decoder to achieve single-digit WER on LibriSpeech. The architecture also achieves 10.88% test CER on Aishell-1 (9.28% on dev) when trained from scratch. Internally, trained networks develop a depth-graded hierarchy: shallow layers act as fast acoustic filters, while deep layers form slower semantic integrators whose latent representations segregate into part-of-speech manifolds and word-level spike events. Deep-layer firing rates follow lognormal distributions with a sparsity gradient, mirroring hierarchical processing observed in human cortex in vivo. Our biologically grounded architecture combines competitive ASR with favorable modeled compute and memory cost.

Learning Epidemic Threshold Equations from Simulation: REEBLRA, an Interpretable Bayesian Classification Framework for Reproduction Numbers

Jomar F. Rabajante, Norvin P. Bansilan, Maria Czarina T. Lagura-Arreza, Destiny SM. Lutero, Emerson R. Rico

The basic reproduction number, $R_0$, is central to infectious-disease modelling, yet analytic expressions can be difficult to derive for high-dimensional, nonlinear, or structurally complex models. We introduce REEBLRA ($R_0$ Equation Estimation using Bayesian Logistic Regression Algorithm), an interpretable framework that learns explicit surrogate equations for epidemic thresholds from parameter--response data. Rather than directly regressing $R_0$, REEBLRA estimates $\Pr\{R_0(\boldsymbolθ)>c\}$ for parameter vector $\boldsymbolθ$ and threshold $c$. It provides a binary equation for classifying epidemic invasion ($R_0>1$) and successive threshold equations defining ordered brackets that approximate $R_0$, the expected number of secondary infections caused by a typical case in a fully susceptible population. The resulting equations support parameter screening, sensitivity interpretation, threshold classification, and exploratory analysis when analytic derivation is cumbersome. Bayesian logistic regression using standardized polynomial-like features and proper weakly informative priors yields regularized estimates, posterior uncertainty, and finite coefficients under complete or quasi-complete separation. We establish a mechanistic--surrogate concordance principle linking the full fitted-score gradient to the local gradient of $\log R_0$ under explicit regularity and tangency assumptions, allowing conditional interpretation of threshold directions. Across seven analytic-label benchmarks associated with ODE models, including a 15-compartment five-group SIR system, five-fold binary AUC was 0.999--1.000 and ordered-bracket accuracy was 0.951--0.998. REEBLRA complements rather than replaces mechanistic derivations, bridging simulation, machine learning, and reproduction-number analysis. An accompanying Shiny app supports implementation.

QUBO-Based Conformer Selection with Quantum-Classical Hybrid Optimization for Multiconformer Ligand Modeling

Jiyun Lee, Haeseong Kim, Junho Lee, Joonsuk Huh

A ligand can adopt multiple conformations within the same binding site, and these conformations can interact differently with the surrounding protein. Multiconformer modeling must therefore determine which candidate conformations to retain and what occupancies to assign to them. An established automated approach uses mixed-integer quadratic programming (MIQP) with hard occupancy and conformer-count limits for this selection. Here, we reformulated conformer selection as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem with finite penalties and discrete occupancies. We solved the QUBO using a quantum-classical hybrid solver. We compared this workflow with the MIQP-based qFit-ligand workflow across 83 paired X-ray ligand entries using the same experimental inputs and the same qFit-ligand processing protocol. For each entry, both final models were evaluated against the same composite-omit map generated from the common input structure before the two workflows diverged. Quantum-qFit produced higher ligand-region real-space correlation coefficients (RSCC) in 62 entries 74.7% and lower occupancy-weighted torsional strain in 66 entries 79.5%. Similar directional trends remained in the 28 entries with matched final conformer counts. The molecular Electron Density for Individual Atoms score (EDIAm) decreased in most entries. This result shows that higher regional map agreement did not necessarily correspond to stronger atom-level density support. Overall, the results show that the conformer-selection formulation can change which ligand states are represented from the same experimental data and provide a QUBO framework for studying multiconformer ligand selection.

Vagus nerve stimulation strengthens beta- and gamma-rate auditory steady-state responses in rat cortex

Shinichi Kumagai, Tomoyo Isoguchi Shiramatsu, Karin Oshima, Yohei Ishishita, Kenji Ibayashi et al. (9 authors)

Background: Gamma entrainment using sensory stimulation (GENUS) is under study as a treatment for neurological disease, but cortical engagement is variable. Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) modulates cortical state and sound-evoked beta-gamma activity, suggesting that it may strengthen responses to rhythmic sensory input. Objective: To determine whether VNS strengthens auditory steady-state responses across beta and gamma rates and whether the effect depends on cortical region and sensory drive. Methods: Electrocorticography (ECoG) was recorded from core and belt auditory cortex, insular auditory field (IAF), and anterior insular cortex (AIC) in seven isoflurane-anesthetized rats before and 90 min after initiation of 30-Hz cervical VNS. Responses to 500-ms click trains at 20 and 40 Hz were quantified using inter-trial phase clustering (ITPC), event-related spectral perturbation (ERSP), and debiased weighted phase-lag index (WPLI). Results: VNS increased click-evoked P1-N1 amplitude. During 40-Hz trains, 40-Hz ITPC and ERSP increased only in auditory core. During 20-Hz trains, 20-Hz ITPC increased in core, belt, and IAF, whereas ERSP increased only in core; 40-Hz harmonic responses increased only in core. AIC showed no change. Within auditory cortex, WPLI increased selectively at frequencies present in each click train and not in the alpha band. Conclusions: VNS strengthened beta- and gamma-rate stimulus-locked responses in auditory-driven cortex without globally increasing synchrony. VNS may therefore enhance auditory cortical target engagement during GENUS, although effects on disease pathology or clinical outcomes remain untested.

An autoencoder outperforms two transformers on prediction of gene expression from chromatin accessibility

Jesse G. Meyer

Models that predict RNA abundance from chromatin accessibility are rarely compared under the same features, targets and metrics. We built one benchmark on 126,604 paired single-nucleus profiles from 19 human studies with a shared vocabulary of 50,000 peaks and 3,185 genes, ten 5x2 cross-validation folds and one scoring target, and compared seven implemented pipelines and two reference predictors across 70 trained runs; each pipeline keeps its own input normalisation and native training target. The scButterfly-B pipeline, built on dual variational autoencoders, outperformed every transformer pipeline, including an autoregressive Perceiver model (Omic Translator) and a support-free reformulation of Cisformer, on per-gene and within-cell Spearman correlation in all sixty paired fold comparisons, with 3.8 times shorter training. Transformer pipelines come within 0.02-0.04 on within-cell profile shape but lag by 0.10-0.24 on per-gene correlation. On two withheld cell types per-gene correlation is low; controls show the podocyte values are typical of within-type correlation for seen cell types of the same size, whereas Paneth values match a cell-agnostic shuffle for every Omic Translator arm. Within the transformer family, prespecified ablations show the Perceiver encoder was worth +0.0439 in within-donor per-gene correlation over full self-attention, a chromosome-aware genomic attention prior reduced it by 0.0096 (exact donor-level sign-flip test, Holm-adjusted p = 1.2e-4), and decoder output order had no detectable effect. A training-mean profile reaches 0.314 within-cell correlation with no input and a constant at the true expressed-gene positions reaches 0.996 per-gene correlation, so oracle-support protocols are reported only as diagnostics. Code, results and an audited release are provided.

A Reverse-Gold Theorem: Positive-Data Identification by Substrate-Bounded Contrastive Learners

Rakesh Sengupta

Gold's theorem shows that no learner can identify an arbitrary language in a superfinite class from positive presentations alone. We prove a positive result of a different shape. Fix an innate recursive contrast operator $R$ mapping any string to a finite set of structurally bounded perturbations of it, and a discriminative scorer of bounded capacity trained by InfoNCE-style contrastive estimation \citep{vandenoord2018,macollins2018} on self-generated perturbations. Under a faithfulness condition relating operator, scorer, and target, the trained scorer achieves PAC conditional identification from positive data alone: with high probability it ranks all but an $\varepsilon$-fraction of positives above every out-of-language perturbation in their own orbit, with sample complexity polynomial in the capacity, the orbit size, the score bound, and $1/\varepsilon$. The negatives are constructed by the learner, not supplied by an oracle, and no global normalizing constant over strings is ever computed. The misranking bound follows from the geometry of the contrastive loss---per-example loss floors plus a definite loss increment under misranking---yielding an $\varepsilon^{-2}$ rate, together with an explicit approximation gap that quantifies the cost of in-language (``colliding'') perturbations and vanishes for orbit-pure operators. The ranking guarantee lifts to a global membership classifier under a margin condition, and the same operator composed with the learned scorer is a normalization-free Metropolis--Hastings generator. We instantiate the hypotheses for substitutable context-free languages \citep{clarkeyraud2007} and the subregular classes \citep{heinz2010,hayashikamigaito2025}, and state an open conjecture locating the construction in the mildly context-sensitive hierarchy.

Generic finiteness of solutions to parametrized systems of generalized polynomial inequalities

Stefan Müller

We study parametrized systems of $\mathit{generalized}$ polynomial inequalities, $(c \circ x^B) \in C$, for a positive variable vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n_>$, a $\mathit{real}$ exponent matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$, a positive parameter vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^m_>$, and a positive, blunt coefficient cone $C \subset \mathbb{R}^m_>$. This setting includes (i) generalized polynomial equations, $A \, (c \circ x^B) = 0$, arising e.g. from power-law dynamical systems with real exponents (and involving a real coefficient matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n' \times m}$), and (ii) generalized semi-algebraic sets. Our main result guarantees the generic finiteness of solutions modulo unavoidable exponential fibers. Building on a framework for parametrized $\mathit{polynomial}$ systems developed by Müller and Regensburger, we reformulate the problem as a system of $\mathit{binomial}$ equations on the coefficient set $P = C \cap Δ\subseteq \mathbb{R}^m_>$, where $Δ$ is a product of (standard) simplices. Solvability is governed by the monomial-dependency subspace $D$. Using elementary arguments from analysis and topology, including a logarithmic boundary analysis and Sard's theorem, we control the accumulation of solutions on the boundary and in the interior of $P$. We show that, if $\dim P \le \dim D$, then generically in both the measure-theoretic and topological sense, there are finitely many solutions on $P$ (and hence finitely many exponential fibers of solutions in $\mathbb{R}^n_>$). As an application, we prove generic finiteness of steady states (within forward-invariant sets) for parametrized power-law dynamical systems with real exponents.
