Fractional Charge at Dislocations from Band-Inversion Cancellation

Vladimir Juričić, Rodrigo Soto-Garrido

Band inversions with opposite Chern numbers cancel in the Hall response and yield counterpropagating boundary modes that can gap out. However, a bulk probe of the topology that survives such a cancellation is lacking. We here show that dislocations in inversion-symmetric two-band crystals with cancelling band inversions bind a half-integer charge, which reveals the quantized bulk polarization of the bond-centered obstructed atomic limit realized by such crystals. To demonstrate this, we continuously deform a broad class of two-band lattice models into decoupled chains, without closing the bulk gap or breaking the inversion symmetry, where the dislocation charge follows from a count of occupied bonds. Notably, the fractional part of this charge is insensitive to local perturbations of the dislocation core, and it therefore persists after the in-gap core state is removed, as we show analytically in the chain limit and numerically away from it. In the Weyl-semimetal counterpart, the response to a magnetic flux separates this crystalline charge from the charge set by the Chern number of each momentum slice. We propose that a half-integer core charge that persists after the in-gap peak disappears from the local spectrum can be detected in artificial electronic and optical lattices, with metamaterials providing complementary access to the core modes.

Emergent Fatigue Dynamics from Foundational Interatomic Potentials

Jingye Tan, Benjamin A. Jasperson, Eric Rothchild, Anders Johansson, Rèmi Dingreville et al. (7 authors)

Foundational machine-learned interatomic potentials are trained on large, diverse DFT datasets, but their validation rests on equilibrium states, which does not guarantee correct emergent behavior in large-scale molecular dynamics. Where no direct experimental validation is available, comparisons to physically motivated potentials are valuable for drawing insight. We distinguish static properties, which are thermally accessible, from emergent properties, which require work from an external fatigue stress to drive rare events, and compare the potentials on both. An EAM potential and the NequIP foundational potential both agree closely with reference data for many near-equilibrium properties, but NequIP predicts an anomalously low intrinsic stacking fault energy ($\sim7$ times lower than EAM) and thus predicts far more plastic deformation under fatigue cycling of a nanoscale flaw modeled after the classical Griffith crack problem. Per-atom energy distributions after loading show that the two potentials differ most at energies far above what is thermally accessible. We also document memory-related crashes and silent extrapolation as practical concerns for practitioners of these foundational model forms.

Constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks in Finite Element Model Updating: Closed-Form Hyperelastic Strain Energies from Heterogeneous Experiments

Kian P. Abdolazizi, Kevin Linka, Christian J. Cyron

Calibrating a hyperelastic constitutive model for a soft material traditionally poses two problems: an expert must choose its functional form, and several homogeneous tests must determine its parameters. Neural constitutive models solve the first, but either remain black boxes or draw on a library of candidate terms fixed in advance. Full-field measurements, such as those from digital image correlation (DIC), promise to solve the second, replacing those tests by a single heterogeneous one. Two gaps remain: flexibility during training with interpretability afterwards, and a way to train such a model on one heterogeneous test. Here we close both. We embed a constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold network (CKAN) in finite element model updating (FEMU) and train it by minimizing the mismatch between the predicted and the measured kinematic fields and reaction forces. Pruning and symbolic regression then reduce the trained network to a compact closed-form strain energy that satisfies every constitutive requirement. On perforated natural-rubber membranes, the CKANs reproduce the DIC displacement fields with median errors between one and two percent, and transfer to a hole pattern withheld from training. On a perforated silicone plate, a CKAN trained on one stretch profile at a single load reproduces that profile and the measured reaction force to within a few percent. On an aortic specimen inflated under pressure, an anisotropic CKAN predicts the inflated three-dimensional shape. The discovered closed-form energies match the CKANs they replace on the training data and can be compared directly with established models.

Two distinct notions of speed in antiferromagnets

Paul Herrgen, Stephan Wust, Christin Schmitt, Yaryna Lytvynenko, Lorenzo Baldrati et al. (13 authors)

The terahertz resonance frequencies of antiferromagnets characterize their precessional dynamics but do not necessarily imply faster laser-induced quenching of magnetic order. Here, we investigate the distinct roles of magnetic compensation and electronic scattering phase space by comparing ultrafast demagnetization in the antiferromagnetic charge-transfer insulator NiO, the metallic antiferromagnet Mn2Au, and a ferromagnetic Ni reference. The extracted response in Mn2Au is approximately four times faster than in NiO and substantially faster than in Ni. These observations are consistent with exchange-mediated scattering between antiparallel sublattices enabling rapid loss of antiferromagnetic order without requiring net angular-momentum transfer to an external reservoir, provided sufficient electronic scattering phase space is available. In insulating NiO, restricted electronic scattering channels reduces the ultrafast response. Our results distinguish ultrafast demagnetization from exchange-enhanced precession and identify the interplay of spin-flip probability and electronic scattering rate as a key factor governing the nonequilibrium response of compensated magnets.

Perfumer: A Large Language Model-based Agentic Framework Accelerating Structural Relaxation and Electronic Band Prediction for First-Principle Calculation

Zhiwei Li, Yuhao Zhou, Yikai Yang, Haonan Wang, Qing Ye et al. (7 authors)

First-principles structural relaxation and band calculations require substantial computation and expertise. Here we present Perfumer, a large language model-based agentic framework that couples Quantum ESPRESSO with two predictive models. Across tested structures, pre-relaxation with Perfumer-relax model followed by QE refinement yielded a 2.75-fold overall speedup over QE alone. Perfumer-band model combines a periodic graph neural network with a latent Hermitian spectral head returns 12 band energies at any requested wavevector, preserving reciprocal lattice periodicity and time-reversal symmetry without constructing Hamiltonian. Trained on 125,153 self-curated band structures, Perfumer-band model reaches a median per-material mean absolute error of 0.157 eV on 2,607 test materials, including lanthanide, actinide and magnetic compounds, from held-out composition families spanning 85 elements, taking about 97 ms per material on two consumer CPU cores. The agent turns natural-language requests into relaxation-to-band workflows, from a single uploaded structure to automatically constructed new candidates and returns predicted properties.

Hybrid Plasmon-Phonon Eigenmodes and Vortex States in a Graphene Ring on a Silica Substrate

Yanxu Guo, Oleh Demianyk, Vladimir R. Tuz

We investigate hybrid plasmon-phonon eigenmodes and localized electromagnetic vortex states in a graphene ring supported by a dispersive silica substrate. The interaction between graphene plasmons and infrared-active optical phonons of the substrate gives rise to distinct spectral branches of surface polaritons, whose dispersion characteristics are established analytically and validated by full-wave numerical simulations. Geometric confinement of these hybrid surface excitations produces spectrally separated families of localized multipolar eigenmodes with corresponding azimuthal symmetries. Particular attention is devoted to quadrupolar eigenstates, which exhibit nearly identical normalized spatial field distributions despite their substantial spectral separation and different attenuation characteristics. We demonstrate that coherent superposition of orthogonal quadrupolar field patterns gives rise to localized vortex states with opposite azimuthal phase winding. The preservation of the spatial eigenmode structure across distinct spectral branches is consistent with a quasistatic description in which the resonator geometry governs the modal symmetry, whereas the dispersive material response determines the spectral properties. These findings reveal a route toward controlling structured electromagnetic near fields through plasmon--phonon hybridization and establish a physical basis for multiband vortex states in deeply subwavelength graphene resonators.

Triplet adiabatic potential energy surfaces for the O($^3$P) + C$_2$(X$^1Σ_g^+$, a$^3Π_u$) reaction and quasiclassical trajectory dynamics

A. Veselinova-Marinova, S. Gómez-Carrasco, L. González-Sánchez, A. Zanchet, P. G. Jambrina et al. (6 authors)

We present three global adiabatic potential energy surfaces (PESs) for the O($^3$P) + C$_2$(X $^1Σ_g^+$, a $^3Π_u$) to CO(X$^1Σ^+$) + C($^3$P) reaction, computed at the icMRCI+Q/aug-cc-pVTZ level. This reaction is relevant to combustion chemistry, atmospheric processes, and astrochemistry. Since the energy difference between the $^1Σ_g^+$ and $^3Π_u$ states of C$_2$ is smaller than the zero-point energy, both states mix upon interaction with the approaching O atom. Consequently, the PESs exhibit a double-well topography in the reactant valley. Beyond this feature, the three PESs display markedly different topographies. The 1 $^3A''$ PES features a deep minimum 7.16 eV below the O + C$_2$ entrance channel. In contrast, the 2 $^3A''$ and 1 $^3A'$ surfaces possess shallower collinear wells ($\approx$ 5.7 eV) and strong angular anisotropy arising from avoided crossings with higher-lying triplet states. The analytical fits reproduce the ab initio data with root-mean-square (RMS) deviations of 0.022--0.028 eV over an energy interval exceeding 13 eV. Quasiclassical trajectory (QCT) calculations reveal higher reactivity on the barrierless $1\,^3A''$ surface compared to the $2\,^3A''$ PES, where an angle-dependent entrance barrier reduces the cross sections. Furthermore, cross sections for reactions initiated from C$_2$(X $^1Σ_g^+$) and C$_2$(a $^3Π_u$) are remarkably similar for the three PESs, regardless of the initial rovibrational state.

Unusual increase in electrical and thermal conductivity of severely deformed CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy: Roles of disordering and nanostructuring in electron and phonon transport

Jacqueline Hidalgo-Jiménez, Payam Edalati, Md Amirul Islam, Makoto Arita, Dmitry Chernyshov et al. (7 authors)

This work investigates how defect engineering and nanostructuring via severe plastic deformation influence electrical and thermal transport in the high-entropy alloy CrMnFeCoNi. High-pressure torsion (HPT) for up to N = 4 turns produces a hierarchical defect structure, including deformation twins at low strains, dislocations and Lomer-Cottrell locks at intermediate strains, and a high fraction of high-angle grain boundaries at large strains. While hardness naturally increases and saturates at ~520 Hv, both electrical and thermal conductivity exhibit an unusual increase upon HPT processing (from 0.953 MS m-1 and 10.3 W m-1 K-1 after homogenization to 1.003 +- 0.005 MS m-1 and 14.4 W m-1 K-1 after N = 4). Analysis based on the Wiedemann-Franz law indicates a modest increase in electron transport (~5%) and a significant increase in phonon transport (~100%) after HPT processing. The phonon mean free path remains small on the order of 0.6-1.1 nm, and comparison with the Cahill-Pohl model shows that heat transport occurs near the minimum thermal conductivity limit, where vibrational modes are strongly scattered. This suggests that the enhancement in thermal transport is not governed by conventional defect scattering, but it is hypothesized that modifications in atomic-scale disorder, such as deformation-induced short-range ordering, are responsible for this unusual behavior. Specific heat capacity increases after HPT, consistent with stored defect energy. The linear thermal expansion exhibits a non-monotonic behavior, decreasing initially due to internal stresses and relaxation effects, and increasing at higher strains due to grain boundary-induced anharmonicity. These findings highlight the complex interplay between defects, atomic-scale disorder, and nanostructure in governing the electrical and thermal properties of severely deformed high-entropy alloys via electron and phonon scattering.

Reward misalignment as an opportunity for materials discovery

Ryotaro Okabe, Shoki Kishida, Abhijatmedhi Chotrattanapituk, Dominic Bollettieri, Takuya Isogawa et al. (8 authors)

Reward misalignment is usually regarded as a failure of artificial intelligence. In materials discovery with generative AI models, however, disagreement between predefined rewards and expert judgment may reveal materials that the rewards overlook. We investigate this possibility using electronic flat bands as a prime example. Experts mostly agree with a numerical bandwidth score, but their disagreements are systematic and cannot be explained by an estimated 5% rate of mistaken labels. We learn the experts' preferences from pairwise comparisons and use them to guide a crystal diffusion model. The learned preferences and numerical score agree on the flattest bands but diverge elsewhere. For example, the preferences favor KCo2As2 and CoGa, reported compounds with partially flat bands that the score would discard. The framework also extends to synthesizability and phonon properties. Our findings suggest that reward misalignment can expose opportunities for materials discovery beyond what fixed rewards are designed to find.

Dual Action of Electrolyte Additives in High-Voltage Li-Ion Cells

Aidin I. OBrien, Bingning Wang, Stephen E. Trask, Adam Tornheim, Chen Liao et al. (6 authors)

Electrolyte additives have outsized effects on cell performance, but determining their mechanism of action can be difficult. Here, we evaluate whether simple electrochemical tests can reveal which electrode (cathode, anode or both) is targeted by specific additives. Our experiments quantified the rates of reductive and oxidative side-reactions in NMC532 vs. graphite cells containing 12 different electrolyte formulations. We found that gains in interphasial stability were highly correlated, with successful additives decreasing both types of side-reactions by similar magnitude. This dual action could be indicative of the role of crosstalk on cell degradation, as soluble species formed on one electrode can decompose at the other end of the cell. Although electrochemical tests are insufficient for understanding mechanisms of improvement, they can reveal interesting effects that complement other characterization tools.
