Old Matches, New Frames: Schedule-Gated Stereo Memory from Human Vision to Machine Matching

Ruiqi Liu, Yang Lu, Yanlan Kang

Human vision and video stereo networks both reuse disparity evidence across time, yet a refresh of the input can invalidate a match formed milliseconds earlier. What tells a system whether an old match still counts? We answer this from limited human data in three steps. First, nine temporal mechanisms share a frozen binocular-energy front end and an equal-capacity readout, so held-out behavior isolates the temporal selection rule: on 240 conditions from 10 observers, down-weighting aged evidence that has lost binocular support lowers subject RMSE from 12.6% to 10.3% and reproduces a period-dependent reversed-depth illusion, across all 59 readings of the unpublished protocol and 8 front-end perturbations. Second, four propositions establish what such data can identify: gating is identified at the level of the refresh schedule, and candidate binding is invisible to standard stimuli but exposed by a single-eye geometry flip that separates the readouts by up to 7.1%. Third, the identified rule becomes a two-field stereo memory that separates sample validity from hypothesis support. It removes a 50 ms recovery lag after geometry flips, and its regime analysis explains why plain integration stays preferable on MPI-Sintel, where 71.1% of pixels change appearance but not disparity.

Learning Trajectories Leave Decodable Traces in Neural Network Weight Space and Internal Representations

Patrick Krauss, Achim Schilling, Andreas Maier, Thomas Kinfe, Claus Metzner

Neural networks trained on the same task develop related weight-space structures, yet remain distinguishable after learning. Do these differences merely reflect alternative solutions, or do they preserve information about individual learning histories? We tested whether final weights reveal initialization, task order, and earlier trajectory states. Training reorganized the ensemble: initial pairwise distances did not consistently predict final distances. Nevertheless, all 30 trained networks were correctly matched to their initial weights among ten candidates per dataset. Networks sharing an initialization also developed recognizable final hidden representations across different batch schedules, although direct comparisons with initial activity did not recover identity. Learning order remained readable as well. A linear probe decoded the order of two earlier tasks with 100% accuracy on 80 held-out networks after ten epochs of a common final task. Earlier intermediate weight states were partly predictable, but individual initial weights could not be reliably reconstructed. Final weights thus retain selective historical information rather than a complete trajectory record. Perturbed networks recovered classification performance without returning to their unperturbed training paths. This separation persisted across datasets, intervention times, and network layers. Moreover, 2,157 of 2,160 aligned endpoint pairs showed no positive loss barrier at sampled interpolation points, indicating low-loss connections between distinct solutions rather than convergence to one weight vector. Together, these findings show that a shared task constrains networks to related successful solutions without erasing their individual histories. Final weights reflect not only what a network has learned, but also where learning began and how it unfolded.

Inferring Neural Activity Changes under Recording Uncertainty with Transformers and Variational Inference

Nilson Chapagain, Pooja Chapagain

A difference in recorded firing rate across days may reflect a change in neuronal activity, missed spikes or recording a different neuron. We present a statistical approach that combines neuron tracking with Bayesian estimates of stationary mean recorded firing rates. Bounds under assumed differences in spike detection connect recorded rates to biological rate changes. We assess recording loss and uncertainty in rate changes in simulations, and count-model fit in recordings. We also compare two transformer estimators with Gaussian and flow variational inference under a shared temporal negative-binomial model. Flow variational inference agrees most closely with reference posteriors, while predictive performance varies across mice. Rate-change intervals contain true simulated changes less often than intended. In UnitMatch recordings from five mice, some inferred changes remain supported when spike detection is allowed to differ between days; others depend on equal detection. These interpretations require a correct neuron match. The approach identifies which inferred rate changes remain supported under specified recording assumptions and where uncertainty limits interpretation.

A multilayer network-based modelling framework for epizootic haemorrhagic disease virus transmission in France

Viney Kumar, Gaël Beaunée, Sandie Arnoux, Fabien Corbière, Benoît Durand et al. (6 authors)

Epizootic haemorrhagic disease virus serotype 8 (EHDV-8) emerged in France in September 2023 but its transmission dynamics remain poorly characterised. We adapted a stochastic, spatially explicit compartmental model developed for bluetongue to reconstruct the 2023-24 EHDV-8 epidemic in cattle in mainland France. The model represented transmission across cantons, incorporating cattle demography, pasture cover, relative Culicoides abundance and temperature-dependent vector processes. Three networks represented between-canton spread through short-range vector dispersal (pasture network), non-commercial cattle movements between pastures of the same farm (farm network), and cattle trade (trade network). Using approximate Bayesian computation with sequential Monte Carlo (ABC-SMC), we calibrated the model to canton-week seroprevalence estimates derived from bulk-tank milk measurements. Calibration narrowed the plausible range of the baseline vector-mediated transmission parameter and favoured higher movement intensities through the farm network, although movement intensity remained uncertain. Simulation-based parameter recovery showed that calibration generally identified parameter regions capable of reproducing epidemic patterns comparable to the pseudo-observed data, although predictive performance varied among datasets and was typically lower than that obtained with the generating parameters. When applied to observed data, the calibrated model captured several features of the observed weekly median seroprevalence across sampled cantons, although discrepancies remained at some sampling times and predictive uncertainty was substantial. These results demonstrate how bulk-tank milk surveillance can inform the reconstruction of EHDV-8 epidemic patterns within a national-scale transmission framework. This framework provides a basis for future evaluation and optimisation of vaccination strategies.

Biases and Limits of AlphaFold Predictions for Non-Globular Proteins

Maria Cristina Aspromonte, Alexander M. Monzon, Miguel A. Andrade-Navarro, Oriol Bárcenas, Viktor Bartosik et al. (25 authors)

AlphaFold has transformed protein structure prediction, making structural models accessible across the life sciences. We explore experimentally validated cases of non-globular proteins to uncover where predictions diverge from biologically relevant structural states and how molecular context shapes these discrepancies, guiding a more informed interpretation of AlphaFold models.

Micrograph B-factor estimation via Wilson statistics

Ayelet Heimowitz, Amit Singer

The single-particle cryo-electron microscopy imaging process is known to cause exponential decay in the amplitude and power spectra of proteins. This decay is modeled by the B factor and is currently corrected as a post-processing stage of the 3D reconstruction. As a consequence, all experimental images in the dataset are assumed to share the same B factor. In this paper, we suggest a novel approach wherein the B factor is determined and corrected as a preprocessing step to the reconstruction. We show such an approach can improve reconstruction resolution.

SCOPE: Single-Cell Omics Prediction from Histology via RNA-Protein Prior Enhancement

Leyang Tao, Xuan Gong, Luyuan Xie, Arjun Beniwal, Yuqi Tan et al. (6 authors)

Predicting the RNA and protein content of cells from routine H&E images could extend spatial molecular profiling to existing slide archives. However, both H&E-spatial proteomics and H&E-spatial transcriptomics paired datasets are limited by the number of available markers and the insufficient availability of high-quality, cell-resolved data. We present SCOPE (Single-Cell Omics Prediction via RNA-protein prior Enhancement), a unified framework that leverages biologically complementary information between RNA and proteins to address the limited availability of single-omics supervision. Our key insight is that, although RNA and proteins represent distinct molecular modalities, their biological relationships provide valuable shared knowledge that can benefit single-omics prediction tasks. To this end, SCOPE introduces biologically informed biomarker priors to enhance shared gene-level representations, enabling knowledge transfer across RNA and protein modalities through gene-protein correspondence and molecular associations. By exploiting these complementary relationships, SCOPE enables both prediction tasks to benefit from a shared backbone enriched with cross-omics biological knowledge. Furthermore, its biomarker correlation design facilitates the prediction of unseen markers, extending spatially resolved molecular profiling beyond directly supervised targets. Across test datasets spanning 16 organs, SCOPE improves the average RNA Pearson correlation over the strongest baseline by 8.8%. On two external protein cohorts, it improves the average Pearson correlation over the strongest baseline by 9.7% for seen markers and 11.9% for unseen markers.

The Shape of Individual Fitness Distributions and the Dynamics of Ecological Communities

Julie Rowlett, Leo Tingqvist

Developing a mechanistic understanding of biogeography and biodiversity that predicts species abundances, distributions, and fluctuations is a central goal of community ecology. We develop a model, that can be seen as an extension of Hubbell's neutral theory, in which a focal species competes with the aggregate of all other species in a local community of fixed size. Each individual has its own competitive ability, and the shape of the distribution of these abilities is incorporated in the model, represented by distributions rather than single species-level values. Under complete mixing, symmetric competitive encounters without ties recover Hubbell's neutral model. We further incorporate immigration from a larger metacommunity and derive a diffusion approximation for the focal species' relative abundance. The model shows that competitive ability distributions with the same mean and variance can nevertheless yield different patterns of local abundance. Thus, species that appear equivalent when summarized by trait statistics need not be ecologically equivalent. Our framework links individual-level variability to population-level persistence and abundance and provides a way to examine how competition and immigration jointly shape ecological outcomes.

A structure-based model of the bacterial chemosensory array reveals how the scaffolding protein CheW tunes sensory responses

David Hathcock, Yingxin Zheng, Yuhai Tu

We introduce a structure-based Ising model of the bacterial chemosensory array to investigate how the scaffolding protein CheW tunes signaling responses. The model couples the activity states of core signaling units and of CheW subunits within hexameric rings on a lattice with the structure observed in cryoEM experiments. Recent experiments show that increasing CheW participation in hexameric rings raises response cooperativity in non-adapting cells and sensitivity in adaptation-proficient cells. Our lattice model quantitatively reproduces both effects, while mean-field theory shows that CheW increases the effective coupling between core units, sharpening the response in both cell types. The statistics of CheW placement (complete hexameric rings versus random distribution) substantially alter how responses vary with CheW abundance. Moreover, different coupling strengths can yield the same static response yet markedly different activity fluctuations and dynamics. CheW abundance further controls a crossover from relaxational to oscillatory fluctuations, whose location depends on the spatial distribution of CheW. These dynamical signatures, including oscillations predicted near native CheW levels, are testable by single-cell dynamics measurements. Combined with CheW titration and our modeling results, these measurements can probe the array's assembly dynamics and allosteric couplings. Beyond the chemosensory array, our approach can generalize to other large protein complexes, providing a framework to study the functional role of scaffolding proteins and allosteric interfaces in signal processing.

A Dual-SOM Learner Model of Instructional Efficiency within Cognitive Load Theory

Spyridon Revithis

This paper presents a novel methodological framework, integrating a dual self-organising map (SOM) neural network architecture with Cognitive Load Theory (CLT), for assessing instructional efficiency within learning environments. By representing curriculum content as a directed acyclic graph of topic dependencies, the proposed model captures collective learning behaviour across two core dimensions: academic performance and subjective/introspective mental effort. During SOM training, representative data sets of successful learner trajectories are compressed into two topologically ordered, identically structured neural networks: PSOM (performance space) and ESOM (mental effort space). By pairing these behavioural maps, the model serves as a decision-support tool that enables instructors to simultaneously pinpoint structural instructional design defects, such as hidden topic prerequisites, and evaluate collective cognitive load profiles. The implementation and performance of the PSOM component (and, by extension, its symmetrical twin, ESOM) is linked to an earlier modelling study employing a SOM with identical structure and encoding semantics situated in the same domain (i.e., a learning environment). This paper combines the study of behavioural phenomena with theoretical work on human cognitive architecture via SOM neurocomputational cognitive behavioural modelling.
