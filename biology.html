Mammalian gonadal development: origins, specification and differentiation of ovary and testis cell lineages

Serge Nef, Maeva Guy, Marie-Christine Chaboissier

Testicular and ovarian development requires the coordinated formation and assembly of multiple cell types that enable these organs to fulfill their roles in sexual differentiation, reproduction, and endocrine function. A defining feature of gonadal development is that both testes and ovaries arise from a common bipotential primordium, the genital ridge, composed of multipotent progenitors. In this Review, we synthesize current knowledge on the origin, specification, differentiation, and function of the major fetal gonadal somatic lineages, drawing primarily on mouse and human studies. We describe how the adrenogonadal primordium (AGP) gives rise to the gonadal primordium (GP), how coelomic epithelium-derived progenitors generate the supporting, steroidogenic/stromal, and supporting-like cell lineages, and how these progenitors become progressively fate-restricted. We highlight recent evidence for early lineage priming within spatially organized domains of the coelomic epithelium. We further examine the molecular pathways and cellular processes governing sex-specific differentiation of these lineages and discuss remaining gaps in our understanding of how bipotential gonads differentiate into testes or ovaries, and their implication for human health.

BioEmu2: Scalable Emulation of Biomolecular Dynamics

Michael Gastegger, Adam E. Foster, José Jiménez-Luna, Tim Hempel, Sarah Lewis et al. (30 authors)

Despite major advances in structure prediction, modelling the motions and interactions that drive biomolecular function at scale remains a central challenge. We introduce BioEmu2, a generative model for atomically resolved biomolecular structures combining static structure prediction, equilibrium sampling and time evolution, that samples orders of magnitude faster than molecular dynamics (MD). Trained on experimental and synthetic structures and extensive MD data, it supports multi-component systems containing proteins, nucleic acids, modified residues, ligands and ions. Structure-prediction accuracy is comparable to top open-weight biomolecular folding models, and a context parallel mode enables prediction of megadalton complexes. Equilibrium distributions approximate MD references with free-energy errors near 1 kcal/mol across tested proteins and complexes. Time propagation predicts long-timescale dynamical trajectories and kinetics of binding and conformational changes. Generated structures can be controlled by conditioning on residue contacts or steering with user-defined bias potentials. Enhanced sampling enables free energy calculation involving rare states. BioEmu2 makes the study of biomolecular ensembles and dynamics scalable.

Spatial model of neurogliovascular unit: arachidonic acid can control the stability

Darya V. Verveyko, Andrey Yu. Verisokin, Pavel O. Lukin

Arachidonic acid metabolites can have both vasoconstrictor and vasodilator effects, thereby influencing neuronal activity. In this work, to analyze possible patterns of activity of nervous tissue in response to changes in the metabolic profile, a mathematical model of a spatially distributed system of connected neurogliovascular units was developed. We simulate the influence of arachidonic acid metabolites on the dynamics of the neurogliovascular unit during cortical spreading depression in order to determine the ways in which astrocytic activity influences the spatiotemporal characteristics of the propagating cortical depression wave. It is assumed that in the future the results of the theoretical study can be used to find ways to remove nervous tissue from pathological conditions that occur in epilepsy, migraines and a number of neurodegenerative diseases, including those associated with the occurrence of cortical depression waves.

Self-organized fractional silencing of pyramidal neurons under global depolarization in a spatial neuron-glial microcircuit model

Stanislav V. Lagosha, Andrey Yu. Verisokin, Darya V. Verveyko, Alyona V. Berezhnaya, Nadezda A. Brazhe et al. (6 authors)

Pathological transitions in nervous tissue, including epileptiform discharges and cortical spreading depolarization, depend on the interplay between cellular and interstitial mechanisms. We develop a biophysically detailed two-dimensional model of the neurogliovascular unit (NGVU) integrating neuronal populations in the Hodgkin-Huxley formalism, astrocyte-regulated extracellular potassium dynamics, extracellular neurotransmitter spillover, and noradrenergic neuromodulation. Numerical simulations show that inhibitory interneurons enter depolarization block (DB) at significantly lower extracellular potassium concentrations and stimulation intensities than pyramidal neurons. Early loss of inhibition shifts the local excitation-inhibition balance and triggers network hyperexcitability. In the spatially extended network, exceeding a critical stimulation threshold induces a transition from heterogeneous but spatially uniform firing to network fragmentation into stable active microclusters with self-sustained high-frequency firing. Pyramidal neurons with lower excitatory synaptic input receive less recurrent excitation, which protects them from DB. By continuously releasing potassium ions and glutamate into the extracellular space, pyramidal neurons drive strong depolarization in more densely connected neighbors and push them into deep DB. Simulations of localized astrocytic dysfunction predict a spatial limit for potassium clearance defects. Small damaged cores are fully compensated by diffusion and uptake by surrounding healthy astrocyte units. However, above a critical damage size, activity-dependent potassium buildup and firing positive feedback disrupt homeostasis in surrounding units, promoting persistent depolarized centers with self-sustained, spatially segregated pathological activity.

The Four-Step Action Loop with Feedback: A teachable perception-action framework for everyday self-regulation

Sundeep Kapila

Everyday stress is largely a learned pattern: a familiar cue, a fast reading of threat, an automatic reaction and a body that stays activated. Body-based practices such as mindfulness, interoceptive training, Somatic Experiencing and yoga target these patterns, but they are usually taught as techniques without a map of the mechanism they act on. Pain neuroscience education suggests that understanding such a mechanism can itself change experience. No comparable model exists for everyday self-regulation. This paper proposes the Four-Step Action Loop with Feedback, developed over six cohorts of a workshop for non-clinical adults. The loop describes any action as four steps: Sense (exteroception, proprioception and interoception), Process (perception as inference, weighted by context and safety), Choose (habitual reaction or goal-directed response after a pause) and Execute (autonomic and somatic output). Feedback from each outcome acts beneath all four steps, through synaptic plasticity, habit formation and allostatic load. The paper integrates the perception-action cycle, information-processing stages of choice, interoceptive inference, the autonomic space model and the defence cascade. It places a set of practices on the loop: full sensory awareness, grounding, orientation, resourcing, the pause, reduced stimulus and choice, slow movement and relaxation, each with a proposed mechanism and an assessment of the evidence. It also describes the model's translation into four practitioner skills. Seven testable propositions are derived for future evaluation. The model is offered as a teachable framework for wellbeing, not a treatment.

Critical slowing down in hive temperature does not provide actionable early warnings of honey bee colony collapse

Daniele Proverbio, Els Weinans, Davide Frizzera, Desiderato Annoscia, Francesco Nazzi et al. (6 authors)

Early warning signals (EWS) based on critical slowing down (CSD) have been proposed as model-independent indicators of approaching ecological regime shifts and may help anticipate honeybee colony collapse. However, whether theoretically expected signals can be reliably observed and provide useful prediction in real-world systems remains uncertain. Here, we combine structural analysis, mechanistic modeling and longitudinal observations to assess the dynamical plausibility, detectability and predictive accuracy of CSD-based EWS for honeybee colony collapse. Our results show that the presence of EWS is not equivalent to successful prediction of the transition. The analyzed models exhibit regime shifts accompanied by CSD, but detecting these signals in finite and noisy observations requires longer and more densely sampled time series than typically available for practical monitoring. Moreover, in empirical temperature records, CSD-like trends were detectable but provided limited discrimination between colonies that subsequently collapsed and those that survived. Declining mean temperature provided slightly greater discrimination than conventional CSD indicators, although its predictive value remained modest. Non-Gaussian correlated noise and the imperfect correspondence between hive temperature and colony strength further constrained EWS performance. These findings show that EWS can exist at the dynamical level without becoming useful predictors at the observational level, at least for our case study, thus cautioning against treating CSD-based indicators as generic predictors of ecological collapse.

From Novel Combinations to Informative Absence: Structural permission, communicative opportunity, and form choice in language learning

Yanzhe Zhao

Language learners must infer which novel expressions are grammatically licensed and when continued absence is evidence against them. Our generative theory unites these capacities via counterfactual expression opportunities, decomposing utterance generation into structural permission, communicative opportunity, and form choice. First, shared predictive continuations recover structural permission for unseen word-construction pairings across 30 artificial grammars (mean AUC 0.945 after 120 sentences). Second, under hierarchical uncertainty, we derive a joint evidence law linking omissions to context diversity and within-context repetition, and an exact finite-sample context-refinement inequality. Thus, observing related expressions calibrates expected use to sharpen negative evidence, and higher expected use can drive steeper posterior declines for stronger-permission candidates (reversing initial order after six omissions in a fixed-rate example). Third, we prove that while raw counts confound semantic availability and form selection, scene-level opportunity observations identify both. Empirically, reanalyzing adult and child artificial-language experiments shows meaning-conditioned restriction; judgments of restricted combinations improve held-out production predictions across 399 participants (+0.036 nats/production). Naturalistic child-directed and child speech (11 families, six corpora) shows pervasive source-level usage clustering beyond lexical, number, and dependency-role controls, grounding hierarchical rate uncertainty. Finally, modeling causative judgments across five languages shows that structural compatibility and calibrated usage probabilities draw on distinct distributional, semantic, and discriminative cues. Together, these findings show how the organization and calibration of communicative opportunities govern the evidential value of linguistic absence.

Nutrient limitation drives Turing instabilities in cross-feeding microbial communities

Jan Kocka, Steven A. Redford, Jaime Agudo-Canalejo, Kabir Husain

Microbial communities are shaped by metabolic interactions, often in the form of cross-feeding in which microbes exchange metabolites via diffusion through the environment. We show here that the diffusion of cross-fed metabolites generically leads to a Turing instability in resource-poor environments, driven by the feedback between local metabolite availability and cell growth. A coarse-grained model of community biomass identifies the few dimensionless parameters that control the instability, most notably the ratio of environmental energy supply to cellular maintenance costs. We find that the detailed topology of the metabolic interaction network can be coarse-grained into a single effective parameter to quantitatively characterise the instability. Beyond the onset of the instability, the community forms compact aggregates whose merger is prevented by a competition for shared resources. Overall, our results demonstrate how spatial structure can arise as an inevitable consequence of metabolic interactions in a microbial community.

Classical visual illusions in vision-language models under an instruction ladder

Eiji Watanabe

Vision-language models (VLMs) often name the "illusory" answer when shown a classical visual illusion, but it is unclear whether that answer comes from the picture or from knowledge about famous figures. We drew 300 new stimuli of 25 classical illusions in three conditions--the illusion, the illusion with its inducing elements reversed, and a control with the inducing elements removed--and asked about each image at eight rungs of an instruction ladder that runs from a false fact, through a neutral question and an instruction to report the physical truth, to telling the model the name of the illusion and finally the true answer. Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Haiku 5.5, with thinking off and on, gave 460,800 answers. When asked for the physical fact, all four settings almost never gave the illusion answer, and they resolved real 10% differences in the same displays. When asked how the picture looks, the illusion answers returned, and they did not vanish after the true answer was stated. Two measures that cancel position preferences and recognition of the figure suggest that much of this depends on the stimulus: among side answers, Sonnet with thinking chose the illusion side in 85% of cases after being told the truth (95% CI 74--96%), with 16 of the 25 illusions showing a stimulus-driven effect. Yet for five figures--the Fraser spiral, Kanizsa triangle, Pinna--Brelstaff rings, Ouchi pattern and, for Haiku, the rotating snakes--the models gave the illusion answer to the control images as well, which suggests that they answered from the figure rather than from the effect. A false fact was mostly followed on control images but not on illusion images. The stimuli, prompts, all responses and the code are released for testing further models; the Python code that draws the stimuli is parametric and can generate new illusion images for other studies with models or human observers.

Hierarchical Hybrid-Coordinate Flow Matching for Accurate and Physically Plausible Apo-to-Holo Protein Generation

Minjong Kim, Jaemin Sim, Dongwoo Kim, Myunghyun Jang, Eun-Sol Kim et al. (6 authors)

Predicting holo-like protein conformations from apo structures is essential in structure-based drug design. Most existing apo-to-holo models operate in Cartesian space and achieve strong global accuracy. However, high global accuracy does not ensure physically valid local backbone geometry. Internal-coordinate models offer local control and validity, but regressive generation can amplify angular errors, known as the lever-arm effect. We introduce CrypticFlow, a hierarchical flow-matching model that combines these two complementary representations. An ESM-3-conditioned E(3)-equivariant graph network first generates sparse C$α$ anchors in Cartesian space. A conditional midpoint Transformer then recursively fills the gaps in local internal coordinates. During recursive generation, the decoder constrains the distance between each midpoint and any sequence-adjacent parent anchor to the corresponding apo C$α$-C$α$ distance. On the D3PM test split, CrypticFlow achieves the lowest median and mean C$α$ RMSD at published baseline capacities. Across five independent training models, the median and mean direct RMSD are 1.333 $\pm$ 0.056 angstroms and 2.766 $\pm$ 0.041 angstroms, respectively. The quality of local geometry also improves substantially. Our model achieves 99.58 $\pm$ 0.02% adjacent-distance fidelity and 95.87 $\pm$ 0.25% pseudo-angle fidelity, measured within 0.2 angstroms and 20° of the holo reference. Then, we reconstruct the predicted C$α$-only traces into all-atom structures using the cg2all coarse-to-all-atom backmapping method and evaluate their physical and geometric quality with MolProbity. Our model achieves a MolProbity score of 3.034 $\pm$ 0.054, outperforming the evaluated baselines. These results show that a hierarchical flow-matching model with hybrid coordinates can recover global accuracy while retaining local fidelity.
