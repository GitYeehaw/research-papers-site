Dimensional Crossover and Correlated Spin Dynamics in the Chain-Like van der Waals Antiferromagnet AgCrP$_2$S$_6$

C. S. Saramgi, Y. Shemerliuk, S. Selter, S. Aswartham, B. Buechner et al. (7 authors)

We report a comprehensive investigation of the magnetic properties and spin dynamics of the chain-like van der Waals antiferromagnet AgCrP$_2$S$_6$ using magnetization, magnetic susceptibility, and $^{31}$P nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). The magnetic susceptibility exhibits a broad maximum near 250 K, characteristic of strong short-range antiferromagnetic correlations in a low-dimensional system. A classical Fisher-chain analysis gives an effective intrachain exchange scale $J/k_B\approx 140$ K, while a quantum $S=3/2$ nearest-neighbor-chain calculation yields $J/k_B\approx 114$ K. A constrained AF-FM zig-zag-chain calculation is also compatible with the susceptibility, supporting a dominant quasi one dimensional antiferromagnetic exchange scale of order 100 K. $^{31}$P NMR measurements provide microscopic insight into both static and dynamic spin correlations. The $K-χ$ relation displays two distinct linear regimes separated near $T^\ast\approx 250$ K, with different slopes indicating a change in the effective transferred hyperfine couplings. Below $T_{N}$, the relaxation follows $1/T_1\propto T^5$, consistent with magnon-mediated relaxation in the ordered state. The temperature dependence of the internal hyperfine field yields a critical exponent $β\approx 0.36$, consistent with three-dimensional universality. Together, these results establish AgCrP$_2$S$_6$ as a quasi-one-dimensional $S=3/2$ antiferromagnet embedded in a layered van der Waals host, in which local probes directly resolve the crossover from correlated intrachain spin dynamics to anisotropic three-dimensional magnetic order. More broadly, AgCrP$_2$S$_6$ provides a useful van der Waals platform for studying dimensional crossover, correlated spin dynamics, and hyperfine-coupling evolution in low-dimensional antiferromagnets.

Stochastic Discrete Variable Representation: Tensor Network Eigenstates on General Potential Energy Surfaces

Hannes Hoppe

Tensor network states such as tree tensor network states (TTNS) or matrix product states (MPS) allow very compact representations of high-dimensional wave functions. Using them efficiently relies on a compact representation of the Hamiltonian, and many methods are limited to the sum-of-products (SOP) form. General potential energy surfaces (PESs) do not have this form. To overcome this issue, a stochastic discrete variable representation (SDVR) is presented. The matrix elements of the potential operator in the node-local basis are estimated stochastically by importance sampling of grid configurations. The estimator of the matrix elements is unbiased, and the PES is only evaluated pointwise. The variance is reduced by representing parts of the potential with a 2-mode representation and only sampling the remainder. Sampling noise biases the eigenvalues. A noise-control scheme mitigates this bias. Final energies are obtained from an unbiased variational Monte Carlo estimator. The method is demonstrated by calculating vibrational states of CH3 (6D) and acetonitrile (12D), as well as the zero-point vibrational energy of uracil (30D). For all three systems, the energies agree with the exact references to sub-wavenumber accuracy.

A fast two-phase model for PEM fuel cell impedance

Andrei Kulikovsky

We report a physics-based matrix model for PEM fuel cell impedance that takes into account liquid water transients in the cathode catalyst layer (CCL). This model is an extension of our previous single-phase matrix model for functionally graded CCLs. The solution to the boundary-value problem for the perturbation amplitudes is constructed from basic solutions to the respective Cauchy problem with constant coefficients on numerical cells. The single-phase model employed analytical solutions to this problem. Here, for a larger system of ODEs, the analytical solution does not exist and we used spectral decomposition of the ODE system matrix to obtain the basic solutions numerically. Our impedance model is two orders of magnitude faster than a model based on a standard, general-purpose boundary-value solver. Fitting the matrix model to an experimental spectrum takes about a minute on a laptop. The developed matrix method can be used to numerically solve high-order linear boundary-value problems with variable coefficients.

Direction-Selective Anomalous Quantum Transport from Saddle-like Topological Surface States Coupled to an Electronic Instability in YbSb$_{2}$

Rudra Dhara, Pritesh Srivastava, Jack Tregidga, Rahul Sharma, Shivani et al. (17 authors)

We report direction-selective anomalous quantum transport in epitaxial YbSb$_{2}$ thin films associated with an interplay between anisotropic topological surface states and an electronic instability. Quantum oscillations reveal a quasi-two-dimensional orbit with a non-trivial Berry phase and Dirac-like dispersion, observable for current along the $a$-axis but absent along the orthogonal in-plane $c$-axis. Together with first-principles calculations, these observations support its assignment to a saddle-like topological surface state. Its oscillation amplitude exhibits a field-dependent, non-monotonic temperature evolution that departs from conventional Lifshitz-Kosevich behavior. Raman and coherent phonon spectroscopy reveal additional low-temperature modes consistent with a density-wave instability. Their pronounced polarization anisotropy and colossal field-induced Raman enhancement suggest a strongly anisotropic, field-enhanced electronic susceptibility associated with the density-wave modes. Our findings point to dynamical coupling between anisotropic topological surface states and a field-responsive density-wave instability as a route to direction-selective anomalous quantum transport.

A New Computational Model for the Spin-Free Sum-of-Squares Energy Bounds of Electronic Hamiltonian

Yuming Bao, Yancheng Wu, Dongdong Ge, Yinyu Ye

Optimizing spin-free sum-of-squares (SOS) energy bounds for electronic Hamiltonians leads to semidefinite programs whose expanded size grows rapidly with the orbital dimension. We derive an exact reformulation of an established spin-free SOS model as a convex quadratic problem over a single symmetric matrix constrained between zero and the identity. Explicit recovery maps connect feasible quadratic-program iterates to certificates in the original SOS formulation, and identify the Frank--Wolfe gap with the separation between recovered primal and dual objectives. We implement the quadratic action through GPU-accelerated Coulomb contractions and solve the reformulated problem using a cached implementation of fully corrective Frank--Wolfe, referred to here as the dimension-reduced simplicial method (DRSM) that caches operator products and optimizes convex-combination weights in a small corrective quadratic program. Numerical experiments cover 65 FCIDUMP models, molecular geometries, and regular hydrogen rings, including original-coordinate recovery checks on nine instances and comparisons with FISTA and a boundary-point SDP solver. On eight H100 GPUs, a cold-start calculation for C$_{720}$ with 10,080 spatial orbitals reaches a freshly recomputed Frank--Wolfe gap of $8.82\times10^{-9}$ hartree in 5.02 hours. Full-space hydrogen-ring calculations reach 10,000 orbitals, with cyclic reduction providing further computational savings. These results show how exact reformulation, data structure exploitation and reuse of Coulomb actions make optimization of the selected large-scale SOS relaxation practical.

Nanostructuring Semiconductor Surfaces Using Self-Assembled Metal Nanoparticles for Optoelectronic Applications

S. Catalán-Gomez, M. Ibáñez, P. Guo, V. Braza, B. Vázquez et al. (17 authors)

Semiconductor nanostructures are key light-management elements because their geometry can strengthen light-matter interaction, suppress reflection, and enhance optical coupling. Yet their adoption across material platforms is constrained by lithography, multistep pattern transfer, persistent masks, and material-specific etching. Here, we introduce a lithography-free two-step strategy in which thermally evaporated self-assembled Ga nanoparticles act as self-consuming masks during Ar inductively coupled plasma reactive-ion etching. Correlated transmission electron microscopy on GaAs reveals the formation mechanism. Comparable erosion of the Ga nanoparticles and exposed substrate progressively converts Ga-capped pedestals into pure semiconductor nanocones while preserving crystalline order. Nanocone dimensions extend from tens to hundreds of nanometres, and surface coverage is tunable through Ga deposition and plasma power. The same process produces nanocones on GaP and Si and dense nanostructures on polycrystalline hybrid metal-halide perovskite without detectable PbI2 formation. During nanostructuring, the sun-weighted reflectance reaches minima of 11-15% on the monocrystalline substrates, whereas the perovskite achieves 0.5% after complete mask consumption. The approach therefore combines a self-assembled mask, its consumption, and pattern transfer in a single physical etching step, providing a simple route to light-coupling surfaces for III-V, Si, and perovskite optoelectronics.

ANTIOCH-VS: From Sequence to Hits with Modular, Open-Source Ensemble Virtual Screening

Hale S. Hasdemir, Venkata Sai Sreyas Adury, Pratyush Tiwary

Structure-based virtual screening needs a receptor conformation that fits the ligand class, but for most targets no ligand-bound crystal is available -- and a single predicted model or an apo structure often misses the binding-competent state. We present ANTIOCH-VS, an open-source framework that runs an entire in silico screening campaign, from a protein sequence to a ranked shortlist of candidate binders, as one plug-and-play workflow. It first builds a conformational ensemble by reduced-MSA subsampling with ColabFold, then uses AlphaFold2-RAVE (AF2RAVE) -- short molecular-dynamics simulations with State Predictive Information Bottleneck analysis -- to pick out docking-ready, Boltzmann-weighted metastable states. It then detects the binding pocket, prepares receptors and ligands, docks the library across the ensemble with DOCK6 or AutoDock Vina, builds a consensus ranking, and triages the top hits by physics-based metadynamics (CTMD). We benchmark it on two very different targets, JAK2 kinase and ADRB2 GPCR, using their DUD-E benchmark sets of known binders and property-matched decoys. Starting from sequence alone -- no ligand-bound crystal, no manual intervention -- the AF2RAVE ensembles gave top-20 shortlists strongly enriched for true binders: a 20% hit rate for JAK2 and 60% for ADRB2 (random ~1-2%), on par with ligand-bound crystals and, for the kinase, above single AF2 models and an apo crystal. The full pipeline runs end to end on a workstation or HPC from a single command, or each module can be run on its own with user-supplied inputs. ANTIOCH-VS is freely available at https://github.com/tiwarylab/antioch-vs

Memory-gated transient anomalous transport of quasiparticles out of equilibrium

Pranay Venkatesh, Tianchu Li, Srijan Bhattacharyya, Andrés Montoya-Castillo

Anomalous transport is ubiquitous across condensed matter, materials science, and biophysics. Yet, in far-from-equilibrium settings, what determines when, how much, and in which direction transport departs from diffusion remains a fundamental open question. By adopting the dispersive Holstein model of small polarons as representative of a wide class of dissipative systems, we show that a signed bath memory time governs the direction and the strength of the transient departure, and that when it vanishes, diffusion emerges without an asymptotic delay. Tuning the dissipative environment from resonant to off-resonant with the carrier density of states, transport turns from subdiffusion to superdiffusion as the memory time changes sign. Microscopically, we trace this memory effect to a competition between polaron formation and coherent polaron motion. These findings establish a unified memory-based description of transient anomalous transport, offering a path to control nonequilibrium quasiparticle flow through environmental tuning.

Classical phase unwrapping enables large-time-step unitary quantum Krylov subspace diagonalization

Tushar Kumar Jain, Rahul Maitra, Nicholas J. Mayhall

For computing molecular energies on early fault-tolerant hardware, unitary quantum Krylov subspace diagonalization (UQKSD) avoids both the deep circuits of quantum phase estimation and the Pauli-term sampling of variational methods by extracting the energies from a projected real-time propagator. At a small time step $τ$, nearly linearly dependent Krylov vectors make the overlap matrix ill-conditioned. Our tests at fixed total evolution time show that additional Krylov vectors then add no accuracy, while the thresholded energy becomes increasingly sensitive to the choice of cutoff. A larger time step improves the conditioning and reduces the Krylov dimension $M$ needed for a target accuracy, but the energies are then known only modulo $2π/τ$. We present Classical Unwrapping for Resolving Eigenvalues (CURE-QKSD), a classical post-processing method that leaves the UQKSD circuits and generalized eigenvalue problem unchanged and recovers the missing multiples of $2π/τ$ from phase-consistency landscapes of one or, when needed, two UQKSD runs. In noiseless tests on six molecules with 12--20 qubits, all 28 targeted energies are recovered in their aliased regimes with final errors of at most $1.1\times10^{-7}$ Ha. Under Gaussian noise with $σ=10^{-4}$, the median error of the ground or first excited state reaches chemical accuracy at large aliased time steps with $M=20$--$65$, whereas uncorrected principal-branch energies are off by $0.7$--$115$ Ha. CURE-QKSD therefore makes aliased time steps usable for UQKSD calculations with smaller, better-conditioned Krylov bases.

SOPPA with the Complete Third-Order A Matrix: Singlet Excitation Energies

Lina Krarup Knudsen, Annika Weisberg Eenholt, Simone Leigh Engell Robinson, Juliane H. Fuglsbjerg, Kasper F. Schaltz et al. (8 authors)

We present the derivation and implementation of the complete third-order correction of the A matrix in the Møller-Plesset polarization propagator approximation. A new contribution to the third-order correction was discovered and we thus report the first results obtained with the complete third-order A matrix. The performance of the Second Order Polarization Propagator Approximation including the third order A matrix (SOPPA-A3) method in calculation of vertical excitation energies was assessed by comparing to the reference methods FCI or CCSDTQP. A substantial improvement over SOPPA was found with the inclusion of the complete third-order A matrix as the SOPPA-A3 method yields excitation energies of a quality comparable to CC2. The inclusion of the new contribution results in a change in the individual excitation energies of varying size and direction depending on the molecule and state and the contribution should therefore be included for a formally complete third-order treatment of the A matrix.
