Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success

Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

OpenTac: An Open Ecosystem for Customizable, Scalable, and Transferable 3D Tactile Sensing

Ariel Slepyan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Jiafeng Gu, Anagha Rajendra Dangle, Yinzhe Zhou et al. (15 authors)

We present OpenTac, a novel sensor and a full-stack ecosystem for tactile skins that are highly customizable, scalable, and readily deployed in downstream robot control and learning applications. Our core contribution is twofold. First, we develop a scalable resistive tactile skin with a structured deformable overlay that converts tangential loading into spatially varying pressure. Differential responses across neighboring "taxels" support decoding of local shear vectors, and the common-mode response encodes normal force. Second, we provide a highly customizable sensor design tool that is able to automatically generate fabrication files for these tactile skins from 2D outlines and developable 3D surfaces, including from abstract descriptions such as text (e.g., "design a sensor for a size 9 shoe insole"). We develop an ecosystem around the OpenTac sensor, including the CAD design tool, a sensor simulator, cross-sensor transfer, and fast/adaptive data acquisition. We demonstrate the broad applicability of the OpenTac-CAD sensor design tool by designing and fabricating more than 30 sensors across a variety of robot embodiments. We characterize the three-dimensional tactile fields produced by OpenTac with a commercial, high-precision force/torque sensor. We demonstrate the broad applicability of 3D tactile sensing across contact-rich feedback control and manipulation policy learning on four real-world robot embodiments. Additionally, adaptive acquisition enables kilohertz-rate partial readouts, exceeding full-array raster scanning on the same sensor by 19.2X at 16 sampled taxels per update for a 1024-taxel skin. We provide all hardware, software, and result videos are at opentac.github.io

Workload-Aware Monitoring for AI-RAN

Babak Azkaei, Georgios Exarchakos, Kishor Chandra Joshi

AI-RAN platforms are expected to share CPU and GPU resources between radio workloads and AI services. Aggregate telemetry reports operating conditions, but timing changes may reflect different workloads, processors, or measurement boundaries. We propose workload-aware monitoring that associates radio-work descriptors with processing paths, timing boundaries, and operating context before comparing like with like. In 16 over-the-air downlink experiments, hybrid dispatch (CPU for transport blocks (TBs) below 4,096 bytes and GPU otherwise) has 29.66% and 22.93% lower mean host-callback durations than direct GPU dispatch, without and with a synthetic GPU co-runner, respectively. Grouping locates the separation in small TBs, while large-TB callback-duration ranges overlap. These observations identify a candidate for complementary CPU/GPU use.

Extending Harmonic Function Methods via Root Locus for Coordinated Path Generation

Matteo Frascarelli, Lucia Pallottino, Alberto Landi

We consider the offline generation of coordinated paths for N interchangeable robots and N goals in planar environments with polygonal obstacles. After a conformal map to a circular domain, starts and goals become the poles and zeros of a rational function R, and the paths are the branches of its Generalized Root Locus (GRL), i.e. the streamlines of the logarithmic potential -log R. We show that, for every phase outside a finite set of critical phases and a set of boundary phases, the induced gradient motion drives each robot to a distinct goal along pairwise disjoint paths that never reach the obstacles; the robot-goal assignment is produced by the construction, and with the gain used as a clock all robots move as the closed-loop roots of the GRL and arrive together at a prescribed time. Obstacles become streamlines through successive Milne-Thomson reflections, and for a finite number of reflections a finite test certifies safety for the potential that is actually computed. Reciprocal root-locus planning is recovered as a special case. The method is illustrated on two polygonal workspaces, 100 random instances compared with independent harmonic planning and artificial potentials, and a scene with ten robots and ten obstacles.

DiffDreamer: Coupling World Models with Differentiable Simulation for Agile Quadrotor Control

Fanxing Li, Yuxiang Huang, Shengyang Wang, Fangyu Sun, Shuyu Wu et al. (10 authors)

Tasks such as quadrotor dynamic-gate racing require agents to anticipate external motion while maintaining precise high-frequency control. World-model-based reinforcement learning provides a natural solution by learning latent dynamics for future imagination, yet it often entangles environmental evolution and the robot's own dynamics in a single learned rollout, where model bias can affect policy optimization efficiency. We propose DiffDreamer, a differentiable reinforcement learning framework that tightly couples a learned world model with differentiable simulation for actor optimization. In one optimization step, the learned world model propagates latent states that capture external dynamics and history-dependent features, while the differentiable simulator propagates the robot's explicit physical state. We further decompose the actor objective into a critic-driven pathway for non-differentiable task rewards and a simulator-driven pathway for differentiable motion rewards. Experiments on aggressive tracking and dynamic-gate racing show that DiffDreamer improves training convergence over the model-free, model-based, and differentiable-RL baselines, and final performance over these baselines and classical planning and control methods. Additional experiments assess reward decomposition and the odometry-conditioned relative-state representation through ablations. We also provide proof-of-concept real-world flight experiments for validation.

Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models

Atiksh Bhardwaj, Edward W. Duan, Junwon Seo, Prithwish Dan, Wei-Chiu Ma et al. (7 authors)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution? Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. In experiments with the open-source VLA $π_{0.5}$ and across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, this approach is competitive with or outperforms VLM-based monitors in task failure detection, as well as methods which require failure data for training. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

Humanoid Mobility via Attention-based Contacts

Junzhe He, Kehan Wen, Chong Zhang, Clemens Schwarke, Zewei Zhang et al. (10 authors)

Humanoid mobility over complex terrain requires accurate foot placement and coordinated whole-body motion capable of robust adaptation. Reinforcement learning (RL) policies trained without motion references generalize well across terrains, yet eliciting effective whole-body coordination through reward design and exploration alone remains challenging. In contrast, reference-based methods yield natural motions but struggle to adapt beyond the predefined trajectories in their training data. We introduce a learning framework that bridges these two approaches by using motion data strictly as a training prior. An explicit, self-supervised, attention-based contact planner predicts future body-environment contacts, while a contact-conditioned tracker translates these plans into coordinated whole-body motion. Together, they establish contact as an interface between sensory observations and control actions. This abstraction allows the policy to internalize the contact-aware structure of locomotion rather than directly replicating kinematics. Consequently, a single policy can exploit data-driven motion priors for natural whole-body coordination while retaining the flexibility to discover environment-dependent behaviors through reinforcement learning. Building on the learned contact planner and tracker, a subsequent fine-tuning stage bootstraps their acquired capabilities to explore environment variations beyond those covered by the original motion data. Operating entirely from onboard observations without any reference inputs at deployment, this framework enables versatile humanoid mobility in challenging environments.

Controlled Radio Fault Injection and Telemetry Evaluation for Open 5G RANs

Babak Azkaei, Maxim Bîtiu, Georgios Exarchakos, Kishor Chandra Joshi

Radio access network (RAN) telemetry reveals radio conditions, protocol behavior, and their consequences for applications. Evaluating its usefulness requires controlled impairments and observations of the same execution. We present C and Python channel brokers and a fault injection workflow for software 5G testbeds connecting an OCUDU base station and a software UE through ZeroMQ. The brokers apply scheduled, directional transformations to I/Q samples and restore the link, while the workflow jointly records applied faults, native and programmable (codelet) telemetry, and application delay. We demonstrate this capability through 45 comparative trials of brief uplink interruptions and stepped additive noise. These use cases distinguish recognizing a radio change from measuring its service consequence: native missing-feedback events retrospectively identify interruptions, whereas noise changes decoding-error measurements without producing those events. Median on-time round-trip times remain near 30 ms, but the prespecified comparisons show mean increases in on-time 95th-percentile delay of 354 and 604 ms for one and five interruptions, and 113 ms for noise. Adding receive-side radio link control (RLC) codelet summaries did not improve interruption classification on held-out trials. The framework provides a reusable way to evaluate measurement choices under known radio faults, before using them in monitoring or learning systems.

Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models

Yufeng Liu, Bohao Fan, Juntu Zhao, Xinyi Wang, Hang Yu et al. (14 authors)

Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To learn these signals without fine-grained progress annotations, Echo constructs successful, failed, and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision properties. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

Geometry-Dependent Properties of Physically Crosslinked PPy-PVA Hydrogels Enable Tunable Iontophoretic Drug Delivery

Heather Jin Hee Kim, Mohsen Alishahi, Kyuin Park, Mikaila K. Roncevich, Larissa M. Shepherd et al. (6 authors)

Objective: Iontophoretic drug delivery has primarily relied on current modulation to regulate drug transport. Here, we investigate hydrogel geometry, microstructure, and local electric fields as alternative mechanisms for controlling transdermal delivery. Methods: Bovine serum albumin (BSA) was encapsulated in polypyrrole-polyvinyl alcohol (PPy-PVA) hydrogels. Five geometries, including Hilbert curve-based designs, were fabricated to vary pore architecture and local electric field distributions. We optimized PPy content, drug loading, and current density. Electric fields were evaluated using finite element analysis, and microstructures were characterized by scanning electron microscopy. Drug transport was assessed using Franz diffusion cells with artificial membranes. Results: Hydrogel geometries exhibited distinct microstructures and electric field distributions. Iontophoresis substantially enhanced permeability compared with passive diffusion, while individual geometries produced different cumulative release, flux, and permeability profiles. The average iontophoretic permeability was (3.80 +/- 1.93) x 10^-5, exceeding previously reported values using artificial membranes. Conclusion: Geometry-driven fabrication of drug-loaded hydrogels enables tunable transport characteristics under both iontophoretic and passive conditions. Significance: Geometry-mediated control offers an alternative approach to regulating iontophoretic drug delivery without requiring individual current-density modulation.
