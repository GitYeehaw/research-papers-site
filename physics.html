Boundary $c\leq1$ Liouville CFT from the Bootstrap

Panos Betzios, Paul Ghiringhelli, Olga Papadoulaki, Ioannis Tsiares

We construct boundary Liouville conformal field theory at central charge $c\leq1$ with FZZT-type boundary conditions and show that it satisfies the Cardy--Lewellen sewing constraints on the disk and the annulus. Using the analytic bootstrap we derive the so far missing boundary three-point and bulk-to-boundary structure constants and a consistent disk one-point function as explicit meromorphic functions of the Liouville momenta. In sharp contrast to $c\geq25$, the open-string spectrum between two ``timelike'' FZZT boundaries is discrete, yet non-trivially consistent through open-closed duality with the continuous closed-string spectrum of Ribault and Santachiara.

Quantum Error Correction in Nearly Critical Toric Codes

Zack Weinstein, Samuel J. Garratt

We investigate quantum error correction protocols for topological codes deformed away from their stabilizer limits, particularly as they are pushed towards quantum phase transitions into trivial phases. Our focus is primarily on quantum information stored within the ground-state manifold of the transverse-field toric code (TFTC), subjected to independent bit-flip and phase-flip decoherence. Although the transverse field induces strong quantum fluctuations of $m$ anyons in the ground states, we show that projective measurements of the "bare" toric code stabilizers can nevertheless be used to construct an efficient decoding algorithm which exhibits a finite threshold to both types of decoherence, even when the transverse field is tuned infinitesimally close to the quantum critical point. Our construction of the optimal postmeasurement decoders---that is, the optimal decoders assuming that the stabilizers are first measured---crucially relies on the stoquastic nature of the TFTC Hamiltonian, which affords a description of its ground states in terms of a classical lattice gauge theory. Using stoquasticity, we first prove that stabilizer measurements act unitarily on the undecohered TFTC ground states to excellent approximation, and that the optimal postmeasurement decoder can be efficiently implemented via Monte Carlo simulation of a constrained lattice gauge theory. We then construct the optimal postmeasurement decoder for the TFTC states under bit-flip and phase-flip decoherence, where we numerically demonstrate a finite threshold close to the quantum critical point. We provide theoretical evidence (without a rigorous proof) that stabilizer measurements constitute an optimal decoding strategy in this setting. For phase-flip decoherence, we further prove that the optimal postmeasurement decoding threshold is independent of the transverse field strength throughout the topological phase.

Quantum Pointwise Dimension (QUPID): An algorithm for identifying classical chaos in quantum dynamics

Jonathan Berkheim, David J. Tannor

The Pointwise Dimension is a diagnostic that reveals the effective dimensionality of classical trajectories in a $2N$-dimensional phase space. The essence of this diagnostic is to find a scaling according to which density changes with radius $r$ around a point of interest in phase space. Particularly, regular trajectories are confined to an $N$-dimensional invariant torus, while chaotic trajectories explore a $(2N-1)$-dimensional energy surface. In classical mechanics, this trajectory density can be found by counting recurrences to within the sphere of radius $r$. In quantum mechanics, probability density is intrinsic, making the Pointwise Dimension compelling, but surprisingly this has not been studied in the quantum regime. In this work, we implement the Pointwise Dimension in the quantum regime using the Husimi representation of the quantum wavepacket. This implementation entails a long-time average of the time-dependent Husimi representation to obtain a stationary phase space density, and a radial spatial integration of this density over a sphere of radius $r$. The classical-quantum correspondence is excellent for both autonomous and non-autonomous systems, adding a new diagnostic to the study of quantum chaos. Finally, we explore the possibility, both classically and quantum mechanically, of obtaining the Pointwise Dimension from a time correlation function.

Deconfinement Confined to the Heaviest Neutron Stars: Strange Quark Matter from a Flavor-Dressed Contact Interaction

Aftab Ahmad, Noreen Akhter, Alfredo Raya

We study cold, electrically neutral, beta-equilibrated quark matter with u, d, and s quarks described by a symmetry-preserving contact interaction whose parameters are fixed in vacuum. The vector repulsion is varied as a fraction 0 < eta < 3/2 of the scalar coupling, and results are given with and without the flavor dressing of the effective coupling. Pure quark matter is not self-bound in this model, and no value of eta produces a star that reaches two solar masses while satisfying the tidal constraint from GW170817; the heaviest configurations owe their mass to an envelope of constituent quarks. We then construct hybrid stars with a relativistic mean-field hadronic phase fitted to GM1 saturation properties, using both Maxwell and Gibbs constructions and a deconfinement bag shift. Tying deconfinement to chiral restoration and requiring a continuous pressure yields an upper bound on this shift. Within that bound, quark matter appears inside stable stars only for eta < 1/2, never below about 4 n0, and only in stars between 2.2 and 2.35Msun, over a stable mass window of at most 0.02Msun. The vector strength that pure quark stars need to become massive prevents deconfinement in hybrid stars. Radii and tidal deformabilities of intermediate-mass stars are therefore set by the hadronic EoS alone; with the softer SLy4 EoS the transition moves to the maximum-mass configuration itself.

Breaking the quantum barriers for Graph Collision

Arjan Cornelissen, Amin Shiraz Gilani, Shiva Kintali, François Le Gall, Subhasree Patro et al. (6 authors)

In the graph collision problem, the task is to decide whether a known graph on $n$ vertices contains an edge with both endpoints marked, given query access to the input bits specifying the marked vertices. We first refute the conjectured $Ω(n^{2/3})$ query lower bound with a bounded error quantum algorithm using $\widetilde O(n^{92/139})$ queries. We then refine this approach to obtain a $\widetilde O(n^{11/17})$ query algorithm. We also obtain a $\widetilde O((nq)^{11/17})$ query bound for all (partial) Boolean output functions with positive certificate complexity at most 2 and alphabet size $q$, contrasting a known $Ω(n^{2/3})$ lower bound for such problems with large alphabet sizes. We complement these algorithms with an $Ω(\sqrt{n\log n})$ quantum query lower bound for graph collision, exceeding the $O(\sqrt n)$ certificate complexity barrier for the positive adversary method.

Reading Why: Born-Measured Attention with Exact Word Evidence on Light and Qubits

Teerawat Tongloy, Jiraporn Pongsopa, Rangsan Jomtarak

When a language model answers, we can usually only estimate why. We build a transformer in which every step is a Born measurement - attention $|\langle q|k\rangle|^4$ normalised over each row, a measured product in place of the feed-forward nonlinearity, and a Born readout. With its denominators held at their values for the input, each class probability is a polynomial in the word amplitudes, so its integrated-gradient evidence is computed by a finite quadrature with no quadrature error: the words' contributions sum to the class probability above its no-information share, to $10^{-11}$ or better. On e-SNLI natural language inference, with about 98k parameters outside the embedding, the model reaches $76.98 \pm 0.17$% test accuracy, level with a softmax transformer of the same size ($76.62 \pm 0.36$%). Deleting the word its evidence ranks first changes the answer in 45% of test pairs, out of the 53% any single deletion can change, against 32% for the softmax model explained the same way and 40% by its attention x gradient. Attention can look more like the words annotators highlighted, yet accounts for the answer less well: deleting the word it ranks first changes the Born Transformer's answer in 32% of pairs. The same weights compile exactly to optoelectronic optics and to qubits, and a coherent attention layer becomes one circuit equal to the model (852 CNOTs on 23 qubits). On IBM's ibm_kingston, reading only the attention overlaps by Hadamard tests, the device gave the model's answers on two short test pairs and kept or changed them under deletion as the model does; the evidence itself did not yet survive the device, and we work out the shots and calibration that measuring it on hardware would need.

Interpretable Quantum Photonic Generative Models

Dat Chi Le, Oleksandr Kyriienko

We propose a physical generative model for learning correlated multivariate data based on homodyne detection of photonic continuous-variable (CV) circuits. We use an $N$-mode photonic circuit in which two-mode squeezing provides correlations, local phase shifts tune the strength and sign of these correlations, and an adjustable interferometer redistributes them across modes. We train the circuit by matching its Pearson correlation matrix to the one obtained from data. We apply affine post-processing to adjust the marginals independently while keeping the relations intact. We test the model on the multivariate distribution of standardized log returns of financial assets, showing that the photonic model can represent rich correlation structures. We reproduce empirical correlations with residuals below $10^{-4}$ for five variables and recover the correct correlation signs for $94\% \pm 1\%$ of 190 correlation pairs at twenty variables, with $86\%$ of the residuals below $0.1$. As a separate technical contribution, we develop an approach for simulating Kerr gates with small nonlinearity via a Hubbard-Stratonovich expansion, making them suitable for CV-based simulators. Our results establish CV photonic circuits as a platform for interpretable generative learning, suitable for representing and fast sampling of distributions across different application domains.

Bright Siren Cosmology Beyond Fisher: DALI Forecasts for Third-Generation Gravitational Wave Observatories

Mayara H. Pacheco, Josiel M. S. de Souza, Odylio D. Aguiar, Márcio E. S. Alves

In this work we assess non-Gaussian extensions of Fisher forecasting based on the Derivative Approximation for Likelihoods (DALI). We simulate ensembles of bright siren catalogs for third-generation observatories, adopting a Cosmic Explorer (CE) and a triangular Einstein Telescope (ET) network, frequency-domain inspiral waveforms, and selection-motivated priors for counterpart detection. We reconstruct approximate likelihoods using Fisher and the first DALI correction (Doublet), and propagate the inferred distance information to cosmological inference in a w0waCDM model. We find that the Doublet approximation provides tighter cosmological constraints than the Fisher approximation, reducing the inferred uncertainties by approximately a factor of two in agreement with non-approximate likelihood (called Exact) results. For our GW network onfiguration, 300 bright-siren detections up to z=1 yield an H0 precision of ~2.7% with the Doublet approximation, compared with ~2.5% for the exact likelihood. The adopted non-Gaussian GW priors induce a systematic displacement of the inferred luminosity distances from the fiducial values, which is also present in the exact GW reconstruction and propagates into the cosmological parameters. Compared with the exact cosmological likelihood, the Doublet results differ by only ~10% on average in the parameter uncertainties, compared with ~195% for the traditional Fisher approximation. The Doublet approximation also substantially reduces the computational cost, by a factor of ~55 for the GW analysis and by ~12 for cosmological likelihood evaluations, yielding an estimated reduction of ~55 in the total computational cost of the combined analysis. Our results show the potential of DALI-based approximations to provide accurate and computationally efficient tools for forecasting in bright siren cosmology.

Toric gravitational instantons with four fixed points

Lars Andersson, Mattias Dahl, Gustav Nilsson

We classify the simply connected toric gravitational instantons whose torus action has four fixed points. They are the hyperkähler multi-Eguchi--Hanson and multi-Taub--NUT metrics with four collinear centres, the asymptotically flat instantons on Li and Sun's manifold $X_3$, up to scale one for each twist at infinity in $(0,1)$ for a fixed orientation of the torus, and, up to scale, a single further instanton with an ALF end, which is not hyperkähler. The last two are Hermitian for neither orientation. In particular, the toric asymptotically flat instanton on $X_3$ with a given twist is unique up to scale, as conjectured by Li and Sun. The proof combines the solitonic form of Geroch's conjecture, which turns smoothness into polynomial equations for the soliton data, with a convexity principle adapted from Aksteiner. At a fixed asymptotic parameter, the cone errors of the conical metrics with a given rod structure are the gradient of a strictly convex function of the rod lengths, and the strict convexity gives uniqueness. Existence follows from an explicit solution for the ALF end and, for the AF ends, from Li and Sun's existence theorem. We also outline how a chart of Aksteiner identifies these AF instantons with those found by Teo.

An Exact QFT/Lattice Map for Fermions

Shu-Heng Shao, Nikita Sopenko

We construct an exact map between continuum fermion fields and lattice fields in 1+1 dimensions. This map rewrites a continuum Hamiltonian as a lattice Hamiltonian with nearest-neighbor couplings and an infinite-dimensional local Hilbert space. The resulting lattice model reproduces exactly the energy spectrum, global operator algebra, chiral symmetries, and anomalies of the continuum QFT on any finite lattice. Unlike domain wall fermions, truncating the local Hilbert space introduces no extra massless modes, and the entanglement entropy of an interval converges as the truncation is removed. The lattice local algebras are of type I and lie between the type III continuum algebras of nested spatial regions, in accordance with the split property. We demonstrate this construction explicitly for $N$ Majorana fermions, realizing the $O(N)_L\timesO(N)_R$ lattice chiral symmetry. We also construct an exact lattice model for the Schwinger model and numerically reproduce the mass gap $e/\sqrtπ$.
