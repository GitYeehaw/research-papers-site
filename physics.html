Solving the SU(2) BFSS model

Kabir Bajaj, Henry W. Lin

We numerically solve the ground state wavefunction of SU(2) BFSS matrix quantum mechanics. We compare ground correlators against islands obtained using the matrix bootstrap and find agreement up to five significant figures. The power law tail of this wavefunction determines the three-point amplitude, which agrees with the computation of Herderschee and Maldacena. Beyond the ground state, we extract the non-perturbative four-point scattering amplitude using Lüscher's method. In the appropriate regime, we find agreement with the Born-Oppenheimer approximation that yields the well-known $v^4/r^7$ interaction. We also find explicit bound states with positive energies in various SO(9) irreps. These are closely related to the bound states whose existence was recently discovered by OpenAI. Finally, we identify metastable resonances in the spectrum.

Nonperturbative string dynamics in a (2+1)D lattice gauge theory on a superconducting quantum processor

Sanziana Stelea, Anthony Gandon, Francesco Tacchino, Jad C. Halimeh, Ivano Tavernelli

Electric flux strings underpin confinement in gauge theories. In two spatial dimensions, their evolution involves competing processes of charge motion, pair creation, and transverse deformation. Accessing these dynamics beyond perturbative limits challenges classical approaches, as entanglement growth restricts the times accessible to tensor-network simulations. Here we use quantum simulation to investigate a (2+1)D Z2 lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter and an explicit magnetic plaquette interaction, spanning strongly confined and nonperturbative deconfined regimes. Using 63 superconducting qubits, including 12 ancillas, we evolve initially localized strings with static or dynamical endpoint charges. Spatially resolved measurements reveal rapid charge and flux spreading in the deconfined regime, enhanced matter occupation in the string interior near the pair-creation resonance, and suppressed spreading at strong electric fields. String-path occupations further probe transverse rearrangements between configurations connected by plaquette operations. Retaining separate matter and gauge degrees of freedom makes local Gauss-law constraints directly accessible for error detection. Combining these constraints with ancilla checks and noise rescaling, we measure dynamics in circuits containing up to 15 second-order Trotter steps. Our results establish quantum simulation as a practical approach to exploring nonperturbative string dynamics in two spatial dimensions, with gauge redundancy serving as an experimental resource.

Singustars: Between Black Holes and Compact Stars

Pablo Laguna

The Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff equations admit singular solutions in which a negative point mass is surrounded by a self-gravitating fluid. For a $Γ$-law polytropic equation of state, these solutions, which we call singustars, are thick shells bounded by two zero-pressure surfaces around a naked, repulsive, negative-mass singularity, with a Schwarzschild exterior. They form a two-parameter family, labeled by the mass $M$ and the compactness $C\equiv M/R$. Singustars join regular polytropes as the central negative mass vanishes and reach compactness beyond the Buchdahl bound. From exact equilibria and their radial modes, we find that singustars with $Γ= 2$ are stable only for $M\lesssim0.63$ and $C\lesssim0.25$, a region that nonetheless contains configurations more compact than any stable regular polytrope, although none with a light ring. In the numerically resolved parameter space, unstable singustars have a single unstable radial mode; many are energetically unbound. Nonlinear evolutions in ingoing null coordinates show that the end state depends on the perturbation. For the collapsing models studied, backward tracing of outgoing null rays identifies an event-horizon generator with a past endpoint on the singularity, before the first trapped surface appears. Outward perturbations disperse unbound shells and leave the singularity naked, while a bound shell migrates toward, and oscillates about, a stable configuration with the same conserved masses.

Finite-Key Readiness and Authentication-Key Replenishment in QKD-Assisted UAV Edge Computing

Dang H. Le, Ban Q. Tran, Susan Mengel

A service that needs fresh quantum keys must wait for key release, and may be denied it, before protected computation starts. We quantify this cost for a trusted stationary UAV serving two ground gateways, each obtaining its keys from one finite-key decoy-state BB84 attempt with parameters fixed in advance. A count-level Monte Carlo model, reproduced by a semi-analytic model, links key acceptance to protected upload, computation, and download, including failed attempts and hover energy. At 26 dB effective optical loss, with authentication hashing at its proxy-benchmarked, near-zero cost, fresh keys raise the 95th-percentile completion time of both tasks from 10.37 to 28.65 s; hover energy grows in proportion. The scalar rate models evaluated here miss the deadline limit on the block size. The pulse budget lies in a window bounded below by authentication-key replenishment and above by the deadline; for a fixed source, channel, and acceptance model, the authentication debit shifts the floor, whereas hashing cost does not. At a 35-s deadline the largest tested loss with a non-empty window is 27 dB at 1\% misalignment and 22 dB at 2\%; at 3\% no tested setting has one. Conditional on the assumed acquisition probability, the selected configurations meet this deadline with simultaneous lower confidence bounds of 96.9--97.3\% on held-out trials, at 26 dB and at 18--20 dB with 2\% misalignment. A high-cost stress reference that charges hashing in full narrows the window and lowers these loss limits to 26 and 20 dB at the reference processing capacity. A small rise in error rate or loss pushes the deadline probability well below its target and drains authentication keys, while the protocol's security parameter is unchanged.

Structure of correlations for the single-$j$ model using quantum information theory

Javier Faba, Vicente Martín, Luis Robledo

We say that a system undergoes a quantum phase transition when the mean-field ground state of a given Hamiltonian spontaneously breaks its symmetries depending on the Hamiltonian parameters. It is a widely accepted view that this phenomenon enables the mean-field state to absorb the maximum amount of correlations without losing its mean-field structure. However, it is still uncertain how those correlations are incorporated within the mean-field state and how the correlation structure changes when we get closer to the exact ground state by restoring the symmetries. In this work, we answer these questions by studying the single-$j$ model, which is a realistic fermionic nuclear model with abelian and non-abelian broken symmetries. We find that both types of symmetries have very different effects for the correlations within the mean-field state. This makes unclear the concept of the symmetry breaking process as a mechanism to absorb the maximum amount of correlations. Moreover, the symmetry restoration redistributes existing `fake' correlations between paired orbitals to more complex subsystems.

Quantum Thermal Transistor via Nonlinear Environmental Coupling

Laiba Hayat, M. Tahir Naseem

Thermal amplification enables sensitive control of heat currents, providing a basis for heat management and thermal signal processing in quantum devices. For a harmonic oscillator coupled to emitter, collector, and base thermal reservoirs, we recover the absence of thermal amplification when system-reservoir interactions permit only single-quantum exchange. A controlled weak-nonlinearity expansion, supported by numerical stationary solutions, shows that two-quantum exchange at the emitter enables amplification and predicts its onset. When two-quantum exchange dominates at the emitter and collector, varying the base temperature redistributes populations between even and odd oscillator sectors. This changes the emitter and collector heat currents far more than the base current, enabling large thermal amplification. We propose a realization using a longitudinally coupled auxiliary two-level system and bath spectral filtering, specifying conditions for its elimination to yield the required oscillator dynamics. These results establish nonlinear system-reservoir coupling as a resource for controlling heat flow and enabling thermal amplification.

Gravitational radiation from a particle in circular orbit around a wormhole: the nonrotating case

Shauvik Biswas, Sayan Chakrabarti

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

The energetic cost of loading classical data in quantum computers

Joao F. Doriguello, Alessandro Luongo, Harshit Verma

Quantum algorithms for machine learning, chemistry, and cryptanalysis often assume coherent access to classical data at negligible physical cost. We instead treat data loading as a hardware process and quantify the dynamic energetic cost of a quantum random access memory (QRAM) query on a superconducting processor. Using the metric-noise-resource framework, we relate query fidelity and power to gate duration, relative two-qubit drive strength, and the distribution of attenuation across a multi-stage refrigerator, while accounting for thermal photons and gate errors. We analyze the linear-depth multiplexer and the noise-resilient bucket-brigade architectures, adapting the latter to a native superconducting gate set and refining its fidelity analysis using the fully decomposed gate schedule. For a representative bucket-brigade QRAM query with a $12$-qubit address register we obtain a query fidelity of $0.969$. The query consumes $3.51\times10^{-4}\,J$ of dynamic energy, corresponding to an average dynamic power of $4.38\,W$ and a peak of $64.4\,W$. Our optimization shows that, at fixed target fidelity, the operating point and attenuation profile can change the average power by a factor of two or more.

Multidimensional Quantum Block Turbo Codes

Khaled Jebari, Luiz Anet Neto, Ramesh Pyndiah, Jean-Louis de Bougrenet de la Tocnaye

The theory of turbo iterative decoding was brought into the quantum coding framework through the development of quantum serial turbo codes and quantum block turbo codes. In this paper, we present a theory for multidimensional quantum block turbo codes, a turbo decoding technique for multi layer product codes. We describe its algorithm and simulate the performance for the 3D case on a depolarizing channel.

Simulating closed- and open-system dynamics of Tavis--Cummings lattices on fault-tolerant quantum computers

Soumya Sarkar, Rakshit Gharat, Nam Nguyen, Gavin Brennen

Tavis--Cummings lattices couple cavities containing several emitters and host polaritonic many-body dynamics whose real-time correlations are costly to compute classically in two dimensions, especially when unequal emitter relaxation or dephasing rates break permutation symmetry. We analyze logical resources for fault-tolerant closed- and open-system simulation of an atom-resolved lattice with $L$ cavities, $N$ emitters per cavity, and photon cutoff $n\_{\max}$, including target observable estimation. Building on ladder-operator block encoding, we construct circuits for photon hopping and local light--matter exchange and combine them to encode the lattice Hamiltonian. By updating source and destination photon occupations in place, the hopping circuit eliminates the auxiliary $L\lceil\log_2(n\_{\max}+1)\rceil$-qubit photon register used by two-register implementations. Across three uniform-coefficient examples with $16$ to $256$ cavities, the controlled walk uses $4.75$--$8.11%$ fewer Toffoli gates than the paired two-register implementation under matched shared preparation, precision, control, and workspace conventions. For closed systems, we implement real-time evolution with generalized quantum signal processing and bound the cost of estimating retarded cavity spectral functions. For open systems with photon loss, emitter relaxation, and dephasing, we use a sampled first-order dilation and bound the runs and timesteps needed to estimate transient photon statistics such as $g^{(2)}(0)$. With state-preparation and measurement costs explicit, these estimates provide a reference for assessing fault-tolerant light--matter lattice simulation costs.
