Temperature-Independent Logarithmic Current Slope in Interdiffusion-Broadened Silicon pn Junction toward Low-Power Thermometry

Sungjae Hong, Jeffrey A. Ivie, Tzu-Ming Lu, Christopher R. Allemang

With the development of cryogenic technologies such as quantum computing, there is an increasing need for low-power cryogenic thermometers that minimize self-heating. Here, we report an anomalous temperature-independent logarithmic current slope, $d(\ln J)/dV$, in heavily doped silicon p$^{+}$n$^{+}$ junctions with significant dopant interdiffusion and highlight the advantages of our device as a low-power, cryogenic thermometer. Electrical characterizations from 2 to 420 K reveal that, at low forward bias, $d(\ln J)/dV$ remains nearly constant, which yields a voltage-temperature responsivity that is nearly independent of bias current. The observed transport cannot be fully understood with conventional diffusion or direct band-to-band tunneling. Instead, the results suggest the contribution of trap-assisted tunneling. These results establish interdiffusion-broadened silicon p$^{+}$n$^{+}$ junctions as a route to low-power temperature sensors while providing insight into carrier transport in heavily doped semiconductor junctions.

Internal friction and frequency-dependent conductivity in the bivariate site energy model for the mixed alkali effect in glasses

Justus Leiber, Sven Lohmann, Quinn Emilia Fischer, Philipp Maass

The mixed alkali effect is fundamental to understanding ion transport in glasses. It manifests itself in strong changes of various ionic transport properties when one type of ion is successively replaced by another type. Recently, we have developed a new theory of thermally activated hopping in bivariate random site energy landscapes that takes into account site occupation statistics according to generalized Fermi distributions and mixture kinetics in accordance with linear irreversible thermodynamics. The theory was successfully applied to predict the behavior of partial conductivities and their activation energies at low frequencies corresponding to the dc limit. Here we show that the theory is capable to capture the salient features of the mixed alkali effect observed in ac ion conductivities and in the peculiar behavior of the internal friction.

Shadow Molecular Dynamics for General Nonlinear Flexible-Charge Models

Jos Thomas, Anthony P. Baldo, Rae A. Corrigan Grove, Mehmet Cagri Kaymak, Joshua Finkelstein et al. (13 authors)

Machine-learned interatomic potentials have greatly expanded the reach of molecular dynamics simulations, but complex chemical environments often require explicit treatment of long-range electronic response, including charge transfer and polarization. Physics-based flexible-charge models capture these effects but require global nonlinear charge equilibration at every time step to obtain Born-Oppenheimer energies and forces. This process is computationally expensive and can lead to instabilities unless tightly converged. Here, we introduce a shadow molecular dynamics framework for flexible-charge models with arbitrary local nonlinearities and long-range multipolar electrostatics. We replace the exact Born-Oppenheimer potential with a nearby shadow potential obtained by exactly equilibrating a partially linearized electronic energy functional. This provides conservative forces and reduces the global nonlinear equilibration to a quasi-diagonal linear problem that can be solved directly at low cost. Auxiliary dynamical electronic degrees of freedom, propagated through an extended-Lagrangian dynamics, capture the long-range electronic response and define the reference state for the linearization of the energy functional. GPU-accelerated simulations with over 100,000 atoms demonstrate that the shadow potential closely follows the tightly converged Born-Oppenheimer surface, while maintaining long-term stability at a computational cost approaching that of a fixed-charge model. These advances establish an efficient and broadly applicable framework for machine-learned interatomic potentials that explicitly incorporate long-range electrostatic interactions and environment-dependent electronic response.

Cage-enforced i-wave altermagnetism in boron-carbon clathrates

Meiyan Wang, Li Zhu

The spin splitting of an altermagnet arises from a rotation that relates its two magnetic sublattices. In known cases it is fixed by the crystal structure. Here we show that guest-host frameworks separate the two roles. In the boron-carbon clathrates $M$B$_3$C$_3$ the ordered cage removes the translation between the two guest sites and enforces cubic $i$-wave altermagnetism, with six symmetry-enforced nodal planes, for any magnetic guest in the compensated state; strain retunes the class to $d$- or $g$-wave. We demonstrate the platform with GdB$_3$C$_3$: its compensated antiferromagnetic ground state is a narrow-gap semiconductor with spin splitting of 0.19 eV at the conduction-band edge, and its weakly pinned Néel vector switches the helicity-resolved optical response on and off according to the magnetic point group. Thermodynamic stability at high pressure and dynamical stability at ambient pressure indicate that it can be made by the route established for this family.

Identifying the Shear Piezoelectric Coefficient using Physics-Informed Neural Networks under Exact Boundary and Initial Constraints

Suhas Suresh Bharadwaj

The shear piezoelectric coefficient $e_{15}$ governs the shear-mode electromechanical coupling in piezoelectric micromachined ultrasonic transducers (PMUTs) and surface acoustic wave (SAW) devices, but measuring it typically requires destructive resonance testing on dedicated specimens. On simulated data, $e_{15}$ can instead be recovered non-destructively from sparse, noisy subsurface wavefield observations using a constrained physics-informed neural network (PINN). By building exact spatial distance functions and temporal multipliers directly into the network output, the clamped, grounded boundaries and resting initial state hold automatically, leaving the loss with only the interior equations of coupled electro-elastodynamics and the data misfit. Non-dimensional scaling brings all governing coefficients within one decade of O(1), and the setup removes the boundary and initial-condition penalty weights that soft-constraint methods must tune. In a six-way comparison (plane-strain PZT-5H, manufactured wavefield, 30 dB noise), a soft-penalty baseline with adaptive weights converges to an erroneous $e_{15}$ in four of the five runs (51.7% mean error), and fixed penalties reach an error of 0.85% only after a second-order optimization stage. The constrained formulations reach mean $e_{15}$ errors between 0.15% and 0.51% with held-out displacement errors below 1%. Within these variants, the spatial multiplier gives rapid first-order convergence, and the quadratic temporal multiplier lowers the early-time electrostatic residual by a factor of 6.8 versus a linear one. On finite-element data along one subsurface line, the full formulation recovers $e_{15}$ with 1.19% mean error at 30 dB and keeps it between 0.65% and 2.71% across a 40 to 0 dB noise sweep. Finally, training on displacements alone still recovers $e_{15}$ with mean errors of 0.45% on finite-element and 1.64% on manufactured data.

Local and itinerant orbital contributions to the gyromagnetic response of paramagnetic metals

Garima Ahuja, Mirco Sastges, Dongwook Go, Shobhana Narasimhan, Stefan Blügel et al. (6 authors)

Orbitronics exploits orbital angular momentum to generate and manipulate angular-momentum currents for controlling magnetisation, offering new approaches to magnetic memory and logic. A quantitative description of this process requires establishing how orbital angular momentum contributes to magnetic moments and, consequently, to the gyromagnetic response that governs magnetisation dynamics. Here, we present a first-principles framework for calculating the effective gyromagnetic tensor (g') of paramagnetic metals, consistently incorporating local and itinerant orbital contributions, spin-orbit coupling, and both state- and occupation-change effects. Applying the framework to V and Pt, we find that orbital paramagnetic responses are comparable to or larger than spin contributions and substantially modify g' from the free-electron value. The atom-centered approximation can underestimate the orbital response by approximately a factor of two, while neglecting occupation changes produces significant errors in the calculated g'. Our calculated g' values for V and Pt agree with experiment, while their pronounced dependence on band filling reveals a route to engineering angular-momentum responses through electronic-structure control. The results establish the modern theory of orbital magnetism as an essential ingredient for describing gyromagnetic response and provide a framework for identifying materials with enhanced orbital functionalities.

Amplitude-conserving frequency demultiplexer based on a spin wave delay line

Gaël Yann Thiancourt, Thibaut Devolder

Propagating spin waves have emerged as promising candidates for signal processing and information transmission. We present a theoretical investigation of the generation, propagation and collection of spin-wave wave packets in micrometer-wide magnetic conduits, highlighting their potential for analogue frequency demultiplexing in space and time. We clarify the roles of the excitation duration and of the spin wave dispersion relation, as well as the influence of the spin-wave linewidth in setting the spatio-temporal evolution of propagating spin-wave wavepackets excited by Gaussian field pulses. We issue rules for the spatio-temporal separation of wavepackets that carry different frequencies. We finally propose an amplitude-conserving frequency demultiplexer based on a spin wave delay line of 60x5 $μ$m$^2$ footprint.

Piezogyration and elastogyration in ternary chalcopyrites from first principles

Asier Zabalo, Fernando Gómez-Ortiz, Aldo H. Romero, Eric Bousquet

Natural optical activity arises from the spatial dispersion of the optical response and is described by the rank-two gyration pseudotensor. Its dependence on mechanical stress and strain, quantified by the piezogyration and elastogyration tensors, respectively, has been investigated experimentally for decades. However, a systematic first-principles description of these effects remains largely unexplored. Here we present a first-principles methodology to compute the full piezogyration and elastogyration tensors, and apply it to a set of representative ternary chalcopyrite crystals. The gyration of the unstrained crystal turns out to be a poor predictor of the magnitude of the elastogyration response, which we find to be a property inherited from their parent zinc-blende structures. The elastic compliance tensor further shapes the piezogyration response, with particularly pronounced effects in AgGaS$_2$, a well-known achiral yet optically active crystal. Finally, we explore strain-induced chirality through the trace of the gyration tensor, the only pseudoscalar contribution to the natural optical activity that is linear in the tensor components and encodes information about chirality.

FACTO: fast atom-centered tensor operator for accurate and efficient foundational machine-learned interatomic potentials

Siyuan Liu, Chang Han, Sheng Gong, Yanping Yang, Tianze Zheng et al. (9 authors)

High-throughput inference remains a central challenge for equivariant machine-learned interatomic potentials. Here we introduce FACTO, an atom-centered equivariant architecture that confines evolving learned state and dense channel mixing to atoms, expressing neighbor interactions as fully fusible reductions. We complement this design with custom register-accumulated aggregation kernels and tensor-core FP32 emulation, accelerating inference while retaining FP32-level energy conservation under emulated arithmetic in molecular dynamics. Comprehensive evaluations on Matbench Discovery, MLIP Arena and MatCalc demonstrate high throughput alongside strong predictive accuracy and physical reliability. On Matbench Discovery, the four FACTO-OAM variants show top-tier performance by overall rank sum among 46 models with complete scores, advancing the accuracy-throughput Pareto frontier. On MLIP Arena, FACTO achieves state- of-the-art stability under extreme heating and compression, alongside high fidelity in hydrogen combustion. On MatCalc, FACTO outperforms the compared baselines in structural and elastic predictions while retaining competitive accuracy for energetic and thermal properties.

Pressure-correlation phase diagram unraveling quantum phase transitions in altermagnetic $ε$-FeOOH and realization of piezo-anomalous Hall effect

Arijit Mandal, Nayana Devaraj, Birabar Ranjit Kumar Nanda

We establish a pressure-correlation phase diagram that yields multiple pressure-tunable quantum phase transitions in iron oxyhydroxide ($ε$-FeOOH) across three critical pressures, $P_{c1}>P_{c2}>P_{c3}$. The transitions arise from the competition among electron-correlation, crystal-field, and Hund's coupling. First-principles calculations reveal that the earlier reported high-spin (HS) to low-spin (LS) crossover at correlation-sensitive $P_{c1}$ is accompanied by a transition from charge-transfer to Mott insulating (CTI-MI) phase, and a change in the spin-quantization axis (SQA) from $\hat{y}$ to $\hat{x}$. The HS-LS crossover and CTI-MI transition is driven by the simultaneous increase in crystal-field splitting and decrease in Hund's coupling. The critical pressure $P_{c2}$ governing the structural transition from centrosymmetric $Pnnm$ phase to noncentrosymmetric $P2_1nm$ phase is found to be independent of electron-correlation. When correlation is strong, a third critical pressure ($P_{c3}$) emerges in the lower-pressure regime of the phase diagram. Validating the recent experimental observation, $P_{c3}$ is responsible for changing the SQA from $\hat{z}$ to $\hat{y}$. By exhibiting altermagnetism in both $P2_1nm$ and $Pnnm$ phases and possessing finite anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) only for SQA along $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{y}$, $ε$-FeOOH enables a unique piezo-anomalous Hall effect where one can switch on the AHC at a lower pressure and change its direction at a higher pressure.
