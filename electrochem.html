The Acridine Polymorph Landscape: Characterization and Selective Control by Additives

Adam Lahouari, Hengyu Zhou, Nikolaos Galanakis, Michael Kilgour, Daisuke Kuroshima et al. (10 authors)

Polymorphism in molecular crystals presents a major challenge for controlling crystallization. Here, we combine experiment and theory to characterize the polymorph landscape of acridine and investigate its selective control by additives. Melt crystallization experiments resolve the previously unknown structure of form VIII, while topological crystal structure prediction recovers all seven known unsolvated polymorphs with determined crystal structures. Combining a fine-tuned machine-learned interatomic potential with anharmonic free energy calculations reproduces the experimentally observed stability relationships and reveals a shallow free energy landscape. Finally, experimentally observed additive-induced polymorph preferences correlate with mixed-crystal substitutional defect energies and topological similarities between additive and acridine crystal structures. Together, these results provide a comprehensive picture of acridine polymorphism, linking crystal structure, thermodynamic stability, and additive-controlled crystallization.

Dynamical quantum simulations as laboratories for quantum information

Su Yeon Chang, Hailey S. Murray, Brian Barch, Greeshma Oruganti, Luis Pedro García-Pintos et al. (8 authors)

Dynamical quantum simulations are commonly designed to access a particular response function or correlation function. Yet the underlying protocol prepares a family of many-body states that contains information well beyond the observable for which the simulation was originally designed. In this work, we explore the idea of using these states as laboratories for quantum information. As a case study, we consider the local-pulse protocols used to compute Green functions in finite XX and XXX spin chains, and we interpret opposite source rotations as encoding a classical bit into the many-body system. This allows us to follow how the resulting record propagates, how it is encoded across local subsystems, and how readily it can be accessed with different measurements. We also find a clear separation between representation and readout. While fermionic covariance data exactly reconstruct the signed response in the XX chain, simple spin measurements recover a larger fraction of the locally available information in the XXX chain. This reversed ordering persists when we move from finite-pulse discrimination to local source-angle estimation. Going beyond the source record, the same reduced states also allow us to reinterpret results in terms of Bell state recovery and quantum communication rates, as well as providing direct access to the evolving bipartite and genuine multipartite entanglement of the system. Our results show that dynamical quantum simulations developed for response calculations can naturally be used to ask a much broader set of quantum-information questions about the many-body states they already prepare.

On-the-fly Estimation of Quantum-Corrected Thermodynamics in LAMMPS

Robert Ramji, Ilya Mazalov, Ricardo Buarque, Alexandria Do, Xiaoxu Ruan et al. (6 authors)

We present a native implementation of the Two/Three-Phase Thermodynamics (xPT, $x \in \{2,3\}$) method within the LAMMPS molecular dynamics framework, termed FixXPT. xPT is an approach for obtaining absolute, quantum-corrected thermodynamics (entropy, enthalpy, free energy, heat capacity, diffusivity) from the density of states function, obtained from the velocity autocorrelation function of an equilibrium trajectory. FixXPT addresses non-trivial integration challenges, including atom reordering, compute scheduling, FFT backends, unit systems, and partitioning of atomic velocities into molecular modes. Two autocorrelators are provided: a Wiener-Khinchin FFT correlator with one cumulative window-end operation, and an in-line multi-tau correlator whose memory footprint grows only logarithmically with the window length, removing the principal scaling bottleneck for long analysis windows. The velocity history of each window is distributed across the MPI ranks, so the memory the fix adds to a node stays nearly constant as ranks are added and its cost falls with the number of GPUs. Replaying identical velocity frames through FixXPT and the stand-alone pyxPT post-processor reproduces every thermodynamic observable of well-sampled groups to within 0.1 to 0.5% across the full analysis-mode matrix; sparsely populated ionic species agree within their statistical uncertainty, and the multi-tau VACF converges monotonically to the FFT result as its buffer grows, from 0.8% to 0.1% drift across a 16-fold buffer sweep. The code is distributed as a drop-in LAMMPS package.

Back-End-Of-Line Integration of 2-Terminal Ferroelectric Hafnia Synaptic Weights on Commercial CMOS Process

Alexander Flasby, Anwesha Panda, Nathan Savoia, Laura Bégon-Lours

Functionalising the back-end-of-line (BEOL) of CMOS with emerging materials is a key route to neural network accelerators and neuromorphic systems. In this work, we demonstrate for the first time the integration of 2-terminal ferroelectric synaptic weights in the BEOL of as-delivered commercial 180 nm CMOS dies. A novel material stack is proposed, in which a WOx/[HfO2-ZrO2] bilayer is sandwiched between TiN and Mo electrodes. For devices annealed at 450 °C, programming below 1.8 V is achieved with a cycle-to-cycle variation below 3 %. Synaptic weights annealed at 500 °C exhibit more gradual weight updates, while the tested underlying NMOS transistors remain functional after the complete integration process. The trade-off between thermal budget and on-device training performance is illustrated using hardware-aware neural network simulations.

StudDFT: A quantum chemistry program for educational and research purposes

Stanislav K. Ignatov

StudDFT is a quantum chemistry program for Hartree--Fock (HF), density functional theory (DFT), and second-order Møller--Plesset (MP2) calculations of small and medium-sized molecules. It is intended primarily for teaching and student research, but its accuracy suffices for routine research calculations. Version 1.30.2 implements the RHF/UHF, RKS/UKS, and RMP2/UMP2/PUMP2 schemes in Gaussian basis sets, including effective core potentials (ECP); LDA, GGA (BLYP, BP86, BPW91, PBE), meta-GGA (r$^2$SCAN), global hybrid (B3LYP, PBE0) and range-separated hybrid ($ω$B97, $ω$B97X, $ω$B97X-D) functionals; analytical gradients and Hessians; geometry optimization, transition-state and reaction-path (IRC) searches, potential energy surface scans, harmonic frequencies and IR intensities, thermochemistry, counterpoise corrections, D3(BJ) dispersion, polarizable continuum solvation, density fitting (RI-J/RI-JK), linear-response TDDFT, and fractional-occupation smearing. The program has been verified against ORCA and PySCF on 50 molecules (30 for optimizations): the mean absolute deviations of the single-point energies do not exceed 7 $μ$Ha for HF, MP2, and the LDA, GGA, and global hybrid functionals except PBE and PBE0, whose systematic shifts of several tens of $μ$Ha are traced mainly to the value of $μ$ in the PBE exchange; those of the optimized interatomic distances and harmonic frequencies do not exceed 1.2$\times$10$^{-4}$ Å and 1.2 cm$^{-1}$. Corrections made since the first public version (0.82), mainly affecting vibrational frequencies, are documented with their effect on the verification results. Unlike large multi-language packages, StudDFT is a monolithic Fortran code with OpenMP parallelization, which simplifies its use in courses. Binaries for Windows and Linux, the documentation, and all test inputs and outputs are freely available.

Electric-Field Induction Motor with Discrete Circuit Elements and Improved Rotor Dynamics

Alex S. Miller, Jeffrey H. Lang

Electric-field-based actuators and machines lead the design space at the nanoscale and in biological systems, while having the potential to be used in favorable macroscale applications which prioritize minimal mass, performance in a vacuum, or performance in high temperatures. Electric-field induction motors consist of a set of stator electrodes which induce opposite charges onto a movable rotor surface. In earlier work, rotor surfaces for electric-field induction motors have been made of single continuum materials, which are limited in possible resistivity, permittivity and circuitry characteristics. To enhance design freedom, we develop a rotor for electric-field-based machines which may be constructed out of discrete circuit components between a finite number of conductive vanes. Magnetic-field induction motors, which are common in machinery, often utilize ``deep bar'' rotors, consisting of conducting bars which decrease the rotor time constant with increasing frequency using the skin depth effect. We propose, analyze, design and demonstrate a macro-scale electric-field induction motor which uses circuitry on the rotor surface to emulate the ``deep bar'' design, providing improved torque over a wide range of speeds.

Electric Field-Induced Hole- and Electron-Type Flat Bands in Twisted Double Bilayer Graphene

Zhihao Jiang, Dongkyu Lee, Alfred J. H. Jones, Youngju Park, Paulina Majchrzak et al. (14 authors)

Application of a finite electric field and small twist angle between two graphene bilayers leads to widely tunable electronic structures that support emergent phenomena. Here, we measure the electronic structure of twisted double bilayer graphene with twist angles of 3.1$^{\circ}$ and 6.0$^{\circ}$ using micro-focused angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (microARPES) on in situ gated samples. At a twist angle of 6.0$^{\circ}$, we find that the dispersion around the valence and conduction band extrema is unchanged compared to separate graphene bilayers, whereas electric field tunable hybridisation gaps and band flattening effects occur at higher binding energies. In the 3.1$^{\circ}$ structure, we find that the resulting superlattice drives dispersion changes around the valence and conduction band edges. Increasing the gate-induced doping in the bottom bilayer leads to the formation of flat bands between the valence band maximum in the bottom bilayer and conduction band minimum in the top bilayer. Our experiments establish combinations of twist angle and electric field that potentially support strong electron-hole excitations and that provide a benchmark for the theoretical description of moderately twisted double bilayer graphene.

Unconventional SOC-activated Altermagnetic Spin Splitting and Half-Angle Locking of Spin Transport

Weiwei Chen, Ziyu Zhou, Pan Gao, Yanan Pan, Zhongjun Li et al. (8 authors)

Conventional altermagnetic spin splitting arises from staggered exchange and sublattice-dependent anisotropic hopping without requiring spin-orbit coupling (SOC). Here we derive a low-energy effective model showing that SOC and staggered exchange can jointly activate additional spin splitting on momentum planes where the nonrelativistic contribution vanishes. In hexagonal $α$-MnTe, this SOC-activated contribution produces d-wave spin splitting near $Γ$ with out-of-plane spin polarization. Its leading angular pattern counter-rotates by half the in-plane Néel-vector rotation angle. First-principles spin-transport calculations reveal a corresponding approximate half-angle locking between the Néel-vector orientation and the electric-field directions maximizing the signed longitudinal and transverse spin conductivities. Analytical transport calculations based on the effective model trace this locking to the same angular coupling between momentum and the Néel vector that governs the spin-splitting pattern. Our findings connect the microscopic mechanism of SOC-activated altermagnetic spin splitting to directional spin transport, providing a theoretical basis for probing Néel-vector orientation and controlling spin-current generation.

Microscopic constitutive freedom in metamaterials

Zhongjian Mei, Chuanhao Ge, Nengyin Wang, J. Stuart Bolton, Tongyang Shi et al. (7 authors)

Metamaterials expand the range of accessible macroscopic responses through architectural design. Here, a cross-scale design strategy is introduced that combines microscopic control of static constitutive properties with spectral engineering through macroscopic architecture. Beyond shaping frequency-dependent responses, this strategy modifies the corresponding broadband performance bounds in passive systems by engineering the static parameters entering causal constraints. The concept is realized in granular activated carbon, where reversible sorption enhances equilibrium compressibility. Extrapolation of a multiscale model supported by low-frequency measurements to the zero-frequency limit yields an effective static bulk modulus approximately half the conventional nonsorbing isothermal reference value. This reduction substantially lowers the corresponding causal minimum-thickness bound for rigidly backed absorbers. A cascaded hierarchical composite architecture further redistributes absorption toward the target frequency band, achieving near-perfect absorption with a normal-incidence absorption coefficient above 0.9 across most of the 40--6400 Hz range. Together, static constitutive control and macroscopic spectral design enable ultrabroadband sound absorption beyond the conventional isothermal thickness benchmark without violating causality. This strategy offers a new approach to extending the attainable performance of metamaterials.

Free-vortex-wake dynamics of airborne wind energy kites in crosswind flight

Yinong Tian, Shan Wang

The wake of an airborne wind energy kite is shaped by its crosswind path and, for on-board generation, by the slipstreams of its rotors, and in farms it reaches downstream units. It is computed here by a vortex step method with a time-marching free vortex wake, implemented by the authors in their open-source code OpenFAWE with a lumped-mass tether, actuator-disc rotors releasing vortex rings and several kites. It reproduces an independent vortex step code to 10^-9 on the rigid-wing Makani M600 and within 0.3% in lift and 0.9% in drag on the TU Delft V3 leading-edge inflatable kite, matching its Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes lift within 3.1%. For fixed kites the lift deficit of a finite wake is the far field of its starting vortex, which fixes the wake length before a run, and the vortex core must stay below the narrowest panel width. With these settings the helical tip-vortex pair of the M600 on its loop lowers its lift by about 0.5% from 5 to 13 m/s. Free rotor rings do not converge with the time step, whereas an undeformed slipstream tube converges once its rings are refined at the wing and raises the lift by 1.9%. A slipstream deflected by the wing lowers it by 3.5%, so the treatment near the wing sets the sign of the rotor effect. A second unit one loop diameter downstream loses 0.5% of its lift and 2.2% of its aerodynamic power, with or without rotors.
