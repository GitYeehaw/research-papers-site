Secret-shared teleportation of a two-qudit gate with a deferred recipient

Tanay Roy

An unknown quantum operation, like an unknown quantum state, cannot be perfectly copied or stored for later use. A specific gate can be embedded in an entangled resource and teleported, but its destination is fixed when the resource is created. Here we show that the capability to apply a two-qudit entangling gate can instead be secret-shared among many parties: the shares are distributed in advance, and a later key release lets exactly one of them, chosen after distribution, exercise it. A provider encrypts half of a Bell pair held with a user into $n$ shares, one per candidate node. The selected node, given the key qudits, completes an exact non-local $\mathtt{CSUM}^{k}$ gate with the user's data, in every measurement branch and for any qudit dimension, with the power $k$ chosen by the user alone. Any coalition of fewer than $n$ candidate nodes, or the key register alone, holds no information, unselected nodes cannot tell whether a selection occurred, and the key cannot decrypt twice. For two candidates the registers form a $((3,5))$ threshold scheme. Encoder and decoder are explicit $O(n)$ circuits. The construction supports jointly authorized and anonymous gate application, advancing distributed quantum computing and quantum network capabilities.

Baker--Akhiezer sections from Higgs bundles and Landau levels in hyperbolic band theory

Duong Dinh, Steven Rayan

As observed by Kienzle and the second author, rank-two $\mathcal{O}(3)$-twisted Higgs bundles on the projective line encode all hyperbolic crystals of genus two: the spectral curves are the unit cells, and the Hitchin fibers are the abelian Brillouin zones. In a uniform magnetic field of two flux quanta, the lowest Landau level is one-dimensional at every nonzero momentum, and, as found by Iengo and Li, its dimension jumps to two at the zero momentum. We identify the states of the lowest Landau level with the Baker--Akhiezer sections of the Higgs bundles, and their zeros, the vortices, with the Baker--Akhiezer divisors. A family of nonzero momenta limits to the zero momentum exactly when each of the two vortices approaches the image of the other under the spatial inversion, and the states of the jumped level are labeled by the directions of limiting. On the Higgs bundle side, the zero momentum is the point where the Hitchin fiber intersects the Hitchin section. Such a family of Higgs bundles limits to the Hitchin section, thereby crossing to a lower stratum of the \BB stratification, an example of the phenomenon investigated in terms of Baker--Akhiezer divisors by the first author with Figiel and Teschner.

Time-optimal quantum dynamics under fixed-norm generators

Oskar A. Prośniak, Karol Życzkowski

We investigate the dynamics of quantum systems whose evolution is governed by linear generators of fixed unitarily invariant norm. For this broad class of evolutions, we derive a universal time-optimal generator of the dynamics converting one given state into another. It provides a time-optimal non-Hermitian Hamiltonian for pure state conversion. We then use this general result to derive quantum speed limits for mixed-state evolutions governed by Lindbladians and non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. Our findings follow from the geometric properties of the group $GL(n,\mathbb{C})$, in particular the geodesic distance. The derived bounds hold for an arbitrary system size $n$, depend only on the norms of the evolution generator and the initial and final states, and do not require performing any time-averaging.

Weighted hypergraph states as a resource for quantum metrology

B. J. Alexander, M. S. Tame

Quantum correlations can be used to improve sensing precision beyond classical limits, yet the correlated resource states needed can be challenging to prepare and preserve. Here, we study the family of weighted hypergraph states and identify subclasses characterized by their underlying graph topology that exhibit enhanced metrological performance. Using the quantum Fisher information and a novel Dicke state basis representation, we show that these subclasses outperform both separable and standard weighted graph state benchmarks over broad ranges of edge weights corresponding to weak controlled operations between qubits representing the vertices of the graph. We provide analytical formulas for an arbitrary number $N$ of qubits, supplemented by a general study, while focusing a detailed analysis on the example case of $N=4$. By identifying the critical probabilities for local dephasing, we show that adding higher-order weighted hyperedges for $N$ qubits can enhance metrological robustness, allowing the resulting states to retain a quantum advantage at higher noise levels. These probe states exhibit favorable scaling, enhanced precision, and noise tolerance. Our findings establish weighted hypergraph states as viable, structurally tunable and robust resources for quantum-enhanced metrology.

Persistence of Unitary Almost Mathieu Operator Gaps Ten Dry Martinis, Five on the Rim

Dan S. Borgnia, Matthew Faust, Long Li

We determine exactly which gap labels are realized by open spectral gaps for the unitary almost Mathieu operator at every irrational frequency and all coupling constants ($0<λ_1,λ_2\leq1$). When both couplings are strictly less than one, every permitted gap label is realized by an open gap, including in the critical case ($λ_1=λ_2$). When at least one coupling equals one, both lifts of every odd winding label are realized by open gaps, whereas all even winding labels are collapsed. The proof combines quantitative almost reducibility and Aubry duality with a monotonicity principle for gap heights.

Functional Tensor Trains for High-Order Greeks and Exposure Tails of Multi-Asset Structured Products

Javier Lopez-Piqueres, Atithi Acharya, Yue Sun, Niraj Kumar

Risk management of worst-of autocallables needs price sensitivities (Greeks) up to third order and the potential future exposure, a tail quantile of the note's future value. In Monte Carlo, each order of differentiation multiplies the sampling noise by a factor that grows with the observation frequency, and nested simulation turns the noise of its inner paths into a bias of the quantile. We instead solve the backward pricing recursion, storing the continuation value as an $hp$ functional tensor train (hp-FTT) whose piecewise-polynomial cores are split at the barriers, so that worst-of barrier events act exactly, with rank one. For Gaussian transitions we show that the polynomial degree sufficient for an error $\varepsilon$ grows only as $\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}$. One solve then gives every Greek at any spot without sampling noise, by integrating the likelihood-ratio weight against the stored value, and the values it stores at future dates replace inner simulation. Against the strongest Monte Carlo estimators we could build or find published, on worst-of autocallables observed monthly on three to five assets, the order of the Greek and the accuracy decide which method is cheaper. At $1\%$ accuracy Monte Carlo is more than an order of magnitude cheaper for deltas, second-order Greeks go either way, and hp-FTT is cheaper for every third-order Greek, by up to two orders of magnitude. At $0.1\%$ hp-FTT is cheaper for every Greek we could check, under geometric Brownian motion and local volatility alike. For the exposure alone Monte Carlo is cheaper at $1\%$ and hp-FTT at $0.1\%$, and a solve run for the Greeks yields it for little more than scenario sampling. Sampling pays in variance for each derivative and hp-FTT in rank for each asset, so Monte Carlo keeps low-order Greeks at percent accuracy, rarely observed contracts and many assets.

Topologically Protected Edge States in a Single Flux Qubit

Pablo Perez-Fernandez, Manuel Pino

We show that topological edge states can be realized in a simple superconducting circuit composed of an inductively shunted flux qubit. The Josephson potential of that qubit forms a honeycomb lattice similar to the case of graphene. The additional shunting element produces a quartic potential along one flux direction, which effectively creates a boundary in flux space. We map analytically the low-energy dynamics of the circuit to an extended Su--Schrieffer--Heeger (SSH) chain, in which a quartic potential is added to the usual SSH Hamiltonian. This mapping is confirmed by numerical simulations of the full superconducting circuit. We argue that chiral symmetry still holds for the edge states of the extended model around a chiral point in parameter space. Close to this point, we numerically find that edge states in our superconducting circuit are topologically protected against perturbations that obey chiral symmetry, as is the case for charge noise. Our results show that topological effects previously thought to occur in multi-qubit systems, such as protection against decoherence, can also be found in much simpler single superconducting qubits.

Charge Pumping Leptogenesis from the Symmetric Mass Generation Phase on a Four-Leg Ladder Lattice

Runze Chi, Meng Zeng, Tao Xiang, Yi-Zhuang You, Bertrand I. Halperin et al. (6 authors)

Quantized charge pumping in quantum Hall systems is a bulk topological property, typically manifesting on gapless boundaries as spectral flow through chiral edge modes upon flux insertion. When the boundary is anomaly-free, it can be symmetrically gapped by symmetric mass generation (SMG) interactions. However, the effects on quantized charge pumping at an interacting gapped boundary are unclear. We address this using density-matrix renormalization group simulations of a four-leg ladder quasi-(2+1)d realization of the (1+1)d 3-4-5-0 lattice chiral fermion model with six-fermion interactions at one edge. We find gapless modes near the interacting edge for both weak and strong couplings, but the edge can be in a fully gapped SMG phase at intermediate coupling. We also identify spontaneous breaking of the model's $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry above a critical interaction strength. Across all three boundary phases, we find quantized charge pumping with the same pumped charge per flux quantum. The spatial distribution of the pumped charge varies between phases. Besides charge pumping, we study how a single injected fermion propagates under open boundary conditions. In the SMG phase, an interaction-induced flavor-mixing current develops at the endpoints of the gapped edge, which is absent in the weak- and strong-coupling limits. This current reveals a charge-redistribution mechanism unique to the SMG phase. Additionally, we propose an analogy between charge pumping in this model and a topological leptogenesis scenario in the (3+1)d Standard Model. The anomaly-free chiral U(1) symmetries of the 3-4-5-0 model play a role analogous to the baryon-minus-lepton symmetry of the Standard Model. In both settings, charge pumping can be associated with a continuous deformation along a classically defined but quantum-anomalous direction, while a discrete subgroup remains an exact quantum symmetry.

Hyperbolic scaling of the small-exponent limit in magnetic nonlinear electrodynamics

Jonathan A. Rebouças, R. Martínez von Dossow, Edson Otoniel

A magnetic spacetime can approach Schwarzschild while its electromagnetic characteristics remain singular in the same parameter limit. We study this separation in fractional-power nonlinear electrodynamics as the exponent tends to zero with asymptotic mass, magnetic charge and nonlinear coupling independently fixed. The extraordinary polarization admits a common physical time direction only outside a boundary that grows as the inverse fourth root of the exponent. On this radial scale, the lapse tends to unity but the constitutive angular response retains a nondegenerate barrier and finite scaled critical data. An exact representation of the exterior electromagnetic energy yields complete second-order corrections to the orbit and impact, separating mass and charge contributions and their distinct cancellations. Constraining the mass to equal the electromagnetic self-energy preserves the leading critical constants while changing the approach from fourth-root to square-root corrections. Returning rays remain inside the admissible exterior and define a limiting scattering function with a calculated logarithmic strong-deflection coefficient. In the weak regime, constitutive bending decays as the inverse fourth power of scaled impact, whereas finite mass supplies a slower inverse-impact tail. We determine their competition scale, its accessibility above the critical impact, and the accuracy of the two-term approximation. Independent energy, orbit and scattering calculations check the expansions and resolve their parameter-dependent convergence. The analysis shows how a nearly vacuum background can support a distinct exterior optical limit through the nonlinear constitutive response.

The Quartic Gap Between Quantum and Dequantized Algorithms for Linear Algebra Is Tight

Shouvanik Chakrabarti, Dylan Herman, Junhyung Lyle Kim, Guneykan Ozgul

Quantum algorithms for linear algebra that access a matrix $A$ through quantum random access memory (QRAM) prepare quantum states proportional to the solutions of linear systems and related problems. Dequantized classical algorithms, when given sampling and query access to $A$, return sampling and query access to an approximate solution. Both kinds of algorithms use a number of queries that is polynomial in the normalized Frobenius norm $ρ=\|A\|_F/\|A\|$ and does not directly depend on the dimension. For linear systems at fixed condition number and accuracy, quantum algorithms use $\widetilde O(ρ)$ queries, whereas the best dequantized algorithms use $\widetilde O(ρ^4)$. We prove that this quartic gap is tight in the worst case, up to a logarithmic factor. For every constant $κ_0>1$, we construct dense linear systems with condition number at most $κ_0$ for which sampling from the solution within a sufficiently small constant total variation distance requires $Ω(ρ^4/\logρ)$ classical queries but only $Θ(ρ)$ quantum queries. The same separation holds, for errors below smaller thresholds, for positive-definite systems, least squares, ridge regression, matrix powers and square roots, imaginary-time and real-time Hamiltonian evolution, and sampling from the top principal component. Our results therefore show that many quantum algorithms for linear algebra, achieve a near-quartic speedup in the worst case, over classical algorithms with sampling and query access. All of these classical lower bounds follow from the same framework, which is based on a lower bound for sampling from distributions that depend on a fixed set of Fourier coefficients of an unknown Boolean function.
