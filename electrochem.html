Quantum amplification with a linear quantum coupler

Changqing Wang, Yao Lu

Josephson parametric amplifiers (JPAs) are essential components for quantum-limited microwave measurement, but their dynamic range is often constrained by Kerr and other pump-induced nonlinear effects. In this work, we systematically study the performance of a parametric amplifier based on the linear inductive coupler (LINC). Starting from the driven-circuit Hamiltonian, we derive the effective three-wave-mixing amplifier model, including leading flux-driven nonlinear corrections relevant to realistic operation. We analyze the resulting gain response, saturation behavior, and intermodulation distortion, comparing them with representative state-of-the-art JPA designs. Our results show that the symmetry-protected structure of the LINC suppresses static Kerr nonlinearity. Moreover, suitable circuit-parameter optimization can further reduce the dressed-squeezing and driven-Kerr effects, leading to considerable improvements in power-handling metrics. These results identify the LINC architecture as a promising platform for high-linearity Josephson parametric amplification.

Effect of superplastic deformation on the intergranular corrosion resistance of austenitic steel 321 with different types of UFG structure: A case of homogeneous and heterogeneous microstructure obtained by ECAP

A. V. Nokhrin, V. I. Kopylov, M. K. Chegurov, S. V. Shotin, C. V. Likhnitskii et al. (11 authors)

The effects of superplasticity (SP) on the structure, hardness, and intergranular corrosion (IGC) resistance of UFG 321L steel are reported. By increasing the number of the ECAP cycles (N) and varying the ECAP temperature, and annealing temperature, the degree of the microstructural heterogeneity was changed. Regardless of the microstructural homogeneity degree, UFG steels demonstrated the possibility of realizing the SP effect at temperatures below the onset temperature of dynamic recrystallization. The dependence of ductility of the UFG steel on the test temperature is non-monotonic with a minimum at 450-600C. The maximum elongation to failure (~250%) was achieved at a SP temperature of 800C. The SP deformation of the UFG steel is controlled by the power law creep. It is shown that the hardness of UFG steel samples after SP is equal than the hardness of UFG steels annealed at the same temperature. It is also shown what SP leads to a decrease in the IGC resistance of the UFG steel. Heterostructured steel have reduced ductility, as well as lower resistance to IGC after tension tests at room temperature (RT). The minimum IGC resistance is ensured at N=2, and the maximum - at N=4. The IGC resistance of homogeneous UFG samples after tension testing exceeds the IGC resistance of coarse-grained steel. Tension test at a RT has the greatest negative effect on the IGC resistance of heterostructured steel, and the least negative effect on the IGC resistance of homogeneous steel. Increasing the number of ECAP cycles enhances the ductility of the UFG steel at 750-900C. Increasing the ECAP temperature results in a simultaneous decrease in ductility and flow stress. Heterostructured steel exhibits minimal values of the activation energy of SP deformation. Increasing the number of ECAP cycles and increasing the ECAP temperature lead to an increase in the activation energy of SP deformation.

Dynamics of a Single-Domain Dissipative Magnetic Gyroscope in Electric and Magnetic Fields

Y. B. Band, Igor Kuzmenko

We formulate the coupled spin--rotation dynamics of a single-domain symmetric top ferromagnetic needle (a magnetic gyroscope) in static magnetic and electric fields. The conservative Hamiltonian contains the rotational kinetic energy, the magnetocrystalline and shape anisotropy, the Zeeman energy, the electrostatic energy of the dipole induced in the conducting needle, the linear magnetoelectric coupling, and the relativistic motional (spin-orbit) coupling. Gilbert damping is introduced, and in an electric field, Debye relaxation of the induced electric dipole of the conducting needle also results in dissipation. We give the equations of motion in the laboratory and body-fixed frames, with singularity-free quaternion kinematics. For a 120-nm-long single-domain cobalt needle in a 0.5 T field we find: (i) because the anisotropy field ($\approx 1.3$ T) exceeds the applied magnetic field, the spin reverses direction several times during the initial Gilbert-damping transient, and the parity of the number of reversals determines whether the easy axis ends up parallel or antiparallel to the spin; (ii) after the transient the needle librates about the field---precession accompanied by nutation at twice the precession frequency---with a period of about 20 ns, consistent with the libration period $2π(I_1/μB)^{1/2}$, while the Zeeman and rotational kinetic energies oscillate out of phase and the total energy is conserved; (iii) a static electric field $\mathbf E$ exerts on the conducting needle a shape-dependent polarization torque that tends to align the long axis of the needle with $\mathbf E$. The torques due to the magnetoelectric interaction and motional magnetic fields ($\lesssim1~μ$T) are negligible compared with the polarization torque. A conducting needle can therefore serve as a magnetometer only if $E\ll E_c$.

Localized corrosion resistance of ultrafine-grained austenitic steel 321 obtained by Rotary Swaging: The role of annealing conditions

A. V. Nokhrin, M. K. Chegurov, N. V. Melekhin, D. A. Zotov, V. I. Kopylov et al. (7 authors)

The object of this study was austenitic steel 321, which has an ultrafine-grained (UFG) structure formed by Rotary Swaging (RS). RS leads to the formation of a two-phase macroscopically heterogeneous and, at the same time, a microscopically homogeneous UFG structure with an increased content of martensite. It has been shown that secondary TiC nanoparticles form in the annealing at 450-550C, the reverse transformation of martensite into austenite occurs at 550-650C, and recrystallization begins at 700C. Electrochemical studies of the resistance of the UFG steel to general corrosion, intergranular corrosion (IGC), and pitting corrosion (PC) have been carried out. It has been shown that the UFG steel after RS has a reduced IGC resistance. The surface of steel rods after RS has a reduced hardness and corrosion resistance compared to the central part of the rods. It has been shown that in the temperature range of 450-550C, there is a slight increase in the microhardness, and a significant decrease in the IGC and PC resistance of the UFG steel. Recrystallization at 700-800C leads to an increased PC resistance and IGC resistance. Annealing conditions have been determined that ensure a good combination of strength, ductility and corrosion resistance of the UFG steel. It was demonstrated that to explain the non-monotonic variation in the localized corrosion rate of UFG steel during annealing, it is necessary to consider not only the well-known effects of martensite and delta-ferrite, but also the precipitation of TiC particles and the changes in the free energy of non-equilibrium grain boundaries.

The MC-QTAIM: Why, How, and When?

Shant Shahbazian

The multi-component quantum theory of atoms in molecules (MC-QTAIM) generalizes the original QTAIM framework developed by Richard Bader and colleagues, extending atoms-in-molecules analysis beyond ab initio electronic wavefunctions. MC-QTAIM facilitates the examination of the AIM structure in systems and states that require MC wavefunctions, which describe systems containing multiple distinguishable classes of quantum particles. This advancement broadens the applicability of AIM analysis to a wide range of quantum systems and states that were previously inaccessible. These include exotic molecules, such as positronic species; exotic quantum states, such as superposed proton states in molecules with two- or multi-well potential energy surfaces; and non-adiabatic states of conventional molecules. In this review, I aim to explain the fundamental principles of MC-QTAIM analysis in accessible language and explore some of its potential applications. Additionally, the general formalism for energy partitioning in MC systems, called MC Interacting Quantum Atoms (MC-IQA), is introduced. This scheme unifies the energy partitioning of numerous quantum systems and encompasses the IQA method originally developed by Pendas and colleagues, as well as the previously developed two-component IQA (TC-IQA) scheme - developed by our group to analyze positronic systems - as special cases.

Second-Order Polarization Propagator Approximation to the Quadratic Unitary Coupled Cluster Framework I: First-Order Properties

Kamal Majee, Sudipta Chakraborty, Amrita Manna, Ján Šimunek, Jozef Noga et al. (6 authors)

We have developed a new formulation for calculating first-order properties in the quadratic unitary coupled-cluster singles and doubles (qUCCSD) method, using a second-order polarizable propagator approximation. The accuracy and reliability of this new framework of the proposed approach have been assessed through the calculation of ground- and excited-state dipole moments and oscillator strengths for a set of small molecules. The Hermiticity of the UCC similarity-transformed Hamiltonian allows molecular properties to be evaluated without introducing left-hand eigenvectors, thereby simplifying the theoretical formulation and computational procedure. Furthermore, the proposed formulation preserves the size-intensivity of transition moments, a property that is not generally guaranteed within the conventional non-Hermitian equation-of-motion coupled-cluster framework. To assess the performance of the proposed method, we have benchmarked both ground- and excited-state properties for 11 small molecules against the extrapolated full configuration interaction (exFCI) reference values and compared with the conventional coupled cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) and third-order algebraic diagrammatic construction (ADC(3)) results. The qUCCSD method yields average errors comparable to those of CCSD while demonstrating significantly improved accuracy over the ADC(3) method.

Charge-transfer excitation drives cooperative phonon-assisted exciton self-trapping in NiPS$_3$

Long Cheng, Alexander Holm Ohrt, Oliver Nagy, Yu Zhao, Chenyu Wang et al. (19 authors)

Carrier self-trapping is a fundamental relaxation pathway in strongly correlated quantum materials, yet the microscopic lattice motions that drive it have remained largely inaccessible to direct observation. Here we investigate the van der Waals antiferromagnet NiPS$_3$ upon excitation across its ligand-to-metal charge-transfer gap, and reveal a nonequilibrium pathway that initially generates unbound carriers before they evolve into correlated excitonic states. Using ultrabroadband optical-pump terahertz-probe spectroscopy, we simultaneously track carrier localization and the associated lattice dynamics in real time. The transient conductive response quenches with a time constant of $\sim$109 fs and evolves into a localized exciton state, accompanied by pronounced renormalization of Ni-S optical phonons and the emergence of a transient 2.7 THz vibrational mode. Temperature-dependent measurements, together with mode-resolved dielectric dynamics and symmetry analysis, identify the 2.7 and 8.0 THz responses as spectroscopic indicators of exciton self-trapping and point to a cooperative two-phonon mechanism. These findings establish the excitation pathway as a key parameter controlling carrier localization and many-body relaxation in strongly coupled quantum materials.

Spin and Orbital Angular Momentum Textures at Chiral Molecule-Metal Interfaces

Gyanu P. Kafle, Amos Afugu, Zhen-Fei Liu

Chiral molecular adsorption can modify the spin and orbital character of metal surface states, providing an electronic-structure basis for interpreting spin-dependent interfacial phenomena. Using first-principles calculations with spin-orbit coupling, band unfolding, and an atom-projected orbital angular momentum (OAM) analysis, we investigate spin and OAM textures at chiral heptahelicene/Au(111) interfaces and compare them with clean Au (111) and an achiral coronene/Au system. Adsorption changes the orientations of the spin and OAM vectors and reduces the sampled OAM magnitudes of Au-derived surface states near the Fermi level. The two heptahelicene enantiomers exhibit textures related by mirror and time-reversal symmetries, whereas the comparison with coronene identifies changes that also occur without molecular chirality. These results clarify how molecular adsorption modifies interfacial spin and orbital character and establish symmetry constraints relevant to chirality-dependent photoemission.

Indentation of pre-tensioned cortex-like fibre networks: tension, local stretching and the signature of defect architecture

Shubham Agarwal, Olivier Noël, Martin H. Müser

Indentation is widely used to infer the mechanical state of biological materials like the actomyosin cortex. It reduces a single force-indentation curve to an apparent modulus. The cortex, however, is a pre-tensioned, heterogeneous fibre network. We show that this inference is ambiguous. The same amount of weakening can leave the indentation force unchanged or lower it tenfold, depending on where it lies relative to the probe. The force reflects how the probe couples to the tensioned, load-bearing structure, not the amount of weakening. We combine molecular dynamics simulations of a pressure-loaded, crosslinked fibre network indented by a rigid sphere with a continuum membrane solution. We represent the simulated force-depth response by a two-term fit, $F\propto aδ+bδ^{3/2}$, with $a$ reflecting the global tension, and $b$ the stiffening with depth. At a fixed fraction of weakened bonds, diffuse weakening lowers the force by a factor of about 1.5. A weak cluster beneath the probe lowers it 8-12-fold, because the slack cluster decouples the probe from the tension. When the clusters are displaced from the contact, the force returns to within 15% of the healthy value. Two opposite effects cancel. The clusters soften the whole layer, which lowers its tension and the force. But they also channel the load through the intact network beneath the probe, which stiffens it locally and raises the force. The shape of the curve retains what the force level hides. The coefficient $a$ reflects the softened layer when clusters beside the probe keep the force near the healthy value. The local exponent $α=d\ln F/d\lnδ$ increases with depth in every configuration except when a cluster lies beneath the probe, where the two-term form no longer applies. Detecting localised weakening requires spatial sampling and analysis of the curve shape, not a single fitted modulus.

Pair distribution function oscillations at electrostatically stabilized nanocrystal surfaces reflect nanocrystal lattice periodicity

Vivian R. K. Wall, Andrew Yang, Bryan A. Sanchez Monserrate, Ahhyun Jeong, Christian P. N. Tanner et al. (16 authors)

Nanocrystals (NCs) stabilized electrostatically by short, multivalent inorganic ligands exhibit rich interactions and phase behavior believed to hinge on the configuration of ions at the NC surface, yet these molecular details have not previously been measured. Here we use double-difference pair distribution functions (ddPDFs) of total X-ray scattering to determine the configuration of Sn$_2$S$_6^{4-}$ ligands on PbS NCs suspended in multivalent electrolyte. The measured ddPDFs exhibit a $\sim$3-$\mathring{A}$ wavelength oscillation that extends $\sim$10 $\mathring{A}$. Comparison with a PbS NC model decorated with Sn$_2$S$_6^{4-}$ ligands indicates ligands are aligned $\sim$radially, suggesting bidentate binding. Calculated partial PDFs show that the oscillatory signal is dominated by nanocrystal-Pb to ligand-Sn correlations. A simplified model that replaces the ligand with a strongly scattering Pt atom near the NC surface confirms the oscillation period in the ddPDF originates from the NC lattice constant. Nanocrystal lattice periodicity therefore merits consideration when interpreting ddPDFs of colloidal NCs.
