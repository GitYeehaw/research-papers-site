ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters

Ruixi Yu, Dairu Liu, Xuchuan Chen, Zekun Qi, Zekai Li et al. (12 authors)

Frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in robot control, suggesting that broad pretraining provides knowledge valuable for physical action. Yet most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies still rely on much smaller VLM backbones, leaving this potential largely untapped. We ask whether frontier-scale representations can provide a stronger foundation for robot learning. We introduce ScaleVLA, which connects a 552B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash backbone to a trainable action DiT, and systematically compare VLMs from 2B to 552B under a shared training setup. We evaluate three fundamental capabilities of robot foundation models. In sample efficiency, just 10 demonstrations outperform a 2B backbone trained with 50 (87.8% vs. 85.6%). In generalization, LIBERO-PRO success reaches 45.1%, compared with at most 31.3% for smaller backbones. In precise manipulation, real-world charger insertion improves from 4/10 to 9/10 successes, with gains concentrated in contact-sensitive alignment. Despite its scale, ScaleVLA achieves 216 ms median latency per action chunk. Our findings suggest that frontier VLMs can serve not only as reasoning agents, but also as powerful representational foundations for efficient and generalizable robot policies.

Hyperlyve: Parallelized Enumeration and Exact Lyapunov Stability Certification for Deep ReLU Networks

Brian Yarbrough, Jesse Wayment, Jingbo Wang, Philip E. Paré, Shreyas Sundaram

We introduce Hyperlyve (Hyperplane Partitioned Lyapunov Verification and Enumeration), a framework for proving the asymptotic stability of nonlinear dynamical systems, including those with neural network controllers. Hyperlyve learns and formally certifies continuous piecewise-affine (CPA) neural Lyapunov functions within a counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) loop. Hyperlyve's key innovation is a parallel algorithm that enumerates the hyperplane arrangement formed by the ReLU activations of deep networks and certifies that Lyapunov conditions hold within each polyhedral region. We derive sound and complete regional tests to verify the asymptotic stability of the exact smooth dynamics (rather than a piecewise-linear approximation) within a compact domain of interest. Furthermore, we formally characterize the computational complexity of the CPA neural Lyapunov verification problem as co-NP-complete, filling a key gap in the literature. Empirical results demonstrate that Hyperlyve enumerates ReLU networks over 150-times faster than competing tools, which contributes to Hyperlyve proving the asymptotic stability of systems that are currently intractable for state-of-the-art verifiers.

SADL: On the Demand-Supply Geometry of Representation Collapse

Junhan Sun, Yigui Feng, Mingzhe Liu, Haoyan Xu, Lei Ye et al. (10 authors)

In Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), a well-constrained projector output does not by itself prevent representation collapse. We find that, from initialization, the encoder representation is dominated by a component shared by different inputs. A batch-normalized projector removes this component, so regularization after the projector cannot correct it, and the differences between representations shrink until they collapse. We propose SADL (Supply And Demand Loss), which keeps the original training objective and constrains the encoder directly when needed. SADL compares the representation differences that the projector output demands with the differences the encoder can retain and pass on to the projector, and adds a constraint on the encoder only when supply falls short of demand. It works with different Demand Regularizers and introduces no auxiliary networks, additional trainable parameters, or architectural changes. In single-task and jointly trained four-task LeWorldModel (LeWM) and INTACT experiments, SADL lowers the collapse rate over ten random seeds from 50% to 0%. After one epoch of training, encoder effective rank is 3.4-6.4 times that of the corresponding baseline without SADL, and latent prediction error falls to 1/20-1/303 of it. Without additional tuning, SADL stays robust as its loss weight spans orders of magnitude, and with several Demand Regularizers INTACT exceeds 95% mean search-free control success. On four image datasets, SADL also consistently improves accuracy and stability across random seeds. These results show that matching the representation differences a regularizer demands with the differences the encoder can supply leads to faster and more stable representation learning in control and image classification.

Lazy-MOSAIC: Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World Models

Itamar Mishani, Mukund Krishnakumar, Oren Salzman, Maxim Likhachev

Long-horizon manipulation tasks require a robot to perform a sequence of physical interactions with its environment, typically combining several different skills. Planning such behaviors efficiently is challenging: given a library of parameterized skills, solutions lie in a vast hybrid space of skill sequences and their parameters, and evaluating each candidate skill's feasibility and effect with a high-fidelity world model such as a physics simulator is computationally expensive. MOSAIC, a recent skill-centric approach, lets the skills guide a non-sequential search toward the regions of this space where they are likely to succeed; however, it pays the full cost of simulation for every parameterized skill it considers. In this work, we introduce Lazy-MOSAIC, a lazy planning framework that instead searches over hypothesized skill outcomes. A cheap learned skill-effect world model predicts, in a fraction of a millisecond, a skill's feasibility and the world state it produces. Lazy-MOSAIC then uses these predictions to focus expensive simulation on promising candidate skills and, when a hypothesis is invalidated, repairs only the affected part of the search, while ensuring that every skill in a returned plan is validated in simulation. We evaluate Lazy-MOSAIC on cluttered-shelf retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty and demonstrate it on a physical robot, showing that it plans substantially faster than baselines with fewer simulator queries, while matching or exceeding their success rate in every setting. A Scalable Decentralized Event-Triggered Topology-Switching Protocol for Verifiable Safety in Multi-Agent Systems

Amber He Wei, Shiyu Cheng, Andrew Clark, Bruno Sinopoli

This paper develops a decentralized event-triggered topology-switching protocol that allows agents to disconnect from the communication network while preserving state safety and excluding Zeno behavior. We establish a robust barrier function condition that guarantees safety as long as the estimation error remains within a prescribed threshold. Each agent uses locally computable certificates to determine when to connect and disconnect from the network. The certificate construction improves scalability by reducing the complexity of topology-dependent computations from $O(2^N)$ to $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of agents. We formally prove that the protocol ensures the estimation error does not exceed the prescribed threshold and is non-Zeno. An alternating method computes an error-weighting matrix and a quadratic barrier function that guarantee safety under the proposed protocol. Finally, we evaluate the protocol on four interconnected microgrids. Compared with maintaining network connectivity at all times, the protocol reduces the time agents remain connected while keeping phase and frequency within their safe operating region.

GHySSeg: Real-Time Geometric Ground and Road Segmentation for Hybrid Solid-State LiDAR

Martín Palos, Fernando García

Hybrid solid-state LiDAR sensors are making their way into production vehicles, yet the data they produce differs structurally from that of the established spinning mechanical sensors behind today's autonomous-driving datasets: it is ring-free, dense, cluttered, and concentrated in a frontal field of view. The classical geometric methods that autonomous vehicles rely on for drivable space computation and navigation assume the ordered ring-and-column structure of a 360-degree spinning LiDAR and degrade or fail on this data, while learned alternatives are computationally heavy, hard to certify, and trained almost exclusively on spinning-LiDAR data. We demonstrate that the same dense, per-curve geometry is instead an opportunity to close the divide between the industry's preferred sensors and autonomous-driving research. We present GHySSeg, a purely geometric method that segments ground and drivable road from a single hybrid solid-state LiDAR frame by tracing each scan curve and reading two geometric signatures, a step in height and a fold-back in range, with no training and no graphics processing unit. To evaluate it, we release HySoLiD-Drive, the first public benchmark dedicated to ground and road segmentation on an automotive hybrid solid-state LiDAR, comprising 100 manually labeled and 7000 unlabeled frames recorded by two vehicles in real driving. On this benchmark, GHySSeg surpasses both geometric and learned state-of-the-art baselines across ground and road segmentation, reaching an intersection-over-union of 0.907 for road and 0.896 for ground at over 0.96 precision, while running in real time at about 22 milliseconds per frame on a single core.

Input-to-State Stability in Distributed Adaptive Identification of Nonlinear Systems without Input Sharing

Moh Kamalul Wafi

This paper studies continuous-time distributed adaptive identification of unknown nonlinear systems over directed communication networks without requiring source or neighboring inputs to be shared. Each sensing node reconstructs the source state while adapting local estimates of the unknown linear and nonlinear dynamics using only locally available information and neighboring state estimates. A network-level error representation is derived to characterize the interaction among the communication topology, observer dynamics, parameter uncertainty, and nonlinear mismatch. Norm-based, structured Lyapunov, and robust LMI conditions are established to guarantee that the resulting coupling operator is Hurwitz. Unlike the corresponding discrete-time formulation, the proposed continuous-time adaptive laws do not require normalization. A Lyapunov analysis establishes boundedness of the adaptive parameters and asymptotic convergence of the estimation and source-tracking errors in the unperturbed case. Input-to-state stability is further established under bounded predictor mismatch and source disturbances. Numerical studies over star, cyclic, and path topologies support the theoretical results and demonstrate approximately linear computational scaling with the number of sensing nodes.

RESILAGENT: A Three-Pillar Architecture for Cyber-Physical Resilience in Electrical Power and Energy Systems

Nikolaos Kekatos, Georgios Koutidis, Konstantinos Antonakopoulos, Stylianos Basagiannis, Sokratis Katsikas et al. (9 authors)

Electrical Power and Energy Systems face hybrid threats that combine cyber intrusions, physical sabotage and climate-driven stress, and routinely outpace today's siloed monitoring stacks. We present RESILAGENT, a concept architecture organised around three operational pillars, Sensing & Data Intelligence, Resilience Analytics & Reporting, and Incident Response & Recovery, mediated by a Cloud-based Orchestrator and exercised through a closed Detect-Simulate-Decide-Sign loop. The framework fuses agentic artificial intelligence and multimodal large language models for cross-modal detection, real-time digital twins for what-if simulation, and a permissioned blockchain anchor for tamper-evident threat intelligence and CER/NIS2-aligned evidence. We instantiate it on two scenarios, a transmission operator under coordinated cyber compromise, sabotage and heatwave, and a distribution operator under a supply-chain compromise during a storm, and report an IEEE-bus micro-benchmark that quantifies each pillar through false-data-injection attack detection, cyber-physical fusion lift, N-1 contingency screening and anchor cost.

Warm Starting Neural Dynamics Models From Few Jacobian Samples

Maxime Rochkoulets, Joppe De Jonghe, Mariya Ishteva

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) leverages a model of the environment, used for planning and generating samples without interacting with the real world. Motivated by evidence that informed initialization significantly improves the efficiency of MBRL, we propose TDec-Init, an algorithm to warm-start neural dynamics models using transition Jacobians. Using a decoupling algorithm based on the decomposition of a tensor of stacked Jacobian samples, we recover weights and flexible nonlinearities, and use them to initialize a feedforward neural network. In the low-data regime, we show on several benchmark environments that our approach outperforms random initialization and value training, and either matches or outperforms Sobolev training, while requiring fewer training steps between episodes.
