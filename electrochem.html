Confinement of hybridized light modes in nonlinear-optical superpotentials

Federico Presutti, Ryotatsu Yanagimoto

In this work, we present a spatial and temporal light trapping mechanism induced by coherent frequency conversion. We consider a wave-mixing process, where a pumped nonlinearity mediates a frequency-domain beamsplitter interaction between two signal modes. When these wave modes are counterpropagating, temporally trapped eigenmodes emerge at the zero of the nonlinearity profile. We establish that such spatio-temporal trapping has a direct connection to supersymmetric quantum mechanics, which ensures the existence and robustness of the trapped modes and allows us to derive analytic solutions of the bound eigenmodes. Backwards sum-frequency conversion may thus enable a unique class of active photonic memory devices, whose design parameters are compatible with the state of the art in integrated nonlinear photonics. We propose proof-of-principle experiments to study this nonlinear-optical trapping effect on thin-film lithium niobate. In addition, as a case study, we show how the temporal trap can be used alongside parametric gain. Our work unveils potential applications ranging from classical integrated photonics to multimode quantum nonlinear photonics.

Screening strongly correlated materials in solid oxide cells with quantum simulation

Ouns El Harzli, Jelle Bor, Linde Wester, Leonid Zhukov, Hanl Park et al. (12 authors)

We develop an end-to-end framework for assessing a quantum-computing application in industrial materials science: the screening of strongly correlated oxygen-electrode materials for SOFCs and SOECs. We formulate the materials problem in terms of interface observables including oxygen-vacancy formation energies, vacancy migration barriers, and electronic conductivity. We describe how these observables can be obtained using a diagrammatic GW-based embedding, either GW+DMFT or self-energy embedding theory (SEET), applied to a DFT-screened shortlist. The atomistic electrode/electrolyte interface defines the electronic structure problem from which the correlated impurity models are constructed. Quantum impurity solvers would provide the finite-temperature impurity Green's functions required by the embedding. The computational challenges that classical solvers may encounter as the interacting active space grows, together with the polynomial scaling of quantum Hamiltonian simulation for efficiently represented discretized models, open an opportunity for quantum advantage. We identify the benchmarks needed to assess this opportunity through convergence of vacancy and migration energetics and comparison with the best applicable classical solvers. We provide illustrative logical qubit counts and identify the thermal state preparation, simulation, measurement, and repeated-solve costs needed for a complete resource assessment. We connect microscopic observables to macroscopic engineering metrics using a continuum model for mixed ionic--electronic conducting oxygen electrodes that couples oxygen surface exchange, solid-state oxygen transport, electrode microstructure, and finite electrode thickness. Finally, we estimate the potential worldwide business value of improved electrode materials under SOFC and SOEC deployment scenarios informed by public technical targets and electrolyser deployment data.

Spin-Dependent Transport in One-Dimensional Mercury- and Silver-Mediated DNA Nanowires

Arpan De, William Livernois, Simon Vecchioni, Yoel P. Ohayon, M. P. Anantram

Metallo-DNA nanowires serve as a versatile, programmable scaffold for molecular-scale electronics. In this work, we demonstrate that periodic heavy-metal coordination fundamentally reshapes the electronic landscape of DNA, generating dispersive, metal-centered conduction bands rather than isolated localized states. Driven by enhanced inter-layer coupling, where second nearest-neighbor hopping plays a crucial role: the conduction bandwidth can approach 1 eV, more than double that of canonical DNA. These broad bands suggest enhanced robustness of transport against structural fluctuations. As a result of relativistic effects, in these quasi-one-dimensional materials, spin-orbit (SO) coupling enhances band dispersion and governs spin-dependent transport. We predict that although ligand hybridization reduces atomic SO splitting, the effective SO splitting in these nanowires is comparable to the next-nearest-neighbor hopping strength, which results in considerable spin mixing. We find that Hg2+-coordinated nanowire exhibit pronounced spin-flip conductance comparable to spin-conserving conductance across the LUMO manifold. This efficient spin conversion remains robust over extended molecular lengths and is accompanied by a set of highly delocalized unoccupied states with oscillating nodal structures, readily accessible under low-bias transport measurements. In contrast, the Ag+-coordinated nanowires exhibit weak spin-flip scattering that is more than an order of magnitude smaller than spin-preserving transport. This duality establishes the choice of coordination metal as a decisive parameter to govern metal-metal interaction, electron delocalization, and spin-dependent transport. Overall, our findings demonstrate how relativistic effects and quantum confinement intersect in these bio-templated, quasi-one-dimensional molecular conductors.

Nanopillar metasurfaces uniting high optical reflectivity with ultracoherent nanomechanics

Alexander Jung, Anastasiia Ciers, Hannes Pfeifer, Albert Hansson, Johanna Örgård et al. (9 authors)

Mechanically compliant mirrors are key components in cavity optomechanics. However, the requirements to achieve the best optical and mechanical performance are usually in direct competition. A high reflectivity for tightly focused beams entails thick reflectors, whereas ultracoherent flexural nanomechanics necessitates thin membranes. We eliminate this trade-off by introducing high-refractive-index nanopillar metasurfaces on top of a thin membrane. We pattern a 50 micrometer-diameter array of nanopillars to form a photonic crystal. The flat band behavior of guided resonances in the photonic crystal enables a reflectivity of 99.82(1)% for a tightly focused beam of 7 micrometer waist. The combination of two guided resonances creates a 100 nanometer-wide high-reflectivity band around 1550 nanometer. Simultaneously, the nanopillar patterning realizes a density-modulated phononic crystal that isolates an ultracoherent localized mechanical mode with a $Q\cdot f$-product of $2\cdot 10^{14}$Hz. The demonstrated nanopillar metasurfaces enable lightweight mirrors for room-temperature quantum optomechanics and for use as levitated mirrors or lightsails.

Unifying Time-Independent and Time-Dependent Franck--Condon Pictures in Quantum Mechanochemistry via the Linear Coupling Approximation

Vladimir V. Rybkin

Quantum chemistry has achieved impressive results in modeling and interpreting mechanochemical transformations at the molecular level. An essential question, the time profile of force application, has however attracted less attention. Here, the Franck--Condon theory of quantum mechanochemistry, previously formulated for the sudden-force limit, is generalized to arbitrary time-dependent force profiles using the linear-coupling approximation. Solving the driven harmonic oscillator yields a closed-form, time-dependent effective Huang--Rhys factor: a natural measure of vibrational excitations upon force application. It is governed by the Fourier transform of the force derivative and recovers the sudden- and fixed-force limits. At the conceptual level, this linear coupling picture links mechanochemistry to vibronic spectroscopy and Marcus electron theory. For a shock-front force ramp, vibrational excitation is shown to be suppressed in frequency, only low-frequency modes being excited under realistic laser-shock-peening conditions.

Analytical Modeling of Modulated Heating for Three-Dimensional Anisotropic Optical Thermometry of Multilayered Cylinders

William Spirnock, Dihui Wang, Heng Ban

Thermally relevant cylindrical geometries are used in a plethora of applications with thermal property differences depending significantly on material complexity. Manu- facturing processes also induce substantial anisotropy ratios resulting in directionally dependent thermal transport. However, for cylindrical specimens, current thermal metrology resolves only isotropic thermal properties, assumes perfect thermal inter- faces, and offers no formulation for temperature spatial dependence. In this work, we present an analytical framework that models the frequency dependent thermal transport of orthotropic arbitrary layered cylinders to be used as a forward model for existing optical thermometry techniques. This formulation captures the effects of non-negligible thermal boundary conductance and offers the first closed-form solution of a projected Gaussian laser, a common form of heating in thermal metrology, hav- ing a finite elliptic spot size on a curved surface. The novel anisotropic cylindrical model was compared with literature derivations involving two-dimensional isotropic transport for cylinders by applying simplifying assumptions. For single- and two- layerisotropicsamples, thethree-dimensionalmodelagreedwithestablishedsolutions with negligible error, while predictions for orthotropic multilayer samples agreed with finite-element simulations within 1.5% across a range of material properties and laser geometries. Comparison with anisotropic planar models identified conditions under which a planar approximation can reduce computation time. These results estab- lish the first forward model for noncontact characterization of anisotropic thermal properties in arbitrarily layered cylinders.

Multireference Polarizable Embedding for Core-Level Spectroscopy in Aqueous Solution: XPS and XAS of Glycine

Chiara Sepali, Pietro Dal Cin, Alicia Marie Kirk, Sonia Coriani, Chiara Cappelli

Core-level spectra in solution are governed by the interplay of core-hole relaxation, electron correlation, specific solute--solvent interactions, and environmental polarization. We report the first application of RASSCF--RASPT2 combined with the fluctuating-charges polarizable embedding to X-ray photoelectron (XPS) and X-ray absorption (XAS) spectroscopy in aqueous solution. The protocol couples a multireference treatment of core-ionized and core-excited states with molecular-dynamics sampling, and is assessed for the carbon and nitrogen K-edges of glycine in the gas phase and in water. The calculated spectra are compared with experiment and with fixed-charges electrostatic embedding. Gas-phase ionization and absorption features are accurately reproduced. In solution, configurational averaging captures inhomogeneous broadening, while XPS peak positions, relative intensities, and widths depend strongly on the solvent parametrization. The best-performing polarizable model improves the spectral profile, although aqueous ionization energies remain overestimated. For XAS, the carbon K-edge is reproduced close to the experimental energy scale, whereas the nitrogen K-edge line shape is recovered after rigid alignment. These results support multireference polarizable embedding as a promising framework for core-level spectroscopy in solution.

Orbital-coherence memory in electrical transport and its self-consistent Maxwell response

Pengfei Zhu

An internal electronic coherence changes electrical transport only when it feeds back on the physical current. We develop a reduced description of this feedback and distinguish its exact consequences from the assumptions required for a microscopic realization. An opposite-parity two-orbital Hamiltonian identifies a current-coupled orbital force. Under an explicitly stated moment closure and collision ansatz, it yields a passive three-state response with an exponentially damped oscillatory memory. The resulting conductivity redistributes a fixed optical weight and predicts a nonmonotonic dependence of dc conduction on coherence damping at fixed remaining parameters. The orbital reduction constrains the coupling and internal frequency through a shared bare splitting, providing a test beyond unconstrained spectral fitting. A charge-conserving spatial extension couples to Maxwell equations through a positive energy balance and separates the constitutive response from longitudinal collective and transverse electromagnetic modes. We formulate complex-conductivity and transmission tests, including the limits of parameter identification over a finite bandwidth. The model supplies a conditional connection between orbital dynamics, transport memory, and electromagnetic observables; conductivity alone does not establish a uniquely quantum mechanism, and a material-specific collision calculation and closure-error assessment remain necessary.

Phase-Matched Chirps for Energy-Efficient Excitation of Magneto-Mechanical Resonators

Sarah Reiss, Fabian Mohn, Jonas Faltinath, Tobias Knopp

Magneto-mechanical resonators (MMRs) are a recently proposed class of passive, wireless devices used for sensing and tracking. The operating principle of MMRs relies on an excitation of the sensor by oscillating magnetic fields, followed by an inductive readout to measure the sensor's dynamics. One challenge lies in generating efficient excitation signals to ensure maximum energy harvesting by the sensor. In this simulation study, we focus on chirped magnetic-field excitations and compare their performance with conventional constant-frequency excitation for both sinusoidal and rectangular waveforms. The results indicate varying levels of efficiency, with a sinusoidal linear down-chirp excitation signal yielding the largest deflection angle, offering the best potential for reducing the energy consumption and improving the temporal resolution of the measurement.

Precise Determination of Electric Dipole Polarizabilities of Sr Clock States Using Linear-Response and Equation-of-Motion Relativistic Coupled-Cluster Methods

Somesh Chamoli, Sudipta Chakraborty, B. K. Sahoo, Ján Šimunek, Jozef Noga et al. (6 authors)

We present accurate values of the electric dipole polarizabilities ($α$) of the ground and excited states of Sr using four-component relativistic coupled-cluster (RCC) theory within the linear-response (LR) and equation-of-motion (EOM) frameworks. We first investigated accuracy of the ground-state polarizability using the LR-RCC method in both spherical and Cartesian coordinate representations. Upon obtaining consistent results for the ground state, we determine the polarizabilities of the excited states using the EOM-RCC method within the finite-field approach. The uncertainties in our results are assessed by examining the completeness of the basis set, higher-order relativistic corrections, and contributions from higher-level excitation configurations beyond the truncation in our RCC method. We also discuss various procedures for extrapolating contributions to the complete basis set limit and examine their respective limitations. Our calculated ground-state polarizability is found to be inconsistent with the latest atomic calculation, but agrees with other calculations and experimental value. Using the differential $α$ value from the EOM-RCC method, we estimate the fractional black-body-radiation-induced frequency shift of the Sr $^{1}$S$_{0}$$\rightarrow$$^{3}$P$_{0}$ clock transition to be $-5.40\times10^{-15}$.
