Hyperkagome antiferromagnetism in the iron garnet Almandine Fe$_3$Al$_2$Si$_3$O$_{12}$

Minnong Zhang, Harald O. Jeschke, Daniel Guterding

Antiferromagnets on the hyperkagome lattice provide a three-dimensional route to strong magnetic frustration, but experimentally accessible realizations with well-established microscopic Hamiltonians remain scarce. Garnets are a broad family of materials in which magnetic ions can form two interpenetrating hyperkagome lattices, giving rise to spin glass, spin-liquid and hidden-order physics. Here we identify the iron aluminosilicate garnet almandine, Fe$_3$Al$_2$Si$_3$O$_{12}$, as a spin-$2$ hyperkagome antiferromagnet. Using density functional theory energy mapping, we determine a Heisenberg Hamiltonian for the high-spin Fe$^{2+}$ moments and find dominant nearest-neighbor antiferromagnetic exchange, as well as significant longer-range interactions. Classical Monte Carlo simulations of this Hamiltonian, combined with quantum-to-classical rescaling of the spin length, show a transition to antiferromagnetic order at $T_{\rm N}\approx9.8$K, in excellent agreement with specific heat experiments. The resulting non-collinear magnetic state is naturally described by magnetic toroidal octupolar order, which we connect to known results for the highly frustrated hyperkagome antiferromagnet gadolinium gallium garnet, Gd$_3$Ga$_5$O$_{12}$. We also calculate magnetic neutron diffraction amplitudes as well as static and dynamic structure factors using semiclassical dynamics, providing quantitative predictions for future neutron scattering experiments. Our results establish almandine as a chemically accessible realization of frustrated hyperkagome magnetism in the garnet family.

Surrogate-Assisted Compact-Model Extraction for SKY130 CMOS at 77 K

Aaron Wang, Olivia Gzamouranis, Vaibhav Lohia, Hershel Fei Xun Weiner, Olivia Seidel et al. (9 authors)

Cryogenic circuit design requires compact transistor models that reproduce measured low-temperature behavior and run in standard circuit simulators. We fit BSIM4 parameters to measured I-V curves of SkyWater 130 nm (SKY130) transistors at 77 K using a neural surrogate of NGSpice. The surrogate maps compact-model parameters and device geometry to the complete set of output and transfer characteristics. Extraction is a bounded least-squares search: a bank of NGSpice simulations supplies initial candidates, finite differences of the surrogate supply the local Jacobian, and NGSpice re-simulates the resulting cards before selection. On 18 measured geometries, we compare a search over seven card parameters with a search over 43 parameters selected by NGSpice sensitivity. Relative to NGSpice re-simulations of the previously released cards, the two searches reduce the in-sample mean relative root-mean-square error (RRMS) by 17% and 26%, respectively. The 43-parameter search has the lower overall error, but the seven-parameter search has the lower mean on high-bias curves, and some individual curves have higher error than with the released cards under both searches.

Variational Monte Carlo for Quantum Excited States via Nested Low-Rank Approximation

Minchan Jeong, J. Jon Ryu, Se-Young Yun, Gregory W. Wornell

The electronic Schrödinger equation encodes a molecule's electronic structure and quantum properties, but solving it scales exponentially with the number of electrons. Solving this equation with neural-network variational Monte Carlo (VMC) has reached chemical accuracy on the ground state of small molecules; extending this success to excited states is constrained by two existing paradigms. Penalty-based methods need $K$ Laplacians per step but require tuning the overlap-penalty weights against the energy gaps they aim to compute. Natural excited states VMC (NES-VMC) is penalty-free, but it combines the $K$ ansatzes into one determinant, whose local energy requires $K^2$ Laplacians per step. We resolve this trade-off via low-rank approximation (LoRA) reparameterization of the rank-$K$ variational objective. The reparameterized objective admits a VMC-compatible stochastic gradient estimator via MCMC sampling from the per-state Born densities, and a sequential nesting scheme recovers the lowest $K$ eigenstates in eigenvalue order. The resulting algorithm, NestedLoRA-VMC, inherits a global optimality guarantee from the LoRA principle. On the first-row atoms Li through Ne at $K=10$, its accuracy is comparable to that of NES-VMC and of a state-of-the-art penalty-based method.

Variational and geometric time-dependent density-functional theory of electronic systems

Éric Cancès, Théo Duez, Jari van Gog, Asbjørn Bækgaard Lauritsen, Mathieu Lewin et al. (7 authors)

A central problem in time-dependent density-functional theory (TDDFT) is to construct a time-dependent many-electron wave function that reproduces a prescribed time-dependent electron density. This problem admits a natural geometric interpretation, which in turn leads to at least two distinct formulations of TDDFT. The first is the usual variational formulation of TDDFT, in which the density constraint is enforced with a real-valued local potential. The second is geometric TDDFT, introduced in recent works, in which the same density constraint is enforced with an imaginary local potential, or equivalently with a Hermitian nonlocal potential. In this work, we develop this unified viewpoint for continuous electronic systems and systematically compare the two formulations. We formulate the corresponding time-dependent Kohn-Sham schemes and derive a number of exact conditions for geometric TDDFT, including conditions associated with causality, time-reversal symmetry, uniform-coordinate scaling, translational invariance, energy and momentum equations of motion, one-electron self-interaction, and the adiabatic limit. The paper also contains a mathematical section where we provide a fully rigorous justification of some parts of the text. We particularly discuss the mathematical properties of two operators underlying the two formulations of TDDFT and establish sufficient conditions for their invertibility. We explain the functional-analytic subtleties underlying the geometric interpretation of TDDFT and the two formulations of TDDFT. This unified perspective clarifies the mathematical and conceptual relation between variational and geometric TDDFT and provides a foundation for the development of approximations within geometric TDDFT.

Shared-State Inverse Synthesis of Dual-Mode Conformal Metasurfaces for Low Scattering and Telemetry

Mohamed R. Khodja, Ghala A. Alomar

A conformal surface that alternates between sectoral low scattering and directive telemetry poses a shared-hardware inverse problem when both modes must use one finite passive impedance alphabet, although their spatial codebooks may differ. This paper formulates and solves that mixed continuous--discrete problem at the effective impedance-boundary level. Reference-centered Tikhonov preimages prescribe restricted radiation data; inverse-source null-space freedom then moves each current within its data-equivalent class toward impedance profiles quantizable by the same alphabet. A line-searched Gauss--Newton update, common-state assignment and projected-centroid updates, and exact forward refinement determine the currents, two codebooks, and one passive library. Data invariance, the current-to-impedance differential, the optimal state update, monotonic mismatch reduction, and a forward perturbation bound are derived. A full-mutual-coupling two-dimensional TM study of a $4λ_0$-diameter cylinder uses 64 physical cells and one shared eight-state library. On a 256-unknown validation model, the worst power ratio over $\pm10^{\circ}$ monostatic sectors and incidence angles from $-30^{\circ}$ to $30^{\circ}$ is $-11.6$ dB relative to a PEC cylinder; telemetry directivity is 13.1 dB with $-13.6$-dB peak sidelobes and a $1^{\circ}$ pointing error. A separate Fourier--Galerkin formulation, converged through 513 complex modes without reoptimization, gives $-11.36$ dB and 13.02 dB for the corresponding worst-sector and directivity metrics. The results establish feasibility at the effective impedance-boundary level; they do not establish unit-cell, bias-network, or platform-level realizability.

Atomistic Insights into the All-Optical Switching of GdCo Alloys with Minimal Gd Concentration

Paul-Iulian Gavriloaea, Jackson L. Ross, Richard F. L. Evans, Roy Chantrell, Stéphane Mangin et al. (6 authors)

Single-pulse all-optical helicity-independent switching (AO-HIS) is most commonly observed in Gd-based ferrimagnets near magnetization compensation. Recent experiments, however, demonstrated switching in Pt/Co/Pt-based heterostructures containing only a small amount of Gd at a Co/Pt interface. Here, we use atomistic spin-dynamics simulations to test an effective interfacial GdCo-alloy picture and to determine how species-dependent spin-bath coupling controls the minimum Gd concentration required for reversal. We compare a three-monolayer Co film with Gd confined to the surface layer and a three-dimensional uniformly alloyed GdCo model. Within the present uniform-heating model, robust transient toggle-like reversal is obtained far from compensation when the Gd coupling to the thermal bath is substantially weaker than that of Co; for λCo = 0.02 and λCo = 0.001, reversal occurs down to 1.5 at. % total Gd for selected pulse parameters. Increasing the Gd content accelerates reversal, consistent with the interpretation of Gd as an angular-momentum reservoir, whereas dilute interfacial Gd produces slower and more stochastic dynamics. These results show that compensation is not a fundamental requirement for ultrafast AO-HIS and identify the effective Gd spin-bath coupling as a key control parameter. Quantitative comparison with experiment will require explicit optical absorption, stack-dependent heat flow, dipolar fields, and a stable-final-state switching criterion.

Self-attention is a Dyson equation for many-body machine learning

Jarvist Moore Frost

Why does symmetric sum-pooling (DeepSets) predict ground-state potentials to sub-meV accuracy, yet fail for correlated wavefunctions and excited states? Sum-pooling is a mean-field (Hartree) model: its $O(1/N)$ off-diagonal Jacobian suffices for nearsighted ground-state energies, but erases nonlocal two-body correlations. We show that self-attention is a real-space Dyson iteration whose Boltzmann (softmax) kernel acts as a two-body self-energy. Because physical Hamiltonians act pairwise, Löwdin's 2-RDM theorem and the Galitskii--Migdal relation guarantee that iterating a two-body propagator captures the full energetics of correlated matter. When fitting a fractal backflow determinant, DeepSets blurs the nodal surface onto a mean-field envelope, whereas self-attention recovers the fractal attractor. Seen through this lens, the unreasonable effectiveness of attention is not an accident; in a universe built from pairwise interactions, two-body attention is all you need.

Multilevel AlScN Ferroelectric Diode Crossbar Arrays with Sub-200 nm Cells for Selector-Free Nonvolatile Memory

Kwan-Ho Kim, Zekun Hu, Krishna Karthikeya Chemudupati, Taarana Jammula, Yinuo Zhang et al. (11 authors)

Passive crossbar arrays are a leading route to high-density nonvolatile memory and in-memory computing, but without a selector the memory element itself must supply the nonlinearity that suppresses sneak-path currents. Here we scale aluminum scandium nitride (AlScN) ferroelectric diodes (FeDs) in both dimensions that matter - the ferroelectric is thinned to 10 nm and the junction diameter is reduced to 100 nm. We further integrate 200 nm cells into a 2-kilobyte (128 * 128) passive crossbar. At a junction area of 0.0314 um2 this is a ~625 reduction in cell area, at equal array capacity, relative to kilobyte-scale AlScN FeD crossbars built on 5 μm cells. The 10 nm Al0.64Sc0.36N layer reverses its polarization under pulses of 50 ns full width at half maximum at a pulse-energy density of ~16 pJ μm-2, corresponding to ~0.5 pJ per 200 nm cell. Pulsed programming at 17.5 V and 1 kHz yields an ON/OFF ratio of 4.2 * 103 in a 200 nm cell, while DC characterization of 283 randomly addressed cells of the 2-kB array gives ON/OFF and rectification ratios at 5 V of 43 33 and 61 74, from which we derive the sub-array size that selector-free reading currently supports. Eight conductance levels (three bits) written into a 200 nm array cell remain resolvable for 103 s, and the two extreme states are stable beyond 5 * 104 s. Mapping the measured, nonuniformly spaced levels onto a ResNet-20 network recovers 91.7 +- 0.1 % CIFAR-10 accuracy after quantization-aware training, against a 92.2 +- 0.1 % full-precision baseline. AlScN FeDs thus combine nanosecond switching, three-bit storage and sub-0.05 μm2 cells in a BEOL-compatible, selector-free crossbar.

Accessing Electrode Edges: Upright Mounting for Site-Specific Cryogenic Atom Probe Tomography of Lithium-Ion Batteries

Syeda Ramin Jannat, James O. Douglas, Neil Mulcahy, Lukas Worch, Aigerim Omirkhan et al. (12 authors)

Cryogenic atom probe tomography (cryo-APT) is increasingly being used to map the three-dimensional distribution of highly reactive species in heterogeneous materials, including those found in battery systems. However, site-specific preparation of localised features and liquid-solid interfaces remains challenging, particularly when regions of interest (ROIs) are concentrated along electrode edges that are difficult to access using conventional mounting geometries. Here, we present a cryo-APT workflow incorporating an upright-mounting procedure that enables targeted specimen preparation of localised regions using plasma focused ion beam (PFIB) microscopy and milling under cryogenic conditions. We first demonstrate the approach using an electrolyte-wetted graphite electrode, successfully preparing and analysing a specimen containing both an edge-localised electrolyte deposit and the underlying graphite and Cu current collector. We then apply the workflow to a cycled graphite electrode, targeting Li dendrites concentrated around the electrode perimeter and successfully preparing and analysing the targeted Li-rich region. Beyond battery systems, this approach offers potential for site-specific cryo-APT of edge-localised features across a broader range of material systems, where conventional specimen preparation geometries may limit access to ROIs.

A Free Energy Approach to Modelling Disordered Hyperuniform Patterns

Alex Mamaev, Enno Igel, Axel Voigt, Marco Salvalaglio

We present a free-energy-based approach for modelling disordered hyperuniform patterns. Specifically, we devise a phase-field crystal model with a correlation kernel that simultaneously selects for a continuous range of degenerate wave modes. At small wave vectors, we construct the correlation function by inverting the Ornstein-Zernike relation to approximate the structure factor of a hyperuniform pattern. Our model demonstrates a crossover between liquid, crystalline, quasicrystalline, and long-lived disordered states depending on the specified free energy parameter and driving force. We show that, for a fixed driving force, the specific structures and wave vectors selected by the density field can be controlled by modulating the width of the correlation kernel. Furthermore, we show that the scaling of the low-wave-vector region of the correlation function can accurately reproduce a specified hyperuniform exponent, independently of the structural properties at larger wave numbers. Our results establish a free-energy-based framework for describing disordered hyperuniform states, demonstrating and enabling independent control over structural selection and long-wavelength density correlations.
