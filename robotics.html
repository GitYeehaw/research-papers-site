DexLink: Synthesizing Dexterous Behaviors from Chained Hand-object Poses

Meenal Parakh, Deep Patel, Beining Han, Jia Deng

Synthesizing diverse dexterous manipulation policies is challenging: existing simulation-based approaches rely on human demonstrations which are difficult to scale, or use generic reward functions, which provide limited control over the hand-object interactions. We propose DexLink, which addresses these limitations by synthesizing diverse dexterous behaviors from sparse, reusable hand-object interaction templates. Given key task waypoints, DexLink retargets static interaction templates to target objects using an optimization that preserves spatial hand-object relationships. It then links the retargeted poses into an approximate reference trajectory and uses residual reinforcement learning to refine this reference into a closed-loop policy in simulation. Experiments spanning single-hand, bimanual, articulated, and dynamic tasks demonstrate that DexLink provides fine-grained control over task timing, grasp types, and high-level execution. The modular interaction templates transfer reliably across object scales, instances, and categories; without object-specific human demonstrations, the framework also matches the performance of dense reference-tracking methods. Finally, zero-shot deployment of policies trained purely in simulation shows promise for the simulation-based framework.

MERLoc: Robust Robot Localization in Dynamic Environments with Pose-Based Retrieval over 3D Gaussian Splatting References

Michael Baumgartner, Dario Mylonopoulos, Sammy Christen, Markus Gross, Moritz Bacher

Robots operating in previously mapped spaces must localize accurately despite moving people and rearranged objects. Most localization pipelines match the query against a sparse set of captured reference images retrieved by appearance, which is brittle under scene change and repeating structures. We present MERLoc, which turns a single metric 3D Gaussian Splatting scan into a dense Functional Reference Memory. Reference views are sampled over the navigable space for the robot's morphology and sensors at a target density and rendered offline. Pre-encoding them for the chosen off-the-shelf localizer removes the reference encoder from inference. A pose-based retrieval strategy then selects references by spatial proximity and viewing direction rather than appearance, sidestepping visual ambiguities whenever a prior exists. Across one classical and four learned localizers in simulated and real dynamic scenes, pose-based retrieval matches or outperforms appearance retrieval in most cases, even when the latter is gated by the same prior. On real data, it lowers position RMSE by 15-60 % in nine of ten backend/split combinations, and the rendered memory outperforms real-image caches posed by Structure-from-Motion. The system runs onboard a Jetson Orin NX at 5 Hz, enabling a navigation policy to successfully traverse a hallway with people roaming and rearranging objects.

One Ocean, Many Tasks: A Data-Grounded Simulation Suite and Benchmark for Marine Robotics

Shuaijun Liu, Qifu Wen, Xingwei Chen, Xinwen Hu, Feiyang You et al. (7 authors)

Simulation is essential for marine robotics, yet existing platforms often trade off between visually realistic worlds, high-throughput experimentation, and ocean-condition realism grounded in real data. We present OneOcean, a data-grounded simulation suite and benchmark built around a unified spatiotemporal ocean-environment product that harmonizes bathymetry with data-driven currents and tides, with optional pollution fields for cleanup-related evaluation. On top of this environment representation, OneOcean defines a task ladder spanning navigation and station-keeping under currents, waypoint and route following, depth-profile tracking, area scanning, pipeline inspection, and multi-agent coordination, including formation transit, fish protection and patrol (herding), and surface pollution localization, containment, and cleanup. The suite supports multi-agent settings with 2-10 vehicles. Our simulator supports three to six Degrees of Freedom (DoF) and produces standardized metrics and reproducible run manifests, enabling consistent aggregation across tasks, difficulties, and scaling settings. Experiments across dataset variants and scenes systematically stress planning and control under data-grounded disturbances and reveal trade-offs among heuristic baselines, Behavior Cloning (BC), and a Large Language Model (LLM)-based planner.

Memory Is Not Ground Truth: Revising Navigation Authority under Unreliable VLM Evidence for Persistent Semantic Navigation

Sumukh Shrote, Shenbagaraj Kannapiran, Eric Eaton, Camillo Jose Taylor

Vision-language models (VLMs) enable robots to reason about semantic and spatio-temporal context, verify task-relevant objects, and guide sensing and navigation. Persistent memory can reduce redundant search for field robots, but when should a memory continue to guide behavior? Repeated misses may suggest that a memory is stale. Our experiments show why this evidence can remain ambiguous under VLM-based perception. Using a Boston Dynamics Spot with four fixed cameras and a persistent spatio-temporal visual memory, we measure negative-verification behavior across targets, viewpoints, language queries, and repeated revisits. False-negative rates vary substantially across objects, nearby revisits often reproduce the same detector outcome, and some matched target-present and target-removed experiments yield identical miss histories. This ambiguity persists under changed observation geometry, identity-preserving query reformulation, and conditioning on a previously verified target image. These findings raise two questions: is the object still there, and is this memory still useful for navigation? We introduce a dependence-aware, reversible memory-update mechanism that avoids double-counting correlated misses and stops using unsupported memories as direct navigation goals without claiming that the object is absent. Later positive evidence can restore a memory's use for navigation. One resident onboard VLM is shared across memory verification, semantic frontier reasoning, and in-motion perception. In three matched real-robot decision contexts, an episode-local baseline selected a stale memory as its navigation objective in every case, whereas our method selected semantic frontiers in all three. A complementary trial re-verified the target during motion and promoted fresh memory. These results support revising memory actionability rather than interpreting failed detection as object absence.

Cairn: Object-Centric 3D Trajectory Priors for Steering Frozen VLAs

Agelos Kratimenos, Kostas Daniilidis, Evangelos Chatzipantazis

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) are capable low-level controllers, but they tie where objects go to the layouts they were trained on: when objects are moved or obstacles block the way, they carry them to the wrong place or into collisions. Fine-tuning on new layouts needs robot data for each one and erodes skills the model already has. We introduce Cairn, which keeps the VLA frozen and steers it at test time along learned 3D trajectories of the object in its gripper. A small prior reads point clouds of the scene and the held object, along with its pose when grasped and the instruction, and samples SE(3) trajectories up to its release that lead it around obstacles and turn it to fit through gaps. The prior and the VLA form the two levels of a hierarchical planner in which feedback runs both ways: the VLA's intended motion chooses and reshapes the trajectories, and the chosen one guides the VLA's denoising. Its predicted actions, mapped to the held object's poses through the grasp, are pulled toward the next waypoint, while the VLA's weights stay untouched. Across 1,941 held-out LIBERO scenes with displaced objects and obstacles, Cairn raises the success rate of a frozen π0.5 from 24.5% to 95.1%, above that of a π0.5 fine-tuned on our training layouts, without hurting standard LIBERO. On the third-party LIBERO-PRO benchmark, it raises success from 27.3% to 38.0%, and from 54.4% to 81.1% on the episodes it steers. On a real Franka, with a prior trained purely in simulation, it guides the frozen DROID π0.5 around an obstacle and to a placement at the limit of the arm's reach, where it otherwise fails. All code, models and data will be made public.

FineDex: Bridging Fine-Grained Human-Object Interaction Generation to Dexterous Humanoid Manipulation

Yujia Zeng, Yuxin Chen, Yuzhen Song, Runhan Huang, Chensheng Peng et al. (9 authors)

Dexterity is, at its core, finger-level control, yet existing dexterous manipulation and human-object interaction (HOI) generation methods rarely let one specify how an interaction is performed: five-fingered hands are often reduced to grippers, or finger usage is copied from individual human demonstrations. We present FineDex, a framework that makes finger-level intent both expressible and executable. FineDex learns coordinated human body, hand, and object motions that follow fine-grained instructions on hand selection, individual fingers, and contacts with articulated objects. To bridge generation and execution, FineDex leverages generated interactions as actionable priors for an agent that discovers physically feasible contact strategies through practice in a physics-based digital twin. By adapting at test time without per-instance retraining, FineDex generalizes across fine-grained instructions and object configurations. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art HOI generation quality and fine-grained controllability, substantial improvements in dexterous manipulation success, and successful deployment on a real dexterous humanoid robot.

Adaptive Control Barrier Functions for Nonlinear Singularly Perturbed Systems

Prashanth Krishnamurthy, Farshad Khorrami

Control barrier functions (CBFs) have emerged as a flexible and efficient approach for enforcing dynamic safety constraints. In this paper, we consider a class of uncertain nonlinear systems with singular perturbations and develop an adaptive CBF framework for such systems to enforce safety constraints on both the slow and fast subsystems in the overall singularly perturbed nonlinear system with uncertain parameters. We first develop a robustness property showing that if an adaptive controller for the slow subsystem satisfies the slow CBF condition with an appropriate safety margin, then for sufficiently small values of the singular perturbation parameter $\varepsilon$, the slow subsystem remains within an $O(\varepsilon)$-neighborhood of its safe set. We then consider the more general case of safety constraints on both the fast and slow subsystems and provide conditions under which joint slow-fast safety is achieved despite the parametric uncertainties. The reduced-order slow model and the slow and fast CBF conditions are evaluated using an online parameter estimate and the parameter mismatch between the estimated and true reduced-order models is addressed via a state-dependent robustness margin in the CBF inequalities. To illustrate the proposed framework, we present simulation studies for two systems with parametric uncertainties: a synthetic two-time-scale fourth-order example system and a unicycle with a fast turn-rate actuator and an obstacle avoidance safety constraint.

RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies

Insup Lee, Hojin Jeon, Sunguk Shin, Seoungbin Bae, Taekwan Kim et al. (7 authors)

Since vision-language-action (VLA) policies can inherit the slow pace of human demonstrations, accelerating these demonstrations before fine-tuning can lead to faster VLA policies without an additional speed-selection module at deployment. Several offline speedup methods have focused on identifying segments that require less precision and assigning predefined speedup factors to the segments. However, even within a single segment, a speedup factor suitable for steady motion may be too high when precise alignment is required. To this end, this paper presents RePace, an offline reasoning framework that determines how much to accelerate each part of a demonstration. For the same recorded movement, RePace measures how much each candidate speedup factor shortens the sequence and compares the resulting observation-action pairs used for training. Given the task instruction, a pretrained language model uses the visual context and original motion of successive windows to interpret these candidate retiming effects and select a speedup profile for the complete episode. Experimental results demonstrate that RePace outperforms the baselines in overall task execution efficiency on both LIBERO and RoboCasa-Kitchen. On all three tasks with a gripper-equipped Franka Research 3 arm, RePace matches or exceeds the acceleration baselines in success rate and completes successful trials faster.

BOP-Refer: Benchmark for Monocular 3D and 2D Object Grounding

Tomas Hodan, Vineet Bhat, Martin Sundermeyer, Jonathan Tremblay, Yash Patel et al. (15 authors)

We introduce BOP-Refer, the first benchmark for monocular 3D and 2D grounding of everyday and industrial objects sourced from 9 diverse BOP datasets. We pair images from these datasets with VLM-generated and human-verified referring expressions, and derive accurate ground-truth 3D and 2D bounding boxes of the referred objects from their 3D models and 6DoF pose annotations. Our systematic evaluation of 17 frontier VLMs across the major proprietary and open-weight families (Gemini, GPT, Claude, Qwen, Gemma) shows that the best model, Gemini 4 Carbon, reaches 72.6% AP_IoU2D in 2D but only 12.4% AP_IoU3D in 3D, despite the more lenient IoU thresholds in 3D. The predicted 3D boxes are usually centered on the target objects in the image but often off in depth by more than the object's size, suggesting that metric scale estimation, which needs reliable object size priors, is currently by far the main source of error.

Real2SimLab: A Unified Platform for Real-to-Simulation Transfer

Yongchang Zhang, Jie Huang, Yunhe Ni, Ningyu Zhou, Guangyu Zhao et al. (13 authors)

Real-to-simulation transfer, which builds simulations from real-world observations, has long been studied in research and industry. Recently, coding agents have become capable of generating simulation programs from video recordings, and several benchmarks have been proposed to evaluate them. However, each individual benchmark defines its own inputs, deliverables, and scoring pipeline. This leads to $N \times M$ integrations when evaluating $N$ methods on $M$ benchmarks. To this end, we present Real2SimLab, a unified platform for real-to-simulation transfer. It evaluates a method by running its written program and scoring the output simulation with the original scorer of each benchmark. Moreover, Real2SimLab provides a pipeline to create training data and reward annotations for real-to-simulation methods. Our pipeline carefully avoids data contamination by filtering out the video recordings that exist in benchmarks. For each video recording, a program is first produced, and then our pipeline computes its reward with the same code that Real2SimLab uses for evaluation. So far, it supports 47 coding agents and 5 non-agent methods on 6 benchmarks. Both the platform and Awesome-Real2Sim, our research map of nearly 300 real-to-simulation papers, are open source, and we invite the Real2SimHub community to build them together.
