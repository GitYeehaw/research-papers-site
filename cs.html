Optimal Linear Regression Without a Variance

Artin Tajdini, Jasper C. H. Lee, Jonathan Scarlett, Kevin Jamieson

We study high-probability linear regression in the infinite variance regime, wherecthe regression noise has only a finite conditional $α$-moment $ν$ for some unknown $α\in(1,2)$, and the covariates may only have finite moments up to a given order (e.g., 4th moment). We give a black-box reduction that converts finite-variance regression procedures into weak-moment procedures. The reduction is based on empirical residual pruning and is agnostic to the moment exponent, the noise scale, the covariance, and the design moment. Under a finite fourth-moment condition on the covariates, any finite-variance subroutine attaining the sub-Gaussian rate yields the following when the number of samples $n$ is above a suitably defined threshold: Without knowing $α$ or $ν$, we have with probability at least $1-δ$ that $ \|\widehatθ-θ^*\|_Σ \lesssim_α ν^{1/α} \big(\frac{d+\log(1/δ)}{n}\big)^{\frac{α-1}α}, $ where $d$ is the dimension, $Σ$ is the covariate covariance matrix, and $\|v\|_Σ:=(v^\topΣv)^{1/2}$. We further prove a matching minimax lower bound that holds under the same conditions. The reduction is computationally efficient whenever the finite-variance subroutine is. We also obtain a distribution-free prediction guarantee under a bounded raw $α$-moment assumption on the response, and give an analogous $α$-agnostic reduction for mean estimation.

A Methodologically Rigorous Benchmark of Linear Regression,RNN, and LSTM for S&P 500 Forecasting:Identifying Sources of Performance Inflation in Financial DeepLearning

Hari Sitaula, Joseph Opitz

This study examines one-day-ahead forecasting of the S&P 500 closing level using a 2006-2025 dataset that includes fundamental price data, macroeconomic indicators, and engineered technical features. We benchmark three model families, namely linear regression, vanilla recurrent neural networks (RNN), and long short-term memory networks (LSTM), within a unified preprocessing and chronological evaluation pipeline. A central contribution of this work is a methodological audit of common practices in the deep-learning-for-stock-prediction literature. We identify three sources of result inflation that frequently appear in published methodologies: wavelet-denoised evaluation targets, the absence of a naive persistence baseline, and best-of-N replicate reporting. We then re-evaluate all model families under a corrected protocol that controls for each. Performance is reported using root mean squared error, mean absolute percentage error, and the Pearson correlation coefficient, averaged across N = 30 independent replicates per configuration. We compare models on accuracy, stability across replicates, and architectural depth sensitivity, using the naive persistence forecast (predicting today's close as tomorrow's close) as a primary baseline against which all candidate models must demonstrate improvement.

MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models

Chang Nie, Chaoyou Fu, Songlin Liu, Yiqing Hu, Caifeng Shan

Deploying large language models (LLMs) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: (a) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; (b) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms (pretraining and post-training adaptation). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313$\times$ and prefill latency by 6.9$\times$ at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5$\times$ and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

On the Complete Feature Set for ADHD Classification in Children and Adolescents Based on EEG Signals

Javier Sanchis, Álvaro Navarro, Jorge García-Carrasco, Tahar Kechadi, Juan Trujillo

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition that can affect attention, activity and impulse control. Electroencephalography (EEG) records brain activity as children and adolescents perform cognitive tasks. However, comparing features derived from these recordings requires consistent signal preparation, shared analysis windows and documented quality decisions. We curated 669 existing BALLADEER recordings from 156 participants across five task conditions so that researchers can compare seven EEG feature families on the same time intervals for each participant and task. The dataset links continuous signals to precomputed measurements from 115 feature definitions or templates and records the quality decisions made for each recording. Analyses must still account for repeated measurements within participants, residual artefacts and differences between acquisition systems. Technical validation covered signal integrity, numerical checks on feature estimators and completeness of the released files. In every task, the median proportion of recording time retained after preprocessing was 100%. To illustrate reuse, we report saved participant-held-out evaluations comparing XGBoost using all seven families on eight-second windows with a predictor based only on recruitment-group prevalence in the training fold. Recruitment-group discrimination was stronger in Robots than in the three Slackline conditions. These descriptive results from overlapping cohorts do not establish diagnostic validity. The dataset, curation software and aggregate results for the predictive example are publicly available.

Non-penetrating World Models

Yuhang Dai, Yinwei Wu, Ziyue Lin, Han Ji, Xingyi Yang

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

Model Carving: Training Transformers That Optimize Their Own Topology

Kaden Zheng, Pratyusha Sharma

Machine learning has largely kept separate the choice of architecture and the process of training. A classic Transformer, for instance, trains a fixed set of attention and MLP sublayers at every layer, in the same order, and optimizes the weights within. This uniformity is a convention rather than a principled design choice, and it is likely suboptimal: the best architecture for a given task may be smaller, more efficient, and far from uniform. We believe that, ideally, training itself should shape the model's topology. To this end, we introduce Model Carving, which learns both a Transformer's weights and its architecture in a single run. By applying learnable, scalar gates to each sublayer, we allow the training loss to remove the model's own components during training -- freeing a smaller heterogeneous network inside. We evaluate Model Carving by pre-training standard Transformers from 140M to 2.7B parameters and looped Transformers from 140M to 1.3B. At equal training compute, carved models reach a lower validation loss than dense models trained with up to 1.7 times more compute. Furthermore, carved models also outperform size-matched models produced by pruning or distillation, suggesting that the best way to obtain small models for edge devices is to carve them during training, not to compress a large one afterwards. This advantage both grows with scale and carries over to downstream tasks such as few-shot evaluation and generation. Because the removed sublayers no longer run, carved models also cost up to 34% fewer FLOPs per token and decode up to 1.6 times faster without cost in quality. Our results show that architecture, like weights, should be learned and not fixed in advance, and that inside every large transformer is a smaller, faster, stronger one waiting to be carved out.

Ranking-Preserving Coreset Selection for Efficient Evolutionary Hyperparameter Optimization

Mamoona Tasadduq, Maryam Bashir

Hyperparameter optimization (HPO) is essential for high predictive performance, but its computational cost becomes prohibitive when candidate configurations require repeated training on large datasets. This paper proposes a ranking-preserving coreset (RPC) selection framework for accelerating evolutionary HPO. The method constructs a compact coreset that preserves the relative ranking of hyperparameter configurations observed on the full training data. Coreset selection is formulated as an evolutionary optimization problem that maximizes Spearman rank correlation between full-data and subset-based configuration rankings while penalizing subset size. The selected coreset is then used for HPO with three evolutionary algorithms: Genetic Algorithm (GA), Cross-generational elitist Selection, Heterogeneous Recombination and Cataclysmic Mutation (CHC), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). Experiments on seven tabular, two text, and two image benchmark datasets show that RPC substantially reduces HPO time while maintaining test accuracy comparable to full-dataset HPO across decision tree, text classification, and image classification tasks. Across datasets and optimizers, HPO-stage speedups range from approximately 1.14x to 8.80x, while the one-time coreset construction cost is accounted for separately through end-to-end runtime and break-even analysis. On 20 held-out configurations excluded from coreset construction, RPC achieves higher ranking correlation than same-size random sampling on 8 of 11 datasets (dataset-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p = 0.014). These results show that ranking preservation extends beyond the configurations used during selection. The one-time construction cost can be amortized when the coreset is reused across HPO runs.

Topology Hub Optimal Transport: Eliminating Long Context Dilution at Flash Speed

Dylan Forde

Long-context models use sparse or structured attention to avoid quadratic cost, yet retrieving a small set of relevant facts embedded among hundreds of thousands of distractors degrades as normalization mass disperses with length. Hub-and-spoke optimal transport (OT) replaces the dense $L \times L$ matrix with a linear-size graph over local windows and $K$ shared hubs balanced by Sinkhorn scaling. Building on block-wise tail refinement and fixed-support transport topology (arXiv:2605.08123, arXiv:2609.07954), we introduce Topology Hub Optimal Transport (THOT): (1) bounded-support capacity gating via simplex sparsemax ($O(L\log L)$) or sorting-free token-local thresholded ReLU ($O(L)$), driving excess distractor mass to zero; (2) a three-sweep $1+2$ annealed row-closed Sinkhorn schedule that damps bipartite residuals; (3) an exact rank-$(D+2R+1)$ One-Reference-Tile adjoint in batched split-factor Triton kernels (BF16 Tensor Cores, FP32 potentials); and (4) a chunked exclusive-prefix causal variant with verified zero future leakage. On NVIDIA L4, sparsemax THOT trained at $L=128$ reaches 97.40% zero-shot recall of fixed bindings at $L=262{,}144$ under pure task loss ($α=0$) and 98.83% at $α=0.05$ (matched by $O(L)$ thresholded ReLU at $α=0.05$, vs. 80.60% at $α=0$), while softmax-gated hub OT drops to 3.52%. In bidirectional operator microbenchmarks, our kernel runs forward+VJP in 100.1 ms at $H=8, D=64, L=262K$ (2.2x faster with 4.0x smaller compiler temporaries than latent softmax) and 1.34 ms vs. 4.37 ms for cuDNN FlashAttention at $L=4{,}096$; multi-layer LM runs use the unfused path. Zero-shot WikiText-103 multi-needle retrieval hits 100.00% (128/128 queries at $L=65K$) vs. 0.78% for short-trained RoPE FlashAttention, and compact 4-layer WikiText-4K runs show modest causal and masked LM gains.

Perceived Differences in Activity and Experience Between Developing Software With and Without AI

Rubash Mali, Juho Leinonen, John Edwards

The fundamental changes in software engineering driven by generative AI will have profound effects not only on the number of available software engineering jobs but also on what software engineering itself looks like. As the career evolves, questions arise about what the skillset and interests of the next generation of software engineers will be and whether long-time engineers will continue to be attracted to the career. In this paper, we contribute to answering these questions by studying how software development with AI tools is perceived to differ from traditional development and how the differences are modulated by years of programming experience. We find that, in terms of programming activities, engineers report less talking with peers about technical problems, less decomposition, less searching of documentation, and less using a debugger, while they report more task switching. We also find experiential differences, including less deep focus and less sense of control over code and design. We call these the different craft results, in contrast to core code-production activities such as writing, refactoring, and fixing code, which show only small mean differences. We also find that these differences are amplified by years of programming experience (an experience gradient), with software engineers who started programming before the age of generative AI perceiving greater changes in both activity and developer experience. We discuss our findings and their implications on training of the next generation of engineers and on engineers who may have difficulty transitioning to the AI-infused world of software engineering.

The Optimal Sample Complexity of Agnostic PAC Learning for Partial Concept Classes

Liang-Yu Zou

Let $\mathcal H\subseteq\{-1,+1,\star\}^{\mathcal X}$ be a partial concept class of finite VC dimension $d$. We construct a deterministic agnostic PAC learner that, for every $\varepsilon,δ\in(0,1)$, uses $O((d+\log(1/δ))/\varepsilon^2)$ i.i.d. examples from an arbitrary distribution $\mathcal D$ over $\mathcal X\times\{-1,+1\}$ and returns a total concept $\hat h:\mathcal X\to\{-1,+1\}$ satisfying $er_{\mathcal D}(\hat h)\leq \min_{h\in\mathcal H}er_{\mathcal D}(h)+\varepsilon$ with probability at least $1-δ$, where $er_{\mathcal D}(h)=\mathbb P_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal D}[h(x)\neq y]$. This establishes the optimal sample complexity, matching the lower bound of Alon et al. (FOCS 2021).
