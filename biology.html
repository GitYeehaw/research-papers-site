A multi-patch model for sylvatic hematophagous vector dynamics: the impact of landscape anthropization

Ivric Valaire Yatat-Djeumen, Lukas Eigentler, Orville Wright Happi-Tchakounté, Imma Tcheferi, Pierre Couteron

We develop a multi-patch model for sylvatic hematophagous vector--(wild) animal interactions with nonlinear migration terms that depend on landscape anthropization. Every patch is a suitable habitat for both animals and vectors, but anthropization reduces the animal carrying capacity and induces animal and vector displacement. We considered three dispersal types. Under unconstrained global dispersal, displaced individuals can move to any other patch. Under the more realistic random and constrained neighbor dispersal, displaced animals move to a neighboring patch chosen either at random or preferentially among less anthropized ones. We parameterized our model for tsetse flies (Glossina spp.) in the districts of Campo, Nyé'été, and Ma'an, Ocean division, Cameroon. We characterized the equilibria and their local stability analytically for unconstrained global dispersal, and found numerically that, under both neighbor dispersal regimes, populations progressively expand before settling into a stable equilibrium. Under random neighbor dispersal, the effect of anthropization on migration had a much larger impact on steady state densities than its effect on animal carrying capacity. Under constrained neighbor dispersal, populations spread noticeably more slowly, and numerous patches maintained extremely sparse populations. We further investigated invasion speed under different anthropization scenarios. For random neighbor dispersal, invasion times decreased upon introduction of newly anthropized regions, while for constrained neighbor dispersal, anthropized regions either increased or did not affect invasion times. Approximating invasions by pulled traveling waves, we found that anthropized regions act as ``invasion highways'' or ``invasion barriers'' depending on the dispersal type. We conclude that more empirical data on animal dispersal are needed to understand these dynamics.

Decoupling the clocks of learning and computing in spiking neural networks

Michalis Pagkalos, Panayiota Poirazi

Biological intelligence thrives on minimal power despite its noisy, imperfect components, with neurons that communicate through voltage spikes. Brain-inspired computing seeks to bring such efficiency to real devices, which requires training methods tailored to them. The standard way to train spiking neural networks, backpropagation through time, computes weight updates through every step of a simulated device, so faithful or long simulations can make training costly and unstable. Here we show that spiking networks can learn reliably and efficiently by decoupling the clock of learning from that of computing. Our method, ChronoProp, calculates weight updates from each neuron's spike counts in fixed time bins using its measured input-output function. Compared with backpropagation through time, ChronoProp reaches similar accuracy in image classification, with about 3 times less training time and 20 times less memory at the finest timestep tested, and up to 10 times fewer spikes. On spoken-word benchmarks, ChronoProp is far more accurate in recurrent networks at fine timesteps, and with simple, hardware-compatible techniques it approaches the best published models. These networks also tolerate simulated hardware defects and limitations better than those trained with backpropagation through time. Therefore, ChronoProp enables stable and efficient training in simulations close to the continuous time of analog hardware.

Quantum chaotic signature in non-empirical ensemble of DNA base pairs

Mehdi Ameri

The statistical study of many-body chaotic quantum systems would indicate important phenomena such as short-range level repulsion and long-range spectral rigidity. We investigate such behavior in an ensemble of gas-phase guanine-cytosine (GC) and adenine-thymine (AT) Watson-Crick (WC) base pairs by calculating their Kohn-Sham molecular orbital energy levels via ab initio density functional theory (DFT). We track the probability distribution, adjacent-gap ratios (AGR), number variance, and spectral compressibility of the unfolded level spacings to see whether their statistics follow the statistics of the eigenvalues of random matrices. After comparing the results to Poisson, semi-Poisson (SP), and Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) statistics, it becomes evident that the statistics of orbital energy levels agree closely with the semi-Poisson distribution, pointing to an intermediate regime between integrability and chaos, characterized by level repulsion and strong long-range correlations. Potential biophysical implications for charge transfer and related biological processes are also discussed.

Multi-view fnirs identifies neurophysiological subtypes in post-stroke aphasia

Zijian Kang, Bingbing Lin, Jinglei Ni, Meifang Tian, Wai Ting Siok et al. (7 authors)

Post-stroke aphasia is clinically heterogeneous, and conventional classifications may not capture differences in brain functional organisation. We tested whether resting-state and task-based functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) could identify neurophysiological subgroups alongside clinical assessment.Seventy-nine patients underwent unsupervised clustering using three fNIRS feature views: resting-state activity and connectivity, language-task activation, and rest-to-task connectivity reconfiguration. Similarity network fusion and spectral clustering integrated the views. Clinical and behavioural measures were excluded from clustering.Three clusters (n = 32, 17 and 30) were identified. This solution had the largest eigengap and was the only candidate significant after permutation testing and Holm correction (adjusted P = 0.001). It was stable under subsampling (mean adjusted Rand index, 0.773), but agreement fell to 0.217 when the resting-state view was removed. Clusters differed in most regional intrinsic-activity measures and all resting-state connectivity summaries, with few differences in task activation or connectivity reconfiguration. Their profiles showed higher right anterior prefrontal activity, distributed fronto-sensorimotor and parietal alterations, and reduced interhemispheric connectivity, respectively.Cluster membership was not significantly associated with conventional aphasia subtype (Cramér's V = 0.287), and baseline clinical language scores and task performance did not differ between clusters.These findings identify three neurophysiological subgroups distinguished mainly by intrinsic activity and resting-state functional connectivity, not clearly aligned with conventional aphasia classifications or baseline clinical measures. Multi-view fNIRS analysis may complement clinical characterisation, requiring independent and longitudinal validation.

MolGPAO: Molecular Generative Pre-trained Autoregressive Octree with Speculative Decoding and Sub-Millisecond Seam Healing for 3D Molecules

Shangqian Chen, Guojiang Zhao, Zheng Cheng, Jiankun Wang, Hongshuai Wang et al. (6 authors)

Autoregressive generation over adaptive 3D octrees (Uni-3DAR) offers an intrinsic $O(N \log N)$ sparsity advantage over dense $O(N^2)$ equivariant diffusion and flow models on large drug-like molecules, yet lags in geometric equilibrium, closed-shell valence stability, and property fidelity due to Morton-seam coordinate jumps, leaf-heavy compute with root-count trapping, and target-shortcut collapse in internal critics. We introduce MolGPAO (Molecular Generative Pre-trained Autoregressive Octree), a standalone 3D octree architecture and single-output speculative tree-decoding engine using zero external surrogates, zero reinforcement learning, and zero inference-time MLIP relaxation. MolGPAO unifies: (1) non-trapping 6-D ScaleFreeBudgetEmbedding with L5-to-L1 covalent packing priors; (2) a FlashAttention-2 pause-and-gather speculative octree funnel ($m_{s1} \to m_{s2} \to 1$, $M_{out} = 1$); (3) an anti-shortcut ground-truth TwoStageOctreeProcessCritic with 64-RBF covalent edge gating and $SO(3)$-invariant 12-channel multipole norms (blind QM9 $r = 0.9867$--$0.9989$); (4) a $<0.5$ ms 4-layer $SE(3)$ PostTreeGraphRefiner for Morton-seam healing, expected-XYZ bonded supervision, selective CFG, and zero-heavy-displacement valence reconciliation; and (5) bubble-free stride-2 GPU leaf decoding. Across GEOM-Drugs (0.49 GPU-h), disjoint 50k/50k QM9 (0.43 GPU-h), and PBE0/def2-TZVP-verified OE62 (0.56 GPU-h), MolGPAO lifts drug-scale valid-connected yield 6.78x (12.7% to 86.2%, 99.83% atom stability), runs 7.3x faster (or 1.65x faster at 2.0x yield) than 100-step FlowMol at $N=100$, beats 156 GPU-h PropMolFlow on QM9 HOMO (-12.7%) and dipole (-21.0%) at 362x lower compute, and doubles live DFT hit rate on OE62 (33.3% vs. 16.7%) at 92.8x lower compute.

Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao Reconstruction of Discrete space Population Dynamics

Junda Ying, Xinyu Wang, Peijie Zhou, Lei Zhang

Reconstructing the continuous time dynamics of cell state evolution from sparse snapshot observations can be formulated as an unbalanced optimal transport problem. To study such dynamics at a coarse grained cell type level, unbalanced optimal transport must be extended to discrete state spaces. Inspired by discrete flow matching, we extended the dynamic Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) problem to discrete spaces, and derived a closed form for its solution. For particularly large state spaces or more general models, we proposed an unbalanced discrete flow matching as a numerical approximation method, which provides a Markovian reconstruction of the optimal WFR flow. Our discrete WFR framework also stays stable across different coarse grain resolution. Finally, numerical experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed approach, and applications to real biological data supported the plausibility of the reconstructed dynamics.

KnitNet: Learning to Search at Inference Time for Exact RNA Inverse Design

Antonia Panescu, Xin Wang, Haoran Xu, Qingyu Chen, Carlos Oliver et al. (6 authors)

RNA inverse design seeks nucleotide sequences that fold into specified target structures. Existing methods rely on learned generators that can miss exact structural matches, especially beyond their training distributions, or on costly search over a vast, multimodal sequence space. Search is further complicated by globally coupled nucleotide interactions, as correcting one pairing error can create new errors beyond a fixed repair scope. To address this, we introduce KnitNet, an inference-time scaling framework that learns from verifier feedback to refine sequences for exact fold-back matching. Starting from candidates generated for a target secondary structure, KnitNet uses mismatched folds to infer nucleotide dependencies and adapt its joint repair strategy, while an online surrogate learns to predict which edits are promising. This makes search progressively more informed and compute-efficient. Crucially, KnitNet generalizes across RNA structural regimes by changing only the optimization signal to match the verifier required by each setting. Across five secondary-structure benchmarks, KnitNet increases exact solve rates by an average of 21 percentage points over continued sampling with 70% less time-to-solve. On OpenKnot57 specifically, KnitNet is the first method to achieve exact computational matches for all complex, pseudoknotted structures. Beyond 2D, KnitNet improves 3D structural self-consistency over per-target RL by 7.8% while being 39.3% faster, demonstrating generalization and effective inference-time scaling across RNA design regimes.

From the Deterministic Hook Effect to Stochastic Reliability in Ternary Complex Formation

Deepak K. Agrawal

Ternary binding systems occur in signaling, scaffold-mediated assembly, and targeted protein degradation. A mediator brings two partners into a productive ternary complex, but excess mediator can hold them in separate binary complexes. Consequently, ternary complex formation can increase and then decrease as the mediator dose increases, producing the hook effect. Deterministic dose-response curves describe the average behavior but do not indicate whether individual small compartments reliably form the complex. A minimal reversible binding network was studied using deterministic equations, exact stochastic simulations, and a simple catalytic extension. The deterministic model recovered the hook-shaped response. In stochastic simulations, small volumes often failed to accumulate the required number of complexes, even where the deterministic prediction was favorable. At low mediator doses, failure reflected a shortage of mediator molecules; at high doses, material accumulated in free or binary-bound forms. Larger volumes increased the range of mediator doses supporting reliable formation, while stricter copy-number thresholds narrowed that range. Faster ternary-complex completion or slower breakup improved reliability. In the catalytic model, product formation also depended on how early productive complexes formed. These results show why a favorable average binding response does not necessarily ensure reliable assembly in small reaction volumes.

Exclusion and internal structure in the association/dissociation kinetics of DNA-binding proteins in the fast diffusion limit

Xiangting Li, Tom Chou

In many canonical biophysical and biochemical contexts, complex diffusing DNA-binding proteins undergoing nonspecific adsorption and desorption can exist in different conformational states. Equilibrium binding theories and kinetic models of competing ligands provide a basis for describing these systems. Coupling the internal conformational kinetics of bound proteins to exclusion and diffusion requires tracking both protein positions and binding states. Closed-form, tractable mathematical models that accurately describe the stochastic dynamics of structured proteins that bind to and diffuse along DNA are lacking. Here, we develop a new coarse-grained stochastic model of nonspecific DNA-protein binding in the fast diffusion regime, from which we derive an effective Markov model and its corresponding mean-field approximation that incorporates protein footprint size and conformational switching. We fit our new model quantitatively to previously measured dynamics of Replication Protein A (RPA) binding to ssDNA. Finally, by including multisegment binding states, our analysis captures facilitated exchange, revealing how multisegment architecture balances high affinity with rapid remodeling. Our theoretical model applies broadly to many other DNA-binding protein systems, including nucleosomes, ssDNA-binding proteins, and other multisegment complexes, providing accurate predictions and new strategies for evaluating experimental data.

The evolution of extreme spatial heterochiasmy in amphibians: interactions between sex-specific meiotic program and chromosome architecture

Ezgi Unal, Paris Veltsos, Wen-Juan Ma

Heterochiasmy, sex differences in meiotic recombination, is usually described in terms of genetic-map length, potentially obscuring more consequential differences in crossover location. Amphibians provide striking examples of extreme spatial heterochiasmy, with male recombination concentrated near chromosome ends and female recombination broadly distributed, yet the evolution and genetic basis of this pattern remains poorly understood. Here, we combine a review with a phylogenetic comparative synthesis of male chiasma location across 97 amphibian species. Male crossover localization is phylogenetically structured, and ancestral reconstruction favors predominantly interstitial recombination at deeper anuran nodes, although not conclusively. The strongest correlate of male terminal crossover location is chromosome morphology: telocentric-rich karyotypes retain more interstitial male chiasmata, whereas metacentric chromosomes show more terminal male chiasmata. Genome size and sex-chromosome system show little association. We propose a sequential model in which sex-specific meiotic regulation first establishes a crossover-precursor placement bias, while centromere position influences which precursors mature into crossovers. This model predicts that chromosome fusion, fission or centromere repositioning can alter male chiasma location without requiring changes to the underlying meiotic program. Our synthesis highlights crossover location as a distinct dimension of heterochiasmy with consequences for genome-wide linkage, adaptation, divergence and sex-chromosome evolution.
