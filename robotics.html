On Agentic Manipulation Policies

Chen Chen, Omid Bagherieh, Naveen Nikamanth Appiah Balaji, Jesse T. Gonzalez, Negin Heravi et al. (13 authors)

Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6\% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2\% and 42.9\% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The agent succeeds zero-shot in 104 of 108 real-world trials spanning twelve tasks and three robot instances, reducing failure rates by 20x and 15x relative to strong VLA and WAM baselines. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation.

Scenario Orchestration for Closed-loop Policy Evaluation of Autonomous Driving

Frieda Rong, Zhaoyue Wang, Veer Kapadia, Vishaka Rajani, Aren A. Babikian et al. (9 authors)

Scenario-based simulation is central to evaluating autonomous-driving policies, but a scenario scripted before execution (i.e., with predetermined, fixed actor routes and speeds) may not preserve its intended interaction once the tested ego policy responds in closed loop. Scripted scenarios cannot adapt to unanticipated ego behavior, while learned traffic actors may remain reactive without reliably realizing a particular scenario intent. We introduce a scenario orchestrator that continuously monitors the joint traffic state and adapts non-ego actors through re-timing, re-routing, and role reassignment for creating a specific scenario intent. At each step, the orchestrator solves a constrained optimization problem that preserves the intended interaction while limiting interventions and maintaining traffic feasibility. We further distinguish scenario realization from policy failure, allowing a test to first be judged based on whether the intended interaction occurred and then by how the ego policy responded. Experiments in the CARLA simulator across multiple scenario families and driving policies show that orchestration realizes targeted interactions more reliably than scripted and learned actor-level baselines, particularly under variations in initial conditions and ego behavior. These results support closed-loop scenario orchestration as a tool for controlled and reproducible policy evaluation.

Locating Contact on Tendon-Driven Continuum Robots From Tendon Measurements Alone via Constrained Moving Horizon Estimation

Haitham El-Hussieny

Tendon-driven continuum robots can reach through constrained spaces, but contact along their bodies is invisible to the sensors at their motors. We show that this is a structural limitation rather than a tuning problem: the map from contact forces to tendon measurements has a null space of dimension at least seven, so no estimator that leaves the contact support free can locate contact. When the obstacle geometry is known, however, non-penetration and complementarity make the contact force locally identifiable, and we prove that the resulting system with unknown contact inputs is locally exponentially strongly detectable. We present a constrained moving horizon estimator (MHE) that encodes these conditions and runs in real time as one quadratic program per sample, and we give a local, mode-wise robust stability result for it. In a finite- element benchmark of a two-section robot pressing and sliding on a wall, the estimator located contact to 0.9 mm and estimated contact force to 0.22 N, compared with 65 mm and 0.72 N for a tuned extended Kalman filter (EKF) with state projection. The estimator tolerated millimeter- level errors in the obstacle position and unmodeled wall friction, under which the projected EKF failed. Under randomized model mismatch over 40 trials, including unmodeled tendon friction, it remained better in force and location in every trial. Without contact, the MHE offered no advantage over the EKF, which confirms that its benefit comes from the contact constraints. This report extends a letter submitted to IEEE Control Systems Letters with complete proofs and additional experiments.

Grasp-TAGS: Task-Aware Grasp Selection via Critic Distillation for Prehensile Loco-Manipulation

Xun Tu, Minghao Zou, Karthik Desingh

Loco-manipulation, which requires mobile manipulators to coordinate locomotion and manipulation over long horizons, is a key capability for large-object rearrangement in applications such as service robotics and warehouse automation. Despite recent progress, reliably executing these tasks remains challenging because performance depends on complex robot-object and object-environment interaction dynamics. Existing approaches address this challenge either through model-based methods, which often require accurate dynamics models, or through learning-based methods, which reduce this requirement but typically assume a fixed or predefined initial grasp. However, different geometrically stable grasps can lead to substantially different downstream manipulation performance. Therefore, we propose Grasp-TAGS, a task-aware grasp selection framework for goal-conditioned large-object rearrangement. We train a unified PPO-based reinforcement learning policy across multiple initial grasp configurations and distill the learned critic into a lightweight grasp utility network that ranks candidate grasps using only the candidate grasp configuration and goal information. At inference time, we select the grasp with the highest predicted downstream utility for the downstream policy to complete the task, before even making a contact. Experiments demonstrate that Grasp-TAGS improves rearrangement success over both fixed single-grasp policies and random grasp selection, achieving an average success rate of 89.84% compared with 79.87% for the best-performing single-grasp policies, an improvement of 9.97 percentage points. Grasp-TAGS also reduces final orientation errors across all evaluated objects. For more qualitative results, refer to the supplementary webpage: https://rpm-lab-umn.github.io/Grasp-TAGS-web-page/

Geometric Pairwise Classification of Affine Constraints over Ellipsoidal Regions

Igor M. L. Pataro, Martina Mammarella, Fabrizio Dabbene, Teodoro Alamo

This paper presents a deterministic geometric procedure for analysing affine constraints over fixed ellipsoidal regions, motivated by chance-constrained set approximation. Analytical tests classify inequalities as always violated, never violated, or cutting. Pairwise tests determine whether two inequalities can be strictly violated at a common point, yielding a compatibility matrix. The tests follow from quadratic optimisation and the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions, with explicit treatment of tangencies, parallel normals, and degenerate inequalities. The formulation applies in arbitrary finite dimensions and provides geometric information for subsequent bounds on simultaneous sampled violations and safe-region approximation.

Computer-Use Agents Are Embodied Generalists

Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck

In many areas of AI, general-purpose models have increasingly replaced handcrafted methods and specialized architectures. We argue that embodied AI is poised for a similar shift. Our key claim is that embodied tasks can be formulated as interaction with digital software. Specifically, we build software that renders an agent's view of a physical environment on a screen and provides controls for acting in it. A computer-use agent (CUA) can then perform embodied tasks through the same screenshots and keyboard and mouse actions that it uses to operate ordinary software. Using a single CUA design and changing only the software, our entries win first-, second-, third-, and sixth-place prizes across four ECCV workshop competitions, without any competition-specific agent tuning. These results suggest that the skills CUAs learn on software, such as visual grounding, precise clicking, and exploration, already carry over to embodied tasks, giving CUAs a far broader scope in embodied AI than previously realized. We therefore advocate that computer-use agents can be embodied generalists. Further, we advocate that, by following the training strategy that made computer-use agents general, embodied AI can converge on a single general model as well, ushering in a new era of general embodied AI. Generalized H matrix stability for power systems

Elinor Ginzburg-Ganz, Yoash Levron, Juri Belikov, George Weiss

The small-signal stability of a power system model with arbitrary loads is investigated, with a focus on locally constant power loads in lossless networks. A central element of the proposed analysis is the matrix H, which represents the partial derivatives of generator powers with respect to their relative angles. The local stability of any equilibrium point is determined by the properties of H if the loads consume locally constant power. Specifically, an equilibrium point is asymptotically stable if H is positive definite and unstable if H is full-rank and not positive definite, while several quantitative relationships between the eigenvalues of H and the system Jacobian matrix are also established. The main contribution is that loads are allowed to be arbitrarily located within the transmission network, thereby generalizing the existing H matrix stability criterion which is valid only if loads are located at the same buses as the generators. The advantage of the proposed criterion is demonstrated through case studies involving systems with up to 2869 buses. Accurate stability assessments are maintained, where alternative methods, such as the NRPS model, are found to be overly conservative. Additionally, a computational acceleration by a factor of 340 relative to Jacobian-based methods is achieved.

Seeing What Matters: Benchmarking Task-Conditioned Actionable Spatial Belief for Manipulation

Yina Jian, Mingkun Xu

When should a robot look at the cup, and when at the portafilter? Spatial-reasoning benchmarks evaluate whether a model can describe a scene, but manipulation needs something more specific: the ability to acquire the particular evidence that makes the current command executable. A view that is informative for one task can be useless for another, even at the same workstation. We introduce Seeing What Matters, a benchmark for task-conditioned actionable spatial belief in simulated tabletop manipulation. Across three domains--coffee workstation, drawer/cabinet, and microwave/appliance--the benchmark provides 860 frozen evaluation cases; expanding across methods, budgets, and inference repeats yields more than 115,000 evaluator episodes under two protocols: closed-loop actionability and same-object functional out-of-distribution generalization. Each case pairs a set of candidate RGB views with a manipulation command, shared scripted skills, and hidden postconditions, so the benchmark directly measures whether a view-selection policy acquires the observations its command actually needs. We also provide a diagnostic reference pipeline that maintains a spatial-functional belief state and ranks views by missing evidence. At primary budget settings, this pipeline reaches 98.8% closed-loop and 98.9% functional-OOD success; ablating the functional or command-binding components collapses performance to near zero. Seven vision-language models, evaluated on the same cases, reach only 26-43%. Replacing the reference pipeline's coverage metadata with frozen RGB-derived scores preserves 98.5%/98.9% success; shuffling the visual scores collapses it to 15%/11%, confirming that the result depends on image content. These gaps suggest that spatial belief for manipulation should be evaluated not by scene-level completeness, but by whether it acquires the evidence the current command requires.

Autonomous Whole-body Loco-manipulation Using a Snake Robot

Adarsh Salagame, Henry Noyes, Haroon Hublikar, Alireza Ramezani

Snake robots can leverage their hyper-redundant morphology for both locomotion and manipulation through distributed body contact but whole-body loco-manipulation is particularly challenging since the entire body serves simultaneously as propulsive mechanism and manipulation tool Prior work on wheeled legged and snake robot platforms has decoupled these functions through dedicated appendages body segmentation or grippers We present closed-loop loco-manipulation with our snake robot COBRA without this distinction such that any part of the body may make contact We develop a contact-implicit dynamic model unifying robot-ground robot-object and object-ground interactions within a Moreau-Jean time-stepping framework and formulate a trajectory optimization over the full-order dynamics guided by a geometric planner and nominal proportional controller to autonomously push an object to a goal location during sidewinding We validate the planner and controller on hardware across varied configurations and present simulation results for a simplified trajectory optimization that resolves robot-object and object-ground contacts

A Cross-Domain TARA-to-NAC Rule Mapping Methodology: From Automotive OEM Networks to Energy Storage Systems

Krishna Teja Medam, Thiago Quinelato

Threat analysis and risk assessment under ISO/SAE 21434 ends with cybersecurity goals, but existing methods give little structured guidance for turning those goals into enforceable network access control rules. Compliance standards such as IEC 62443, ISO/SAE 21434, and ISO/IEC 27001 are usually applied as parallel efforts rather than as one enforcement architecture. This paper presents a six-step methodology that maps cybersecurity goals from a threat analysis and risk assessment to network access control rules, each placed at a named enforcement point and traceable to the threat records behind it. It also presents a unified compliance framework that treats IEC 62443 and ISO/IEC 27001 as a fixed substrate and domain-specific regulation as an interchangeable overlay. The methodology is applied to an automotive original equipment manufacturer plant network, addressing multi-tier supplier authorization and legacy operational technology devices, and then to utility-scale battery energy storage, where North American Electric Reliability Corporation Critical Infrastructure Protection standards and ISO/IEC 27019 replace the automotive-specific standard while the substrate stays constant. In the automotive example, 37 threat entries and 24 cybersecurity goals yield 23 rules covering 11 of the 13 in-scope goals; the energy example yields six rules. Three derived rules have no equivalent in the cited standards. Both examples are desk-based: the methodology is applied to them, not tested on them. The result is a reusable pattern for translating risk assessment into enforcement across critical infrastructure sectors, with empirical validation left as future work.
