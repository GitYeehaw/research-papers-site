Using topology to reveal all mutually unbiased bases in dimension six

Mateo Cárdenes Wuttig, Joseph Tindall

Mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) describe complementary quantum measurements: certainty in one basis gives no information about the others. In dimension six, triples of MUBs are known, but whether a fourth basis could exist remained unresolved for over three decades. OpenAI recently reported a proof that none exists. Here we use the topology of the space of complex Hadamard matrices, which describe transitions between MUBs, to characterize all sets in dimension six. We prove that every mutually unbiased triple belongs to a two-parameter family or is a single isolated triple built from the Fourier matrix. Since none of them extend to a fourth basis, this also proves that no four MUBs exist. Our proof rests on a new topological result that all order-six complex Hadamard matrices except Tao's form a connected space. Connectedness reduces the classification of triples to finitely many overlapping four-dimensional phase balls. We certify with interval arithmetic that these balls cover the space and for every pair of MUBs represented in a ball, we then determine all third bases that extend it. Dimension six is now the first non-prime-power dimension in which all sets of MUBs are classified.

Arithmetic Depth, Minimal Gate Alphabets, and Exact Program-State Resources for Single-Qubit Dyadic Programs

Tony Newton, Dan-Adrian German

Resource analyses for quantum programs commonly provide safe upper bounds on qubits, gates, or non-Clifford operations. We study a single-qubit dyadic fragment in which the deepest exact resource layer can instead be inferred exactly. Starting from the normalized-midpoint misty-state syntax, we define dyadic program states and the real cyclotomic tower $K_D=\mathbb Q(2\cos(π/2^D))$. For the projective single-qubit Clifford-cyclotomic tower $\widehat G_D\subseteq SO(3)$, we prove the relative intersection law $\widehat G_D\cap SO(3,K_d)=\widehat G_d$ for $d\le D$. Combining this descent theorem with an arithmetic-closure invariant for adaptive stabilizer processing yields $Fdepth(U)=Adepth(U)=Rdepth(U)$: the smallest real cyclotomic field containing the Bloch matrix of an exactly synthesizable channel, the shallowest dyadic gate alphabet realizing it up to global phase, and the shallowest dyadic program-state layer sufficient for exact stabilizer-assisted implementation coincide. At that common minimum, known fixed-alphabet synthesis gives minimum primitive-rotation count. We package the result as an exact static resource effect and outline a certificate-producing compiler.

Conformal field theory from automorphic forms on a quantum group

Dalimil Mazac

We reformulate two-dimensional conformal field theory (2d CFT) in the language of noncommutative geometry. To this end, we introduce automorphic forms for the von Neumann algebraic quantum group $SL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. A quantum measure space is a von Neumann algebra equipped with a weight. We study such spaces carrying a weight-preserving action of $SL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. We argue that every unitary bosonic 2d CFT with central charge $c=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2>1$ is equivalent to a quantum measure space with an action of $SL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})\timesSL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. In this dictionary, Virasoro primary operators are quantum automorphic forms and their scaling dimensions correspond to the spectrum of a quantum Laplace operator. The Virasoro crossing equations express associativity of multiplication on the quantum measure space. For compact theories, the algebra is a type III$_1$ factor. Liouville CFT is realized as $SL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$ acting on itself by left and right translations. Virasoro boundary conditions correspond to quantum measure spaces with an action of a single copy of $SL^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. When the boundary spectrum is discrete, these are noncommutative counterparts of compact hyperbolic surfaces. The formulation accommodates models both with and without extended chiral algebras. It bridges the Virasoro conformal bootstrap with categorical and von Neumann algebraic approaches to 2d CFT.

Gravitational-Wave Constraints on $R^2$ Gravity

Pawan Kumar Gupta, Laura Sagunski

Gravitational waves from compact binary coalescences provide a unique opportunity to test general relativity in the strong-field regime. Quadratic $f(R)=R+aR^2$ gravity is among the simplest higher-curvature extensions of general relativity and introduces a massive scalar degree of freedom whose finite interaction range can modify compact-binary dynamics and gravitational-wave emission. We map this theory onto the effective-field-theory waveform for generic massive scalar-tensor gravity, obtaining a theory-specific inspiral phase correction governed by the single coupling parameter $a$. Using Bayesian analyses of the binary-neutron-star events GW170817 and GW190425, we obtain a combined constraint on the quadratic-curvature coupling of $a\lesssim6.2\times10^6\,m^2$ at $90\%$ credibility, corresponding to a lower bound on the scalar mass of $m_s\gtrsim3.2\times10^{-11}\,eV$. This constitutes the strongest astrophysical constraint on the quadratic-curvature coupling, improving the previous best bound by approximately five orders of magnitude and demonstrating the sensitivity of binary-neutron-star inspirals to finite-range scalar interactions in the strong-field regime.

3D Printed Monolithic Optomechanical Chassis for an Unbalanced Field-Widened Interferometer

Ramy Tannous, Dogan Sinar, Sagar Patel, Tabitha D. Arulpragasam, Issa Rishmawi et al. (9 authors)

Additive manufacturing is increasingly being explored as an approach for producing complex structures for optical hardware. In this work, we present a fully monolithic optomechanical chassis fabricated using laser powder bed fusion additive manufacturing. The structure was fabricated from thermally stable Invar36, a low-thermal-expansion alloy that enables high dimensional stability under varying environmental conditions. Our monolithic design incorporates embedded optical mounts, flexures, and alignment features directly into the chassis geometry that exceed the practical limits of conventional subtractive manufacturing. By integrating the optomechanical support structure into a single compact unit, the system achieves a reduced footprint compared to traditional optomechanical assemblies. The results demonstrate the potential of additive manufacturing to produce lightweight, and highly customized optical systems, opening the possibility of new pathways for next-generation quantum sensors and optical instrumentation.

Probing gravity at megahertz frequencies with levitated rotors

Matteo Fadel, Andrea Vinante

The near-field gravitational interaction between masses is always thought of as instantaneous, an assumption experimentally tested only up to 1.7 kHz, where measurements yield a bounds at the $10^{-5}$ s level. We propose a near-field experiment reaching up to megahertz frequencies, in which a levitated microrotor modulates a mass quadrupole and a second rotor measures the resulting gravitational tidal field as a torque. Because the detector rotor acts as a mechanical local oscillator, the megahertz signal is downconverted to a convenient band of a few hertz, and since at fixed geometry neither the torque nor the integration time depends on the rotation rate, higher frequencies are reached at no cost in sensitivity. With parameters within reach of magnetically levitated NdFeB rotors, a signal at 2 MHz yields a torque of $2\cdot 10^{-25}$ Nm, detectable in under an hour at 10 mK. Treating a possible departure from instantaneous gravitational coupling through a field relaxation time, the measured torque could constrain it at the $10^{-8}$ s level, improving on the existing bounds by more than three orders of magnitude. As a byproduct of our analysis, we derive the sensitivity of a rotor operated as a tunable high-frequency gravitational-wave detector, finding that a noise-equivalent strain around $10^{-22}$ Hz$^{-1/2}$ could be achievable in the megahertz band.

Simulating a chiral gauge theory

Laurens Lootens, Hersh Singh

Chiral gauge theories underlie the Standard Model, yet none has been simulated nonperturbatively. Lattice constructions that evade the Nielsen-Ninomiya obstruction entail complex fermion measures or infinite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces. Here we provide a gauge-invariant Hamiltonian lattice regularization of 1+1-dimensional abelian chiral gauge theories built from clock-and-shift qudits with $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge fields, in which the global chiral symmetry and its 't Hooft anomaly survive exactly mod $N$. Anomaly cancellation on the lattice lets the Gauss laws be imposed and solved exactly, reducing the theory to an unconstrained qudit chain. Using infinite matrix product states, we simulate the canonical 1+1-dimensional chiral gauge theory, the 34-50 model, and find excellent agreement with analytic predictions, showing that a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space at $N=6$ reproduces the expected infrared physics of the continuum 34-50 model. This provides an efficient and practical solution to the problem of simulating general abelian chiral gauge theories in 1+1 dimensions, both on classical and quantum computers.

Axial black hole perturbations in purely-metric Horndeski theories

Héloïse Delaporte, Hugo Roussille

We study axial (odd-parity) perturbations about static and spherically-symmetric curvaturised black hole solutions of purely-metric Horndeski theories. We first review the construction of purely-metric theories of gravity with second-order equations of motion and an additional propagating scalar degree of freedom which is purely gravitational. We then revisit the formation, in a subclass of these theories, of a new branch of hairy black hole solutions under tachyonic instabilities of the scalar field near the Schwarzschild solution. Because a non-zero Ricci curvature grows and by analogy with black hole scalarisation, the latter black hole formation mechanism is dubbed curvaturisation. We then apply the first-order approach to the dynamics of axial perturbations and find that it is equivalent to that of axial perturbations in general relativity evolving in a distinct effective metric. We explicitly provide that effective metric in terms of the background and the Lagrangian functions, and show that it shares the same causality structure as the background metric of curvaturised black holes. By turning to the Schrödinger-like formulation of the perturbations, we analyse the scattering potential and conclude that the axial perturbations of curvaturised black holes are linearly stable. Finally, we confirm these findings by explicitly computing the fundamental quasi-normal mode as well as the first five overtones for the quadrupole $\ell = 2$.

Anomaly effective actions for 4d $\mathcal{N}=1$ superconformal field theories

Pantelis Panopoulos, Ioannis Papadimitriou

We present a comprehensive analysis of the Wess-Zumino anomaly effective action of four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=1$ superconformal field theories in components. After reviewing the coset construction of the Wess-Zumino action in the presence of an unbroken subgroup, the gauging of the conformal algebra and the Wess-Zumino effective action for non-supersymmetric conformal field theories with a U(1) symmetry in two and four dimensions, we revisit the superconformal anomalies of $\mathcal{N}=1$ conformal supergravity. Solving the Wess-Zumino consistency conditions we determine both the bosonic and fermionic superconformal anomalies in a two-parameter family of schemes, to quadratic order in the gravitino. Turning on a chiral compensator multiplet and identifying a local super-Poincaré subalgebra of the conformal supergravity algebra without gauge-fixing the coset symmetries, we employ the coset construction and the superconformal anomalies in order to evaluate the Wess-Zumino action for generic $\mathcal{N}=1$ superconformal field theories coupled to conformal supergravity. By construction, this action reproduces the Weyl, R-symmetry, and S-supersymmetry coset anomalies, but its transformation under the unbroken Poincaré supersymmetry differs from the corresponding anomaly of the conformal supergravity functional. We show that there exists a local functional of the coset-invariant old-minimal fields that, when added to the Wess-Zumino action, results in a generalized Wess-Zumino action that reproduces all anomalies of the conformal supergravity functional. The same local functional enables us to determine the super-Weyl anomalies and the Wess-Zumino action of old-minimal supergravity, providing a component version of the known superspace Wess-Zumino action.

Optimal Holding Times for Entanglement Swapping

Van Sy Mai, Richard J. La, Abdella Battou

We revisit the optimization of entanglement holding times in memory-based quantum repeaters for maximizing entanglement swapping throughput under different fidelity constraints - strict, probabilistic, and mean. We consider two heterogeneous quantum links that are equipped with single or multiple memories and differ in decoherence rates, memory capacities, entanglement attempt rates and success probabilities, as well as classical communication latencies. Efficient models are developed to estimate the expected entanglement throughput and the probability density functions of the conditional wait time to swap. The models are then used to satisfy different fidelity requirements by optimizing the maximum holding times for the two links. Finally, our proposed model and approach are validated with a discrete-event quantum network simulator.
