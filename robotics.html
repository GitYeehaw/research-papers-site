Task2Hand: A Contact-Grounded Skill for Parametric Dexterous-Hand Morphology Design

Haoyang Wang, Weibo Gao, Zhengxiong Li, Lingfeng Tao

Dexterous hand design demands repeated expert work across geometry, robot-description code, collision debugging, grasp initialization, and policy training. We present Task2Hand, a Skill: a specification package that turns a functional manipulation brief into a parameterized hand model and routes every candidate through cost-ordered contact, dynamics, and learning gates. Each gate returns a status and an evidence record; on failure, the Skill records the earliest blocker, revises one bounded parameter group, and reruns the cheaper gates. The agent generates, validates, and trains autonomously within declared bounds, and escalates trade-offs, plateaus, and conflicting evidence to a human who sets the next design direction. An evidence-driven design trajectory for bidirectional in-hand manipulation yielded SAGE Hand (Skill-Aware Generative Embodiment): four fingers, 16 actuated DoF, an integrated guiding palm, compact roots, cylindrical tips, and a collision-cleared opposable thumb. Under a matched training protocol, SAGE Hand reaches 45.6% task success on a hexagonal prism against 37.3% for the LEAP hand, and 70.8% on a bicolor cylinder against 51.6%. Dense simulated tactile input more than halves the cylinder drop rate. In a controlled iteration study, the Skill path preserves capture at 164/165 trials while the path without the Skill degrades to 14/165, confirming that the gates act as a regression guard during refinement. Accepted candidates pack with URDF/MJCF, metrics, checkpoints, and a provenance manifest.

Load-Following Dynamics of an SMR-Powered All-Electric Ship with Battery Energy Storage

Ali Mahboub Rad, Jie Zhang

Small modular reactors (SMRs) are being considered as compact, high-energy-density power sources for future all-electric ships. In such systems, propulsion-load variations can propagate through the electrical network, turbine-generator set, steam generator, and reactor, producing coupled nuclear, thermal, mechanical, and electrical transients. This paper presents an integrated dynamic load-following analysis of an SMR-powered all-electric ship with and without battery energy storage system (BESS) support. The model couples an integral pressurized water reactor, a once-through moving-boundary steam generator, a simplified turbine model, a synchronous generator, an AC shipboard electrical network, an asynchronous propulsion motor, and a grid-connected BESS. A propulsion-load reduction is applied through the motor torque command, and the responses of the SMR-alone and SMR+BESS configurations are compared. The results show that, without BESS support, the sudden decrease in propulsion motor power creates a temporary surplus between turbine mechanical power and electrical demand, causing a large over-frequency transient and rapid steam-cycle adjustment. With BESS support, the battery absorbs excess electrical power during the transient, reducing the frequency peak from approximately 61.1 Hz to about 60.06 Hz. The BESS also indirectly smooths the upstream steam-cycle and reactor responses, including steam flow, steam-generator pressure, feedwater flow, coolant temperature, reactivity, reactor thermal power, and steam-generator region lengths. These results demonstrate the importance of modeling nuclear-powered electric ships as coupled multi-domain systems and show that BESS integration can improve short-term load-following performance.

Real-time neuromorphic audio and vision perception for autonomous robotic applications: Integration and experimental evaluation on a rover platform

Guillem Fustier-Castillo, Ruben Ribes-Serrano, Javier Naranjo-Alcazar, Vicent Ortiz-Castello, Pedro Zuccarello

Neuromorphic sensing and computing offer a promising alternative to conventional deep learning pipelines for mobile robots, where perception must run continuously under tight latency and energy constraints. However, most neuromorphic solutions are still evaluated offline or on isolated benchmarks, and their integration into complete robotic systems remains largely unexplored. In this technical report, a four-wheel rover platform is presented that combines three real-time perception pipelines: a classical audio tagging pipeline based on Pretrained Audio Neural Networks (PANNs) running on an NVIDIA Jetson GPU, a neuromorphic audio pipeline in which a spiking neural network (SNN) is deployed on the SynSense Xylo Audio~2 chip, and a neuromorphic vision pipeline in which events from an event-based camera are processed by the SpikeYOLO spiking detector for traffic cone detection. On the Xylo chip, the whole processing chain---from the microphone signal to the classification of low- and high-pitched sounds---is performed on-chip, while the host only configures the device and reads the output neurons. All models are deployed as containerised services and integrated through ROS~2, and a configurable action dispatcher maps the detected classes to robot behaviours, closing the loop between perception and actuation. The platform is demonstrated in a proof-of-concept scenario in which the robot reacts to sound classes detected by both audio pipelines and detects traffic cones with the event camera, showing the feasibility of integrating neuromorphic hardware into a multimodal robotic platform operating in real time.

What Must a Model Tell a Planner? Outcome Interfaces for Risk-Aware Planning After Robot Damage

Hassan Iqbal, Krishna Kumar

When a robot is damaged, it must decide which routes it can still afford, and the amount of risk it may accept can change from one mission to the next. We argue that whether a learned model helps such a robot depends on its decision interface, that is, on what the model hands to the planner. A decision policy tells the planner what to do, whereas an outcome model, a kind of world model, predicts the consequences of each route. We study simulated Unitree Go2 quadrupeds and H1 humanoids with weakened motors, added payloads, or reduced foot friction. We compare four interfaces. The first is a decision policy, and the second is the same policy trained with fixed penalties for infeasible routes. The third is an outcome model that predicts the feasibility and travel time of each route. The fourth, which we call a capability contract, is an outcome model that predicts the chance of success and the travel time of the robot on each terrain and composes them along a route. All share the same collected experience, matched tuning and risk calibration, and an uncertainty guard. Two comparatively low-risk probes let the capability contract rank hazardous terrains about as well as four new trials on every hazard. At a calibrated 5% limit on selecting empirically infeasible routes, outcome models cut declined missions from about 32% to 7-8% on Go2 and from 57% to 35-36% on H1 compared with the plain policy. Fixed penalties narrow this gap, but the best penalty changes with the risk target. Predicting outcomes lets the planner preserve missions by changing routes as risk requirements tighten.

Interactive Semi-Parametric On-Track Learning for Vehicle Dynamics in Autonomous Racing

Jungbin Kim, Youngim Nam, Cheolhyeon Kwon

Autonomous racing requires an accurate vehicle dynamics model near the limits of handling, yet a mismatch between the nominal and actual dynamics inevitably remains. To reduce this mismatch, existing methods learn from on-track data, i.e., data collected while the vehicle drives on the track under racing conditions, by adapting the physical parameters of the nominal model or by learning a residual that captures the remaining mismatch. However, these on-track learning methods typically handle either the physical parameters or the residual separately, which restricts their ability to resolve the mismatch. To address this limitation, we propose an interactive semi-parametric learning framework that learns both the physical parameters and the residual under a single shared prediction-error objective. Since updating one changes the learning target of the other, they are recursively updated in turn, each guided by the other's latest update. Each update is then accepted only if it does not increase the shared objective, so that the objective is guaranteed to be non-increasing and convergent over the recursion. Numerical simulations and hardware experiments show that the proposed framework outperforms the baselines. Compared with the nominal model, it reduces the one-step prediction errors of lateral velocity and yaw rate by 90.0% and 81.6% in simulation and by 54.0% and 59.4% on hardware.

StereoVLM: Binocular Vision-Language Models for Metric 3D Spatial Reasoning

Chi Yan, Guoyang Zhao, Wentao Yuan, Yixiao Ge, Yanning Zhou et al. (6 authors)

Vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit strong semantic understanding but remain limited in their ability to reason about metric 3D space in the physical world. Existing VLMs typically infer 3D scene structure from one or more images without known camera calibration or cross-view geometric constraints. This makes metric 3D inference inherently ambiguous and leaves it unclear whether their spatial predictions are grounded in visual evidence or driven primarily by semantic priors. In contrast, calibrated stereo provides explicit cross-view geometry that resolves this ambiguity and offers a more reliable foundation for metric 3D perception and geometric generalization. We investigate how VLMs can learn transferable 3D representations directly from calibrated binocular observations. To this end, we introduce StereoVLM, the first geometry-aware binocular VLM for metric 3D spatial reasoning. StereoVLM follows a hierarchical learning paradigm spanning from cross-view correspondence and disparity estimation to calibration-conditioned metric depth perception and semantic spatial reasoning. We further find that disparity supervision generalizes substantially better than direct binocular depth supervision on unseen environments. To evaluate these capabilities, we introduce Stereo3D-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark across three levels, from point-level geometry to object-level spatial reasoning. StereoVLM-8B achieves 95.17% depth accuracy ($δ_1$) and a 66.98 average object-level score on Stereo3D-Bench. Intervention results further indicate that the model's performance on Stereo3D-Bench benefits from valid cross-view geometry and cannot be explained solely by additional visual tokens or semantic priors. Hierarchical geometric supervision also yields average gains of 3.54 points across five spatial benchmarks and 4.3 percentage points in RoboCasa manipulation success.

Bayesian Bias Learning-Enabled Probabilistic Voltage Estimation with Unknown Uncertainty Modeling of DERs in Distribution Grids

Yuanshuo Zhang, Ying Zhang, Fei Ding, Jiahao Chen

One key challenge in distribution system state es- timation is unknown input statistics from the growing inte- gration of distributed energy resources (DERs), which may largely deviate from Gaussian uncertainty. Existing model-based estimators often rely on the assumption that all inputs follow known Gaussian distributions, while data-driven methods suffer from overfitting and failure in out-of-distribution scenarios. This paper proposes a Bayesian bias learning (BBL)-enabled probabilistic voltage estimation method in distribution systems with unknown DER generation statistics. The proposed learning- enabled method consists of two stages: probabilistic uncertainty inference and computationally efficient voltage estimation. First, a physics-constrained probabilistic density inference network is developed to learn Gaussian mixture representations of DER generation from historical data. Then, the resulting probabilistic representations, treated as pseudo-measurements, are integrated into a BBL-enabled voltage estimator via a decomposition- and-parallelization mechanism. Furthermore, utilizing a linear analytical solution, the proposed BBL-enabled voltage estimator efficiently captures linearization-induced biases and attains ac- curate probabilistic estimation. By integrating learning-enabled probabilistic uncertainty modeling into the parallel estimation structure, the proposed method achieves uncertainty-aware scal- able voltage estimation. Case studies on two unbalanced IEEE distribution networks show that the proposed estimator achieves significantly higher accuracy and computational efficiency under unknown uncertainty, outperforming recent data-driven and model-based methods.

Humanoid robots for safety-critical electrical switching: A perception-informed runtime constraint filter

Songyang Liu, Yu Hou, Yuqing Hu, Shuai Li

Humanoid robots can reduce personnel exposure in electrical switching, but safe control is challenging because the tool must reach a target switch next to similar devices, where an imprecise operator command can contact a non-target switch. This study proposes a runtime safety filter that checks each operator command during virtual-reality teleoperation of a humanoid robot. The filter integrates a scene model estimated from the robot head camera and a target-conditioned control barrier function quadratic program that keeps the tool away from the non-target switch while passing safe commands unchanged. In real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, with six rounds at each of two standing positions, target operations succeed in all ten rounds with an unchanged target and no non-target actuation. A simulation study achieves 9/9 safe completions, compared with 5/9 for raw teleoperation, demonstrating the feasibility of the method for humanoid switching operations.

BRINA: Bottom-Referenced Inertial Navigation with AKAZE, a Sonar SLAM for Autonomous Marine Vehicles

Adolfo Damiano Cafaro, Patrizio Mariani, Roberto Galeazzi

Forward-looking sonar (FLS) provides reliable underwater perception where optical sensing is limited, but its use for navigation is hindered by the unobserved elevation of each return. As a result, motion estimated directly from sonar images suffers from an unknown metric scale that is commonly resolved using a Doppler velocity log (DVL). We present BRINA (Bottom-Referenced Inertial Navigation with AKAZE), a sonar-inertial SLAM system for marine vehicles that uses the FLS as its only exteroceptive sensor. BRINA estimates the sonar altitude from the first seabed return in each beam and uses it to project matched features onto the seabed, providing metric odometry without a DVL. We further derive a bound relating the remaining scale error to seabed tilt and slope variability. The method is evaluated in simulation with an autonomous underwater vehicle, and in field experiments with uncrewed surface vehicles over ten missions, using two FLS systems under different operating conditions.

Generalist Action Critic: Harnessing Frontier Models in Autonomous Driving

Yun Yan, Shizhi Hu, Zhichao Li, Yang Guan, Shiqi Liu et al. (13 authors)

Frontier models have demonstrated remarkable general-purpose reasoning capabilities, opening new possibilities for intelligent decision making in the physical world. In autonomous driving, many recent approaches seek to leverage these capabilities through additional adaptation for action output, particularly in scenarios where existing models underperform. We introduce Generalist Action Critic (GAC), a zero-shot framework that combines frontier models' general-purpose reasoning with the planning competence of specialized driving models through an inference-time harness. In GAC, specialized planners generate multiple candidate trajectories and make an initial selection, while a frozen frontier model serves as an action critic that assesses the selected trajectory against scene evidence and either retains it or reselects from the available candidates. The harness combines camera images with spatial overlays, provides driving context and reasoning principles through textual instructions, and regulates the information available at each stage to support trajectory assessment and reselection. On HUGSIM, GAC raises DrivoR's HD-Score to 43.2, achieving state-of-the-art performance among methods trained without additional simulation data. GAC also consistently improves closed-loop driving safety across three planners, with relative HD-Score gains of up to 32.1%. Experiments on NAVSIM v1 further reveal how GAC improves planning performance by correcting up to 52.13% of eligible unsafe trajectory selections while introducing new failures in only 0.67% of originally high-quality selections. These results demonstrate the potential of harnessing frontier models to improve driving safety through inference-time cooperation with specialized planners.
