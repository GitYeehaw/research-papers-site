Two messages suffice for quantum interactive proof systems

Yupan Liu

Quantum interactive proof systems can be parallelized to three messages, as shown by Kitaev and Watrous (STOC 2000), perhaps their most striking distinction from classical counterparts. In this work, we establish that any quantum interactive proof system can further be parallelized to two messages, with the verifier sending halves of EPR pairs (``quantum public coins'') and, after the prover's response, using only stoquastic verification. Technically, we strengthen ${\sf QIP}(3) = {\sf QIP} = {\sf PSPACE}$ of Jain, Ji, Upadhyay, and Watrous (JACM, 2011) and fully characterize two-message quantum interactive proof systems by proving \[ {\rm qq}-{\sf StoqAM} = {\rm qq}-{\sf QAM} = {\sf QIP}(2) = {\sf QIP} = {\sf PSPACE}. \] Our main technical contributions show that Close Image to Uniform (CIUniform), which is naturally in the stoquastic version of ${\rm qq}-{\sf QAM}$, is $\sf PSPACE$-hard via transforming the sum-check protocol of Shamir (JACM, 1992) and Shen (JACM, 1992) into a CIUniform instance consisting of an efficient sampler whose image distribution is exactly uniform for some input distribution when the underlying quantified Boolean formula is true.

Nonabelianization map for the $W_{3,c=2}$ conformal blocks. Part I

Qianyu Hao

This work is inspired by the nonabelianization construction of Virasoro conformal blocks. We start the process of defining the nonabelianization map which expresses $c=2$ $W_3$-algebra conformal blocks on a Riemann surface $C$ from Heisenberg blocks on a branched triple cover $\tilde{C}$ of $C$. This generalized nonabelianization map uses the datum of a $\mathfrak{gl}_3$ spectral network on $C$ and we find that this map also depends on the soliton degeneracies corresponding to the spectral network even though the soliton data didn't show up explicitly in the Virasoro case. This nonabelianization construction defines new formulas for $W_3$-algebra conformal blocks on $C$ in terms of fermion correlation functions determined by the Heisenberg blocks on $\tilde{C}$. We provide a check of the proposed construction associated to the more challenging $(123)$ type branch points which start to show up in three-fold covers. To be more precise, the simplest example that we focus on has only two of the $(123)$ type branch points and we show that in this example, our nonabelianization construction gives the desired two-point correlator and $τ$-function.

Simple design of adaptive protocols for quantum metrology with limited data

Ricard Ravell Rodríguez, Jesús Rubio

Recent sensing experiments have used a Bayesian notion of precision gain to adapt control parameters for fixed measurements. Here, we advocate a simple design principle based on Personick's minimum mean square loss formalism to extend this procedure in order to include measurement optimisation, determining the optimal measurement for each probe and providing a prior-dependent precision gain to optimise the probe itself. This offers a Bayesian counterpart to protocol design based on the Fisher information, with a similar algebraic structure but incorporating the full prior. We apply this principle to adaptive phase estimation with single-mode Gaussian probes, showing that joint optimisation outperforms both adaptive homodyne detection and a fixed-probe strategy optimised for the initial prior, with a maximum reduction of 32% in mean posterior variance relative to homodyne detection. Our results illustrate the potential of simple Bayesian design for quantum metrology with limited data when measurement bases can be controlled.

Small-world networks yield tunable quantum codes at short circuit depth

Samuel Bevins, Gregory Bentsen

Monitored random quantum circuits naturally generate quantum error-correcting codes. We generate quantum codes with tunable properties by implementing random Clifford circuits on Watts--Strogatz `small-world' networks and tailor code properties via three knobs: nonlocal graph rewiring, measurement rate, and circuit depth. On fully rewired networks, circuits of logarithmic depth produce codes with a finite rate and an extensive mean contiguous distance with failure probability substantially lower than codes produced from local circuits. These small-world codes form at a circuit depth growing as $\ln n$ compared to polynomial depths required for codes produced from local networks. Rewiring also moves the purification transition to higher measurement rates and changes its critical exponents. Connectivity is thus a knob for designing codes.

Massive de Sitter correlators at one loop

Amara McCune

Fermions and massive fields charged under an unbroken symmetry couple to the inflaton only in pairs, so their signals in primordial correlators begin at one loop. Templates for these signals are mostly late-time estimates. Starting from a representation of massive de Sitter correlators as finite Mellin-Barnes integrals, we compute the exact squeezed coefficients of these signals and their corrections away from the squeezed limit. The leading coefficient factorizes into a hard part and a soft vertex. Late-time estimates treat the hard part exactly but approximate the soft vertex by its late-time limit, whereas we compute both exactly. Without a chemical potential, the exact coefficient differs from the late-time one by a universal factor that depends only on the masses in the loop and the coupling at the soft vertex. For the heavy Standard Model loops, the exact signals are ten to thirty times larger than their late-time estimates. With a chemical potential, the ratio depends on the model, and at our benchmarks it reaches a factor of thirty for helical vectors and about a hundred for the fermion triangle, where we agree with recent exact results. Away from the squeezed limit the leading term soon fails, mainly through a drift in the phase of the oscillation, which we derive for scalar and fermion loops.

Advancing the Theory of Pretty Good Quantum Operations

Bikun Li, Guo Zheng, Liang Jiang

Finding optimal quantum measurements or recovery maps is often demanding. Pretty good measurements and Petz maps offer explicit approximations achieving at least the square of optimal performance by the Barnum-Knill bound. We unify these operations in a semidefinite programming framework with a tunable power, extending them to broader quantum tasks. We prove a stronger bound for powers above one up to three that peaks at quadratic power. For structured channels such as stabilizer codes under Pauli noise, large powers steer recovery to a maximum likelihood decoder for maximally mixed inputs.

On the existence of constants of motion for multipolar test-body dynamics

Paul Ramond

We show that the conservative equations of motion of an extended test body, with force and torque sourced by spin-induced and adiabatic tidal multipoles of arbitrary order, are the flow of the Hamiltonian $H=-\tfrac12μ^2+μU$, where $μ$ is the body's kinematic mass and all multipoles enter through a covariant scalar potential $U$. For such bodies, a certain notion of mass and a spin norm are shown to be exactly conserved, at all multipolar orders and in any background. We then apply our framework to the Kerr spacetime, and construct extensions of the Carter and Rüdiger constants through hexadecapolar order (quartic in spin). They are found to exist if, and only if, the body's multipoles are those of a Kerr black hole. Together with the energy and angular momentum, they make the dynamics of a spinning test black hole in Kerr perturbatively integrable through that order. Lastly, at the next, quintic-in-spin order, an analytic criterion implemented numerically reveals that neither constant admits a continuation, for any conservative multipoles. In the conservative, test-body limit, black-hole binaries thus carry a dynamical hidden symmetry shared by no other compact objects, which persists to high multipolar order but ultimately breaks.

Optimal Statistic for a Stochastic Gravitational Wave Background using Pulsar-Timing--Astrometry Cross Correlations

Kimberly K. Boddy, Jeffrey S. Hazboun, Kris Pardo

Astrometric measurements have emerged as a viable probe of gravitational waves (GWs), complementing pulsar timing searches by providing overlapping and higher GW frequency coverage. At its core, the search for GWs using astrometry is very similar to the search with pulsar timing data: GWs induce a change in pulse arrival times along the line of sight to a pulsar and a transverse angular deflection in the apparent position of a star. In analogy to pulsar timing, we develop a correlation statistic between pairs of stellar deflections for astrometry to search for a stochastic GW background. With a common formalism for both astrometry and pulsar timing, we also develop the cross-correlation statistic between stellar deflections and pulsar timing residuals. We estimate the signal-to-noise ratios from the correlation statistics and provide scaling laws in the weak- and intermediate-signal regimes.

Tensionless M5-brane in light-cone gauge. The spinor moving frame method is wanted

Igor Bandos

We perform the complete light-cone gauge fixing for the tensionless limit of the M-theory 5-brane the action for which was recently obtained in [1]. The problem of gauge fixing of local fermionic $κ$-symmetry becomes quite nontrivial because the projector of that is constructed from the gauge field strength which in its turn is required to be nonvanaishing and nontrivial in this limit. To resolve this problem the spinor moving frame method has been adapted. The corresponding moving frame is constructed on a vector quadratic in the gauge field strength, which is required to be nonvanishing and nondegenerate in the tensionless limit, and the derivative of the PST (Pasti-Sorokin-Tonin) scalar. The symmetries of the gauge fixed action and equations of motion are briefly discussed. To make a comparison with the case of usual tensionless p-branes (null-p-branes), those which do not carry worldvolume gauge fields, we also elaborate their spinor moving frame formulation.

Self-guided estimation of the stabilizer fidelity with single-copy Pauli measurements

M. Torres, J. Gidi, L. Pereira

The stabilizer fidelity quantifies the non-stabilizerness of pure states, but it is hard to estimate because it requires optimizing over $2^{O(n^2)}$ stabilizer states for $n$ qubits. Here, we propose a self-guided estimation protocol for the stabilizer fidelity of an unknown state, pure or mixed, which requires only Pauli measurements on single copies. The protocol moves through the graph of neighboring stabilizer states, obtains the fidelities of a whole stabilizer basis from a single direct fidelity estimation, and reuses previous outcomes, which also provide a restart point that mitigates the barren plateau of a random start. In addition, it certifies the global optimum when the lower confidence bound of the estimated fidelity exceeds $\cos^2(π/8)$. In numerical simulations of up to 14 qubits, the protocol reached the optimum in all runs, with the number of iterations remaining small and increasing only slightly with $n$.
