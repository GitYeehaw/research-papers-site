A Central Limit Theorem for the Driver Site Frequency Spectrum

Armaan Ahmed, Jasmine Foo, Kevin Leder

We consider a supercritical two-type continuous-time linear birth-death process with mutation and selection, in which wild-type individuals give rise to mutant offspring with a larger net-growth rate. We previously had obtained first-order law of large number results for the driver site frequency spectrum. We return to obtain a second-order central limit theorem, in which we show stable convergence of a scaling of the deviation of the SFS from its first-order limit. We provide a central limit theorem at both large detection times and sizes. Then, we apply these results to obtain confidence intervals for a class of consistent estimators for the fitness advance.

ZENO: Nonlinear filtering with Zakai Equation Neural Operators

Aaditya Prasad, Steven W. Flavell, Brian DePasquale

State inference and parameter learning for nonlinear filters is a core computation for scientific machine learning with varied applications in engineering and neuroscience. The unnormalized filtering distribution is governed by the Zakai equation, a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) that combines the forward Kolmogorov operator (known as the Fokker-Planck equation) with observed likelihood updates. Although solving these high-dimensional SPDEs is often impossible analytically and intractable numerically, advances in operator learning and physics-informed machine learning have made it feasible. Harnessing these advances, we introduce the Zakai Equation Neural Operator (ZENO), a model that optimizes a neural operator against a Zakai Equation based physics-informed loss to approximate the exact unnormalized filtering distribution for latent stochastic dynamical systems. We use this ZENO-inferred posterior distribution in an expectation-maximization (EM) framework to perform parameter learning and state inference. We demonstrate ZENO's ability to accurately infer the posterior distribution and learn its corresponding SDE parameters in three simulated stochastic dynamical systems (Double-Well bistable system, Van Der Pol (VDP) oscillator, and Lorenz system) and compare its performance against established methods for latent SDE inference and learning. We show that ZENO recovers previously observed limit cycles representing forward and backward locomotion from a whole-brain imaging dataset of immobilized C. elegans nematodes. ZENO is a novel approach to the problem of nonlinear filtering and dynamical inference through the lens of partial differential equations and operator theory.

Computational De Novo Design of Miniprotein Candidate Neutralizers Targeting the IL-23 Cytokine Subunit p19

Raghav Paladugu, Fardina Fathmiul Alam

Interleukin-23 (IL-23) drives T-helper-17 inflammation underlying inflammatory bowel disease and psoriasis; anti-p19 antibodies are clinically effective but large, costly, and injectable. De novo miniproteins are an attractive alternative, yet published designs target the IL-23 receptor; the IL-23 cytokine itself has not been addressed. A further obstacle is that the standard confidence score (ipTM) tracks construct length rather than interface quality. We computationally design miniprotein binders against the IL-23R-binding face of p19 (PDB 3DUH) and score every design with ipSAE, a length-robust interface metric derived from AlphaFold-initial-guess predicted aligned error. A conventional RFdiffusion/ProteinMPNN/ColabFold pipeline yields no reliable predicted interface across 972 designs (best ipSAE 0.30), and a scoring artifact, in which single-sequence ColabFold under-rates binder pLDDT by approximately 56 points, explains an earlier misleading "binders do not fold" signal. An interface-optimized hallucination method (BindCraft), scored identically, yields 42 of 50 designs above ipSAE 0.60 (best 0.804). An AlphaFold-independent geometric check reproduces the conventional failure: no top-ranked conventional design reaches the buried area or interface hydrogen-bond count of the weakest lead. Five all-alpha-helical leads (72-104 residues) collectively engage all six targeted hotspots; two dock within the intact p40/p19 heterodimer and all show favorable Rosetta energetics. A p40-alone counter-screen returns ipSAE 0.00 for all five, indicating no spurious p40 interface. To our knowledge, these are the first computationally proposed de novo miniprotein candidates designed to occlude the IL-23R-binding face of IL-23 p19. All results require experimental validation.

What Transfers Across Protein Pockets? Experience Reuse in Molecular Optimization for Agentic Structure-Based Drug Design

Zaifei Yang, Weiyu Chen, Yaqing Wang, James Kwok

LLM agents optimize a ligand in a protein pocket by editing it step by step and docking each edit. Recent agents first spend extra dockings probing the complex with controlled edits; guidance induced from these probes is reported to help more than the chemical knowledge the model already carries. The probes are then discarded with the instance that produced them, so the obvious next step is to keep them in an experience base, letting a new pocket inherit what earlier ones already paid for. Such a base is read through a key: the record properties under which past edits are pooled and a new one is looked up. Using the full log of one such run over the CrossDocked2020 test-set pockets, we ask whether its keys recur on a pocket the base has never seen and still separate improving from worsening edits. \crchange{Using} the contacts at the site being edited, the action, and the round as \crchange{a} key, the base separates them sharply. Within our analysis, this separation is largely explained by how much the edit changed the molecule's size, the action, and the round, none of which describes the target. That is not because the target is irrelevant: naming it outright separates them several times more sharply. For a pocket absent from the base, transfer must rely on a description rather than its identity. Among the exact-match descriptions we evaluate, the more specific ones rarely recur, whereas the coarser ones provide little separation; the signals that do transfer primarily describe the edit and the molecule it produced rather than the target. Under the representations and lookup scheme studied here, an experience base may be more useful for prioritizing an agent's own proposals for docking than for directly prescribing what to build.

Earth embeddings are decomposable records of the land surface

Robin Young, Srinivasan Keshav

Ecological monitoring, carbon accounting and biodiversity assessment increasingly rely on annual Earth embeddings--learned vectors that compress a year of satellite observation for every ten-meter pixel on Earth, consumed in place of the imagery itself. What these vectors mean has not been established. Here we decompose two widely used embedding products and show that they contain individual ecological features, including fire, forest clearing and crop type, validated against independent reference data on events across six continents. The recovered concepts date abrupt canopy loss to the first post-disturbance growing season and resolve severity well enough to separate prescribed fire from wildfire. Small fires that recover quickly are detectable in one product but not the other, which instead separates crops more strongly.

Estimation of the Cyclopean Eye from Binocular Free Visual Explorations

Maria F. Alcala-Durand, J. Camilo Puerta-Acevedo, Julián D. Arias-Londoño, Elisa Luque-Buzo, Francisco J. Grandas-Pérez et al. (6 authors)

Binocular video-oculography records separate signals for each eye, yet human visual processing and oculomotor control operate relative to a single, virtual cyclopean eye. This study addresses the estimation of the cyclopean eye during binocular free visual exploration, a target-free paradigm widely used for the screening of neurological disorders. Using eye-tracking data collected at 1 kHz from 113 participants across three cohorts (patients with Parkinson's Disease, age-matched healthy controls, and young controls), we compare two different methods: one based on an Independent Component Analysis and another based on Factor Analysis. The extracted cyclopean signals were evaluated on their ability to preserve clinically critical fixation and saccadic events using both velocity-threshold and pixel-dispersion event detectors, and were validated against references via Dynamic Time Warping. Results suggest that the technique based on Factor Analysis guarantees full identifiability across all subjects and achieves low alignment errors, performing comparably to the traditional Version Signal (point-wise mean) while dynamically adapting to inter-eye asymmetry. Furthermore, the second factor in this technique was empirically null across all fitted models, confirming that a single latent factor fully accounts for the shared binocular gaze dynamics. These findings establish Factor Analysis as an identifiable framework for estimating the cyclopean eye in target-free visual exploration protocols.

Stochastic predator-prey compartment model with cyclic and non-cyclic transition pathways and mortality

Thomas M. Michelitsch, Ayten D. Baher, Michael Bestehorn, Andrzej F. Nowakowski, Alejandro P. Riascos et al. (6 authors)

We study variants of a stochastic SEIRS-SEID compartmental model incorporating presymptomatic infectiousness and disease-related mortality, with independent compartmental sojourn times drawn from arbitrary distributions, in particular Weibull distributions. We consider a large population of agents that occupy the states (compartments) S (susceptible to infection), E (exposed: infected but asymptomatic during the latency period and already infectious, including diseases with presymptomatic infections), I (infected and infectious, with symptoms), R (recovered, immune, and non-infectious), or D (dead). We consider (i) cyclic transmission pathways, where compartmental sojourn times are finite and immunity is temporary, and (ii) non-cyclic pathways, where immunity is permanent. In the absence of mortality, cyclic transmission pathways support an endemic equilibrium above the spreading threshold which is either globally stable or oscillatory (Hopf) unstable, whereas for non-cyclic pathways a stationary disease-free state is emerging. From a stochastic perspective, we elucidate the connection between compartmental sojourn-time distributions and the appearance of fractional operators in the evolution equations: fat-tailed compartment waiting-time distributions necessarily lead to fractional derivatives. For cyclic transition pathways we obtain an explicit expression for the expected number of infection cycles that an agent survives. Our model is validated by a combined Monte Carlo -- Runge -- Kutta simulation scheme and random-walk simulations on Barabasi-Albert networks. It has cross-disciplinary applications beyond epidemiology, including wildfire spreading, certain classes of chemical reactions, population dynamics, finance, economics, and biology.

From Heterogeneity to Function: Enhanced Modular Dynamic Range in Hierarchical Brain Networks

Filipe V. Torres, Mauro Copelli, Leonardo L. Gollo

Hierarchical and modular organization defines brain architecture, yet how it supports flexible computation and functional specialization remains unclear. Although dynamic range is a hallmark of efficient information processing, its distribution across modules and hierarchical levels remains largely unexplored. Here, we show that hierarchical-modular networks naturally generate heterogeneous modular dynamics and firing rates, extending regimes of critical-like dynamics with enhanced intrinsic timescales, reflecting critical slowing down, and diverse scaling exponents without fine-tuning near criticality. Intrinsic timescales increase across hierarchical levels, while their dispersion within levels emerges only when connectivity profiles and firing rates are heterogeneous. We introduce modular dynamic range to quantify how individual modules respond to external inputs and show that heterogeneity enhances both global and module-specific sensitivity, with modular intrinsic timescales increasing with dynamic range. Local stimulation reduces dynamic range in homogeneous networks but enhances it in heterogeneous networks relative to global stimulation. Moreover, selectively stimulating intermediate hierarchical levels maximizes both global and modular dynamic range, revealing a principle of functional specialization. In heterogeneous networks, modular dynamic range is more variable and increases even in unstimulated modules, demonstrating distributed computational benefits beyond directly stimulated regions. Together, these findings establish heterogeneity as a mechanism through which hierarchical-modular networks sustain critical-like dynamics, generate hierarchical timescales, and distribute computational sensitivity, providing a basis for functional specialization without fine-tuning to criticality.

Dynamics of a Harvested Predator-Prey Model with a Generalized Holling Type III Response Capturing Group Defense

Wael El Khateeb, Farjana Akter, Chanaka Kottegoda, Muner Mustafa Abou Hasan

We investigate a predator-prey model with constant prey harvesting and a generalized Holling type III functional response, focusing on the biological and dynamical consequences of allowing the parameter $b$ to be negative. In this regime, predation efficiency declines at high prey densities, potentially reflecting group defense. This change permits an additional coexistence equilibrium and introduces new bifurcation mechanisms affecting population persistence. We establish the existence of a codimension-three Bogdanov--Takens bifurcation, derive a codimension-three nilpotent-saddle unfolding, and investigate a codimension-two generalized Hopf bifurcation and the emergence of multiple limit cycles. Our analysis show that moderate harvesting weakens the protection conferred by high prey abundance; excessive harvesting leaves too few prey to sustain predator growth; and low harvesting preserves strong group defense, requiring high predator density for population persistence.

Phase-based Edge Coupling: Modeling Overlapping Functional Architectures and High-Order Edge-to-Edge Interactions in EEG Networks

Chengcheng Wu, Zichen Shi, Jiayang Xu, Shanbao Tong, Xiaoli Guo

Network neuroscience has traditionally relied on node-centric approaches, which are inherently limited in capturing the brain's overlapping functional architectures and higher-order edge-to-edge interactions. The emerging edge-centric paradigm addresses this gap by shifting the analytical unit from nodes to edges. However, while edge-centric models have been established for DTI and fMRI, comparable EEG methodologies have yet to be developed. In this study, we proposed a Phase-based Edge-centric Coupling (PEC) algorithm for EEG network modelling, where edge time series are derived from instantaneous phase differences of EEG signals and edge-to-edge coupling is quantified via normalized inner product. Simulation tests demonstrated the robustness of PEC algorithm, achieving high accuracy (<10% relative error) in coupling estimation and discriminative capacity (AUROC>0.7) against spurious coupling.When provided sufficient data length(>=16 cycles), PEC outperformed traditional Phase Node-centric Coupling (PNC) in both individual identifiability and cognitive-state identifiability, enabling superior characterization of stable individual neural signatures and dynamic task-dependent functional configurations. Furthermore, unlike PNC's hard-partitioned modularity, PEC captured highly overlapping yet functionally specialized modular structures and connector hubs, aligning with task-engaged cognitive processes. Applied to dyadic interactions, PEC identified distinct cross-brain modules encoding symmetric and asymmetric neural coordination. These findings establish PEC as a valid and powerful tool for EEG network modelling, providing new insights into the brain's complex, multifunctional organization at both intra-brain and inter-brain networks.
