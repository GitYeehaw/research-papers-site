Ricci flow of Integrable Sigma Models, Hitchin Systems and Quantum Seiberg-Witten theory

G. Bonelli, S. Lacroix, T. Pedroni, A. Tanzini

We construct a family of integrable 2d sigma models through 4d Chern-Simons theory and show that their classical point-particle reduction corresponds to a Hitchin system. The integrable Hitchin quantisation then provides a natural candidate Hamiltonian for the one-loop reduced dynamics of the sigma models, which coincides with the Perelman operator in all cases examined. This allows us to study the corresponding spectral problems using 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ gauge theory and 2d conformal field theory methods. For the sausage model, we derive exact quantisation conditions and compute exponentially small corrections essential for the agreement with numerical finite-volume results.

Half-Completeness from Standard Gravitational Dispersion Relations

Lukas W. Lindwasser, Laurentiu Rodina, Justinas Rumbutis

The completeness hypothesis states that quantum gravity realizes every representation allowed by its gauge group. Previous S-matrix arguments used the graviton pole to constrain exchanged representations, including a proof of completeness by imposing an additional high-energy bound on crossing-odd amplitudes. We investigate what follows without this assumption, using standard twice-subtracted dispersion relations, positivity, and crossing symmetry. For external massless SU(2) isospin $J$, we prove ``half-completeness'': either massive exchanges include every isospin $j\le J$, or at least one $j>J$ is required. At $J=1$, we construct an explicit amplitude with only $j=0,1$ exchanges that satisfies these conditions but violates the crossing-odd bound. This establishes that the additional high-energy assumption does not follow from the standard framework. For $J\ge2$, however, we show that lower-only spectra cannot be realized within a class of string-inspired Euler amplitudes, leaving their existence an open question. More generally, for scalar matter transforming under any compact internal symmetry group, we prove that massive singlets of unbounded Lorentz spin must be exchanged.

Plabic promotions beyond cluster: quantum cluster structures and the dual canonical basis

Jian-Rong Li, Michael Oren Perlstein, Ran J. Tessler

Plabic tangles define geometric promotion maps between configurations and, after choosing a lift, between Grassmannians. The main conjecture in \cite{Tangles} is that when the tangle is dominant and solvable the induced algebraic promotion is cluster quasi-homomorphism. This work extends the cluster perspective in two directions. First, we initiate the study of quantum promotions. We conjecture that all promotions defined from dominant solvable tangles are naturally lifted to a quantum cluster quasi homomorphism, and prove for all cases in the literature that the were shown to be (classical) cluster quasi-homomorphisms. Secondly, we define the notion of cluster-Lusztig quasi-homomorphisms, which are cluster quasi homomorphisms that take Lustig's dual canonical basis elements to dual canonical basis element, up to a Laurent monomial in frozen variables, with clearing and normalization chosen uniformly on each content component. We prove the unexpected result that the classical and quantum $m$-unary star promotions fall into this category, and conjecture that all promotions coming from dominant solvable tangles share the same property. We then consider promotions via non solvable cores of intersection number higher than one. We use it to study facets of amplituhedron cells, and to show that some of them do not lie in zero loci of dual canonical basis elements, disproving a conjecture by T. Lam \cite{LamGP}.

Onsager Algebras and Lattice Fermions

Ioanna Kourkoulou, David Tong

We study the exact Onsager algebra of free lattice fermions in staggered, Wilson, and overlap incarnations, working in both Hamiltonian and Euclidean formulations in four spacetime dimensions. In each case, we show that the exact lattice algebra emanates the full chiral symmetry of the low-energy theory: if there are $N$ massless Dirac fermions in the continuum, the Onsager algebra reduces to a $U(2N)$ chiral symmetry. The details of which generators are quantised or ultra-local differ between formulations. There is also a trade-off between the locality of generators and the maximum compact subgroup of the Onsager algebra.

On Born-Infeld-type higher-spin interactions and supersymmetry

Nikita Zaigraev

We develop a simple Lorentz-covariant construction of the Born--Infeld-type cubic interactions $(s, s_1, s_2)$ of massless higher-spin fields with $s + s_1 + s_2$ derivatives in four-dimensiona flat space. For arbitrary spins $s \ge s_1 \ge s_2$, the vertex is formulated in terms of a conserved higher-spin current and its trace. In contrast to the famous Berends--Burgers--van Dam currents, these currents are constructed from higher-spin Weyl tensors of a single chirality, schematically $J \sim \partial C \partial C$. Starting with a general ansatz, we derive recurrence relations fixing the current coefficients, identify the unique non-trivial conserved current after eliminating trivial (fake) vertices, and show that in the range $s \le s_1 + s_2$ the interaction admits a manifestly gauge-invariant representation in terms of higher-spin Weyl tensors. The construction is then extended to $\mathcal{N}=1$ and $\mathcal{N}=2$ supersymmetry using the corresponding off-shell superfield formalisms. In the $\mathcal{N}=1$ case, we obtain the general conserved integer superspin $s$ supercurrent with manifest supersymmetry, which is bilinear in the Weyl supertensors of superspins $Y_1$ and $Y_2$. For identical superfields, $Y_1 = Y_2$, this supercurrent vanishes for odd $s$ and is in agreement with earlier results by Gates and Koutrolikos. In the $\mathcal{N}=2$ case, using harmonic superspace, we construct the principal higher-spin supercurrent whose coefficients are uniquely fixed by the conservation equations. In contrast to the $\mathcal{N}=0,1$ cases, for identical $\mathcal{N}=2$ superfields, the corresponding vertex does not vanish. The results provide a systematic framework for Born--Infeld-type higher-spin interactions and their supersymmetric extensions.

What does quantum parameter estimation estimate? A no-go result for true quantity values

Jacques L. Pienaar, Eran Tal, Asya Ciftci

We examine the conditions under which quantum parameter estimation is compatible with the concept of a ``true'' quantity value: a unique value of the estimated quantity that exists prior to and independently of measurement. While quantum parameter estimation typically assumes that the quantity to be estimated is a parameter, we argue that this assumption can be lifted, opening the way to a more general formulation of estimation that may apply even in situations where the quantity to be estimated is no longer a parameter, but instead a quantum variable susceptible to quantum fluctuations. We identify three assumptions that characterize the way estimation is often presented in the quantum metrology literature, namely, as a procedure for inferring an unknown ``true value", which is assumed to be unique and to exist prior to and independently of measurement. Using our more general framework, we prove a no-go theorem showing that the preceding assumptions are jointly incompatible with two further assumptions: the completeness of the quantum state (no `hidden variables'), and the presence of entanglement between the probe systems and the system under measurement. We note that the presence of such entanglement is a generic prediction of standard quantum theory. Consequently, if one is not willing to accept hidden variables, nor willing to modify standard quantum theory by introducing a new physical mechanism that destroys entanglement at some scale or regime, then a realist account of estimation is ultimately impossible.

Quantum gravity on the bulk cone

Ahmed Almheiri, Matthew Dodelson, Zohar Komargodski

Bulk-cone singularities in holographic correlators encode propagation along bulk null geodesics. The prototypical example is an AdS black hole, where incoming null geodesics can wind around the photon sphere and return to the boundary. These singularities appear in real time in the thermal two-point function at finite volume. We show that conformal field theories dual to gravitational theories at finite $G_N$ cannot have such singularities. We rule out these singularities both for individual microstates and for the thermal state. The main thrust of the argument is to invoke an analytic continuation in the temperature. As a microscopic example, we construct an AdS$_3$ geometry with two colliding shocks which exhibits a bulk cone singularity. We show by an explicit calculation in the CFT that this singularity is smoothed out at finite $G_N$. Qualitatively, the singularity resolution is a consequence of the fuzziness of the bulk solution in quantum gravity.

${\mathcal{O}}(α_s^2α)$ threshold corrections to inclusive $Z$-boson production

Tanmoy Pati, Narayan Rana, Avick Saha

To match the experimental precision targeted by future collider programs, theoretical predictions for Drell-Yan production must incorporate higher-order mixed Quantum Chromodynamics and electroweak corrections. In this work, we present for the first time the ${\mathcal{O}}(α_s^2α)$ corrections to the inclusive cross-section for $Z$-boson production in the threshold limit. By systematically evaluating the missing singlet contributions, we obtain the complete set of ${\mathcal{O}}(α_s^2α)$ quark form factors, which we use to construct the soft-virtual cross-section by exploiting renormalization group invariance, the K+G evolution equation, and universal soft-collinear factorization. Our results capture the dominant threshold logarithmic enhancements, improve the theoretical scale uncertainties, significantly reduce electroweak input scheme dependence, and establish a crucial step toward complete ${\mathcal{O}}(α_s^2α)$ corrections indispensable for future precision programs.

Screening strongly correlated materials in solid oxide cells with quantum simulation

Ouns El Harzli, Jelle Bor, Linde Wester, Leonid Zhukov, Hanl Park et al. (12 authors)

We develop an end-to-end framework for assessing a quantum-computing application in industrial materials science: the screening of strongly correlated oxygen-electrode materials for SOFCs and SOECs. We formulate the materials problem in terms of interface observables including oxygen-vacancy formation energies, vacancy migration barriers, and electronic conductivity. We describe how these observables can be obtained using a diagrammatic GW-based embedding, either GW+DMFT or self-energy embedding theory (SEET), applied to a DFT-screened shortlist. The atomistic electrode/electrolyte interface defines the electronic structure problem from which the correlated impurity models are constructed. Quantum impurity solvers would provide the finite-temperature impurity Green's functions required by the embedding. The computational challenges that classical solvers may encounter as the interacting active space grows, together with the polynomial scaling of quantum Hamiltonian simulation for efficiently represented discretized models, open an opportunity for quantum advantage. We identify the benchmarks needed to assess this opportunity through convergence of vacancy and migration energetics and comparison with the best applicable classical solvers. We provide illustrative logical qubit counts and identify the thermal state preparation, simulation, measurement, and repeated-solve costs needed for a complete resource assessment. We connect microscopic observables to macroscopic engineering metrics using a continuum model for mixed ionic--electronic conducting oxygen electrodes that couples oxygen surface exchange, solid-state oxygen transport, electrode microstructure, and finite electrode thickness. Finally, we estimate the potential worldwide business value of improved electrode materials under SOFC and SOEC deployment scenarios informed by public technical targets and electrolyser deployment data.

Mixed State Deep Thermalization under Generic Quantum Errors

Xie-Hang Yu, Pavel Kos

Pure-state deep thermalization predicts that, after sufficient scrambling, projected ensembles approach universal distributions that contain information beyond the reduced density matrix. In realistic quantum systems, however, noise is unavoidable and the projected states are generally mixed. Whether mixed-state deep thermalization admits an analogous universal limiting distribution has remained unclear. Here we answer this question for quantum dynamics containing local quantum channels, i.e., errors. We show that the late-time mixed-state projected ensemble is completely determined by the normalized Choi state of the error channel and is universal, independent of the microscopic details of the underlying unitary dynamics. We establish this result analytically for dual-unitary circuits, global Haar-random dynamics, and local Haar-random circuits at large local dimension, and verify it numerically in qubit circuits and kicked Ising dynamics. In a solvable setting, we further find exponentially fast convergence to the limiting ensemble. Our results extend deep thermalization to noisy quantum dynamics and establish a direct correspondence between quantum errors and universal random mixed-state ensembles.
