Quantum channel tomography: optimal bounds in the near-boundary regime

Kean Chen, Aadil Oufkir

Quantum channel tomography (also known as quantum process tomography) is a fundamental task in quantum information and quantum computing, aiming to learn the full description of an unknown quantum channel from black-box queries. For channels with input dimension $d_1$, output dimension $d_2$, and Kraus rank at most $r$, [CGO+26] identifies the dilation rate $τ=rd_2/d_1\geq 1$ as a key parameter governing a Heisenberg-to-classical transition in query complexity. While the optimal bounds in the boundary regime ($τ=1$) and the away-from-boundary regime ($τ\geq 1+Ω(1)$) are now known, the precise behavior in the near-boundary regime ($1<τ<1+o(1)$) has remained unresolved. In this paper, we close this gap, thereby fully settling the query complexity of quantum channel tomography. Specifically, we show that the query complexity is - $Ω(rd_1d_2/\varepsilon^2)$ for diamond norm error $\varepsilon$, matching the upper bound by [MB26, CGO+26], - $Θ(d_1^2/\varepsilon+\min\{(τ-1)d_1^2,(τ-1)^2d_1^2d_2^2\}/\varepsilon^2)$ for Choi trace norm error $\varepsilon$. Our results reveal two qualitatively different forms of the Heisenberg-to-classical transition. For diamond norm tomography, the Heisenberg scaling disappears suddenly as soon as $τ$ leaves the boundary regime. In contrast, Choi trace norm tomography exhibits a gradual transition from Heisenberg to classical scaling as $τ$ moves from the boundary regime to the away-from-boundary regime.

Mercury (Hg): a relativistic $N$-body simulation for Horndeski gravity

Ahmad Nouri-Zonoz, Farbod Hassani, Julian Adamek, Emilio Bellini, Martin Kunz

We present Mercury, a relativistic $N$-body code for cosmological simulations in the $α$-basis formulation of Horndeski gravity. Mercury extends a particle-mesh scheme by incorporating the dynamics of the additional scalar degree of freedom and the corresponding modified Einstein equations, expressed in terms of the time-dependent functions $α_{\rm K}, α_{\rm B}, α_{\rm M}, α_{\rm T}$ and the effective Planck mass $M_*^2$. This generalises our previous implementation of Horndeski subclass models, namely $k$-essence and kinetic gravity braiding (KGB), to the general linear Horndeski $α$-basis. The code evolves the additional scalar degree of freedom and its effective stress--energy tensor alongside the nonlinear matter and metric fields, so that nonlinear structure formation sources the scalar-field evolution and feeds back on the gravitational potentials. Through its interface with hi_class, Mercury supports both phenomenological $α$-function parametrisations and covariant Horndeski theories; we demonstrate the latter using nKGB, the Cubic Galileon, and Brans--Dicke models. We validate the implementation against hi_class in the linear regime and recover the expected nonlinear $k$-essence and KGB limits. For the luminal Horndeski models considered here, we show how a running effective Planck mass generates gravitational slip through the scalar anisotropic stress and modifies the friction terms governing the vector and tensor metric perturbations. The evolution of $M_*^2$ also modifies the normalisation and scale dependence of the effective dark-energy density contrast, leaving a corresponding imprint on its power spectrum. The present framework provides an intermediate step towards nonlinear Horndeski simulations, in which nonlinear scalar self-interactions and screening effects can be incorporated consistently within the same relativistic framework.

Tracing source imprints on the gravitational direct wave

Andrew Laeuger, Colin Weller, Ling Sun, Aleyna Koro, Patrick P. Chen et al. (7 authors)

The direct wave component of black hole (BH) ringdown, revealed through quasinormal-mode filters, carries a non-quasinormal-mode imprint of the plunging perturber's dynamics. However, the connection between the source trajectory and the observed direct wave remains incompletely understood. We first show that the full waveform filtered by the frequency-dependent black hole response gives a substantially more localized kernel than the standard rational filter approach. We then show that analytically continuing the Teukolsky source variables to complex coordinates allows for a semianalytic direct wave which closely reproduces numerical integration throughout the entire plunge. Our generalized steepest-descent method clarifies how coherent emission shapes the imprint of source dynamics on the observed direct wave beyond the light-cone picture.

Mutual information to all orders from modular flow: a replica-free approach

Hewei Frederic Jia

We derive an all-orders long-distance expansion of vacuum mutual information between two disjoint balls in generic $d$-dimensional CFTs. The derivation uses a novel radial sewing expansion of the joint vacuum density matrix, together with a modular response formula for relative entropy and the geometric modular flow of a single ball, without analytic continuation in replica number. The expansion coefficients are modular integrals of modular-flowed vacuum correlators. Our formula provides a systematic treatment of arbitrary multi-point sectors, and recovers known results on two-point sector derived from replica methods. The full two-point sector resums into a modular integral of the thermal partition function on $S^{d-1}$, with an effective temperature depending on ball configuration and modular time. We compute the expansion explicitly in CFTs. In two-dimensional Ising CFT, using analytic reductions and multidimensional numerical integration, we obtain the first ten radial orders in the long-distance expansion, which involve sphere correlators with up to twenty spin insertions. The truncated expansion agrees well with lattice data at finite separation, and approximately satisfies crossing symmetry. Analytic calculations also reproduce known expansion coefficients for 2d free compact boson at self-dual radius and its current sector, as well as for free scalars in higher dimensions.

Lee-Yang theorems for $q$-deformed oscillators and doubly-scaled SYK

Arpon Basu, Siddhant Midha

We show that the vacuum partition function of the $q$-deformed oscillator \[ L_q(z):= \langle0|e^{zT_q}|0\rangle, \qquad T_q := \mathsf{a}_q + \mathsf{a}_q^* \] satisfies the Lee-Yang property, i.e., all zeros of $L_q$ lie on the imaginary line for all $q\in[-1, 1)$, proving the conjectures of Okuyama'23. Using the connection between $q$-deformed oscillators and the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) Hamiltonian in the doubly-scaled regime, we show that the annealed SYK partition function satisfies the Lee-Yang property in the thermodynamic limit, that is, all of its Fisher zeros are on the imaginary axis. As a result, we show that the quenched SYK partition function admits a zero-free strip of constant width with high probability, rigorously complementing the large$-k$ replica calculations of Zlokapa and Kiani'26. We show the imaginary-rootedness of $L_q$ by expressing $\varphi_q(z):= L_q(\mathbf{i} z)$ as a locally uniform limit of ``$q$-deformed'' matching polynomials and then showing that those $q$-deformed polynomials are real-rooted. This establishes that $\varphi_q$ is real-rooted, and in fact belongs to the Laguerre-Pólya class. The real-rootedness of the $q$-deformed polynomials yields novel log-concavity relations on the $q$-deformed matching moments \[M_\ell(q):= \sum_{π\in\mathcal{P}_2(2\ell)}q^{\operatorname{cr}(π)}.\] We show the real-rootedness of the $q$-deformed polynomials by using the Touchard--Riordan formula to derive a partial differential equation relating their $q$-derivatives to their $z$-derivatives, and then analyzing the resulting motion of their roots.

Spacetime Emergence in the Memory of a Learning Agent

Philip A. LeMaitre, T. Rick Perche

We show that the causal structure of spacetime can emerge within the memory of a learning agent performing a quantum information task. We train a model-based reinforcement-learning agent to maximize the quantum mutual information between localized quantum probes interacting with a quantum field in Minkowski spacetime, without directly providing it with information regarding the spacetime metric or causal structure. In learning to accomplish this task, the reinforcement-learning agent constructs an internal predictive model whose planner develops the characteristic null structure of spacetime, allowing us to reconstruct the causal structure of the Minkowski metric with high accuracy. Within this task, spacetime geometry is obtained from an agent's successful predictions and actions, providing a first step toward showing that spacetime concepts emerge from operational quantum experience.

Single-pass stochastic cooling for electron microscopy

River Robles, Peter Hommelhoff

Scientific applications of free electrons, such as in electron microscopes and particle accelerators, are limited by the brightness of available electron sources. Accelerators can use phase space cooling to surpass these source limitations, whereas electron microscopes can only rely on particle filtering to increase beam coherence at the cost of reduced brightness. We show that strong electron-light coupling in nanophotonic structures makes it possible to achieve single-pass stochastic cooling inside an electron microscope for a lossless improvement of longitudinal emittance. This approach is laser-free and compatible with normal CW operation of electron microscopes. We derive a fully quantum mechanical model of stochastic cooling valid in the strong coupling regime, finding an analytic asymptotic formula for the optimal cooled energy spread $σ_{E_f}\simeq0.88(\hbarω)^{2/3}σ_{E_0}^{1/3}$, with $\hbar ω$ the cooling photon energy and $σ_{E_0}$ the initial energy spread. Numerical simulations confirm that the energy spread reduction is reflected in an increase in the purity of the electron's quantum state and a reduction of the longitudinal phase space area. Our work paves a path towards even brighter beams for electron microscopy, promising higher image quality, enhanced spectral resolution, and a route to reaching fundamental quantum limits on electron beam phase space area.

Nonlinear gauge maps and the structure of Goldstone loops in canonical inflation

Cheng-Jun Fang, Zong-Kuan Guo

We clarify the nonlinear relation between the spatially flat scalar and Goldstone descriptions of canonical single-field inflation. An exact clock map shows how potential interactions are reorganized into derivative Goldstone interactions. A change of slicing further connects their dilation symmetry to its comoving realization. We then study the Goldstone two-point function at one loop in the decoupling limit. Using identities derived from the general mode equation, we reveal how seemingly independent loop contributions combine and their non-boundary parts cancel once the associated counterterms are included. This reduction requires no explicit mode-function solution. The leading adiabatic long-mode response reduces to a total derivative in loop momentum, which requires an additional regularization prescription.

The Virasoro Bootstrap: from the $c\sim1$ desert to coupled Potts

Stefanos R. Kousvos, Alessandro Piazza, Alessandro Vichi

We develop a numerical conformal bootstrap for two-dimensional CFTs with $c>1$ based on Virasoro conformal blocks, computed with Zamolodchikov's recursion and optimal polynomial interpolation. As a first test we isolate an island around three decoupled Ising models. We then consider three 3-state Potts models coupled through their energy operators, a candidate irrational CFT. Mixed correlators isolate the decoupled theory in an island, and different gap assumptions, motivated by perturbation theory, yield a peninsula that contains the coupled fixed point but not the decoupled one. Finally, we explore the standard single-correlator bound on the lowest-dimension scalar primary in the OPE of two identical scalars. We find that for central charges just above $c=1$ the bound collapses over a wide range of external dimensions. This excludes any theory whose lightest scalar is absent from its own OPE and lies in this range, and supports the conjecture that no compact CFT exists in this region. We explain several features of these bounds analytically.

Surrogate model of gravitational waveforms for quasi-circular, spin-aligned, asymmetric-mass black hole binaries based on the hybridization of WaSABI-C inspirals with numerical relativity waveforms

H. L. Iglesias, D. M. Shoemaker

With the incorporation of aligned-spin dynamics into self-force waveform models, such as the WaSABI-C inspiral model, and with the release of the SXS collaboration's third public catalog of NR simulations, the gravitational waveform modeling community has made significant advances in the modeling of spinning, asymmetric-mass black hole binaries. In light of these developments, we present WaSABINRHybSur2dq15, the first surrogate model to incorporate information from self-force theory, post-Newtonian theory, and numerical relativity. The model is trained on 65 hybrid waveforms for quasi-circular, spin-aligned, asymmetric-mass black hole binaries with mass ratios spanning 1:1.25 to 1:20 and aligned-spins for the primary black hole ranging -0.5 to 0.5. The hybrid waveforms are generated from a set of WaSABI-C inspirals and NR waveforms from the third SXS public catalog. The model covers the parameter domain $1.25 \leq q \leq 15$ and $|χ_{1z}| \leq 0.5$, and includes the $(2,2),\, (2,1),\, (3,3),\, (4,4),$ and $(5,5)$ spin-weighted spherical harmonic modes. The model also spans the entire LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) frequency band (with $f_{low}=20$ Hz) for total masses $M \gtrsim 111.5\,M_{\odot}$. We find that the WaSABINRHybSur2dq15 model faithfully reproduces the hybrid waveforms with mismatches less than $10^{-3}$ for total masses in the range $80\,M_{\odot} \leq M \leq 300\,M_{\odot}$.
