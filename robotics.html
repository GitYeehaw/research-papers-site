Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations

Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov

Sensorized human demonstrations offer scalable data for mobile manipulation, but embodiment differences complicate policy learning. We present Pixel-UMI, a pipeline for learning environment-aware whole-body policies from human data. We first introduce Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR), which combines reconstructed scene geometry with demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to determine robot posture and generate collision-aware joint and base targets. Delay-calibrated action assignment converts these trajectories into actions. However, kinematic differences can leave residual pose errors, creating viewpoint mismatches between human demonstrations and robot observations. We therefore introduce Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM), which uses these errors during training to reduce reliance on mismatched views at deployment. Finally, because estimated actions cannot fully capture contact-rich interactions, we collect on-policy human corrections through DAgger to improve performance. We evaluate Pixel-UMI on five challenging tasks spanning articulated-object manipulation, deformable-object handling, constrained-space reaching, whole-body collision avoidance, and contact-rich bimanual coordination, achieving 87.0% average task progress. Video results are available at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel-umi

Pixel: A Co-Designed Full-Stack Robot System for Learning General-Purpose Mobile Manipulation

Tony Tao, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Deepak Pathak

General-purpose mobile manipulation requires a combination of capable robot hardware, scalable demonstration collection, expressive whole-body control, and effective policy learning. Yet these components are often developed independently, creating mismatches that limit the capabilities of the complete system. We present Pixel, a co-designed full-stack system for learning general-purpose mobile manipulation. Pixel combines a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics and compliant grippers, multiple teleoperation and robot-free human data-collection interfaces, a unified whole-body controller, and a common policy learning pipeline across human and robot demonstrations. A hierarchical learning stack further enables language-conditioned visuomotor control and long-horizon task execution. We evaluate the system across hardware, data collection, and policy learning, and demonstrate mobile-manipulation behaviors requiring dexterity, active perception, navigation, and whole-body coordination. We hope that Pixel provides an integrated and extensible foundation for studying how robot hardware, data, control, and learning can be designed together for robot learning. More results at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel

D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models

Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li et al. (12 authors)

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video's internal timeline. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.

3D-BBDA: Backpropagation-Free Black-Box Domain Adaptation for Robust 3D Point Cloud Recognition

Shivangi Rai, Kunal Jangid, Vaibhav Kumar, Vinod K Kurmi

Robust 3D point-cloud recognition is critical for robotics, mapping, and urban sensing, yet point-cloud classifiers often suffer performance drops under domain shifts caused by sensor changes, environmental variation, and acquisition artifacts. Many existing adaptation methods are difficult to deploy in practice because they require access to source data or the internal parameters of the source model, which may be restricted by privacy, licensing, or security constraints. We present 3D-BBDA, a backpropagation-free black-box domain adaptation framework that adapts a source-trained predictor to unlabeled target data using only black-box outputs. Our method performs pseudo-label guided prototype-based alignment and employs a weighted prediction module to fuse source logits with alignment logits, reducing sensitivity to noisy pseudo-labels. To stabilize prototype memory when pseudo-label coverage is incomplete, we construct a Gaussian-perturbed prototype prior from unlabeled target predictions and use it as a fallback for missing or unreliable class prototypes during alignment. Experiments on four corrupted 3D benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over strong baselines. On ModelNet-40C, 3D-BBDA achieves mean errors of 32.08% (PointNet) and 29.62% (GDANet), outperforming Source-only (35.88% / 34.03%) and BFTT3D (33.78% / 32.36%). 3D-BBDA requires only a single forward pass with no gradient updates making it suitable for real-time, privacy-preserving deployment in transferable 3D recognition pipelines. Project page is available at https://visdomlab.github.io/3D_BBDA

TAMPAgent: Agentic Belief-Space Planning for Mobile Manipulation

Utkarsh A. Mishra, Alperen Degirmenci, Yongxin Chen, Danfei Xu, Tucker Hermans et al. (6 authors)

Open-world robotic mobile manipulation requires acting in physical environments with only partial information. While recent Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) agents show strong open-world reasoning capabilities, existing architectures that rely on direct tool calling struggle with geometric uncertainty and thus are limited to fully-observable tabletop environments. In contrast, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems can solve long-horizon tasks under partial observability but incur high computational costs and require closed worlds. We propose TAMPAgent, an agentic planning framework that unifies the reasoning of LVLMs with the structured constraint satisfaction of TAMP systems. We endow an LVLM agent with a belief-space planning model operating over an extensible API of perception, planning, and control tools. The agent formulates a partial task plan composed of parameterized observation and execution actions, which is subsequently completed using classical search algorithms. After extracting the plan's geometric constraints, the agent leverages its tool API to bind valid parameter values, satisfying feasibility requirements and producing an executable motion plan. Upon execution, the agent continuously updates its belief history and repeats this closed-loop process by interleaving observation and manipulation actions. Evaluated across complex household mobile manipulation tasks on Rainbow Y1 and Unitree G1 platforms, TAMPAgent significantly outperforms direct tool-calling agentic baselines, demonstrating that integrating TAMP constraint satisfaction into agentic planning loops is essential for unstructured, partially observable environments.

Seeing Through the Illusion: Test-Time Scaling for World Action Models

Cheikh Ahmed, Mahdi Mostajabdaveh, Milad Yazdani, Zirui Zhou

World action models (WAMs) jointly generate actions and imagined futures, but a plausible prediction need not be realized by its co-generated action. We introduce WISE (World Action Inference Scaling through Evaluation), a pre-execution selector that scores each sampled rollout on action validity and temporal regularity, inverse-dynamics consistency between the proposed action and the imagined motion, and instruction-conditioned task progress, and executes the highest-scoring candidate while keeping the WAM frozen. With four candidates per decision, WISE raises closed-loop success from 6.0\% to 24.0\% on RoboLab with Cosmos~3 Edge and from 75.1\% to 78.3\% mean success on LIBERO with DreamZero, and exceeds the best existing selector in aggregate by 4.0 and 0.9 points. Component ablations and agreement analyses show that the three scores rank candidates differently and that task progress carries the largest weight in the fused decision.

Dynamical Unbalanced Optimal Transport: Duality, Reachability, and Computation

Dongjun Wu, Anders Rantzer

We study dynamical unbalanced optimal transport for nonlinear control-affine systems in which the control input affects both the transportation of mass and its growth or decay. This phenomenon arises naturally in controlled transport-reaction systems such as cancer treatment, and distinguishes the problem from standard unbalanced optimal transport, where transport and mass variation are typically independent. To address this problem, we combine classical relaxed-control theory with a weighted superposition principle to obtain an infinite-dimensional linear programming reformulation over weighted trajectories. Under suitable convexity and compactness assumptions, we show that this relaxation is tight. Based on the relaxation, we derive a Kantorovich dual formulation in terms of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman inequalities, and establish strong duality over smooth subsolutions. We then utilize the dual formulation to characterize reachability of the system, namely, when a given terminal measure is reachable from a given initial measure. In addition, tractable sufficient conditions for reachability are provided. Finally, we develop an augmented-Lagrangian algorithm based on the dual formulation for numerical computation of the optimal transport plan. The framework is illustrated on a phenotype-structured cancer treatment problem in which therapy simultaneously drives phenotypic adaptation and population growth or death.

Mediating Human-Swarm Interactions Through a Robot Helper: A Shepherding-Inspired Approach

Arihant Yadav, Sophie Jörg, Eakta Jain

This work investigates human-swarm interaction (HSI) for steering large collectives through an intermediary robot helper. While prior robot-swarm control and HSI techniques commonly assume swarm members are addressable, these assumptions often fail for ``ungovernable'' swarms such as wildlife flocks and schools of fish. Motivated by pastoral shepherding, we compare low-level teleoperation to a higher-autonomy motion-primitives interface in a virtual reality (VR) shepherding task under varied swarm cohesion dynamics. Results indicate that motion primitives that operate on collective shape (e.g., boundary-based control) improve task performance and reduce perceived workload.

Reinforcement Twinning via Twin-Calibrated Bayesian Policy Search and Replay-Driven Identification

Miguel A. Mendez, Lorenzo Schena, Romain Poletti, Yannick Lecomte, Sebastiano Randino et al. (6 authors)

The feasibility of updating and synchronising digital twins of actuated systems from operational data depends strongly on how the system is controlled and, consequently, on the data generated during operation. This establishes a direct link between control actuation and model identifiability. This work presents a reinforcement-twinning framework that exploits this link through three computational components. A Gaussian-process surrogate of the physical return combines the twin prediction, used as a calibrated trend, with a discrepancy learned from measured returns, allowing Bayesian policy search to exploit the twin without being tied to its predictions. Candidate policies are proposed around the policy that is optimal for the twin, while measured returns can expand this region. The resulting physical episodes are replayed on the twin after observer-based synchronisation with sparse measurements, and its parameters are identified by combining Bayesian optimisation with gradient-based refinement. Selected information-oriented episodes deploy an excitation policy to improve parameter identifiability. The framework is demonstrated on a Burgers-flow analogue of dynamic-induction control in wind farms, with a deliberately misspecified model structure. Despite substantially biased return predictions, the twin provides a useful starting policy and search region, placing the guided search ahead of unguided Bayesian optimisation from its first physical evaluations. This advantage is maintained when the search region is allowed to expand in response to changes in measured returns, whereas searches over-constrained by the twin-defined region are eventually overtaken by the unguided alternatives. The results support reinforcement twinning as a framework for jointly adapting models and control policies when physical interaction is limited and model discrepancy cannot be neglected.

PanoTac: A Dense Three-Axis Tactile Sensor for Robust Touch-Centric Manipulation

Binglin, Wang, Xiaole, Jiang, Zicheng Teng et al. (10 authors)

Contact-rich manipulation benefits from tactile feedback that captures both detailed contact geometry and interaction forces. When scooping grains, contact patterns help infer spoon orientation, while resistance transmitted through the grasp guides motion. To provide these complementary sensing capabilities, we present PanoTac, a low-cost, compliant tactile fingertip designed for dense three-axis tactile sensing. Each fingertip provides a $12 \times 32 \times 3$ tactile representation, with one normal ($z$) and two shear ($x$, $y$) response components at each taxel. The sensor operates at 60 Hz and costs less than $50 per fingertip. Sensor characterization quantifies response repeatability and consistency across sensor instances and contact locations. We integrate PanoTac into a robotic system with a visuo-tactile pipeline. Across test-tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, the resulting policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 46.7 percentage points. Ablations show that removing either spatial contact information or shear feedback reduces overall performance across the task suite. Our project page is available at https://panotac.github.io.
