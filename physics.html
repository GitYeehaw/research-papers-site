Ohmic warm inflation

Anish Ghoshal, Alessandro Strumia

We show that Warm Little Inflation is anomalous, lacks a suitable inflaton potential, and cannot sustain the thermal bath, as the quasi-static approximation over-estimated the dissipated power. To address these issues, we propose a new friction mechanism and new anomaly-free models based on one fermion charged under a U(1) gauge group in one extra dimension. The four-dimensional lattice version is a ring of $N$ U(1) groups with one fermion at each site, reducing to an anomaly-free version of the warm little inflaton model for $N=2$. The inflaton is a Wilson line, protected for $N\ge5$. Large $N$ allows a suitable monodromy inflaton potential. In the continuum limit, $N\to\infty$, the inflaton velocity becomes a five-dimensional electric field and friction becomes Ohmic dissipation, which seems able to sustain warm inflation compatibly with observations.

Quantum Many-Body Scars from Operator Size

Debarghya Chakraborty, Dario Rosa

We present a general construction of approximate many-body scar states in two copies of a system using a bilocal size interaction that couples one site to its partner on the other copy. For an uncoupled two-sided Hamiltonian whose terms have fixed size in an operator basis, we identify polynomially many Krylov basis states that it annihilates and that, in the thermodynamic limit, become concentrated on a single size sector. The size operator splits them into a tower of equally spaced levels that gives rise to coherent revivals characterized by collective oscillations of energy across the two copies. We provide a concrete realization of this construction in two copies of a general $q$-body nearest-neighbor spin chain, and the SYK model.

Nonperturbative Bounds on the Ground-State Energy of Lattice QED

Paweł Zin, Andrzej Veitia

We establish nonperturbative bounds on the ground-state energy of lattice quantum electrodynamics with naive fermions in Coulomb gauge, within the Gaussian photon approximation. Our central result is a spectral comparison theorem showing that, in the thermodynamic limit, the minimum fermionic energy density over all link configurations coincides with that of the trivial gauge configuration. The proof exploits a logarithmic determinant representation and dimensional reduction, without relying on reflection positivity, and applies to lattices of either parity. Combining this result with the positivity of the electromagnetic and Coulomb Hamiltonians, we derive an explicit lower bound on the interacting ground-state energy. Finite-lattice counterexamples show that the thermodynamic limit is essential.

A Microwave-Photonics Formulation of the Rydberg-Atom Receiver

David Rosser

The performance of Rydberg-atom receivers is seldom stated as a radio-frequency (RF) link budget. The receiver is shown to be an intensity-modulated direct-detection analog optical link whose modulator is the laser-driven vapor. The transmission of a warm vapor is derived in closed form by averaging the four-level response over atomic velocities. The conversion gain, noise figure, bandwidth, intercept points, and spurious-free dynamic range follow from this one function. For a weak probe the instantaneous bandwidth is half the width of the transparency window in two-photon detuning. The local-oscillator beat places the second-order intercept near twice the local-oscillator field, so the local oscillator sets the even-order linearity. For a rubidium transition at 17.04 GHz the closed forms agree with a velocity-resolved density-matrix model to 1% for the slope and the intercepts and to 10% for the bandwidth. They underestimate the group delay by a third. The response is tied to atomic constants, and the sensing volume escapes the Chu limit. However, the noise figure of a warm vapor rules it out as a general replacement for an antenna and low-noise amplifier.

Transpilation-Aware Multi-Vendor QPU Scheduling with Predicted Post-Transpilation Fidelity

Ana Paula Appel

Cluster schedulers already choose nodes, GPUs and accelerators for submitted workloads, enforce quotas and spread load. QPUs from several vendors are now schedulable resources in the same clusters, yet the user still names the device, usually the one with the best average fidelity. For a given circuit that choice is often wrong: compiling onto each device's qubit connectivity adds gates, so a job can meet its required success probability on one device and miss it on another with similar error rates, and checking this exactly by compiling for every candidate is too slow for a scheduling decision. We let the scheduler choose the QPU in two stages. A training-free estimate predicts, in milliseconds and without compiling, the success probability on every candidate and filters out those that would miss the requirement; only the chosen device then compiles the circuit, re-checks it, and binds the job or rolls back. The estimate matches compiling every candidate at about 25x lower latency in simulation, ranks measured success on IBM hardware as well as the post-compilation estimate (Spearman rho = 0.95), reduces requirement violations from tens of jobs to a few, and avoids them under contention without piling jobs onto one device. Across vendors it works when error rates are comparable (IBM and IQM) and fails when published figures overstate a device.

The moments and asymptotic spectral distribution of the Grassmannian ensemble

Aryan Gadhave, Asghar Ghorbanpour, Liam Hickey, Masoud Khalkhali, Allison Klingler et al. (7 authors)

In this paper, we study random density matrices constructed by taking partial traces of projections onto random subspaces of a bipartite Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_{B}$ distributed according to the pushforward measure of the action of the unitary group on the Grassmannian $Gr_{n}(\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_{B})$. This ensemble models a `typical' quantum subspace. All tracial moments of the density operator, as well as mixed tracial moments of reduced density operators, are computed explicitly using Weingarten calculus. There are several consequences of these results. In particular, we prove that the empirical spectral distribution of the density operator converges almost surely to a rescaled Marchenko-Pastur law. We also prove that this ensemble can be written as a sum of asymptotically free random density operators, leading to an alternative proof of the convergence of the spectral distribution. Finally, we compute the asymptotic expansion of the average entanglement entropy.

Optimised 8th-Order Trotter-Suzuki Schemes for Quantum Simulation

Marko Maležič, Johann Ostmeyer

Simulating quantum dynamics in many-body systems is one of the most promising applications of quantum computers. Product formulas are a natural choice for this task, since they map directly onto quantum gates, and increasing their order $n$ could lead to reduced gate depths, provided the parameters are chosen efficiently. So far, schemes beyond $6^{th}$ order have only been built as compositions of second-order schemes, while more general product formulas are known only up to $6^{th}$ order. In this work, we extend our optimisation framework to order $n = 8$, and find both more efficient compositions and the first $8^{th}$ order general schemes. Our formulas improve on the theoretical efficiency of the state-of-the-art schemes by Morales et al and we recommend two schemes explicitly. In simulations of the Heisenberg XZ chain one of our recommended schemes always outperforms those by Morales et al, though it is not always the same scheme that performs best. More generally, we observe that for Hamiltonians split into many non-commuting operators the uniformity of a Trotter decomposition becomes the main indicator of a scheme's performance. To assess the schemes' use on quantum hardware, we simulate noisy circuits with our new Qiskit plugin qiskit-omelyan. At current noise levels, the Leapfrog scheme remains the best choice, but as noise rates decrease toward fault tolerance, our recommended schemes become competitive.

Thermo-electric Response Bound on Fluctuations in Coherent Transport

Gianmichele Blasi, Rosa López, Mykhailo Moskalets, Sungguen Ryu

We establish an accessible lower bound on current noise in coherent electron transport, expressed in terms of responses to reservoir temperatures and chemical potentials. Derived within the Floquet-Nambu scattering formalism using a Cramér-Rao-type inequality, it holds for arbitrary linear combinations of charge and heat currents in normal terminals, including those of hybrid superconducting systems, under static or periodic driving. The bound is tight in experimentally relevant regimes, notably in quantum point contacts with static or oscillating voltages and normal-superconductor junctions, often providing stronger constraints than thermodynamic uncertainty relations. It can be evaluated via experimentally accessible response coefficients, without inferring entropy production or dynamical activity, and provides useful constraints on the precision of nanoscale thermal and quantum devices.

Soft-photon resummation for low-energy $e^+e^-$ colliders

Thomas Dave, Lois Flower, Jérémy Paltrinieri, William J. Torres Bobadilla

We present resummed predictions for $e^+e^- \to μ^+μ^-(γ)$ and $π^+π^-(γ)$ in the multi-photon soft limit, matched to exact NLO. The resummation is performed within the Yennie-Frautschi-Suura framework at the amplitude level. The initial and final-state contributions are included coherently, and NLO accuracy with finite electron, muon and pion masses is achieved. We present our calculation, its implementation in a fast Fortran Monte Carlo integrator, and compare our predictions with the generators BabaYaga, KKMC, Phokhara and McMule, in scenarios inspired by the energy-scan (CMD) and radiative-return (KLOE, BES III, B factories) experiments. We find agreement with BabaYaga, which includes higher-order corrections through a parton shower, at the per-mille level in the cross-sections, and differences of up to two percent with the soft-photon resummation of KKMC. This work represents an important step towards the validation of Monte Carlo generators for low-energy experiments, and will be released in the upcoming new version of Phokhara.

Quantum Dobrushin comparison and locality of fermionic equilibrium states

Lennart Becker, Harman Preet Singh, Stefan Teufel

We prove a quantum analogue of Dobrushin's comparison theorem for lattice fermions. As in the classical case, it is a tool for proving uniqueness of equilibrium states, decay of correlations, and stability of local expectations under distant perturbations. We apply it to fermions with weak many-body interactions added to a quadratic Hamiltonian, allowing pairing, spatial inhomogeneity and long-range interactions. If the quadratic Hamiltonian is gapped, there is a coupling strength below which the thermal equilibrium (KMS) state is unique at every positive temperature and its correlations decay uniformly in the temperature. Its local expectations converge to those of the unique gapped ground state exponentially fast in the inverse temperature. In the same regime, local perturbations perturb locally in a strong sense (strong LPPL): every equilibrium state of a system with a distant defect is locally close to the equilibrium state without the defect, however strong or extended the defect is. The same holds for finite-volume Gibbs states with arbitrary even boundary terms. Without a gap, uniqueness, decay of correlations and strong LPPL hold at each positive temperature for interactions below a coupling strength that depends on the temperature. At high temperature, they hold for general interactions.
