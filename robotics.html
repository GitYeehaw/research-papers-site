Ropedia W0: Scaling Loco-Manipulation World Models across Embodiments with Human Experience

Tianqi Liu, Hanfei Zhao, Qifeng Liang, Hui En Pang, Jiarui Xu et al. (15 authors)

World models enable embodied agents to anticipate action consequences, yet jointly modeling locomotion and manipulation across diverse embodiments remains challenging due to heterogeneous action spaces and limited multimodal supervision. We present Ropedia W0, the first egocentric loco-manipulation world foundation model spanning humans and robots with diverse embodiments. We curate Xp-Data, comprising 10k hours of egocentric human data and 3k hours of robot data with unified multimodal annotations and systematic quality control. A unified kinematic representation encodes locomotion through camera poses and manipulation through projected skeletons, with robot skeletons combining embodiment-specific forward kinematics with extensive manual keypoint annotation and correction. We also introduce Xp-Teleop, a long-horizon loco-manipulation benchmark based on high-quality dexterous humanoid teleoperation data. Evaluations demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across five cross-embodiment benchmarks (DreamDojo, $Ψ_0$, $ω$-HOME, Xp-Teleop, and AgiBot) and on held-out human egocentric video, from zero-shot inference to few-shot and full-data post-training. Scaling human pre-training data improves performance on both human and robot evaluations. After distillation, our model supports real-time, minute-long rollouts, enabling live VR interaction. Applications in policy evaluation, model-based planning, and world-action modeling further demonstrate the model's utility for embodied decision-making.

Physical Words: A Vocabulary for Structuring Latent Reasoning in Robot Manipulation

Jiaming Liu, Xinghao Zhu, Yiming Zhang, Shanghang Zhang, Yinxi Wang et al. (11 authors)

Multimodal reasoning provides robotic foundation models with predictive context needed for reliable action generation. However, language-based reasoning captures task semantics but struggles to express fine-grained physical dynamics, while latent reasoning models future state evolution but offers limited structure for organizing these dynamics into reusable reasoning primitives. To bridge this gap, we introduce PhysWord, a reasoning-before-acting foundation model built on Physical Words, which use atomic action verbs as shared semantic anchors for continuous representations of physical dynamics. Specifically, PhysWord predicts upcoming atomic action words and directly aligns their contextual hidden states with latent targets derived from the corresponding future observations. Building on these primitives, we construct a temporally structured PhysWord space with visual and optional tactile variants of each action word. Autoregressively predicting Physical Words across future timesteps captures transitions between execution stages, providing structured multimodal context for action generation. PhysWord achieves state-of-the-art success rates of 90.3\% on visuotactile UniVTAC and 99.6\% on vision-based LIBERO-Long, and outperforms competing methods on real-world tasks using bimanual grippers and dexterous hands. Further evaluations demonstrate that our structured latent reasoning framework generalizes to unseen scenarios and enables long-horizon manipulation.

CoopUQ: Robust Multi-Robot Social Navigation via Uncertainty-Aware Cooperative Forecasting

Zehao Wang, Jianpeng Yao, Yuping Wang, Yiping Li, Jiaqi Li et al. (12 authors)

Reinforcement learning enables mobile robots to make fast and scalable navigation decisions in constrained human-shared environments. However, directly extending single-robot approaches to multi-robot settings often fails to ensure safety and efficiency, especially under occlusions and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where individual robots may overlook potential risks or make inaccurate predictions. Leveraging cooperation effectively for both prediction and navigation remains a major challenge. We propose CoopUQ, a multi-robot social navigation framework that bridges uncertainty-aware cooperative occupancy forecasting and robust decision making. CoopUQ predicts future occupancy grid maps through cross-robot spatiotemporal feature fusion and further leverages forecasts and their uncertainty to guide policy via constrained multi-agent reinforcement learning, where the cost penalizes navigation through regions predicted to be occupied or highly uncertain. This design enables the policy to adapt its risk sensitivity under distribution shifts. Experimental results across multiple Unity3D-based simulation environments with both in-distribution and OOD settings, including diverse layouts, pedestrian densities, and rushing behavior, demonstrate improved prediction performance and safety over representative baselines. Real-robot experiments further demonstrate its robustness across various scenes. Our code and videos are available at https://coopuq.github.io/.

Where is Physical RSI capability from? An Observational Scaling Law of LLM-as-policy

Zekai Li, Jiayi Yu, Jinlu Zhang, Luyang Niu, Zekun Qi et al. (11 authors)

In this paper, we systematically investigate the mechanisms, scaling behavior, and generalization of physical Recursive self-improvement. We introduce PhysRSI-eval to quantify physical RSI through a five-axis metric: First-Attempt Performance, Efficiency, Success, Budget and Improvement from failure. Different VLMs are evaluated on 18 manipulation tasks under a fixed interaction budget. The analysis of experimental results motivates the Physical Barrel Hypothesis: different capabilities may constrain different parts of improvement. The five-axis metrics also distinguish systems with close public benchmark averages and show that high scores need not come with early or efficient gains. We study observational scaling from two perspectives: predicting the relative performance of VLM agents from their public capabilities, and forecasting later progress from early physical feedback. Together, these analyses ask how quickly and how far an agent can improve. Moreover, we conduct workspace interventions and then analyze what improvement leaves behind. Removing history reduces late-round success, frozen workspaces remain useful on unseen layouts, and three cross-task cases achieve success earlier. The results suggest that scene-specific adaptation and reusable experience can coexist.

GPT-6 Astra Is a Native Visuo-Tactile Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Chen Yang, Linzhe Shi, Hang Zhang, Mu Xu, Jiansheng Fan et al. (6 authors)

Recent advances in multimodal models have enabled GPT-6 Astra to perform robotic manipulation through in-context learning. Yet contact-rich control and tactile perception are often regarded as strengths of specialist policies, leaving open whether Astra can directly understand and use tactile observations. We provide, to our knowledge, the first demonstration that Astra directly interprets vision-based tactile images and uses their contact cues for manipulation. Tactile RGB images enter through its existing visual interface, enabling few-shot closed-loop control on nineteen simulated UniVTAC and OpenViTac tasks with 210 sampled scene seeds per setting. With visuo-tactile input, Astra achieves mean success rates of 93.8% and 81.8%, exceeding the strongest reported specialist baselines in our comparison by 22.9 and 13.1 percentage points, respectively. Vision-only performance also leads these baselines, reaching 76.3% and 80.6%. Trajectory analysis reveals active probing, cautious grasping, contact-guided alignment, and recovery, alongside difficulties with tangential loading and corrective execution. Our findings challenge the exclusivity of specialist policies in contact-rich manipulation and show that fine-grained vision can partially compensate for missing touch. They also identify vision-based tactile sensing as an interface for scaling contact understanding with foundation models and reveal signs of benchmark saturation. These results motivate joint visual-tactile pretraining, harder contact-rich evaluations, and physical-robot validation.

Controllability of polynomial systems is undecidable and not recursively enumerable

Jun Liu, Ivan O. Shevchenko

Sontag conjectured in 1988 that global controllability of polynomial control systems with rational coefficients is undecidable, but that the controllable systems form a recursively enumerable set. We prove the first part of the conjecture and disprove the second. For every homogeneous polynomial vector field $F$ of odd degree with rational coefficients, we construct an explicit polynomial control system $Σ_F$, with one more state than $F$, that is globally controllable if and only if the origin is not globally asymptotically stable for $\dot x=F(x)$. By a recent result, the homogeneous polynomial vector fields with a globally asymptotically stable origin form a set that is recursively enumerable but not decidable. Consequently, global controllability of polynomial systems is undecidable, and the controllable polynomial systems are not recursively enumerable. The same conclusions hold for systems of the form $\dotξ=f(ξ)+Bu$ with a polynomial drift and a constant input matrix.

PICA-WAM: Visual Perturbation Invariance via Cross-Appearance Self-Alignment in World Action Models

Yude Zou, Yifei Yao, Guanbo Jiao, Jianming Ma, Guanjie Zheng et al. (6 authors)

World Action Models (WAMs) jointly predict future video and generate actions, yet remain sensitive to task-irrelevant visual perturbations, i.e., changes in visual appearance that should not affect robot behavior. We identify a gap in conventional WAM training: video prediction encourages representations to preserve appearance information, while action generation does not explicitly enforce invariance to task-irrelevant appearance. Data augmentation exposes WAMs to such perturbations but does not require perturbed and nominal observations to produce consistent action representations, and alignment with external pretrained features does not guarantee the desired invariance. To address this gap, we propose PICA-WAM, a self-supervised training framework that casts perturbation invariance as an auxiliary cross-appearance self-alignment task for WAMs. An exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher processes the nominal observation to provide a deeper-layer target, while the online WAM processes a perturbed observation and aligns its shallower-layer action representation to this target through a lightweight projection head. Across four strong WAMs evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Plus, EBench, and a real robot, PICA-WAM consistently improves robustness under visual perturbations, preserves nominal performance in simulation, and raises the real robot success rate. PICA-WAM further reduces the sensitivity of video features to task-irrelevant appearance and its propagation into actions, as shown by paired layer-wise diagnostics. Data Center Oscillation Isolation and Grid Support: Fundamental Limits and Source-Selective Control

Cheng Feng

The grid integration of large data centers poses a growing power-system stability challenge. Rapidly varying computing demand can induce oscillations, while the facilities must provide a prescribed response to grid disturbances. Existing controls address load fluctuations and grid support but do not establish their jointly achievable performance. This paper derives fundamental limits and develops a source-selective controller for simultaneous oscillation isolation and grid support. A coupled closed-loop model gives a condition for distinguishing disturbance sources and reveals a direct isolation-support conflict under grid-import-power feedback alone; additional electrical measurements can remove this pointwise conflict but leave a conditional cross-frequency sensitivity limit. Guided by these limits, we design a local residual-feedback storage controller through convex frequency-domain optimization with stability, isolation, and grid-response constraints across operating points. Tests on a single-generator interconnection and a modified nine-bus network show stronger oscillation isolation than equal-order optimized comparators while meeting the prescribed grid response across operating points.

Bio-inspired Thrust-Assisted Trotting on a legged-Aerial Robot via Real-Time QP-MPC

Shreyansh Pitroda, Eric Sihite, Alireza Ramezani, Morteza Gharib

This paper presents the hardware implementation of a quadratic program-based controller on Harpy: thruster-assisted bipedal robot to perform thruster-assisted trotting. This is inspired by wing-assisted locomotion observed in birds such as the chukar partridge, which employ thrust vectoring to manipulate the ground reaction force. The proposed controller formulates the thruster-assisted spring-loaded inverted pendulum dynamics as a condensed QP-based model predictive control problem, enabling simultaneous regulation of ground contact force and active thrust vectoring at 500 Hz. A decoupled control architecture is used, where the robot's sagittal dynamics are controlled using QP-MPC while the frontal dynamics is regulated using a cascade orientation stabilization controller. Two experiments were conducted with different forward reference velocities of 0 m/s and 0.1 m/s. While the instantaneous velocity oscillates, the mean velocities can track their respective reference. Additionally, joint tracking error remains within 1 deg while tracking the desired leg length given by the leg trajectory controller and desired swing trajectory given by Raibert's heuristic. The QP-MPC problem is consistently solved with 0.18ms, which is well within the 2ms computation budget while satisfying the GRF friction cone constraint.

Exact model reduction by Lie symmetry groups

Hamed Taghavian, Jens Sjölund

We develop a model-reduction technique based on Lie group theory for general engineering models described by a system of algebraic, ordinary, and/or partial differential equations with an arbitrary number of inputs, outputs, and constant parameters. The proposed method reduces the parameter space of these models without changing their external behavior, that is, their input-output maps. We apply this method to phase-field models used for high-fidelity simulations in electrochemistry to accelerate the analysis and design of metal-ion batteries.
