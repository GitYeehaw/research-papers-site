OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments

Xiang Fan, Yuheng Wang, Jiayuan Yan, Bohan Fang, Wisdom Oluchi Ikezogwo et al. (10 authors)

A single policy that controls diverse robots must not only transfer task knowledge across embodiments, but also adapt its behavior to each robot's capabilities. Existing approaches facilitate transfer through shared action representations, but these representations do not necessarily capture how differences in robot morphology should shape manipulation strategies. We introduce OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy that conditions directly on each robot's kinematics in a specific scene to produce embodiment-specific behavior. This kinematic graph represents the robot's joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity to its visual observation, allowing the policy to account for how its embodiment related to objects and surfaces in the scene. A flow-matching policy predicts relative joint motions for each node, enabling control across robots with different degrees of freedom and kinematic configurations. To learn how physical structure shapes behavior at scale, we procedurally design and filter one million physically feasible robot embodiments; next, we develop algorithms to generate robot trajectories in simulation for all embodiments; finally, we train across diverse simulated manipulation tasks. On 2,000 embodiment-scene-held-out evaluation episodes, OmniAct achieves 63.0% success, compared to 17.8% for pi 0.5 and 57.9% for the strongest baseline; on the task families GPT-6 Astra was evaluated on, it achieves 64.7% against GPT-6 Astra's 41.1%. On a previously unseen real-world robot, it achieves nearly twice the success rate of the next best method, MolmoAct2. Beyond task success, we observe that OmniAct learns distinct manipulation strategies that exploit the capabilities and accommodate the limitations of different morphologies. PyRUA-Dex: Learning Programmatic Policies for Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation with Coding Agents

Feixiang Ruan, Guodong Lv, Kewei Zhang, Wenbo Huang, Yintianrun Zhang et al. (20 authors)

Learning for dexterous manipulation is limited by the scarcity of high-quality demonstrations: retargeting from human video is imprecise, teleoperation gives no force feedback to the operator, and exoskeleton-based collection is hard to scale up. We present PyRUA-Dex, a framework that enables coding agents to develop dexterous manipulation through programmatic policy, without the need of demonstrations. The programmatic policy is developed in two stages: during exploration, multiple agents explore in parallel for task skills and ways to compose them, and a distilling agent condenses the successful attempts into a task library and one program. During refinement, this program is improved through randomized layouts for enhanced robustness and execution efficiency. In simulated environments, PyRUA-Dex achieves SOTA on Bench2Dex with a score of 72.9 and reaches 81.0% success on DexJoCo under full randomization, ahead of every trained policy on both benchmarks. In real world experiments on a bimanual platform with two 22-DoF hands, PyRUA-Dex learns to play piano, write calligraphy, and other tasks efficiently: learning a new piece from sheet music takes as little as 30 minutes. These results suggest that PyRUA-Dex is an effective framework for developing dexterous manipulation policies with coding agents in the absence of high quality dexterous manipulation demonstrations.

ROAM-Bench: Evaluating Embodied Active Spatial Intelligence through Evidence Verification

Xingjian Ran, Xiaoye Mo, Sihao Liu, Li Luo, Taiqiang Wu et al. (10 authors)

Embodied spatial intelligence requires agents to gather information through exploration and interaction, then use it to reason and act. Existing benchmarks insufficiently distinguish correct outcomes from completion of these task requirements. We introduce ROAM-Bench to evaluate these abilities under robot motion and collision constraints. Independent checks use simulation state and episode records to assess outcomes and task requirements, with stronger checks of geometry, coverage, or placement. It comprises 250 manually validated tasks across 11 types in five groups and 27 indoor environments, covering object and room search, counting, geometric judgments, direction reasoning, and relocation. Human evaluators achieve 84% strict task success on 100 sampled tasks, with every correct outcome satisfying the required checks. Across seven frontier vision-language models, we identify two complementary failure modes: correct outcomes with unmet task requirements, and incorrect outcomes despite passing stronger checks. Oracle-input diagnostics show that more complete target observations and calibrated feedback improve final spatial judgments.

iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction

Vansh Kapoor, Max Sobol Mark, Sreyas Venkataraman, Max Simchowitz, Aviral Kumar

World-action models (WAMs) build policies on pretrained video-generation models, on the premise that predicting how a scene evolves helps determine how to act. However, generating future scenes is expensive for real-time control. This motivates efficient proxies for future imagination, but how to train such proxies without sacrificing action-prediction quality remains unclear. An effective proxy need not generate a high-resolution frame; it only needs to provide enough information to distinguish between possible futures. This leads to the design principle behind iWAM: rather than denoising an entire future frame, can we condition the action policy on intermediate features from a single video-model evaluation? We show that the answer is yes. Exploiting the reversible dynamics of flow-matching models, we invert each training future into the corresponding latent noise vector. However, a single velocity evaluation at this latent does not necessarily yield informative features for action prediction. We therefore learn a bidirectional flow map that amortizes future-generation computation, enabling a single model evaluation to provide effective conditioning features. At inference, we sample a latent noise vector from the prior and condition the policy using just one video-backbone forward pass. Across simulated benchmarks and real robots, iWAM recovers the generalization benefits of WAMs while substantially reducing its inference cost.

Exploratory Midtraining for Robot Policies

Kshitiz, Zheyuan Hu, Robyn Wu, Yizhou Li, Yunchu Zhang et al. (14 authors)

Robot policies predominantly learn from human-collected data emphasizing competent behavior, limiting coverage of the low-level behaviors needed to support autonomous improvement. We study how a form of midtraining can be used to build coverage over diverse low-level behaviors that connect challenging states of the robot and its environment to states from which further progress is possible ("connected behavioral coverage"). We also examine how this coverage supports downstream adaptation and online improvement. To this end, we introduce $β$-Data, a dataset collected via adversarial teleoperation, where a human adversary reversibly perturbs the environment while a human operator continues toward completion without resetting. We relabel trajectory segments with local outcomes in natural language and finetune a pretrained policy conditioned on recent action history. The resulting policy exhibits sustained use of more low-level behaviors. Across manipulation tasks in simulation and on three real-robot platforms, our approach yields more favorable data scaling laws, better zero-shot generalization, and more effective use of additional rollout time than training on standard demonstrations. During online finetuning, these policies need fewer rollouts to collect successful experience and reach higher success rates, suggesting that midtraining can prepare robots for both more robust task execution and continued autonomous improvement. Our project page and robot videos are available at https://kshitiz-1225.github.io/exploratory-midtraining-robot-policies/.

Neural Implicit Representation for Bathymetry Estimation using Sidescan Sonar Imagery

Tianxiang Lin, Sacchin Sundar, Fenghao Liu, Onur Bagoren, Katherine A. Skinner

Bathymetry measurement and estimation is critical for understanding the structure of underwater environments. While multibeam echosounders (MBES) provide accurate depth measurements, their deployment on surface vessels limits spatial resolution in deep water. Sidescan sonar (SSS), in contrast, can be deployed on autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) to operate closer to the seafloor, enabling high-resolution acoustic imaging. However, the inherent ambiguity in measurements and the limited observation coverage of sonar datasets degrade the performance of state-of-the-art methods for underwater bathymetry estimation from SSS. In this paper, we propose a differentiable neural rendering framework for SSS that enables effective estimation of bathymetry from SSS measurements. Our approach incorporates a measurement model based on SSS geometry, explicitly accounting for its wide elevation aperture and associated ambiguities. We evaluate our method on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating improved robustness and accuracy compared to alternative approaches.

EmbodiSafety: Benchmarking Risk Understanding and Response in Embodied AI

Erfei Cui, Shengjia Zhao, Yan Qin, Kaixuan Wang, Junming Chen et al. (7 authors)

Progress in embodied foundation models is largely measured by task success, while the physical safety of their decisions remains underexamined. Safety evaluations centered on rule following and binary judgments do not adequately assess whether embodied models understand environmental hazards and make safe decisions. We introduce EmbodiSafety, comprising 1,092 snapshot tasks and 540 trajectory tasks across 844 visual scenarios to assess safety over short and long horizons. Six response modes cover direct execution, refusal, clarification, constrained execution, autonomous recovery, and human escalation. Distinguishing recovery from human intervention makes the boundary of safe autonomy an explicit evaluation target. Our protocol assesses mode correctness, risk identification, and actionable handling, with complete decision accuracy requiring all applicable checks to pass. Claude-Opus-5.5 achieves the highest complete decision accuracy among reference models, reaching 56.1% on snapshots and 61.5% on trajectories. We further develop a data construction pipeline and curate 40k examples for safety alignment. Supervised training on this corpus raises the complete decision accuracy of Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B.

At the Limit: Autonomous Vehicle Drifting under Generative Policies

Jie He, Meixun Qu, Cheng Hu, Lei Xie, Ezio Bartocci et al. (6 authors)

Autonomous drifting requires agile control despite tire-force saturation and highly nonlinear vehicle dynamics. We propose an online reinforcement learning method for flow policies that combines stochastic differential equation (SDE) sampling for exploration with value-based policy optimization. This method supports both training from scratch and fine-tuning a policy pretrained on expert demonstrations. After training, control actions can be generated using either SDEs or ordinary differential equations (ODEs). Experiments on circular and eight-shaped drifting in an F1TENTH digital twin platform show that both training strategies can achieve effective real-time drift control, with tighter tracking, higher success rates, and better robustness under noisy conditions.

Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

Xuanchen Liu, Hejun Zhang, Boxuan Dong, Ying Wu, Heshuai Li et al. (8 authors)

Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies? Inspired by the mirror self-recognition test in animal cognition, we investigate whether robot policies can reliably manipulate physical objects in scenes containing their reflections. We introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that preserves task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints while comparing a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions. Across five policies spanning Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies, every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates: averaged over the three conditions, the drop ranges from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, and it reaches 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, we compare it against geometrically matched non-reflective panels; 50--88\% of mirror-specific failures occur without recorded robot--panel contact, so direct collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses instead reveal recurring behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Targeted diagnostics further characterize grasping near reflections, policy-dependent pushing progress, and physical-target interaction under main-camera occlusion. Real-robot experiments with $π_{0.5}$ and FastWAM also show lower overall success rates under the upright-mirror and mirrored-tabletop conditions. These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror.

CAPER: PartGround and PlanRefine for Reliable Manual-Guided Robotic Assembly Planning

Hanzhao Xi, Zhonghao Li, Suxuan Liu, Shanghan Zhou, Zesheng Chen et al. (7 authors)

Vision-Language-Models (VLMs) used in robot task planning to derive assembly procedures from existing manuals may correctly interpret individual assembly actions yet confuse exact part identities, predict unsupported Part IDs, or produce inconsistent parts across assembly steps. Thus, we propose CAPER (Capacity-Aware Part-grounding and Evidence Refinement), a manual-guided robotic assembly framework that enables VLMs to generate assembly procedures with two modules--PartGround and PlanRefine. PartGround aligns step-level manual evidence with product-specific inventory and part-reference information to identify exact Part IDs and quantities, transforming free-form hardware prediction into evidence-guided assignments represented in a shared Assembly Intermediate Representation (Assembly IR). PlanRefine audits PartGround assignments against finite inventory by removing the lowest-confidence assignments. Uncertain identity cases remain marked for review in the Assembly IR. We evaluate CAPER on the IKEA Assembly in the Wild (IAW) dataset. Experiment results show that CAPER reaches a Finite-inventory Consistency Rate of 83.33% and produces zero observed out-of-inventory Part-ID occurrences. At the plan level, PlanRefine removes all observed inventory-overuse pairs (9 -> 0) and excess units (51 -> 0). Overall, the results indicate that PartGround and PlanRefine improve part reliability, while also enhancing finite-inventory consistency and local prediction accuracy. Project page and video: https://xhzzzz123.github.io/CAPER/
