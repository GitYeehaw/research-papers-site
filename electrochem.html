Generalizing Density Functional Theory with a Particle-Hole Expansion

Shurui Zhao, Madhubani Mukherjee, David A. Mazziotti

Strong electron correlation remains difficult to describe within conventional density functional approximations, particularly when natural-orbital occupations become strongly fractional. We introduce a systematic particle-hole expansion that generalizes density functional theory (DFT) through an explicit functional of the one-electron reduced density matrix. The previously derived cumulant-based correction emerges as the leading term and the thermal (entropic) correction as an infinite-order member whose coefficients are fixed by a single parameter. The expansion can be formulated as a moment problem solved by semidefinite programming with computational scaling comparable to DFT. We show that both the first- and second-order corrections yield closed-form natural-orbital occupations. Applications to periacenes and Fe-porphyrin demonstrate that the second-order correction substantially reduces excessive orbital fractionalization, yielding natural-orbital occupations and fundamental gaps in improved agreement with correlated calculations.

Odd Elasticity in Strain-Gradient Continua: Anisotropy Classification and Non-Hermitian Elastodynamics

Payam Poorsolhjouy

Odd elasticity extends continuum mechanics to active nonconservative systems containing internal energy sources, such as robotic active metamaterials with nonreciprocal feedback. Modeling these systems as effective continua requires relaxing Maxwell-Betti reciprocity, which results in stiffness tensors lacking major symmetry. In this contribution, we extend this framework to 3D strain-gradient odd elasticity to incorporate the length scales inherent in these heterogeneous systems. By applying the full hierarchy of 3D point-group symmetries along chiral and achiral branches, we derive the exhaustive set of fourth-, fifth-, and sixth-order odd elastic stiffness tensors. Further, utilizing the dynamic principle of virtual work, we formulate the equilibrium equations, higher-order boundary conditions, and the generalized acoustic tensor for elastodynamic analysis. The results demonstrate that nonreciprocal moduli survive even under full isotropy in the sixth-order stiffness, under hemitropy in the fifth-order stiffness, and under cubic symmetry in fourth-order stiffness. In contrast, the elastodynamic analysis reveals that under isotropic and hemitropic limits, despite the underlying odd stiffness moduli, the acoustic tensor remains unconditionally Hermitian. Non-Hermitian elastodynamics is shown to require anisotropy. By numerically analyzing a transversely hemitropic odd elastic continuum, we demonstrate that this symmetry reduction results in non-Hermitian elastodynamic phenomena including exceptional points in the dispersion spectra and active energy exchange through simultaneous amplification and attenuation of the elastic wave components. These results provide the necessary theoretical foundation for future multiscale homogenization frameworks for anisotropic strain-gradient active microstructures, and for the design of nonreciprocal strain-gradient metamaterials.

Grow, Compress, and Perturb: A Hybrid Quantum-Classical Pathway to Scalable Quantum Chemistry on Quantum Computers

Hugo D. Nogueira, Siwar Badreddine, Olivier Adjoua, Diata Traore, Guillaume Michel et al. (6 authors)

While Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) variants are among leading candidates for electronic structure calculations on quantum computers, gradient-based operator selection limits their scalability. To solve this, we introduce the Hamiltonian-gradient-free VULCAN (Variational Upbuilding, Layered Compression, Adaptive N-body perturbation) layered VQE framework. Our method dynamically generates ansatz operators using Variational Double Bracket Flow (VDBF) equations. Combined with a linearly scaling minimal complete pool, it creates a pool 4-12 times smaller than QEB (Qubit-Excitation-Based) pools. By appending entire layers simultaneously, Hamiltonian gradient measurements are completely eliminated. Circuit depth is managed via a grow-then-compress scheme using efficient state-overlap measurements, and qubit requirements are reduced by projecting the Hamiltonian onto a dominant-determinant subspace. Using high-performance emulation up to 32 logical qubits, we achieve chemical accuracy at just 5-20% of the measurement cost of batched ADAPT-VQE for hydrogen chains, BeH2 and H2O. For the 32-qubit C3H6, where batched ADAPT-VQE does not reach chemical accuracy, a single VULCAN layer combined with a GPU-accelerated stochastic Epstein-Nesbet (ENPT2) correction does. This stochastic ENPT2 post-processing step enables early termination at chemical accuracy.

ML-COHP: predicting bond descriptors using latent space of a universal interatomic potentials

Daniil S. Alekseev, Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander G. Kvashnin

The integrated crystal orbital Hamilton population (ICOHP) is the standard measure of bond strength in a DFT calculation, but every structure needs its own DFT single point and LOBSTER projection, which takes days for a cell of a hundred atoms, such as a grain boundary or a catalytic nanoparticle. We predict ICOHP from the frozen descriptors of a MACE foundation model. A symmetric pair head reads the scalar and bond-projected vector channels of the two atoms and corrects a pair baseline built from the element embeddings of the same model. We tested the model on the high-entropy diboride (Hf,Zr,Ti,Nb,Ta)B$_2$, Pt clusters on defective graphene and Pt/Pd nanoparticles with adsorbed O$_2$. The error on bonds stronger than 0.5 eV is 1.6-4.1%, about half that of the Gaussian-process model of Ferreira et al. trained on the same data. Trained only on high-temperature HEB$_2$ snapshots, the model reproduces the LOBSTER ICOHP of relaxed grain boundary cells to 0.06 eV in about a second, against 5-51 hours of LOBSTER. The vector channels halve the error compared to the scalar ones, and the baseline keeps the model usable with two training structures. With geometries from a fine-tuned MACE potential, the model gives the bonding of Pt/Pd particles over a continuous O$_2$ coverage, and DFT on these particles shows that O$_2$ costs a Pt-rich shell several times more metal--metal bonding than a Pd shell.

Framework-Conditional Open Materials Generation

Maya M. Martirossyan, Hillary Pan, Philipp Höllmer, Stefano Martiniani

Theoretical predictions of novel materials rely on a variety of techniques, from data-mining-based ion substitution methods to fully de novo generative models. Each approach imposes specific constraints and encounters unique challenges when extrapolating beyond the distribution of known materials to discover new ones. The foremost goal of materials generation is to produce stable, unique, and novel (S.U.N.) structures. Predicted structures that meet these criteria, however, typically result from novel chemical compositions mapped onto known structural prototypes or frameworks. We leverage this heuristic insight to present a new generative paradigm for the de novo prediction of inorganic crystalline materials constrained by their structural framework---i.e., the unit cell shapes and respective particle positions. Our method, termed OMatG-FC (Open Materials Framework-Conditional Generation), learns via a combination of discrete flow matching on the atomic species and stochastic interpolants for the volume scaling of the unit cell lattice, while fixing in-place the fractional coordinates and the unit-volume lattice vectors, dramatically reducing the dimensionality of the problem compared to unconstrained (framework-free) generation. Our method is more flexible than existing prototype-constrained methods, demonstrating capability for discovery of unseen, stable prototypes derived from known structural frameworks. We also investigate the number of unique frameworks in the MP-20 dataset, demonstrating the propensity of duplicate frameworks within the dataset and providing a framework-split version. We benchmark the performance of our model using the LeMat-GenBench suite using three ML interatomic potentials and achieve state-of-the-art S.U.N. rate below the convex hull, eschewing the need to learn atomic packings for materials discovery.

Electric field control of anisotropic spin traffic between spin-filtering and magnon-scattering in a layered antiferromagnet

Mohamed Soliman, Jaime Garrido Aldea, Davy Borowski, Dorye L. Esteras, Neeraj K. Rajak et al. (15 authors)

Layered A-type antiferromagnetic (AFM) semiconductors offer a unique platform for spintronic devices by combining robust magnetism with gate-tunable semiconducting properties. We present a dual-gate field-effect transistor based on CrSBr, a layered A-type AFM with pronounced transport anisotropy and complex spin dynamics. Our device provides independent control over carrier density and displacement field. Systematic transport measurements reveal that the displacement field, rather than doping, predominantly governs the sign and magnitude of magnetoresistance ratio (MRR). We identify the origins of both negative and positive MRR components and connect them to interlayer and intralayer spin-dependent transport mechanisms. We interpret these findings with ab initio calculations and an effective spin-circuit model that reproduces the anisotropic magnetotransport. Our results identify the displacement field as an efficient electrical control parameter of spin transport in CrSBr and suggest that multi-gate architectures could provide similar control in other layered antiferromagnetic semiconductors.

Micromagnetic Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Normal-Mode Formulation of Cavity Magnon-Photon Interactions

Claudio Serpico, Marco Lembo, Giovanni Miano

We investigate magnon-photon interactions in a resonant cavity starting from the coupled Maxwell and Landau-Lifshitz equations linearized about an arbitrary micromagnetic ground state. By introducing an electric vector potential and a suitable interaction Lagrangian, the formulation avoids the cancellation of large magnetostatic contributions that arises in the conventional magnetic-vector-potential description and becomes inaccurate under modal truncation. The magnetization and cavity fields are expanded in the normal modes of the uncoupled magnetic and electromagnetic subsystems, with coupling coefficients given by overlap integrals of the corresponding eigenfunctions. The resulting multimode Lagrangian has a noncanonical structure due to the gyrotropic terms, and the corresponding Hamiltonian formulation is obtained using the Faddeev-Jackiw approach. The radiative magnon-photon interaction is encoded in the resulting Poisson-bracket structure, while the cavity-induced modification of the magnetostatic field enters explicitly in the Hamiltonian. The coupled system is diagonalized through a generalized Bogoliubov transformation, yielding canonical polariton variables that provide a natural basis for quantization. Application to a perpendicularly magnetized YIG disk in a rectangular microwave cavity shows hybridization between the cavity mode and several magnetostatic modes, together with appreciable cavity-wall-induced frequency shifts when the sample is placed close to the conducting walls.

Photon Statistics and Light Squeezing Under Vibrational Strong Coupling from Real-Time Nuclear--Electronic Orbital Theory

Millan Welman, Sharon Hammes-Schiffer

Molecular polaritons have attracted great interest in recent years as a promising avenue for developing optical control of chemical processes. A major open question in this field is the role of quantum optical effects in understanding the molecular dynamics observed in cavities. In this work, we use our recently developed mean-field quantum and full-quantum real-time nuclear--electronic orbital time-dependent density functional theory methods to simulate photon statistics for a cavity mode strongly coupled to a molecular vibrational transition. Our comparative use of these two methods allows us to isolate the contributions of light--matter entanglement to the photon statistics. We consider three initial conditions of the cavity mode: a coherent state, a squeezed coherent state, and a single photon Fock state. These conditions span Poissonian, super-Poissonian, and sub-Poissonian photon statistics. Our full-quantum dynamics initialized with a coherent state show considerable super-Poissonian fluctuations of the photon statistics and light-squeezing of the cavity photons by the vibrational mode. These dynamics are particularly notable considering the quasi-classical properties of the coherent state. The squeezed coherent and Fock state initial conditions also demonstrate noteworthy fluctuations in the photon statistics. All three initial conditions reveal dynamics in the full-quantum photon statistics that differ considerably from the statistics obtained with the mean-field quantum method. Our results demonstrate the importance of considering quantum optical effects for molecules under strong coupling in certain situations.

Light-induced chiral altermagnets from conventional antiferromagnets

Jorge Cardenas-Gamboa, Devika Jayadeep, Aritz Leonardo, Gregory A. Fiete, Jeroen van den Brink et al. (6 authors)

Altermagnets combine compensated collinear magnetic order with momentum-dependent nonrelativistic spin splitting, offering new opportunities for spintronic applications. Here, we propose a route to optically induce altermagnetism through coherent lattice control. Using symmetry analysis, we identify phonon distortions that lift the spin degeneracy of conventional antiferromagnets while preserving the rotational symmetries connecting opposite-spin sublattices. Among these, geometric chiral phonons provide a particularly appealing pathway, linking the emergence of altermagnetism to structural chirality. Taking Fe$_2$TeO$_6$ as a prototype, we demonstrate that a linearly polarized mid-infrared pulse can drive a structural instability through negative biquadratic coupling between an infrared-active phonon and an optically silent, symmetry-breaking mode. First-principles-based lattice dynamics reveal the emergence of a transient chiral distortion, while electronic-structure calculations establish that the light-induced structure exhibits nonrelativistic spin splitting characteristic of altermagnetism. Our results establish a symmetry-guided pathway toward the ultrafast optical control of altermagnetic states through nonlinear phononics.

Characterizing Reaction Types through Linear Free-Energy Relationships in Cavities

Jillian G. Hoffstadt, Davis M. Welakuh, Norah M. Hoffmann

Polaritonic chemistry holds the promise of controlling chemical reactivity through confined light-matter interactions, yet how reactions respond inside optical cavities remains debated. Here, we take a more zoomed-out view, inspired by electrostatic catalysis. We extend nonempirical linear free-energy relationships to cavity-modified reactions and show that their behavior falls into four distinct reaction types set by simple response-crossing rules. Reactions can follow conventional Bell-Evans-Polanyi (BEP) or counterintuitive Opposite-BEP behavior regardless of cavity tuning, or switch between the two as the cavity frequency is varied. We establish this picture analytically and demonstrate it in the Shin Metiu model and in four molecular reactions treated with ab initio electronic-structure methods. Even at this deliberately coarse-grained level, cavity-modified reactivity already shows qualitatively different and strongly counterintuitive behavior, including reactions that switch character with cavity frequency. Yet this complexity can still be reduced to a simple competition between permanent-dipole and polarizability responses. This reaction-type classification therefore provides a new route to organize and anticipate cavity-modified reactivity.
