GazeRIB: Systematic Evaluation of Multi-Reader Gaze-Guided Chest X-ray Learning

Nattakorn Kittisut, Roy M. Gabriel, Marly van Assen, Mohammadreza Zandehshahvar, Carlo N. De Cecco et al. (7 authors)

Radiologists' visual attention can supervise medical image learning, but how reader panel composition affects its value remains untested. We evaluated GazeRIB (Gaze-guided Radiologist-Informed Blackout), which masks unviewed regions during training and uses unmasked images at inference. Eight experts repeatedly read the same 80 chest X-rays (CXRs). We compared individual readers with three-, five- and eight-reader panels across seven architectures and 10 seeds, evaluating 910 models on a separate 2,897-CXR cohort for four-class severity prediction. Every panel improved macro F1 over the no-gaze baseline by 5.33-7.47 percentage points (pp; Bonferroni-adjusted p < 0.007); no individual reader reached significance on this endpoint. Three readers captured 73-80% of the eight-reader gain in macro F1, the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) and the area under the precision-recall curve (AU-PRC). On external validation without retraining, all four panels improved ROC-AUC on SIIM-FISABIO-RSNA by +1.93 to +4.24 pp (p <= 0.004); on MIMIC-Eye no panel differed significantly from baseline, whereas the single-reader average declined (-1.40 pp, p = 0.008). Reader composition therefore influenced performance, although transfer depended on the cohort.

Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models

Byungwoo Jeon, Yoonwoo Jeong, Hyunseok Lee, Minsu Cho, Jinwoo Shin

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. To bridge this gap, we introduce a Latent Reasoning framework in Continuous space (LARC), which alleviates the token-level bottleneck by reasoning over continuous hidden states. Specifically, the training framework consists of two stages: (i) curriculum supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and (ii) information-gain reinforcement learning (RL). First, the curriculum SFT stage gradually converts token-level reasoning into latent reasoning. Subsequently, the information-gain RL stage self-evolves latent reasoning without ground truth supervision. Experiments across diverse text-to-image generation benchmarks show that LARC consistently improves generation quality, prompt following, and compositional fidelity. Notably, LARC achieves state-of-the-art performance on GenEval with a 7%p gain over BAGEL and demonstrates clear test-time scaling behavior, outperforming token-based generation under both parallel and sequential test-time scaling strategies.

Flide: Accelerating Decision Inference with Exact State Forking

Bobo Li, Jiajia Song, Hao Fei, Wynne Hsu, Mong Li Lee

Decision models such as Jev have emerged as a highly promising paradigm for LLM agent systems, providing lightweight and reliable categorical reasoning for rapid System One execution. However, existing generation-oriented inference engines fail to support these workloads effectively, suffering from severe prefix recomputation overheads or distorted output probabilities. To address this challenge, we propose Flide, an efficient and exact inference engine that decouples the logical tree structure of a decision request from its physical execution within a single flattened token stream. Through ForkAttention, Flide navigates request dependencies using GPU-resident indices across both standard attention and recurrent Gated DeltaNet layers, guaranteeing in exact arithmetic that every query branch evaluates identically to an isolated forward pass. By expressing branch topologies directly in GPU-resident metadata, Flide accommodates diverse request mixtures with a compact set of pre-recorded execution graphs while enabling expanding agent histories to resume from checkpoints saved in earlier passes. Extensive evaluations on public hybrid and decision models demonstrate that Flide answers queries up to 9.5 times faster than vLLM and SGLang while staying closest to a float32 reference in total variation distance. By unifying physical execution throughput with mathematical exactness, Flide establishes a principled foundation for scalable, latency-critical decision intelligence in agent systems.

Latent Sparse Attention: Towards Subquadratic Ultra-Long Context Attention

Ales Kubicek, Andrea Caciolai, Liangzu Peng, Sukrit Sumant, Benjamin Hoover et al. (10 authors)

Agentic workflows are increasingly shifting towards long-horizon tasks, such as open-ended scientific work, where models benefit from long context windows. However, at ultra-long context (above 1M tokens), full attention is prohibitively expensive. A promising approach is sparse attention, in which a dedicated indexer selects a subset of keys for the attention. Yet current methods of indexing either still have quadratic cost in context size or lose accuracy at ultra-long context scale. We address this with Latent Sparse Attention (LSA), an efficient attention method that maintains accuracy at ultra-long context. In LSA, keys are hierarchically clustered in a learned latent space, and queries score the hierarchy of clusters instead of scoring every key, making prefill, decode and training faster at long context. We propose two variants, Distilled-LSA (D-LSA), which can be applied to pre-trained models via distillation, and Native-LSA (N-LSA), which is trained natively on the next-token loss alone. On GLM-5.3-FLASH (QWEN-3.8-27B) at 10M tokens, D-LSA speeds up one attention layer by 22.7x (72.4x) in prefill and 4.6x (42.5x) in decode over its original sparse attention (full attention), while keeping similar accuracy across a variety of tasks. N-LSA matches the accuracy of full attention with a training speedup of 1.6x per layer at 128k tokens. LSA offers practical and efficient attention for models to benefit from ultra-long context.

Hierarchical World Models with Implicit Dynamics

Gaoyue Zhou, Yvonne Wu, Zichen Jeff Cui, Nicolas Ballas, Mido Assran et al. (7 authors)

Planning with world models enables test-time adaptation for embodied agents, offering superior generalization over direct policy learning. However, long-horizon tasks remain hindered by cumulative errors and search complexity. Existing hierarchical methods often use rigid macro-actions or latent skills, leading to brittle representations and limited fine-grained control. We introduce ID-WM (Implicit Dynamics World Models), a hierarchical world model that decouples state-space reachability from local control. Our framework utilizes a high-level model to sample feasible transitions across the state manifold and a low-level world model as an inverse dynamics solver to ground these transitions into precise actions. This allows the planner to identify reachable subgoals without being constrained by low-level execution details. By prioritizing local physical feasibility over global behavioral patterns, ID-WM synthesizes novel long-horizon plans from short segments, transcending the specific behavioral modes seen during training. We demonstrate its effectiveness across three navigation and manipulation suites, achieving a 38.5% improvement over state-of-the-art policy and diffusion-based planners. Crucially, by leveraging this hierarchical structure to mitigate compounding errors, our approach yields a 135% performance gain over single-level world model MPC, particularly in complex, unseen configurations for long-horizon tasks. SemEllipsoid: Semantic-aware Shape Abstraction with Ellipsoid

Kuankuan Cheng, Haocheng Yuan, Changjian Li

Representing 3D shapes using a set of primitives - known as shape abstraction - is an actively studied problem in computer graphics with wide applications in shape understanding, analysis, and simulation due to the compactness and computational efficiency of the primitive-based representation. Prior work has primarily focused on geometry-aware shape abstraction, where the objective is to approximate the input shape with a minimal set of primitives while preserving geometric fidelity. However, these methods often overlook semantic consistency, inevitably leading to primitives that span across multiple semantic parts. Such inconsistency can cause undesirable artifacts in downstream tasks such as part-level shape editing or manipulation. In this paper, we introduce a new task - semantic-aware shape abstraction, where the goal is to produce abstractions that are both geometrically faithful and semantically consistent, ensuring that each primitive adheres strictly to a single semantic part without crossing semantic boundaries. We propose SemEllipsoid, a novel method to address this problem using ellipsoidal primitives. The core idea is to inject semantic constraints into the primitive optimization process leveraging the availability of semantic cues from large-scale foundation models. We design a fully differentiable projection-blending-evaluation scheme to measure semantic divergence against input semantic masks, and formulate an alternating optimization strategy to iteratively solve for optimal semantic assignment and primitive configurations. Our method is non-learning-based, requiring no data collection or network training, and generalizes well to shapes from arbitrary categories. It also supports different levels of abstraction according to user demand. Extensive experiments and evaluations validate the effectiveness of our approach across several 3D shape collections.

Dittany: Contribution-Guided Repair Framework for MAS Applications

Hanqi Sun, Yuchen Shao, Hanyi Si, Beibei Xu, Lisheng Wang et al. (6 authors)

LLM-based multi-agent system (MAS) applications can produce useful intermediate results while leaving task requirements unsatisfied. Repairing such executions requires identifying the work still needed and the completed results that can support it. We present \tool, a contribution-guided runtime repair framework for MAS applications. \tool derives expected contributions and acceptance conditions from task requirements and method specifications, and connects their dependencies in a contribution graph. Runtime checks locate unmet conditions and establish which results remain usable. These results guide action-level correction when available inputs suffice, or graph-level recovery when prerequisites or unfinished work need revision. Both repair scopes retain validated contributions and check repaired outputs before subsequent work uses them. We evaluate \tool on 30 applications across six MAS frameworks and 9,000 tasks. It detects runtime failures with 88.28\% precision and 79.18\% recall, and achieves 84.76\% task accuracy, improving over Native execution by 26.74 percentage points and the strongest of four recovery baselines by 21.28 points. Estimated deployment token consumption is 17.92\% above Native, but 24.19\% below Reflexion and 13.05\% below Iterative Rollback. Ablation results show that combining the two repair scopes achieves higher accuracy and lower token consumption than graph-level repair alone.

RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory

Shengguang Wu, Suning Huang, Cherie Ho, Ria Doshi, Hao Zhu et al. (6 authors)

Recent work shows that a frozen robot policy performs far better inside an agent harness, where control code decides when to query the policy, consult vision tools, or command the arm directly. Yet today these harnesses are built by hand and redesigned for each new robot or benchmark. We show that harness design can be automated, offering a strong alternative to finetuning on a novel setup. In RoboArchitect, a coding model observes a robot's rollouts, diagnoses failures, and iteratively revises the harness around the frozen policy. The harness evolves on a set of episodes for its optimization and is evaluated on held-out scenes and tasks. Without updating any model weights, RoboArchitect raises average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes (4.5x) and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks (3.2x), across multiple benchmarks and policies. The gains are largest where the benchmark is far from the policy's training data: wrapping a general-purpose policy, the evolved harness yields a specialist that often outperforms even the finetuned version. Intuitive control strategies are automatically designed, such as lifting a jammed arm clear or reaching in horizontally when an obstacle blocks the way from above. The harness also evolves its own persistent memory, carrying learned experience, such as effective grasp depths, into later decisions. These results suggest that substantial headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, and its design can be effectively automated and optimized without weight updates.

Counting on Dummies: Privacy Amplification with Observable Counts and Truncated Batches

Vitaly Feldman, Audra McMillan, Kunal Talwar

In many practical deployments of Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-SGD), the server processes data in batches. Randomly sampling these batches allows for better privacy bounds via privacy amplification results. Most sampling schemes with provable privacy amplification, including Poisson sampling and random allocation, result in variable batch sizes. The privacy guarantee of these schemes requires that the batch sizes are not revealed. In distributed settings, the server performing the aggregation or any party observing the communication traffic may learn these batch sizes. Furthermore, in both centralized and distributed settings, memory and computation constraints may require each batch to be of some maximum size $s$, necessitating truncation of the batch when it exceeds this limit. It is known that this truncation can result in a significant increase in privacy loss. In this work, we address both of these challenges via a simple mechanism that uses a random number of dummy points to privatize the batch size and maintain the privacy guarantee even under truncation. We demonstrate that our mechanism is easy to implement in a distributed setting, and provide a general analysis of this mechanism for both Poisson sampling and random allocation. Our analysis is designed to support efficient and relatively tight privacy accounting. It also effectively amplifies the privacy of the count releases by subsampling, making the expected number of dummy points asymptotically smaller than what is needed to directly privatize the (sensitivity-one) batch sizes with non-negative noise. For Poisson sampling with truncated batches, we demonstrate empirically that our mechanism achieves better privacy--efficiency tradeoffs than the direct accounting for truncation recently given by (Ganesh, 2025).

ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair

Assaf Singer, Maroun Khriesh, Noam Rotstein, Yoni Kasten, Or Litany

Dense 3D tracking from monocular video requires establishing point correspondences over time, estimating their depth, and inferring their trajectories during occlusion. Existing methods typically learn these tasks jointly, a demanding objective given the scarcity of 4D supervision. We show that strong pretrained 2D trackers and 3D geometry models provide an effective foundation: lifted trajectories, with occluded segments simply interpolated, already rival dedicated 3D trackers using the same geometry. However, lifted trajectories inherit correspondence and depth errors, and under occlusion, their depth comes from the occluder rather than the tracked point. We introduce ReLift, an efficient feed-forward framework that recasts dense 3D tracking as geometric trajectory repair and completion. At its core, a trajectory corrector alternates attention along and across trajectories to preserve reliable observations, repair corrupted positions, and complete missing motion. Using only geometric inputs allows us to train directly on 3D trajectories with simulated lifting errors, decoupling training from images and from any specific tracker or geometry model. Concentrating 4D supervision on trajectory refinement yields a reusable model that consistently improves lifted trajectories across trackers and geometry providers without retraining, so it benefits directly from future advances in either component. On WorldTrack, ReLift achieves state-of-the-art mean accuracy, improving on the strongest baselines by 2.1 APD on all points and 3.0 on dynamic points, with faster configurations remaining competitive with leading 3D trackers while running faster than 4RC.
