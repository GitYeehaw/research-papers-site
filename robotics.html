ProtoVis-Nav: Prototypical Visual Imagination for Visual-Spatial Aligned UAV Navigation

Guangyuan Sui, Yunzhong Hou, Chi Harold Liu

Vision language navigation (VLN) is very challenging for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) since small errors in 3D motion prediction can lead to navigation errors and collisions. Unable to fathom execution outcomes in 3D environments, existing methods often struggle to predict accurate navigation waypoints from language instructions. To solve this, we introduce the Prototypical Visual Imagination framework, shortened as ProtoVis-Nav, which forces the model to imagine an outcome-associated local visual representation for the intended next UAV movement. Specifically, we predict feature prototypes and use their linear combination to estimate the visual feature of the target region. In this manner, the VLN model first imagines the local visual representation of the next intended location, and then uses predicted prototypes as context for generating the final action, promoting alignment between visual context and waypoint outputs. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the ProtoVis-Nav framework and show substantial improvements over existing baselines.

DiSKO: Distribution Steering for Nonlinear Systems using the Koopman Operator

Dimitrios Moustroufis, Panagiotis Tsiotras

This paper studies the problem of data-driven covariance steering for stochastic, nonlinear dynamical systems. We utilize Koopman operator theory to learn, directly from data, an approximate linear representation of the nonlinear dynamics in a lifted space of observable functions of the state. By exploiting this linear model, we design controllers for the mean and covariance of the underlying nonlinear system by utilizing linear distribution steering algorithms. To account for approximation errors in the learned dynamics and the ambiguity in the disturbance distribution induced in the lifted space, we formulate the problem as a distributionally robust covariance steering problem. Furthermore, we leverage a deep neural network (DNN) to simultaneously learn the Koopman observables and the linear model parameters, thus eliminating the need for manual observables basis selection while also reducing the dimensionality of the lifted representation. Additionally, we can promote desirable model properties, such as stability, during DNN training. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework via numerical simulations.

Fictitious Play When Agents Are Not Expected-Utility Maximizers: A Geometric Analysis in $2$-Action Games

Hao Liang, Solaleh Mohammadi, Kaiqing Zhang

Fictitious play (FP) is one of the earliest and most fundamental learning procedures in games, where each agent independently chooses a best response to the empirical distribution of opponents' past actions by maximizing the expected payoff with respect to it. However, learning agents may not always be expected-utility maximizers: they may use nonlinear evaluation functionals to account for the uncertainty induced by the opponents, e.g., via risk measures. In this work, we study nonlinear fictitious play (NL-FP), in which each pure action is evaluated by a general, possibly nonlinear functional of the payoff distribution induced by the opponent's empirical belief. To analyze NL-FP, we first identify a single-crossing property (SCP) of evaluation functionals that preserves the classical geometry of FP in $2\times2$ games. Furthermore, we conduct a novel geometric analysis of the convergence behavior of NL-FP in $2\times2$ games, even when the SCP fails. Beyond $2\times2$ games, we show that the convergence of NL-FP in $2\times n$ ($n\ge3$) games may fail even for continuous-time NL-FP, when the two-action player uses conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) in a $2\times3$ game. In addition, we identify a finite-crossing condition that ensures convergence of the selected continuous-time dynamics and the discrete-time iterates in general $2\times n$ games, which also provides new insights into the global landscape of continuous-time classical FP in such games.

HEIR: Harness Egocentric Intent for Human-Robot Interactions

Yichi Zhang, Bo Wang, Zhenghan Zhu, Yutong Han, Jiayin Zou et al. (14 authors)

For robots to assist, serve, and collaborate with humans, they must not only interpret human requests conveyed through language and multimodal intentional cues, but also execute long-horizon tasks that interleave navigation and manipulation. In this paper, we introduce HEIR, a resource for studying how to Harness Egocentric Intent for Human-Robot Interaction. HEIR pairs human speech, measured gaze, and egocentric video with robot observations and teleoperated navigation-manipulation trajectories, while each session preserves successive episodes, with each request fulfilled through multiple execution subtasks. The captured data, together with human-authored annotations, support the study of intent grounding, execution planning, and task completion within a unified interaction setting. To demonstrate the value of HEIR, we conduct extensive offline experiments to evaluate how models benefit from additional human-centric context across these tasks, together with real-robot experiments that assess execution task completion. HEIR thus provides a valuable resource for studying user-centric robot interaction in naturalistic household environments. Our demo and dataset are available online.

FanCyqlone: A Parallelized Solver for Multi-Horizon Optimal Control

Matisse Teuwen, Pieter Pas, Mathijs Schuurmans, Panagiotis Patrinos

Multi-horizon optimal control aims to select optimal control sequences simultaneously for several possible future scenarios, ensuring consistency at the first stage. While this enables advanced model predictive control schemes that explicitly handle prediction uncertainty, solving these problems tends to be costly. We introduce FanCyqlone, a linear solver that exploits parallelism across both time steps and scenario branches. The method builds on Cyqlone to compute the contribution of each branch and combines these results through a shared root problem. We integrate FanCyqlone into QPALM to develop FanCyQPALM, a solver for multi-horizon optimal control problems with general linear stage-wise constraints. We compare its performance with state-of-the-art solvers on spring-mass and quadruped control benchmarks.

Collaborative Task Allocation for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Systems Through Iterative Clustering

David R. Martin, Brooks A. Butler, Scott Nivison, Magnus Egerstedt, Mohammad Abdullah Al Faruque et al. (6 authors)

Multi-robot systems face the challenge of efficiently allocating teams of heterogeneous robots to tasks. The task allocation problem is complicated by collaborative interactions between robots where teams of robots develop emergent capabilities that enable them to complete tasks that would be inefficient or impossible for individual robots. To address these challenges, we present an iterative clustering algorithm for collaborative task allocation in heterogeneous multi-robot systems. This approach partitions the computationally intractable global optimization problem into smaller, tractable subproblems by iteratively forming clusters of robots and tasks, then optimizing assignments within each cluster. By ensuring robots remain clustered with their currently assigned tasks, we guarantee monotonic improvement in overall utility with each iteration. We analyze the convergence of the algorithm and characterize how cluster size constraints determine which suboptimal assignments could trap the algorithm. In simulation, iterative clustering consistently outperforms simulated annealing, and a group-based auction in both computation time and solution quality, and outperforms a hedonic game approach in solution quality.

Generalized Gated Recurrent Neural Networks for Nonlinear System Identification

Stefano De Carli, Davide Previtali, Mirko Mazzoleni, Fabio Previdi

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks are the two most widely adopted recurrent neural networks for black-box nonlinear system identification, yet they are invariably treated as mutually exclusive structural choices. To move toward a general framework that inherits the properties of both networks, we introduce the Generalized Gated Recurrent Neural Network (GGRNN), a unified architecture that embeds the exact GRU and LSTM network dynamics through homotopies between their respective gating mechanisms. Each homotopy continuously deforms one mechanism into the other, with a vector of homotopy parameters blending the contribution of the GRU and LSTM counterparts. Since benchmark comparisons report no consistent winner between the two architectures, the gating mechanism best suited to a given system is unlikely to coincide with either endpoint. We thus let each recurrent unit learn its own position along these homotopies, obtaining gating behaviors that neither endpoint can express. We pair the GGRNN with a two-stage training methodology: in Stage 1 we co-train all shared and network-specific parameters in parallel at the GRU and LSTM endpoint architectures, while in Stage 2 we freeze the network parameters and optimize only the homotopy parameters. Experiments on the Wiener-Hammerstein and Nano-Drone benchmarks show improved simulation accuracy over the co-trained GRU and LSTM networks on most output groups, and over the reference black-box baselines in nearly all comparisons.

UAV-Flow++: A Unified Suite for Benchmarking and Learning Language-Guided UAV Flight

Donglin Yang, Xiangyu Wang, Yantong Zhong, Wenhao Zheng, Zihan Ding et al. (9 authors)

Language-guided UAV navigation has largely focused on long-horizon route planning, leaving short-range, object-relative flight maneuvers underexplored. To address this gap, we formulate Flying-on-a-Word (Flow), a task requiring a UAV to convert concise natural-language instruction, egocentric visual observation, and current state dynamics into continuous flight actions. Flow encompasses both primitive motion commands and complex object-interactive behaviors, assessing motion intent comprehension and spatial context grounding. To enable policy learning and closed-loop evaluation, we introduce UAV-Flow, a comprehensive dataset comprising 30,692 expert-piloted real-world trajectories across eight distinct motion categories, alongside 10,109 simulation trajectories in the UAV-Flow-Sim environment equipped with task-specific completion criteria. Furthermore, we develop FlowVLA, which undergoes imitation pretraining on mixed real-sim demonstrations and online post-training via Tail-Aware GRPO (TGRPO). By leveraging group reward distributions, TGRPO effectively reinforces high-reward action sequences. Evaluated on 273 held-out UAV-Flow-Sim episodes, pretrained FlowVLA achieves a mean success rate of 75.58%, outperforming the strongest baseline by 6.72 percentage points, which TGRPO post-training further elevates to 93.16% with superior sample efficiency. Physical UAV experiments demonstrate robust execution of object-relative commands in both indoor and outdoor real-world scenes. Together, Flow establishes a novel benchmark and learning framework for language-conditioned flight maneuvers.

XBot-Omni: An Omni-Modal Model for Embodied Understanding and Interaction

Kerui Gu, Xing Fu, Shuoyang Sun, Cheng Peng, Jingru Duan et al. (13 authors)

Embodied interaction requires an agent to integrate multimodal perception with verbal and nonverbal behaviors to effectively communicate its intentions, including speech, body motion, and facial expressions. We present XBot-Omni, a Thinker--Talker--Actor architecture that extends the Omni paradigm to robotic modalities, including body motion and facial expressions, with ``X'' representing the extensibility to robotic-specific modalities. The Thinker processes text, images, audio, and video to formulate contextually appropriate responses, which are then realized by the Talker and Actor through speech and coordinated bodily expressions, respectively. In this report, we innovatively introduce the concept of ``Actor'' as a dedicated component for embodied agents: a coordinated, streaming module that leverages the native representations of the Thinker and Talker within an end-to-end pipeline, introducing only modest latency overhead. This unified architecture enables multimodal generation conditioned on omni-modal inputs directly to prevent information loss in staged designs. We comprehensively evaluate XBot-Omni across seven benchmarks, demonstrating strong multimodal understanding capabilities on both general-domain and robot-centric videos, as well as high-quality, natural, and well-coordinated motion generation. Furthermore, our results validate its capability to generate embodied multimodal responses directly from omni inputs, meeting the diverse requirements of embodied interaction. XBot-Omni enables robots not only to perceive the omni world but also to express themselves through speech and bodily behaviors, extending Omni models from conversational interaction to embodied communication in physical environments.

Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning

Benjamin A. Christie, Tianyu Qiu, Amy H. Choi, David Fridovich-Keil, Dylan P. Losey

Robots that learn from demonstrations should extrapolate their policy despite state perturbations such as sensor noise, scene variation, and compounding errors. One approach to this robustness is regularization. Prior regularizers consider each state on its own and limit how sensitive the policy is to any perturbation at that state. By contrast, we explore how the policy's output shifts when given the same perturbation at different states. Our insight is that, locally, the robot should respond to state perturbations in a consistent manner: e.g., if changing an irrelevant object position does not impact the policy at one state, that same change should not shift the policy output at a nearby state. Applying this insight, we develop inner product regularization (InneR). We show that the inner product on action space induces an inner product on state space through the policy Jacobian (the Fisher information for stochastic policies), and that the null space of this inner product governs which state perturbations have a first-order effect on the policy. By clustering states whose inner products are aligned, averaging the inner products within each cluster, and minimizing the smallest eigenvalues of that average, InneR shapes the learned policy so that nearby states share a common null space. InneR enforces consistent responses to perturbations without limiting policy expressiveness, and can be appended to existing imitation learning pipelines for deterministic or stochastic policies. In simulated experiments, InneR makes learned policies more robust to irrelevant state dimensions and shifts between training and test distributions, whereas norm penalties often perform worse than behavior cloning. On a real robot arm performing three manipulation tasks, InneR raises the average success rate of a diffusion policy from 18% to 57% without additional data.
