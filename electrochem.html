Molecular structure breaks universality in screening behavior of electrolyte solutions

Anna T. Bui, Connie J. Fairchild, Dominic P. W. Thomas, Stephen J. Cox

Electrostatic screening is one of the most fundamental phenomena in electrolyte solutions. While dilute electrolytes are described by the universal Debye--Hückel theory, recent experiments have reported an apparently universal scaling law for screening lengths in concentrated electrolytes, fueling ongoing debate over anomalous underscreening. Yet, liquid state theory has been unable to determine whether molecular details of the solvent, the ions, or both should preserve or break such universality. Using machine-learned classical density functional theory, we compute numerically exact decay lengths across a hierarchy of electrolyte models of increasing molecular complexity. We find that molecular details qualitatively reshape screening: solvent attraction generates a new monotonic decay mode, solvent polarity produces re-entrant behavior, and ionic structure alone can drastically alter screening-length exponents. Consequently, no single scaling law describes screening universally across these molecularly distinct electrolytes. Our results challenge equilibrium interpretations of experimentally observed universality in concentrated electrolytes.

A Microwave-Photonics Formulation of the Rydberg-Atom Receiver

David Rosser

The performance of Rydberg-atom receivers is seldom stated as a radio-frequency (RF) link budget. The receiver is shown to be an intensity-modulated direct-detection analog optical link whose modulator is the laser-driven vapor. The transmission of a warm vapor is derived in closed form by averaging the four-level response over atomic velocities. The conversion gain, noise figure, bandwidth, intercept points, and spurious-free dynamic range follow from this one function. For a weak probe the instantaneous bandwidth is half the width of the transparency window in two-photon detuning. The local-oscillator beat places the second-order intercept near twice the local-oscillator field, so the local oscillator sets the even-order linearity. For a rubidium transition at 17.04 GHz the closed forms agree with a velocity-resolved density-matrix model to 1% for the slope and the intercepts and to 10% for the bandwidth. They underestimate the group delay by a third. The response is tied to atomic constants, and the sensing volume escapes the Chu limit. However, the noise figure of a warm vapor rules it out as a general replacement for an antenna and low-noise amplifier.

Linear Response Theory of Nitrogen K-Edge X-ray Absorption of TCNQ

Alberto Boccuni, Vita Ilakovac, Stéphane Carniato, Jacques Kontak-Desmarais

The linear response of the density is computed in the frequency domain within the generalized Kohn--Sham framework of adiabatic time-dependent density functional theory, employing hybrid exchange-correlation functionals. It is shown that the generalized Casida equations imply a Dyson-like equation for the linear-response of the full frequency-dependent one-electron reduced density matrix of the reference GKS system. We implement this formalism in the \textsc{Crystal} code and apply it to the nitrogen K-edge near-edge X-ray absorption fine structure (NEXAFS) of 7,7,8,8-tetracyano-$p$-quinodimethane (TCNQ), the acceptor in the charge-transfer salt TTF-TCNQ. We interpret the spectrum through a step-by-step construction by building blocks of increasing complexity. This procedure separates the effects of $π$ conjugation, of multiple equivalent absorbing centers and of the ring on the in-plane ($π_\parallel$) and out-of-plane ($π_\perp$) resonances. The theoretical result is confirmed by a comparison with angle-resolved experiments. In contrast with earlier assignments based only on the ground-state density of states, we find that the lowest-energy resonance is dominated by the ground-state KS LUMO contribution. Indeed, comparison with the independent-particle approximation shows that the exact-exchange contribution to the kernel is essential for a correct assignment of the peaks.

Prediction and characterisation of cation topology of pentanary high-entropy alloy (MoWNbTaV)S$_2$

Shubham Agarwal, Tahir Wahab, Jesper Byggmästar, Antonio Cammarata, Tomas Polcar

High-entropy transition-metal dichalcogenides offer properties tuned by composition, yet two basic questions remain open: which arrangement of the metals forms under synthesis conditions, and how it can be recognised in experiment. Density functional theory (DFT) cannot sample the vast space of possible arrangements, and the ground-state energies usually employed to screen them do not take into account the temperatures at which these materials are made. This work solves both issues for the pentanary (MoWNbTaV)S2 monolayer with a Gaussian approximation potential trained on DFT data by iterative learning. Gibbs free energy calculations reveal that vibrational entropy reshapes the stability ranking, while five selected configurations retain their trigonal-prismatic framework during nanosecond molecular dynamics up to \qty{1300}{\kelvin}. Raman and infrared spectra combine parent-monolayer signatures with disorder-activated modes, providing fingerprints for experimental identification. % The predicted lattice parameter follows Vegard's law and agrees within \qty{0.8}{\percent} with X{-}ray diffraction on a sputtered alloy film, which identifies the solid solution but not the arrangement of its metals: distinguishing between arrangements requires the vibrational fingerprints. The potential and its development workflow enable further atomistic simulations and offer a transferable approach to screening related two-dimensional high-entropy materials.

Voltage-Controlled Dielectric Cavity Magnonics in Epitaxial Fe Films on SrTiO3 Single Crystal Substrates

Stijn Reniers, Ekaterina Gorokh, Lukas Nulens, Andries Boelen, Christian Haffner et al. (9 authors)

Cavity magnonics relies on the hybridization between collective spin excitations and well-defined photonic or mechanical cavity modes, enabling fast and efficient coupling of spintronic devices to microwave circuits and optical information carriers. In this work, we present a hybrid material heterostructure that combines an intrinsic dielectric microwave cavity with a ferromagnetic medium, establishing magnon-photon interaction with coupling rates in the order of 0.1-1 GHz. Demonstrating effective voltage-control of the hybrid mode spectrum at cryogenic temperatures, we achieve a highly tunable photo-magnonic material, that could provide significant advancements for high-performance and power-efficient cryogenic RF-applications, including hybrid quantum technology, superconducting electronics and nonlinear microwave devices for unconventional computation and signal processing.

Heat cleaning kinetics and thermal degradation of MOCVD-grown GaAs photocathodes

Gregory Blume, Adam Masters, Colin Kirk, Sushil Poudel, John Hill et al. (10 authors)

We evaluated metalorganic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD)-grown GaAs/GaAsP strained-superlattice photocathodes with integrated distributed Bragg reflectors in a low-voltage Mott polarimeter at Jefferson Lab. Without a protective arsenic cap, surface contamination can develop during transport and installation. Heat cleaning removes this contamination by thermally desorbing material bound to the surface, but excessive heating can also damage the photocathode. We modeled quantum efficiency (QE) in terms of surface contaminant coverage to describe its increase with heating time. QE rose more rapidly and reached a plateau nearly twice as high at $550^\circC$ as at $450^\circC$, while polarization remained near 83%. Further heating reduced QE and polarization. Reflectance measurements showed a shift of the optical resonance to shorter wavelengths, consistent with surface material loss. We also tested the cleaning method in a high-voltage photogun, where QE showed the same initial increase and subsequent decline. The cleaning procedure must therefore balance contaminant removal against loss of photocathode material.

Shift current as a stacking spectroscopy of hexagonal boron nitride multilayers

Sanber Vizcaya, Juan M. Florez, Eric Suárez Morell

Out-of-plane polarization can be tuned by sliding polar monolayers in van der Waals materials; the bulk photovoltaic effect is emerging as a natural optical probe, provided the photocurrent tracks the polarization. We test that assumption by computing the polarization and the shift-current tensor from first principles across the stacking landscape of bi-, tri-, and tetralayer hexagonal boron nitride. The two quantities share one robust property: the vertical response reverses with the polarization while the in-plane one does not. Beyond this rule, the correspondence must be read with caution: stackings with identical polarization carry vertical monochromatic photocurrents of opposite sign; the spectra reorganize and even reverse sign at some photon energies along a constant-polarization plateau; and the strongest vertical peak of the entire pathway sits where the polarization crosses zero. The reason is structural: the polarization counts polar interfaces, while the shift current reads their positions. Broadband illumination reads the domain sign; monochromatic photocurrent maps can read the stacking registry. In a sliding ferroelectric, the bulk photovoltaic effect can operate as a stacking spectroscopy.

General Transport Signatures of Odd-Parity Magnets: Charge--Spin Selection Rules and Temporal--Spectral Separation

Junxi Yu, Pu Zhang, Yichen Liu, Yongpan Li, Cheng-Cheng Liu

While even-parity altermagnets are known for spatially separating charge and spin currents via the crystal spin Hall effect, the defining transport counterpart for odd-parity magnets remains to be established. Here, we establish temporal and spectral charge--spin separation as a general transport signature across all spin-splitting classes of collinear and noncollinear odd-parity magnets with unitary spin-flip inversion $\mathcal U=[C_2^\perp\Vert P]$. This symmetry restricts charge responses to odd orders and $z$-polarized spin responses to even orders, independent of microscopic mechanism and without requiring spin conservation. For ac drives that reverse sign after half a period, charge and spin currents exhibit opposite half-period parities, and their spectra contain only odd charge and even spin harmonics. This allows dc spin rectification without a dc charge counterpart, even in longitudinal transport and beyond weak fields. We show that although antiunitary $\mathcal Tτ$ enforces odd-parity bands, it reverses the charge--spin selection of Berry-curvature and Berry-curvature-dipole responses, providing a direct transport fingerprint distinguishing the two protecting symmetries. We verify the selection rules for all linear and second-order transport mechanisms and demonstrate harmonic separation in a minimal odd-parity model, representative Floquet-engineered collinear MnPS$_3$, and the equilibrium noncollinear VCl$_2$ family.

Atomistic theory of the $d$-wave phonon angular momentum splitter effect

Daniel A. Bustamante Lopez, Dominik M. Juraschek

Phonons offer a bosonic route to angular-momentum transport in solids, but how their momentum-space textures manifest in transport remains unclear. Here we develop an atomistic theory of the phonon angular momentum splitter effect in an antiferromagnetic square lattice with checkerboard-modulated elastic couplings, analogous to the spin-splitter effect in $d$-wave altermagnets. We calculate the transport and accumulation of phonon angular momentum driven by a temperature gradient and find that the transverse response separates into Hall and $d_{xy}$-wave splitter contributions. The splitter contribution requires both the antiferromagnetic order and the checkerboard elastic modulation and exhibits a characteristic $d_{xy}$ angular dependence, whereas the Hall contribution is nearly isotropic. Their combination enables directional control of the edge phonon angular momentum accumulation.

Silver/alumina-capped Janus particles: a dispersible hyperbolic metamaterial

Samhita Kattekola, Raymond R. Dagastine, Alexander Couzis, Ilona Kretzschmar

Hyperbolic metamaterials (HMMs) made of alternating layers of metal and dielectric have been heralded as promising materials for super-resolution imaging and spontaneous emission enhancement sought after in biological applications. However, the practical utilization of HMMs has been limited, primarily by the solid and stiff nature of the underlying substrate, usually a silicon wafer or glass substrate. An ability to transfer HMMs to colloidal substrates would introduce dispersibility to HMMs and potentially enable their use in medical applications while extending the curved HMM geometry to discrete particles. Here, spherical particles with alternating metal and dielectric layers are used to fabricate hyperbolic metamaterial-capped Janus particles (HMMJPs). Alternating layers of silver (metal) and alumina (dielectric) films are evaporated onto polystyrene particle monolayers with and without fluorescent properties to study the impact of particle curvature on the layer structure in the cap. The thickness and number of layers are chosen such that the final cap film resembles the structure of a 4P-HMM film, four periods of alternating silver/alumina, previously characterized as an HMM at wavelengths above 403 nm. The fluorescent character of the polystyrene particles is used to illuminate the pathway of light in the HMMJP clusters as a function of cluster size and geometry. Subsequently, resonance imaging microscopy (RIM) with the HaloElement is used to independently verify the HMM nature of a single HMMJP.
