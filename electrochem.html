Joint Neural Posterior Estimation and Model Selection for Chemical Reaction Networks

J. Arturo Esquivel, Jorge A. Campos-Gonzalez-Angulo, Sergio Pablo-García, Alán Aspuru-Guzik, Vianey Leos-Barajas

Simulation-based Bayesian inference for Chemical Reaction Networks (CRNs) must simultaneously solve two computationally demanding problems: discriminating between overlapping reaction pathways and estimating reaction rate constants. Classical approaches, such as Sequential Monte Carlo ABC, rely on handcrafted summary statistics and scale poorly across large parameter spaces. To overcome this, we introduce a neural inference architecture. Our pipeline utilizes shared 1D-CNNs and a permutation-invariant Set Attention mechanism to learn low-dimensional summary statistics from variable-length, multi-experiment time-series data. These statistics simultaneously condition a model selection network to compute posterior model probabilities and model-specific conditional Invertible Neural Networks (cINNs) that estimate parameter posterior distributions. We demonstrate the practical utility of our approach on a real protodeboronation example, illustrating how it handles structural ambiguity and extracts kinetic parameters from competing catalyst deactivation pathways.

Embossing of Tapered, Isometric Micro-Interdigitated Flow Fields for Enhanced Desalination by Faradaic Deionization

Md Habibur Rahman, Sadia Afrin, Kyle C. Smith

Engineered transport architectures can improve electrolyte distribution and active-material utilization in porous electrodes, yet their implementation remains limited by fabrication methods that compromise precision, throughput, active-material preservation, or scalability. Here, an integrated embossing-calendering method is introduced to pattern Prussian blue analog (PBA) porous electrodes with embedded, micro-interdigitated flow fields (e$μ$-IDFFs) for electrochemical desalination. This non-subtractive, roll-to-roll compatible method forms channels by controlling plastic deformation while simultaneously calendering the electrode, preserving active material and reducing patterning time by 100-fold relative to mechanical milling. Indentation experiments and a closed-form model further show that embossing requires 15-fold less force than stamping, while electrode microstructures show local densification beneath embossed channels. PBA electrodes are embossed with novel e$μ$-IDFFs that use continuous width-tapering with isometric flow paths that promote uniform electrolyte distribution, increase rate capability, and reduce polarization. Compared with e$μ$-IDFFs patterned using less scalable means and non-isometric flow paths, these e$μ$-IDFFs desalinate seawater-salinity feeds near to potable levels with 1.5-fold of its thermodynamic energy efficiency and water recovery. Brackish desalination benefits similarly. Tapered, isometric e$μ$-IDFFs and the embossing method introduced to pattern them are readily translated other electrochemical energy storage and separations systems.

Engineering Color and Angular Stability in Building-Integrated Photovoltaics Using Metal-Oxide Photonic Glasses

Sinan Genc, Talat Ozden, Alpan Bek

Coloring building-integrated photovoltaics poses a dilemma: absorptive pigments cost photocurrent, while ordered photonic structures are iridescent. Amorphous photonic glasses, disordered packings of high-index nanospheres, are proposed to avoid both, yet their angle-independent color has rarely been quantified on a realistic module stack. A non-absorbing scatterer needs a high index with visible transparency, which the Moss rule caps near 2.35. Coupling Mie scattering, the Percus-Yevick structure factor, the exact phase function, and two-flux transfer with absorption and refraction at the front glass, we fix four target colors that a crowd-sourced naming survey calls unambiguously blue, green, gold, and terracotta, and map the recipes reaching them across four metal oxides, four encapsulants, two cell technologies, and wide ranges of particle size, packing fraction, and layer number. All four are reached in a laminated three-layer coating, to within DeltaE00 of 0.8 to 2.5, but not from one oxide: transparent TiO2 renders the blue and only pale warm tones; the green, gold, and terracotta require the blue-band absorption of V2O5, and the low-contrast oxides turn grey at the warm end. The encapsulant governs the color, and the cell technology does not. Disorder does not confer angle independence: the colors hold for an observer moving under a fixed sun but drift by DeltaE00 of 5 to 18 over the day, terracotta least, and a facade lit from above and viewed from below shifts every color towards the blue. Oxide, layer number, and facade geometry must therefore be designed together.

Ensuring Complete Consistency between Hops and Wavefunction Propagation in Fewest-Switches Surface Hopping

Jonathan R. Mannouch

Fewest-switches surface hopping (FSSH) is a popular and versatile approach for performing dynamical simulations beyond the Born-Oppenheimer approximation, in which nonadiabatic effects are incorporated through stochastic hops of the active potential energy surface. However, the FSSH algorithm does not ensure that hops are completely consistent with the underlying electronic wavefunction dynamics. This can lead to significant errors as well as requiring small propagation time steps, which can significantly compromise the computational efficiency of the method. These problems are known to be particularly severe at so-called trivial crossings, for which the nonadiabatic coupling is entirely localized at the crossing point. Here, we solve the trivial-crossing problem by developing a fully-consistent hopping-probability formula, which generates attempted hops that match the time-evolution of the wavefunction across any size time step by construction. A state-tracking algorithm is also utilized to ensure that attempted hops are always accepted at trivial crossings. Through application to systems that contain extremely challenging trivial crossings, we demonstrate that this hopping scheme is capable of obtaining highly accurate results, even when large propagation time steps are used.

Superexchange coupling between a dilute Gd3+ probe and orphan spins in a valence-bond glass

Patrick Colgan, Artur Solodovnyk, Jeremy P. Carlo, Scott Dietrich

The valence-bond-glass state of the 4d^1 double perovskite Ba2YMoO6 combines Mo^5+ singlets with residual paramagnetic "orphan" spins whose coupling is hard to reach with bulk probes. We probe them by electron paramagnetic resonance of a dilute Gd^3+ ion on the Y^3+ site via broadband coplanar-waveguide transmission at 5 K. The isostructural nonmagnetic niobate Ba2YNbO6, doped with the same Gd^3+ probe, is a fixed-lattice control. The Gd^3+ linewidth nearly doubles in the magnetic host. Dipolar coupling to the sparse orphans is nearly an order of magnitude too weak; the excess is instead reproduced by a transferred f-d superexchange of J ~ 0.2 K, so the width is a static measure of orphan-spin coupling. The half-field (Delta m_S = +/-2) satellite collapses in the magnetic host, a dynamical dephasing channel, while the unchanged g-factor marks a zero-mean random internal field. Together the two channels probe the coupling and dynamics of valence-bond-glass orphan spins.

Composition-controlled optical fingerprints and anisotropy of self-trapped excitons in double perovskites

Simone Grillo, Offir Zachs Maishlos, Liya Koren, Rotem Strassberg, Rachel Lifer et al. (10 authors)

Double perovskites (DPs) offer a compositionally versatile platform for photocatalysis and optoelectronics, but their soft lattices favor strong exciton-phonon coupling and self-trapped excitons (STEs), which produce broad, red-shifted photoluminescence (PL). Here, we combine optical spectroscopy and ab initio calculations to study lead-free, Sb-doped Cs2BInCl6 nanocrystals with B-site alloying from Na to K. Alloying tunes the PL energy from about 2.48 to 2.70 eV, while absorption features near 3.8 eV remain nearly unchanged. Calculations reveal that Sb doping enables bright radiative recombination channels and delocalizes the excitonic wavefunction. We identify STEs responsible for the large PL red shift, associated with an upward shift of dopant-hybridized in-gap states. Na-to-K substitution selectively changes the STE lattice distortion, explaining why PL varies strongly with composition while absorption does not. Theory further predicts pronounced PL polarization anisotropy that reflects the STE wavefunction's spatial structure and depends on B-site composition. Time-resolved anisotropy measurements closely agree with these predictions, providing experimental fingerprints of the excitonic wavefunction and its evolution with alloying. Together, these results disentangle the roles of doping and alloying in lead-free DPs and establish polarization-resolved spectroscopy as a probe of STE wavefunctions.

Extending the mean first-passage-time formalism to the reconstruction of high free-energy barriers: A top-start approach

Ying Jiang, Ivan Saika-Voivod

Rare events, such as crystal nucleation in mildly supercooled liquids, are characterized by waiting times that can be much longer than the timescale of the underlying microscopic dynamics. The mean first-passage time (MFPT) formalism [J. Wedekind and D. Reguera, J. Phys. Chem. B 112, 11060 (2008)] provides a route to reconstruct one-dimensional free-energy profiles from the MFPT and steady-state distribution measured along a reaction coordinate, but conventional implementations become inefficient for sufficiently high barriers because spontaneous crossings are rarely observed. We introduce a "top-start" extension that initiates trajectories near the barrier top and reconstructs the free-energy profile from their subsequent evolution away from the barrier toward lower-free-energy regions. We establish the equivalence between the top-start and two-region constructions using a Markov-chain transition-matrix formulation. We validate the method using one-dimensional random-walk simulations across low, medium, and high free-energy barriers, with transition-matrix calculations providing an exact benchmark for the corresponding discretized models. The top-start reconstruction remains accurate in the high-barrier regime where conventional MFPT sampling becomes prohibitively inefficient.

Does Pretraining Teach Chemical Language Models Chemistry?

Leif Sieben, Yoel Zimmermann

Chemical language models (CLMs) are pretrained on large corpora of molecules with the aim of learning representations that transfer to diverse downstream tasks. Here, we systematically study the effects of pretraining using a series of such CLMs across 64 molecular property prediction datasets. We find that the downstream gains of pretraining in CLMs do not scale and performance saturates below gradient-boosting baselines. Across the regimes evaluated, only supervised pretraining on traditional physico-chemical descriptors consistently improves downstream performance for the CLMs tested whereas some forms of pretraining result in models worse than a randomly initialized encoder. While models can resolve molecular structure well, they do so no better than a randomly initialized model, suggesting that, at best, CLMs learn descriptor-like properties in addition to low-level token statistics during pretraining, but surprisingly little chemistry beyond what classical molecular features already capture.

Time-Resolved Phonon Dispersions of Thermally Excited Graphite at the Nanoscale

Mairi McCauley, Christoph T. Koch, Benedikt Haas

Lattice vibrations govern the propagation of heat in materials, and investigating their dynamics through time-resolved phonon dispersion measurements would provide insight into these excitations and their interactions. However, time-resolved measurements that directly resolve the phonon dispersion at the nanoscale have not been previously demonstrated. Momentum-resolved vibrational electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) provides access to phonon energies and momenta with nanoscale spatial resolution. Here, we combine momentum-resolved vibrational EELS with pulsed laser heating and synchronised gated electron detection to observe the phonon dispersion of graphite during controlled heating and cooling on the microsecond timescale. Recording both the gain and loss spectra at defined times relative to laser excitation, we determine the temperature at each time delay and follow changes to the phonon branches as a function of temperature. The momentum-resolved spectra reveal a decrease in energy of the optical phonon branch with increasing temperature accompanied by an increase in peak width. These changes are consistent with temperature-dependent phonon renormalisation and scattering associated with anharmonic interactions. The approach is compatible with detector gating on timescales down to 50 ns, providing a route towards extending these measurements to faster dynamics. This work demonstrates time-resolved phonon dispersion spectroscopy using vibrational EELS and provides a route towards probing temperature-dependent phonon dynamics at the nanoscale with simultaneous energy and momentum resolution.

Direct growth of crystalline spinel GaON films: A wide-bandgap mixed-anion semiconductor

Baikui Li, Zecheng Yang, Wenhao Yin, Zijing Wu, Haitao Zhao et al. (8 authors)

Mixed-anion semiconductors offer opportunities for bandgap engineering beyond conventional multication alloys, but competing phases and disorder complicate their synthesis and the identification of their electronic properties. Gallium oxynitride (GaON) could connect the technologically important GaN and Ga2O3 systems, yet establishing a clear relation between its crystal structure, composition and bandgap remains challenging. Here we report the direct growth of crystalline spinel gamma-GaON films by nitrogen-plasma-assisted pulsed-laser deposition. Varying the nitrogen and oxygen supply yields monoclinic beta-Ga2O3, predominantly disordered films and crystalline spinel GaON. Complementary diffraction, atomic-resolution microscopy and elemental analysis establish the spinel structure and nitrogen incorporation. Temperature-dependent photocurrent spectroscopy reveals a clear redshift of the crystalline gamma-GaON response, supporting assignment of a band-edge transition near 4.3 eV at room temperature. These results open opportunities to investigate mixed-anion wide-bandgap semiconductor physics and develop heterostructures.
