CFTs with Large Virasoro Primary Gaps

Ziming Ji, Jiahua Tian

We construct the first explicit examples of compact unitary bosonic full conformal field theories (CFTs) in two dimensions with a unique vacuum and an all-spin Virasoro-primary gap greater than two. Our constructions use asymmetric orbifolds of lattice CFTs built from even unimodular lattices of sufficiently large minimum norm, ensuring that no charged state lies below the desired gap. The gauged symmetries combine a common orthogonal action on the left- and right-moving currents with independent sign reversals; the latter project out the dimension-two scalar primary $\sum_i J_L^iJ_R^i$ that survives when the symmetry acts identically on both chiral halves. We prove that the gauging is anomaly-free and determine the exact gaps by analyzing the invariant untwisted spectrum and all twisted sectors. From lattices constructed using ternary quadratic-residue codes, we obtain an infinite family with gap three at $(c_L,c_R)=(p+1,p+1)$ for primes $p\ge71$ with $p\equiv23\pmod{24}$. From Barnes-Wall lattices, we obtain an infinite family with gap four at $(c_L,c_R)=(2^m,2^m)$ for odd $m\ge7$. We also obtain two additional examples, with gap three at $(c_L,c_R)=(48,48)$ and gap four at $(c_L,c_R)=(512,512)$.

Expressivity of two-dimensional Gaussian fermion tensor network states

Pok Man Tam, William Musk, Zhehao Dai

Tensor network states (TNS) provide a family of efficiently compressed approximations to physically relevant many-body quantum states. An important quantity that characterizes the computational resources required by a TNS is its bond dimension. While in one dimension an area law for the Renyi-0 entropy guarantees an exact matrix product state representation with a bond dimension independent of the system size, an analogous statement does not hold for general states in two dimensions (2D). This leaves open the question of how the expressivity of 2D TNS is related to the entanglement properties of the states they represent. In this work, we focus on two families of 2D translation-invariant Gaussian fermionic states: single-band BCS superconductors and half-filled two-band insulators (including semimetals). For each family, we prove that an area law for the Renyi-0 entropy is equivalent to having an exact Gaussian fermionic TNS representation with a finite bond dimension determined by the Renyi-0 entropy. We also establish equivalent characterizations in terms of compactly supported Wannier-type orbitals and the momentum-space wavefunction, relating the spatial extent of these orbitals and the structure of the wavefunction to the required bond dimension.

StringQFT: A Multiuse Textbook Resource for AI-Assisted Theoretical Physics

Xingyang Yu

We present StringQFT as a textbook collection for string constructions of quantum field theory and as infrastructure for AI-assisted theoretical physics. The collection comprises 18 volumes, 190 chapters and 3,795 pages, covering brane probes, brane webs, geometric engineering, F-theory constructions, wrapped M5-branes and exact worldsheet constructions. Its open-book benchmark inventory contains 1,073 items, including 499 with automatic finite-answer checking. For retrieval-augmented generation, it provides 1,371 section records linked to textbook sources and PDF locations, together with a local lexical retriever. Training-data preparation is illustrated by 21 private, source-linked examples. For agents, it exposes 19 calculation tools with specified inputs and finite domains, connecting tool use to textbook derivations. We illustrate these uses through specific questions, retrieved passages and finite calculations. The textbook collection and accompanying resources are available at https://github.com/xingyang-yu/StringQFT.

From nonlocal correlations to local symmetry breaking: Asymmetry dynamics of crosscap states

Konstantinos Chalas, Pasquale Calabrese, Colin Rylands

Under unitary dynamics preserving a global symmetry, bipartite entanglement typically grows while an initially broken symmetry is locally restored. Here, we show that this conventional scenario can be reversed when symmetry-breaking coherence is initially encoded in long-range entanglement. We investigate this phenomenon in a family of charge deformed crosscap states, characterized by antipodal entangled pairs with tunable entanglement and symmetry breaking. We first characterize their static properties, finding a Page-like entanglement profile and an entanglement asymmetry that vanishes for subsystems smaller than half the system size, but grows logarithmically for larger subsystems. For symmetric subsystems, we also analyze the symmetry-resolved entanglement and investigate how it evolves as the subsystem is continuously deformed away from a symmetric partition. Following a quench to a free-fermion tight-binding chain, we uncover an unconventional dynamics in which the entanglement entropy decreases whereas the entanglement asymmetry increases, so that initially symmetric subsystems dynamically develop local symmetry breaking. This behavior is quantitatively captured by a quasiparticle picture describing the propagation of antipodal correlations. Our results reveal how symmetry-preserving dynamics can redistribute globally encoded symmetry-breaking coherence, making it locally accessible, and establish the initial entanglement structure as a key ingredient in determining the fate of local symmetries out of equilibrium.

Complete Cosmological Collider Signatures from Right-handed Neutrino Loops

Linghao Song, Jingtao You, Ming Cheng, Hong-Jian He, Xingang Chen

We study the complete cosmological collider (CC) signatures from right-handed neutrino loops with chemical-potential enhancement. These CC signals are induced by the inflaton-right-handed-neutrino interaction and can be extracted in the collinear squeezed configuration, which allows the right-handed-neutrino triangle loop to be factorized into the hard and soft parts. We precisely compute the soft part of the fermion loop for the first time by using the complete mode functions and the full loop integral, and treat the hard part as a tree-level subdiagram. We subtract the large-momentum asymptotic terms of the soft loop integrand and retain their finite contributions to the oscillation. We then evaluate the full spinor-helicity and Schwinger-Keldysh sums to obtain a finite, closed analytic formula for the complex clock coefficient. We further derive a compact formula for the leading CC signals in the large-chemical-potential expansion. We analyze the structure of the soft-part contribution by examining the sum of residues of the Mellin poles of the fermion propagators. For this, we demonstrate that the first local pole on each soft line contributes half of the asymptotic coefficient on one Schwinger-Keldysh path and the remaining local poles on the second soft line supply the other half.

Modeling quantum information scrambling in realistic protein NMR experiments

Alec F. White, Joseph Haslehurst, Daniel R. Moberg, Justin Provazza, Nicholas C. Rubin et al. (7 authors)

Out-of-time-order correlators (OTOCs) are important probes of operator spreading and information scrambling, and have recently been proposed as a means of extracting molecular structural information through nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). Realizing this potential requires accurate modeling of OTOCs in complex molecular environments within experimentally relevant settings. Here, we develop an end-to-end computational pipeline connecting molecular structures to OTOC dynamics in protein systems. Our framework incorporates molecular motion through molecular dynamics simulations, orientational averaging appropriate for powder samples, and spin-network truncation, using an experimentally realizable double-quantum effective Hamiltonian. We apply this framework to a dataset of over 350 carbon-carbon spin pairs across 10 protein systems, spanning interspin distances of 5-17 A. We find that OTOC arrival times scale approximately linearly with interspin distance, revealing system-dependent operator-spreading velocities that enable correlations to propagate over extended distances within milliseconds. Our sensitivity analysis further shows that individual OTOCs predominantly constrain a single structural parameter, primarily associated with interspin distance. Together, these findings highlight the potential of OTOCs to probe spatially extended spin correlations on timescales compatible with solid-state NMR experiments.

Local interference algorithms for observables in large random circuits

Philippe Suchsland, Brayden Ware, Vladislav D. Kurilovich, Vadim Smelyanskiy, Kostyantyn Kechedzhi

In quantum dynamics, it is generally believed that interference effects lead to a sign problem for classical Monte Carlo sampling methods, rendering sampling ineffective. In this paper we show that this is generically not the case for the task of simulating the evolution of observables in random instances of circuit dynamics. Specifically, we consider two examples of observables which give large signals despite the randomness of the dynamics: first order out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs) in unstructured random circuits without conservation laws and time-ordered charge-charge correlators (TOCs) in particle number-preserving random circuits. We show that these observables can be approximated by classical stochastic processes and that these approximations can be systematically improved with a series expansion that incorporates coherent corrections associated with clusters of gates. We design and implement algorithms that combine Monte Carlo sampling and tensor network methods to approximate the series. We observe numerically that the computational complexity of approximating TOCs and OTOCs scales polynomially in the circuit size and in the target precision. We study the accuracy of these algorithms by directly mapping their ensemble-averaged errors to partition functions of replica statistical models, allowing for perturbative analysis and numerics in $1$d brickwork geometries. Our theoretical analysis explains the empirical observation of polynomial complexity of these algorithms and is consistent with polynomial complexity extending to arbitrarily small precision and to the thermodynamic limit.

A Universal Ratio Linking Cosmic Expansion and the Growth of Structure

Anjan A Sen, Purba Mukherjee

We identify a universal relation between the onset of cosmic acceleration ($z_t$) and the turnover ($z_{\rm pk}$) of the linear growth observable $fσ_{8}$. In a spatially flat $Λ$CDM Universe, these two redshifts obey $R \equiv (1+z_t)/(1+z_{\rm pk}) = 1.1596$, independent of the present matter fraction and the overall expansion and growth normalisations. This provides a new consistency test for $Λ$CDM, linking both background and growth dynamics. We further establish the conditions under which this extends to broader classes of dark energy and modified gravity theories.

Nonlinearity-preserving quantum simulation of flows in the early fault-tolerant regime

Hansol Noh, Unam Oh, Sangkyu Baek, Doyeol, Ahn

Ensemble prediction of nonlinear dynamics propagates a probability density whose linear transport equation retains the nonlinear drift, but grid-dependent circuit costs leave end-to-end feasibility in early fault-tolerant quantum computing unresolved. We couple polynomial reduced-order models fitted by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics to Walsh-synthesized Koopman-von Neumann (KvN) transport and surface-code resource estimation. At fixed model dimension and drift degree, per-step transport rotation counts scale polylogarithmically with cells per reduced coordinate. Balancing model accuracy against circuit costs makes reduced-model polynomial degree a design variable for early fault-tolerant quantum simulation. For a cylinder wake approaching saturation, preparation, propagation and sampling to 1% relative root-mean-square field error against the radial model are estimated to take 1.62 h on about 560,000 physical qubits. Estimated three-coordinate wake runtimes span hours to weeks below one million qubits. IBM hardware experiments demonstrate nonlinear radial KvN transport for this wake, using reference circuits to mitigate noise.

Performance analysis of a Rydberg atom THz radiometer concept

Richard J. Murchie, Petronilo Martin-Iglesias, Julian Hofmann, Christophe Buisset

THz receiver technology lies between electronics and photonics; as such, its implementation is not as mature. This warrants investigation into alternative THz receivers such as Rydberg atom sensors, which are highly susceptible to electric fields. In particular, the conversion architecture has been demonstrated to coherently transduce thermal radiation. This system offers room-temperature operation and does not necessarily require a local oscillator at the frequency of the input signal. We provide an assessment of the feasibility of such sensors for THz radiometry within the context of Earth observation.
