Characterizing supersolid phase transitions in trapped quantum gases with tilted dipoles at finite temperature

Julian Kusch, Lauriane Chomaz, Wyatt Kirkby

Motivated by a recent experimental work $[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20327}{arXiv:2608.20327}]$, we theoretically investigate the nature of phase transitions in a trapped dipolar quantum gas upon tuning the contact interaction strength and dipole orientation. Using extended Gross-Pitaevskii simulations including thermal and quantum effects, we map the phase diagram comprising unmodulated superfluids, triangular droplet arrays, and stripe states. We further characterize the order of the transitions separating them through imaginary-time hysteresis analysis. The unmodulated-to-modulated transition evolves from discontinuous to continuous with increasing dipole tilt before becoming discontinuous again at large tilts. The degree of continuity is further affected by particle number, temperature, and confinement. While continuous behavior generally correlates with the emergence of stripe states, this correspondence is not systematic, and finite-size effects also influence the continuity of the transition. The triangular-to-stripe transition is found to be discontinuous at small contact interaction strengths and becomes increasingly continuous upon approaching the unmodulated-to-modulated phase boundary. Our results reveal a tunable transition order in finite dipolar quantum gases and provide guidance for its experimental investigation.

Cumulative locality governs collective quantum charging

Sebastián V. Romero, Xi Chen, Yue Ban

Quantum batteries can outperform their classical counterparts through collective quantum effects, yet the conditions enabling such advantage remain unclear. Using a generic class of free-fermionic chargers we prove that global operators are insufficient for scalable superextensive charging and that a cumulative hierarchy of many-body operators is required, with the structure of the battery determining the degree of advantage. This is obtained by relating the charging power to the norm of the commutator between the battery and charger Hamiltonians and deriving an upper bound only capturing noncommuting terms. Moreover, we revisit the concept of participation number and propose an effective definition that accounts for the relative strength of the contributing terms, correlating it with the charging power scaling. Not limited to quadratic Hamiltonians, our results establish cumulative locality as a key resource for quantum charging advantage.

Dynamical stability of Magnetic Supramassive Neutron Stars

Jorge L. Rodríguez-Monteverde, Sebastiano Bernuzzi, William Cook, Maximilian Jacobi, Brynmor Haskell

We investigate the dynamical stability of strongly magnetized supramassive neutron stars close to the mass-shedding limit using fully general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations in three spatial dimensions. Equilibrium configurations are constructed using a microphysical equation of state and considering poloidal magnetic fields up to maximum strengths ${\sim} 10^{17}\,G$. The initially unstable magnetic fields reorganize into mixed poloidal--toroidal configurations on Alfvén timescales shorter than the stellar rotation period. The subsequent dynamical evolution is governed by timescales set by the Lehnert number. For stars with masses sufficiently lower than the maximum mass, magnetic stresses redistribute angular momentum and drive the development of differential rotation, leading to a new quasi-stationary configuration that remains dynamically stable over several rotation periods. Magnetically-driven winds develop from the outer layers around the equatorial plane and remain baryon-matter dominated with magnetization ${\lesssim}0.2$. Over the simulated timescale, winds carry a total rest mass of order ${\sim}10^{-3}M_\odot$ at outflow rates of up to a few $M_\odot/s$. For stars close to (or beyond) the maximum-mass turning point along the stellar equilibrium sequence, strong magnetic fields trigger gravitational collapse by redistributing angular momentum away from the stellar core. The resulting black hole remnants are characterized by angular velocities up to ${\sim}49\%$ larger and angular momentum ${\lesssim}8\%$ smaller than those of the initial equilibrium stars. Our results show that extreme magnetic-field instabilities can significantly alter the structure of supramassive neutron stars, with possible implications for magnetars and long-lived binary neutron-star merger remnants.

Slow-Roll Inflation at Arbitrary Order

William Giarè

We derive explicit expressions for the primordial scalar and tensor spectra at arbitrary finite order $p$ in the slow-roll expansion, valid both for canonical single-field inflation and $k$-inflation. At any order, the spectra are determined by known combinations of slow-roll parameters weighted by universal coefficients entirely fixed by the free de Sitter solutions, without requiring perturbatively corrected mode functions. Using these spectra, we obtain closed-form consistency relations that connect the tensor tilt $n_t$ and its runnings $α_n^t\equiv d^n n_t/d(\ln k)^n$ to the tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$, the scalar spectral index $n_s$, and its runnings $α_n^s\equiv d^n(n_s-1)/d(\ln k)^n$. The consistency relations are valid for arbitrary $n\geq 0$ and any finite $p\geq 0$, without recursion over lower-order relations. For $k$-inflation, they additionally depend on the scalar sound speed and its scale derivatives. We develop an algorithm that generates both the spectra and consistency relations for any pair $(n,p)$. We reproduce known results through next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N3LO) and extend the explicit calculations up to N10LO.

Complete Modified Logarithmic Sobolev Inequalities for one-dimensional quantum Spin Chain

Li Gao, Jingyu Guo, Sihao Wang

We prove a complete modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality with a system-size-independent constant for Chen--Kastoryano--Gilyén Gibbs samplers on one-dimensional spin chain at every fixed positive temperature. The result holds for Hamiltonians with bounded finite-range interactions, including noncommuting and spatially nonuniform interactions. It yields trace and diamond mixing times of order $O(\log N+\log(1/ε))$. The proof establishes complete spatial entropy factorization and absorbs quasi-local errors as conditional relative entropy at the original joint state and reference. The static estimate also yields a system-size-independent CMLSI for the single-site Petz heat-bath and regularised heat-bath dynamics.

Entanglement Distribution in Quantum Switch Networks: Memory Limits and Achievability

Lei Hu, Alptug Aytekin, Stavros Mitrolaris, Mohamed Nomeir, Subhankar Banerjee et al. (7 authors)

We study the minimum persistent memory required to distribute a graph state through a quantum switch. Specifically, consider a central switch that assists $n$ remote users in distributing a graph state, using independently generated user-switch Bell pairs. Since Bell-pair generation is probabilistic, different links generally succeed at different times. When a Bell-pair is generated, the qubits may need to be stored in memory while waiting for other links to succeed. Recently, new protocols have been proposed to reduce this storage overhead by processing and releasing some switch qubits before all Bell links are available. However, these protocols do not characterize the minimum persistent switch memory required at each stage. Moreover, the local-cover method in the state-of-the-art Piecemaker protocol is computationally expensive for general target graphs. In this work, we characterize the minimum persistent switch memory required after any subset of users' Bell links are generated and processed. We then develop an online stabilizer-transition protocol that attains this limit, which can be implemented in polynomial time in the number of qubits $n$. Remarkably, this optimal protocol can provide substantial memory reductions over existing heuristic approaches. In particular, for the path graph $P_n$ over $n$ users, Piecemaker protocol requires at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ qubits of peak persistent memory for every Bell-links generation order, whereas under the order $(1,2,\ldots,n)$, our protocol requires only one persistent switch qubit. Finally, the same stabilizer-transition framework also provides a direct forward construction for sequential graph-state generation using the minimum number of quantum emitters, equal to the maximum of the height function.

Monopole stability of time-dependent, spherically symmetric backgrounds in Horndeski theory

K. Kiselev, V. Volkova

We study linear perturbations about an arbitrary time-dependent, spherically symmetric background in the most general Horndeski theory. The odd-parity sector of such perturbations, analyzed previously, contains no scalar field perturbation and, hence, leaves the related degree of freedom unconstrained. In this paper, we address this issue by analyzing even-parity monopole ($\ell = 0$) perturbations, which isolate this degree of freedom. We derive the quadratic master action for the only propagating physical mode associated with the scalar field and formulate stability criteria which ensure the absence of ghost and radial gradient instabilities for the high-energy modes. These conditions are necessary for the stability of the background and complement the odd-parity ones. We show that they reproduce the known results in the static and cosmological limits. We also demonstrate that, in the cubic subclass of Horndeski theory on a dynamical background, the monopole criteria impose no constraints beyond those previously obtained for modes with large multipoles ($\ell \gg 1$). As an illustration, we apply the derived stability conditions for the monopole mode to a non-minimally coupled scalar field theory and to a black hole model with linearly time-dependent scalar hair; in the latter case, the monopole conditions further restrict the parameter space allowed by odd-parity stability.

NeST: ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes with sub-$50μ$s stabiliser cycles via spin shuttling

Adam Siegel

Quantum low-density parity-check codes now reach encoding rates up to 50% at useful distances. Their checks however are long-range group actions, not local neighbourhoods, and that connectivity has so far been supplied by neutral atoms. Silicon spin qubits offer the same primitive --- coherent transport of qubit blocks --- through electron shuttling, and on microsecond rather than millisecond timescales. They however travel along fabricated tracks rather than free space: permutations an atom array obtains by moving tweezers must here become collision-free motions on a physical lattice. We here present the first code and spin-qubit hardware co-design. We build an architecture whose geometry mimics that of a family of non-abelian groups, broad enough to host codes of excellent parameters: a hierarchy of nested conveyor-belt loops on a plain square lattice of shuttling tracks, in which every group multiplication becomes a rigid, globally controlled motion of whole qubit trains. We call it NeST, for Nested Shuttling Tracks. A code search over the family returns rate-20% mitten codes up to $[[1400,280,\leq 28]]$, and circuit-level simulations including shuttling dephasing give thresholds above $10^{-3}$ with sub-$50μ$s stabiliser cycles, two to three orders of magnitude faster than neutral-atom implementations. Via extrapolations, we estimate that this largest code may reach a logical error probability per qubit per round of $10^{-14}$, assuming a ten-fold improvement on early shuttling demonstrations, with other physical errors at p=0.1%. Logic is compatible with NeST too: for the [[150,30,10]] code we build a code-surgery gadget in which every qubit again moves only by rigid belt motions, at no qubit cost and a 29% increase in gate layers. This demonstrates that ultra-low-overhead fault tolerance and a fast clock speed are not competing goals, and that silicon spin qubits can supply both.

Towards differential expansion for generic representations

Ya. Kononov, A. Morozov

We report the first results of phenomenological analysis, based on extended set of explicitly calculated colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials in \cite{knotebook}. They concern differential expansion (DE), which separates the dependencies on the knot ${\cal K}$ and the representation $R$. %of colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials. DE is well understood for symmetric and rectangular representations, while for non-rectangular $R$ even the knot-independent basis $Z_R^X$ of DE is not known. We concentrate on two issues: the possible derivation of DE and on improved characteristic of its defect. We discuss how evolution families -- twist knots, double braids and 3-pretzels -- can be used to find DE by a peculiar triangular transformation of evolution coefficients. Once $Z^R_X$ are found for particular family, they are expected to stay basically the same for arbitrary knots -- modulo controllable reductions, characterized by the defect of DE, which puts knots into different universality classes. Contrary to original expectation, these classes appear labeled not by a single number $δ^{\cal K}$, but by a whole non-decreasing sequence $\{k_j\}$, which is squeezed between two staircases, ${\rm entier}\left(\frac{j-1}{δ+1}\right)\leq k_j\leq{\rm entier}\left(\frac{j-1}{{\rm deg}\,Δ}\right)$, where the upper bound involves Alexander polynomial $Δ(q^2)$ and follows from the continuation of the Melvin--Morton--Rozansky-Kashaev limit to $A=q^{-m}$ with $m>0$, when the limit gives a {\it positive} power of Alexander. The old assumption $δ+1={\rm deg}\,Δ$ is oversimplified and fails for 19 non-alternating knots with 11 and 12 crossings. Last, we list the differences of non-rectangularly colored HOMFLY for various mutant pairs to demonstrate their universality (knot-independence) and nice consistency with the spirit of DE.

Efficient Ground-State Manifold Amplification for Rydberg Atom Arrays via Houdayer Cluster Updates

Andrew J. Ochoa, Martin J. A. Schuetz, Helmut G. Katzgraber

We demonstrate a hybrid quantum-classical approach for exploring ground-state manifolds of maximum independent set problems, in which quantum hardware supplies the initial solutions and an entirely classical post-processing algorithm expands them, increasing the solution diversity of quantum hardware measurements by up to several orders of magnitude. Quantum optimization devices, while capable of generating high-quality solutions, can suffer from fundamental sampling limitations. This is particularly problematic for applications that strongly depend on a large variety of uncorrelated optimal solutions. We apply the Houdayer cluster update algorithm to systematically expand quantum-generated solution sets through classical post-processing. We validate our approach on a test bed of 43 problem instances from QuEra's Aquila analog annealing device, achieving diversity amplification factors, defined as the increase in the number of distinct solutions at a given set size, of up to 11923x with a median of 24.3x across different problem scales and hardness parameters. Because exact ground-state degeneracies are known for these instances, the recovered fraction of the manifold can be measured rather than bounded: post-processing recovers a median of 21% of the ground-state manifold, and provably all of it for six instances. We also demonstrate that, in some cases, the classical post-processing can discover independent sets larger than those found by the quantum hardware, thus improving on solution quality and not just diversity. While the method is non-ergodic and cannot guarantee complete manifold coverage, it consistently discovers a larger number of high-quality, and in some cases strictly better, configurations than quantum sampling alone. This work demonstrates how simple classical post-processing can enhance the utility of quantum optimization results.
