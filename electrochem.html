Solute-Aware Stochastic Korhonen Model of Electromigration in Wide vs Near-Bamboo Al-Cu Lines

Narahara Chari Dingari, David R. Heskett

We couple an evolving Cu field to the one-dimensional Korhonen stress equation on random grain maps for wide (10 $μ$m), narrow (3 $μ$m) and near-bamboo Al-Cu lines. Earlier random-microstructure simulations fixed the Cu effect, and most continuum solute models used prescribed structures with all Cu mobile; we join the two and let only dissolved Cu move, the excess staying as immobile precipitate. With $10^3$ maps per condition at 200 $^\circ$C, we report nucleation times and sites, immortal fractions and Black's-law parameters. In cathode-limited lines Cu depletion shortens the median nucleation time 1.15- to 1.20-fold relative to immobile Cu for the cited Cu diffusivity; treating all Cu as mobile overstates this a further 2.24- to 2.36-fold at 2 wt%; the exponent stays near 2. In wide lines with reservoir ends, Cu steps left by the immobile precipitate become the failure sites, removing their Cu-free immortality if a quarter or more of the precipitate blocks Al diffusion. With Cu above the solvus the lifetime is not Arrhenius, as the dissolved fraction rises with temperature: a Black's-law fit at 250 to 300 $^\circ$C overestimates the median nucleation time at 150 $^\circ$C up to 2.33-fold at 2 wt% Cu with an exponent near 2. At the test durations the model qualitatively matches the cathode-side damage in narrow lines and the Cu build-up and mid-line peaks in wide lines but not the cathode-side Cu peaks or longer lives of narrow lines.

Terahertz detection of magnon chirality in polycrystalline CsCuCl$_3$

Juan S. Rodríguez-Hernández, Gabriel F. Freitas, Nicolas M. Kawahala, Rafael S. Freitas, Felix G. G. Hernandez

In antiferromagnets, the handedness of magnon precession is usually measured on single crystals. In a pressed pellet of the chiral helimagnet CsCuCl$_3$, whose grains carry both structural handednesses and all orientations, we found that, from 5 to 7 T, the sub-terahertz magnon absorbs one circular polarization more than 20 times as strongly as the other, which rules out precession about each grain's own axis. Spin-wave theory attributes this to the Zeeman frequency, here two to four times the exchange-enhanced gap, which locks the precession axis to the field in every grain. Magnon chirality in antiferromagnets is thus accessible without a single crystal.

Interface-Engineered Piezoelectricity and Electromechanical Coupling in Two-Dimensional van der Waals Heterostructures

Shivanand Yadav, Raihan Ahammed, Umer Ali, B. S. Bhadoria, Yogesh S. Chauhan et al. (7 authors)

Interfaces between dissimilar two-dimensional (2D) materials provide a route to engineer electromechanical responses that are not intrinsic to the individual constituent layers. Here, we use high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) calculations to investigate 461 van der Waals heterostructures constructed from 57 2D monolayers and establish a materials-design framework linking work-function mismatch, interfacial charge redistribution, dipole formation, and strain-dependent electromechanical response. We find that heterostructure formation generates out-of-plane interfacial dipoles via charge redistribution between the constituent layers, while biaxial in-plane strain systematically modulates them. The resulting heterostructures exhibit two distinct regimes: metallic interfaces with strain-tunable interfacial dipoles and semiconducting heterostructures with well-defined piezoelectric responses. For the latter, Berry-phase (BP) calculations independently validate the charge-density-difference (CDD) approach, which is generally applicable to all representative systems, with piezoelectric coefficients calculated via two methods agreeing to within 10%. Among 24 selected heterostructures, effective biaxial interfacial electromechanical coefficients reach 83.35 pCm-1 . These results demonstrate that the interface can serve as the active element governing electromechanical coupling and provide a computationally guided strategy for designing strain-tunable out-of-plane electromechanical responses in 2D van der Waals heterostructures.

Donnan Grand Canonical Monte Carlo Simulations with Fast Ewald Summation for Calculating Individual Ionic Chemical Potentials

Alexandre P. dos Santos, Amin Bakhshandeh, Yan Levin

We present a Donnan Grand Canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulation method using fast Ewald summation to accurately and efficiently calculate the individual ionic excess chemical potentials of $α_+:α_-$ electrolyte solutions. By introducing a dynamic inner electrostatic potential that acts to penalize net charge fluctuations and maintain average charge neutrality ($\langle Q_t \rangle = 0$), the method enables single-ion GCMC trial moves without sacrificing convergence speed or breaking thermodynamic consistency. Unlike the Widom particle insertion method---which suffers from severe phase-space sampling bottlenecks and requires separate virtual insertions for each ionic species---the Donnan GCMC protocol simultaneously yields the individual excess chemical potentials $μ_+^{ex}$ and $μ_-^{ex}$ corresponding to a resulting bulk concentration $c_b$ with rapid numerical convergence. Varying the reference activity parameter $c_r$ allows us to efficiently construct the complete $μ_\pm^{ex}(c_b)$ curves. Comparison with the Mean Spherical Approximation (MSA) demonstrates excellent agreement for size-symmetric electrolytes, while highlighting the failure of linear-response theories for size-asymmetric ions where electrostatic correlations and cavity effects become strongly coupled.

Generalized dielectric-dependent hybrid functional combining Gaussian and Yukawa screening

Alexey Tal, Fabien Tran, Wei Chen, Stefan Riemelmoser, Alfredo Pasquarello

We develop a generalized Coulomb-attenuated method (GCAM) for dielectric-dependent hybrid functionals that combines Gaussian and Yukawa-type screening functions to provide an accurate representation of the random-phase approximation (RPA) dielectric function. Through systematic fitting to RPA dielectric functions of semiconductors and insulators, and to the Lindhard dielectric function of the homogeneous electron gas, we demonstrate that GCAM significantly reduces errors in the model dielectric function across the full momentum range. However, despite this improved physical description of screening, band gap calculations reveal that GCAM yields larger errors compared to experiment than previous CAM approaches for wide-gap materials, but smaller errors for most narrow-gap materials. This counterintuitive result demonstrates that the success of CAM stems not purely from physical reasons, but also from a partial cancellation of errors. In contrast, for optical absorption spectra calculated within a model Bethe-Salpeter equation framework, GCAM systematically outperforms CAM, showing better agreement with the reference spectra obtained with the diagonal RPA screening in both peak positions and relative intensities. Our findings reveal the important role of error cancellation in dielectric-dependent hybrid functionals.

Quantifying Errors in Chemical Kinetics

Skyla B. White, Paul B. Rimmer, Miles Cramner, Alex T. Archibald

The physical structure of kinetic measurements determines the correct statistical framework for quantifying their uncertainties, yet this framework has never been explicitly derived or systematically applied in the chemical kinetics literature. Here, we derive a forward model for kinetic observations from a minimal set of stated assumptions about the measurement process, and identify the correct statistical treatment for each kinetic parameter as an explicit consequence of that model. We then audit the literature pertaining to a well-studied reaction, examining the kinetics of permanganate oxidation, to establish the scale of deviation from the correct framework. We find that of 330 measurements, 35.8% carry no uncertainty quantification of any kind and none are accompanied by a full propagation of all uncertainties. To demonstrate the practical consequences of departing from the correct framework, we reanalyse a representative kinetic dataset, first checking whether the data are consistent with the forward model and then applying the correct procedures. We find that the impacts are modest for the dataset examined, as expected for quantities determined in a low-uncertainty regime, but that uncertainties in activation energy are systematically underestimated when observed rather than assumed measurement precision is used as the basis for regression weights. We highlight that these consequences become substantially more significant at the limits of current experimental methodologies, where measurement uncertainty is large and the correct distributional treatment can no longer be approximated by standard approaches. Based on this, we propose a set of minimum recommendations for error reporting in chemical kinetics that follow directly from our forward model.

Molecular Pawns on a Surface Chessboard: Computational Investigation of Nanographenes with Atomistic Methods

Antonio Lorenzo Mariano, Jacopo Lupi, Emilio Lorini, Matteo Briganti

Atomically precise nanographenes combine tunable electronic topology with localized, coherent spin states, making them candidate platforms for molecular quantum technologies. This review examines the multiscale theoretical framework governing these systems: the hierarchy of computational methods for describing magnetic properties, the substrate-molecule interface, on-surface synthetic kinetics, and supramolecular self-assembly. We highlight how density functional theory, automated reaction-path discovery, and spin-aware machine-learned interatomic potentials connect static electronic structure to mesoscale dynamics, and how magnetic exchange, optical excitations, and spin transport can be predicted before synthesis.

Altermagnetism of MnSiN2 and MnGeN2 in a wurtzite-based crystal structure

Ilteris K. Turan, Jonathan Agrinya, Walter R. L. Lambrecht

Based on symmetry analysis, the Pna2_1 wurtzite-based crystal structure with Mn as a magnetic group-II element, MnSiN2 and MnGeN2 belong to the class of altermagnets because spin-up and spin-down Mn in the unit cell are related by rotation or mirror plane type operations rather than inversion or translation. Spin polarized density functional theory calculations and quasiparticle self-consistent (QS) GW calculations support the symmetry analysis and identify the lowest-energy spin configuration as Pna'2_1' in agreement with neutron diffraction data. The altermagnetic band splittings allowed for the three different antiferromagnetic ordering patterns are reported and symmetry analyzed. Additionally, spin wave dispersions, chiral magnon spltting, and Neel temperature estimates from the mean-field and random-phase approximations are provided, as well as calculations of the optical dielectric function using the Bethe-Salpeter Equation method. MnSiN2 exhibits both Wannier-type excitons with N-p to Si-s transition character and charge-transfer Frenkel-type excitons with hole on N-p and electron on Mn-d orbital character.

Analytic nuclear gradients for electronic resonances with the equation-of-motion coupled-cluster method and complex basis functions

Robin E. Moorby, Thomas-C. Jagau

We present the general theory for and an efficient implementation of the analytic nuclear gradient for states which are metastable with respect to electronic decay within the equation-of-motion coupled-cluster singles-and-doubles family of methods using complex-scaled basis functions. We highlight the differences of our approach with the complex-absorbing potential method and outline a computational protocol for geometry optimisations and we use the method to compute equilibrium structures and harmonic vibrational frequencies of a number of temporary anion shape resonances and core-ionised Feshbach resonances.

Real-time optimization with online learning for pressure swing adsorption

Carine de Menezes Rebello, Marcio Fontana, Leizer Schnitman, Idelfonso B. R. Nogueira

Real-time optimization (RTO) of pressure swing adsorption (PSA) units requires a process model that can be evaluated many times at each decision, which favours data-driven surrogates of the cyclic steady state. A surrogate, however, represents the plant only under the conditions covered by its training data, and disturbances that are not among its inputs remain outside its description. This work presents an RTO framework for PSA in which a neural network surrogate, optimized by particle swarm, is updated online with data generated at the plant when plant and model diverge. A rigorous dynamic model of a CH$_4$/CO$_2$ separation, integrated to cyclic steady state, served as virtual plant in three scenarios. In the first, five consecutive RTO events with different objectives ran without disturbance; all constraints were satisfied and the largest deviation between surrogate and plant was 1.7 percentage points. In the second, a step in the CO$_2$ feed fraction from 0.40 to 0.50 reduced the CH$_4$ purity at the operating point in force from 92.3 to 76.9%; after the network was fine-tuned on 43 local samples, the new RTO event returned a feasible optimum with a purity of 90.3%. In the third, a progressive loss of sorbent capacity was detected by the accumulated relative deviation of the CH$_4$ purity from a reference, triggering retraining before any constraint violation. Samples generated across the remaining activity range led to infeasible operation, whereas samples generated at the final activity kept the purity above 92.4% after the move transient.
