The 't Hooft model on (A)dS$_2$

Panos Betzios

We solve large-$N$ two-dimensional QCD with fundamental quarks---the 't Hooft model---on rigid AdS$_2$ and dS$_2$. Their isometries preserve the null gauge $A_-=0$ and in affine coordinates the curvature enters the light-front Hamiltonian only through the special-conformal generator of the null line. The $SL(2,\mathbb{R)}$ Casimir then results in an exact meson equation: 't Hooft's equation plus a Legendre operator whose sign is determined by the curvature. On AdS$_2$ it is related to equations proposed in the context of folded/segmented strings and light-front holography. The pion is massless in the chiral limit and obeys a Gell-Mann--Oakes--Renner relation that fixes the chiral condensate, the dimensions cross over from Regge to logarithmic growth once the flux tube exceeds the curvature radius, and the flat-space integrable structure survives. We also solve the problem of a quark interacting with an antiquark placed at the boundary: beyond a critical coupling the string pulls it back to the boundary, resulting in a scattering phase. On dS$_2$ and due to the expansion of space, the mesons are unstable resonances even at $N=\infty$ and eventually dissociate. Their widths are given by Boltzmann factors at the de Sitter temperature.

Exact Analytical Treatment of Collectively Pumped Dicke Superradiance

Avishi Poddar, Nico Baßler, Julian Lyne, Susanne F. Yelin, Helmut Ritsch et al. (7 authors)

We study the dynamics of an ensemble of $N$ two-level emitters undergoing collective decay at rate $Γ$ and collective incoherent pumping at rate $R$, confined to the fully symmetric Dicke ladder. Although the population dynamics are confined to a finite set of rate equations, their quantum-trajectory representation involves infinitely many pump-decay histories. We derive an exact finite continued fraction for the Laplace-transformed Dicke-state populations by resumming the infinitely many quantum trajectories generated by competing pump and decay processes. Our construction provides a physically transparent interpretation of the tridiagonal resolvent and extends to arbitrary permutationally symmetric initial states. In the strong- and weak-pumping regimes, we obtain perturbative transient solutions. We also identify exact relaxation eigenvalues and emission observables at balanced pump and decay strengths. Our solution provides a starting point for extending trajectory-based analytical methods to more general driven-dissipative collective systems.

Optimal quantum discrimination at Liouvillian exceptional points

Aritra Ghosh, M. Bhattacharya

Central to non-Hermitian sensing is the question of whether exceptional points (EPs) can make weak perturbations easier to detect. We show that despite enhanced spectral response, in finite-dimensional Lindblad systems with a unique stationary state and a nonzero relaxation gap, EPs offer no fundamental advantage in how the observation time required for reliable detection of a weak perturbation scales with the perturbation strength. We nevertheless demonstrate that by selectively choosing perturbations that enhance the quantum Fisher information rate, EPs can shorten the observation time needed to reach a predefined detection accuracy relative to a comparable non-EP reference at the same decay rate. As an illustration, we show using an optomechanical model at an EP that we can detect a weak force through the cavity output at the same reliability in less time than a non-EP reference. Our results establish fundamental limits on perturbation detection at EPs and can guide the design of faster non-Hermitian sensors based on optomechanical resonators, superconducting qubits, and trapped ions.

Mediated Interactions between Fermi Polarons in the Strongly Interacting Regime

Renato Pessoa, S. A. Vitiello, Luis A. Peña Ardila

Mediated interactions between quasiparticles lie at the heart of Landau Fermi-liquid theory, yet their behavior in the strongly correlated regime is not fully understood. Motivated by recent experiments [Nat. Phys. 20, 68--73 (2024)], we investigate mediated interactions between quasiparticles emerging from a Fermi sea---Fermi polarons---using zero-temperature quantum Monte Carlo methods. Our main result is that the Landau interaction between polarons remains repulsive in the strongly interacting regime for any coupling strength in the polaronic branch, in contrast to the downward trend observed experimentally near resonance. Deep in the short-range universal regime, our results are insensitive to the microscopic details of the interaction potential, providing robust evidence for the sign of the ground-state induced interaction. In addition, we determine the real-space interaction between static impurities, recovering a Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY)-like behavior at weak coupling and the nonperturbative Born-Oppenheimer result at strong coupling. More broadly, our results establish a microscopic benchmark for how Landau quasiparticle interactions survive beyond the perturbative regime of strongly correlated quantum matter.

Weighing dark matter particles with gravitational waves

Rodrigo Vicente, Francisco Duque, Theophanes K. Karydas, Gianfranco Bertone

It is commonly assumed that the mass of dark matter particles cannot be determined from their gravitational effects when dark matter behaves as collisionless matter. Here we show that extreme-mass-ratio inspirals observable by millihertz gravitational-wave observatories such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) could measure the mass of ultralight dark matter particles in sufficiently dense dark matter spikes. The separation of scales between the two black holes allows ultralight dark matter to form a collisionless density spike around the massive primary while retaining wave effects in its interaction with the stellar-mass companion. We incorporate wave-dependent accretion and dynamical friction into relativistic inspiral waveforms and jointly infer the dark matter particle mass, spike normalisation and binary parameters from simulated LISA signals. For massive black holes at the low-mass end of the LISA population ($M\sim 10^5 M_\odot$), our forecasts show that particle masses spanning $10^{-14}-10^{-11}\,eV$ could be measured to within an order of magnitude.

Classical Verification of Quantum Computation Beyond the Polynomial Hierarchy

Atul Mantri

It is not known whether a classical polynomial-time verifier can verify arbitrary polynomial-time quantum computations with an efficient quantum honest prover without computational assumptions. We show that, relative to a classical oracle, such verification is possible for a language beyond the polynomial hierarchy that is decided exactly in quantum polynomial time. The protocol is public-coin, with perfect completeness and soundness error at most 1/4. To our knowledge, no language with such a proof system was previously proved to lie outside AM relative to an oracle. For every unbounded polynomial-time computable function, the oracle can be chosen so that the message count and the oracle query counts of the quantum algorithm, verifier, and honest prover are bounded by this function of the input length. The message count is optimal up to a constant factor, since for a suitable oracle every interactive proof system for the language uses at least a constant fraction of the messages of our protocol on infinitely many lengths, with a constant depending on its verifier. The language is a variant of recursive Fourier sampling in which code tables encode secrets that address data tables. The tables are evaluated recursively, and the oracle stores only the leaves. The quantum algorithm recovers every secret exactly, and the verifier checks the prover's secrets recursively against random code entries. The recursion restricts the input in a correlated way, as the same secret fixes the code bits and chooses the free coordinate, so Håstad's switching lemma does not apply. We prove a switching theorem for these restrictions, which may be of independent interest. For instances of sufficient height, no constant-depth circuit of size $2^{poly(n)}$ changes its output with probability above $2^{-Ω(n)}$ when the answer of a random instance is flipped by changing a single leaf.

A Quiet Night in the Millihertz Sky? LISA Forecasts from LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA Populations

Ignacio Magaña Hernandez, Antonella Palmese

The high-mass black hole population inferred from ground-based observations can substantially affect the number of binaries detectable by the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA). We use population constraints based on data from the latest LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog (GWTC-5.0), as well as constraints from the O3 search for intermediate-mass black hole binaries, to forecast the number and masses of binary black holes detectable by LISA. Using the released GWTC-5.0 population posterior, we obtain $6.6^{+5.5}_{-2.2}$ expected detections with signal-to-noise ratio $>8$ in 4 years (median and $90\%$ credible interval). The alternative population models we consider use a broken power law with two or three Gaussian peaks for either the primary-mass distribution or a common mass distribution for both black holes. For the three-peak model with the common mass distribution, extrapolating from $300$ to $1000M_\odot$ roughly doubles the median expected number of LISA detections to $\sim14$. The upper end of its $90\%$ credible interval rises from tens to thousands of expected detections, although approximations may limit the reliability of the most extreme counts. For the reference GWTC-5.0 population, $39^{+22}_{-11}\%$ of the binaries detectable by LISA merge during the mission or within $10$ years after its end and are potential multiband sources. Essentially all of these mergers would be detectable by a reference Cosmic Explorer configuration if it is operating at merger. Ground-based measurements of the high-mass population can therefore inform both LISA forecasts and the prospects for observing the same binaries through inspiral and merger.

Stability of Axion--SU(2) Chromomagnetic Condensates with Stochastic Spin-2 Dressing

Sha Azyzy, Azadeh Maleknejad, Vahid Kamali

We investigate whether a stochastic spin-2 gauge-field background can stabilize the chromomagnetic condensate of axion--SU(2) Yang--Mills theory at fixed axion Noether charge in flat spacetime. By coarse-graining the gauge field, we uncover a new class of statistically homogeneous and isotropic spin-2-dressed chromomagnetic condensates satisfying the classical background equations. We then study their fluctuations in two steps. First, at the mean-field (Hartree) level, we determine the fluctuation spectrum and the region where all modes remain positively gapped. Second, we include the leading scattering corrections from fluctuations propagating through the stochastic background. We find that gap survival is controlled by non-Gaussian correlations of the dressing. Statistical positivity constrains the otherwise undetermined higher-point contributions through model-independent consistency conditions, without fixing their detailed momentum dependence. These bounds separate the Hartree-gapped region into a part where a positive Born-level gap is ruled out and a part where stability remains possible. The results are relevant to non-Abelian gauge condensates in early-universe cosmology, including inflation, dark energy, and primordial gravitational waves, and to QCD-like gauge sectors above confinement where thermal effects are subleading.

A Spin on Radiation Reaction with Cuts

Asaad Elkhidir, Matteo Sergola, Pablo Vives Matasan

We derive the radiation-reaction equations of motion for a spinning charged body, the $\sqrt{Kerr}$ particle, through quadratic order in its spin, generalising the Abraham--Lorentz--Dirac equation. Extending an argument of Coleman, we obtain radiation reaction equations by evaluating the self-field of the spinning body on its own worldline, and removing its divergences with dimensional regularisation. We use the resulting equations of motion to compute the electromagnetic waveform emitted in the scattering of the spinning body off a spectator charge, both at tree level and including the leading radiation-reaction correction, which enters at one loop. Then, we apply the construction introduced in a previous work by some of the authors -- where radiation reaction is encoded in unitarity cuts involving Compton amplitudes -- to spinning particles, and recompute the radiation-reaction contribution to the one-loop waveform. We find that the waveform calculated using classical and amplitude methods agree through quadratic order in spin, providing a check on our radiation-reaction equations. Along the way, we also reproduce known results for linear-in-spin self-force effects and for the spinless self-force in six dimensions.

Dynamical quantum simulations as laboratories for quantum information

Su Yeon Chang, Hailey S. Murray, Brian Barch, Greeshma Oruganti, Luis Pedro García-Pintos et al. (8 authors)

Dynamical quantum simulations are commonly designed to access a particular response function or correlation function. Yet the underlying protocol prepares a family of many-body states that contains information well beyond the observable for which the simulation was originally designed. In this work, we explore the idea of using these states as laboratories for quantum information. As a case study, we consider the local-pulse protocols used to compute Green functions in finite XX and XXX spin chains, and we interpret opposite source rotations as encoding a classical bit into the many-body system. This allows us to follow how the resulting record propagates, how it is encoded across local subsystems, and how readily it can be accessed with different measurements. We also find a clear separation between representation and readout. While fermionic covariance data exactly reconstruct the signed response in the XX chain, simple spin measurements recover a larger fraction of the locally available information in the XXX chain. This reversed ordering persists when we move from finite-pulse discrimination to local source-angle estimation. Going beyond the source record, the same reduced states also allow us to reinterpret results in terms of Bell state recovery and quantum communication rates, as well as providing direct access to the evolving bipartite and genuine multipartite entanglement of the system. Our results show that dynamical quantum simulations developed for response calculations can naturally be used to ask a much broader set of quantum-information questions about the many-body states they already prepare.
