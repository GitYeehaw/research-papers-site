Dephasing-Induced Melting of Crystalline Spectral Form Factors

Preethi Gopalakrishnan, Adolfo del Campo

Long-range spectral order can be probed using spectral form factors, where it manifests itself as sharp Bragg peaks at integer multiples of the Heisenberg time in nearly crystalline spectra. We examine how energy dephasing in open systems affects this spectral order and how to control it. We find that Bragg peaks fade algebraically with revival order and dephasing strength, $K_{\rm Bragg}(p)\propto(γp)^{-1/2}$, within an extensive intermediate regime, and persist up to the lifetime of nearest-neighbor coherences despite an extreme decoherence rate. Our results establish the spectral form factor as a probe of spectral order in open quantum systems and identify temporal correlations in spectral fluctuations as a means of controlling it.

Chiral waveguide model for error-correctable quantum computation

Zixiang Hong

We develop a first-principles framework connecting microscopic waveguide scattering to quantum error correction and algorithmic performance. For polarization-encoded single photons interacting with an ensemble of two-level atoms in a chiral waveguide, we derive an effective polarization quantum channel by tracing out the frequency degree of freedom. Using this channel as the physical noise model for a rotated planar code, we observe threshold behavior as the number of atoms $N$ and the atom-waveguide coupling strength $Γ$ are varied. An asymptotic analysis of the microscopic scattering model explains the threshold scaling with $NΓ/σ$, where $σ$ is the photon spectral bandwidth. For identical, lossless atoms with symmetric real coupling and a photon spectrum centered at resonance, the effective noise is strongly biased toward $X$ errors. We then incorporate the resulting logical errors into quantum phase estimation and demonstrate that the noise bias can guide circuit design. In particular, a Hadamard-basis implementation reduces sensitivity to the dominant error, improving algorithmic performance.

The simulability frontiers of photonic quantum computing

Jean Senellart, Benjamin Stott, Stephen Wein, Esperanza Cuenca Gomez, Pascale Senellart et al. (7 authors)

Estimates of when photonic devices outgrow classical simulation are hard to compare: even for single-photon boson sampling, quoted thresholds range from 50 to 98 photons, as they rest on different tasks and assumptions. We propose as a common unit the time a classical computer needs to draw one sample from the device's exact output distribution, up to floating-point rounding. Only exactness applies equally to every kind of light, since the cost of approximate sampling depends on the accuracy required. The simulability frontier is the device size at which our fastest exact sampler produces one sample per second on a fixed machine; this device size increases with better hardware and algorithms. As the time per sample grows exponentially with the number of detected photons, doubling the machine speed moves the frontier by about one photon, whereas a better algorithm changes the growth rate. We measure the frontier with photonic-engines, an open-source GPU library of exact samplers for single photons, entangled photons from quantum emitters, vacuum-single-photon superpositions and squeezed light. Each sampler is validated against a simulator sharing no code with it. On one NVIDIA H100 GPU, with Haar-random interferometers, twice as many modes as photonic inputs and photon-number-resolving detectors, the frontier lies at 38.3 single photons and at 31.0 entangled photons from one emitter. For squeezed light, a timing model fitted to measurements places it at 41.5 squeezed photonic inputs of mean photon number 0.5. For our samplers, coherence alone makes simulation harder: sources that emit a photon with probability one half reach 64.2 when vacuum and photon are mixed incoherently, as for lossy single photons, but only 50.0 when they are in coherent superposition. Feed-forward from mid-circuit measurement of up to three modes lowers the single-photon frontier only from 38.3 to 38.1.

Little Red Dots from Primordial Black Holes: Non-Gaussianity and Accretion Evade $μ$-Distortion Constraints

Shyam Balaji, Bryce Cyr, Sarah R. Geller, David I. Kaiser, Dario L. Lorenzoni et al. (7 authors)

Recent JWST observations of "Little Red Dots" (LRDs) at $z \gtrsim 4$ suggest the existence of early supermassive black holes (SMBHs) with masses $\gtrsim 10^6\,M_\odot$. Their rapid emergence challenges conventional seeding and growth scenarios based on accretion and mergers of Population III stellar remnants, motivating renewed interest in primordial black holes (PBHs) formed from primordial perturbations. Explaining such massive seeds requires power spectrum enhancements that are constrained by cosmic microwave background (CMB) spectral distortions. In this work we perform the first comprehensive Bayesian exploration of non-Gaussian primordial perturbations capable of producing SMBH seeds while remaining consistent with CMB spectral distortion constraints, and examine the role of accretion history in constraining these models. We perform a suite of Markov chain Monte Carlo analyses of power spectrum enhancements (lognormal and cut-power-law) with varying local non-Gaussianity and accretion histories, satisfying the COBE/FIRAS $μ$-distortion constraint with and without the requirement that the model produces SMBH seeds. We find that both models can seed LRDs and evade $μ$-distortion bounds for $|f_{\rm NL}|= O(1)$ and peak amplitude $A= O(10^{-3})$, when standard Eddington-limited accretion is also incorporated. Interestingly, the results exhibit a preference for both positive and negative values of $f_{\rm NL}$. The preference for negative $f_{\rm NL}$ is strongly dependent on the accretion history, and is removed if the black holes have a small duty cycle, $f_{\rm duty}\lesssim 0.1$. These results demonstrate that the JWST high-$z$ LRD population can be seeded by primordial perturbations while remaining compatible with CMB data, and highlight the importance of next-generation CMB spectral distortion missions to test this hypothesis.

The hidden symmetry operators of the asymmetric quantum Rabi model and its two-photon and two-mode variants in closed form

Marcus Kollar, Arka Dutta

The asymmetric quantum Rabi model, a two-level system with splitting $Δ$ and bias $ε$ coupled with strength $g$ to a boson mode, has level crossings when $ε$ is an integer multiple $N$ of the mode frequency. They are due to a hidden symmetry operator $J$, for which no general formula has been available. Here we obtain $J$ in closed form for every $N$. We find that $J$ is given by the parity of the symmetric model times the continuant (ordered tridiagonal determinant) of $N$ first-order operators, which are the Hamiltonians at the intermediate biases $N-1,N-3,\dots,1-N$ minus a common number operator. The symmetry is thus a staircase of $N$ bias steps that closes on the mirror image of the model precisely at integer bias. The proof uses induction on two commutation relations. The same relations hold in the asymmetric two-photon and two-mode models, written with the generators of $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$, so that the same continuant gives their symmetry operators for every order, previously known only for the lowest ones. We also relate the closed formula for $J$ to the known recursion relations for its coefficients and give their terms of lowest order in $Δ$ explicitly.

Mapping of Spacetimes with Neural Networks

Estuti Shukla, David Radice, Rossella Gamba, Sebastiano Bernuzzi

Gauge freedom in numerical relativity complicates the interpretation of simulation coordinates and the comparison of different descriptions of the same spacetime. We present a data-driven neural network method for learning coordinate maps from numerical metric data to a prescribed target gauge. The network is trained by matching the transformed source metric to the target metric, without assuming a functional form for the coordinate transformation. We first validate the method on analytical Schwarzschild and Kerr geometries, recovering known transformations up to the isometries of the target spacetime. We then apply it to numerical black hole evolutions in moving-puncture coordinates. For a non-spinning black hole, the learned map relates the time-dependent numerical metric to a stationary Schwarzschild metric in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates. For a spinning puncture, the method provides an approximate identification with a Kerr background despite departures from Kerr in the numerical spacetime. We use this identification to construct curvature perturbations and evolve them with the Teukolsky equation, obtaining waveforms that closely match those extracted from the full nonlinear simulation. These results demonstrate that learned coordinate maps can relate numerical spacetimes to analytical backgrounds, with potential applications to cross-code validation and black hole perturbation theory.

Enhancing valley splitting in Si/SiGe Wiggle Well quantum dots through integrated stressor-gate architectures

E. S. Joseph, Talise Oh, D. E. Savage, D. S. Stone, DeAnna Campbell et al. (8 authors)

Engineering large and reproducible valley splittings is essential for the scalable operation of silicon spin qubits. In Si/SiGe quantum dot devices, valley splitting arises predominantly from atomic-scale disorder and is therefore inherently random, motivating approaches that introduce a large deterministic contribution. Strain-assisted Wiggle Wells provide a route to deterministic valley splitting by combining an oscillatory Ge concentration in the Si well with shear strain; however, generating the required strain in a manner compatible with large-scale architectures remains a challenge. Here, we propose patterned stressed thin films as a scalable approach for generating shear strain in the quantum well of Si/SiGe quantum dot devices. Finite-element simulations show that compressively stressed TiN films can generate shear strains exceeding 0.1\% at the quantum well, sufficient for deterministic valley splittings of hundreds of $μeV$ in strain-assisted Wiggle Wells. We show that this strain can extend across multiple quantum dots and is maintained in realistic gate geometries, providing a fabrication-compatible approach for scaling deterministic valley splitting to quantum dot arrays.

Beyond $Λ$CDM: Probing Structure Growth and Observational Viability of Generalized ${f\,(Q , \mathcal{L}_{m})}$ Gravity

Raj Kumar Das, Sivasish Paul

$f(Q, \mathcal{L}_m)$ gravity is a well-known extension of $f(Q)$ gravity, obtained through non-minimal or additive minimal coupling in the action's Lagrangian density. Background-level cosmography within this framework, along with comparisons to $Λ$CDM, has been the focus of prior studies. The present work considers a generalized power-law form of the additive minimal coupling, $f(Q,\mathcal{L}_m) = -Q^μ+ α\mathcal{L}_m^{\,ν} + β$, from which the Hubble function $H(z)$ is derived through a novel parametrization. When $ν\neq 1$, the matter continuity equation is rendered non-conserved, a feature that can be interpreted as an effective particle-creation mechanism. Constraints on the model parameters are obtained from Observational Hubble Data, DESI DR2 BAO measurements, and three SNe Ia compilations (Pantheon+, Union3, DES-SN5YR), allowing their cosmographic effects to be examined. Beyond the background level, the perturbed field, Euler, and continuity equations are derived under non-conservation, and their impact on structure growth is studied through a joint analysis incorporating the $fσ_8$ probe. Bayesian evidence, computed relative to $Λ$CDM, shows that the model is favoured by background data in several cases, with moderate support for one realisation, whereas the inclusion of $fσ_8$ drives $ν\to1$ and decisively disfavours the additional freedom, highlighting the importance of growth data in testing such extensions.

Scalar quasinormal modes of the exact spinning Morris--Thorne wormhole

Mark Sukaiti, Davide Batic, Denys Dutykh

We study the quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a minimally coupled massless scalar field on the exact spinning Morris-Thorne wormhole. The problem is posed on the complete two-ended geometry, whose asymptotic rest frames rotate relative to each other. After extracting the axis behaviour and the outgoing phases at both infinities, we compactify the radial coordinate and construct a quadratic eigenvalue problem by two-dimensional Chebyshev collocation. The analytic background yields exact constraints on the spectrum. At fixed azimuthal number $m$, the frequencies $\widehatω=r_0ω$ are mirror symmetric about $\operatorname{Re}\widehatω=3πjm/2$, where $r_0$ is the throat radius and $j=J/r_0^2$ is the dimensionless angular momentum. Each multipole family depends on $m$ and $j$ only through $mj$ and three angular Chebyshev polynomials represent exactly the multipoles $L=|m|,|m|+1,|m|+2$. A conserved flux confines nonthreshold real quasinormal frequencies to the superradiant window $0<ω/(mΩ_-)<1$, where $Ω_-=3πJ/r_0^3$. For the sampled sectors $m=1,2,3$ and spins up to $j=19$, every converged frequency is damped and lies outside this window. Independent integration verifies the low-lying modes, while Kerr spectra provide a separate benchmark for the two-dimensional solver. A limiting radial equation explains why the computed mode frequencies approach the upper edge of the superradiant window with frequency offsets proportional to $(mj)^{-1/2}$ at large $mj$. A complete stability assessment requires further analysis of the unresolved scalar spectrum and of perturbations of the coupled metric-matter system.

Property F implies weak integrality for non-degenerate pivotal braided fusion categories

Justin Asher, Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez, Richard Tuddenham

We prove that every non-degenerate pivotal braided fusion category with property F is pseudo-unitary and weakly integral. In particular, every modular category with property F is weakly integral. Neither sphericality nor pseudo-unitarity is assumed. Passing to the Drinfeld centre extends the result to degenerate categories whose dimensions are positive on the intersection of the Müger centre with the adjoint subcategory; these include every braided fusion category with a pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate. Physically, the result connects the arithmetic of topological orders to the computational power of anyon braiding: non-integer squared total quantum dimension guarantees infinite braid group images, a necessary condition for universal quantum computation.
