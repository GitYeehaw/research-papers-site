Third harmonic generation in the transition metal trichalcogenides

Timothy Y. Chung, Vergil M. Schreiber, Randy M. Sterbentz, David P. Shelton, Eduardo Flores et al. (8 authors)

The transition metal trichalcogenides (TMTs) are an interesting class of layered 2D materials that have strong anisotropic electrical and optical properties. We report on the polarization-dependent nonlinear optical response of exfoliated crystals at the third harmonic using non-resonant excitation with a 1560 nm femtosecond laser. All members of the TMT crystal family explored (ZrS3, HfS3, TiS3, and NbS3) display large anisotropy in their polarized third harmonic generation signal, with anisotropy ratios as large as chi_11/chi_22 ~ 6.5. From the expected nonlinear dependence of these monoclinic and triclinic crystals we extract the relative nonlinear susceptibility coefficients. We find that the anisotropy ratio is largest for ZrS3 and decreases across the family, a trend we associate with the proximity of the third harmonic energy to the strongly b-polarized absorption edge of each material. NbS3, the only triclinic crystal, displays a pronounced anisotropy in thick flakes that is strongly reduced in the thinnest flakes, which we attribute to a partial relaxation of the Peierls-type Nb-Nb dimerization that underlies the triclinic distortion. The large anisotropic nonlinear response in this crystal family sets it apart from other 2D materials and makes it particularly attractive for further investigation.

An Ab Initio Quantum-Chemistry-Based Three-Temperature Approach for Non-Equilibrium Hypersonic Flows: Application to Homogeneous 0-D Chemical Systems

Aakanksha Notey, Sung Min Jo, Marco Panesi

This work presents an ab initio quantum-chemistry-based one-bin, three-temperature (1-bin 3T) model for non-equilibrium thermochemistry of homogeneous chemical systems in their electronic ground state, derived using the multi-group maximum-entropy principle. The proposed framework captures both vibrational and rotational non-equilibrium effects, thereby enabling an accurate representation of thermochemical behavior in high-temperature gases. To account for departures from equilibrium dissociation, a non-equilibrium correction factor derived from ro-vibrational State-to-State (StS) analysis is incorporated into the population distribution. Because this correction is constructed from the quasi-steady-state (QSS) distribution, the resulting model is valid only for systems and conditions under which the QSS assumption holds. The proposed 1-bin 3T model is validated across a broad range of zero-dimensional test cases through comparisons with high-fidelity StS simulations and Direct Molecular Simulations (DMS). The results demonstrate excellent agreement between the reduced-order model and high-fidelity approaches for both dissociating and recombining environments. Overall, the 1-bin 3T model shows strong potential as an efficient and accurate framework for modeling non-equilibrium thermochemical behavior, while keeping computational costs orders of magnitude lower than the high fidelity ro-vibrational StS simulations.

Information-theoretic entropy imaging of quantum matter

Chen Wu, Leonard W. Cao, Lingyuan Lyu, Liam Cohen, Jiahui Nie et al. (16 authors)

Phase transitions can generate intricate spatial organization invisible to spatially averaged probes, yet quantitative measures of this organization remain limited. Here we develop "entropy imaging," an information-theoretic technique that harnesses lossless compression to quantify spatial complexity, predictability, and order in quantum materials. Using this approach, emergent spatial textures can be imaged and converted into an experimentally-accessible observable: the computable information density (CID), a compression-based measure of information complexity that approaches the Shannon entropy rate in the large-system limit. Applied to millikelvin microwave impedance microscopy of graphene, the method reveals a pronounced maximum in the CID near quantum Hall transitions, a quantitative measure of the information-rich spatial organization of the critical landscape. A disordered Hofstadter model reproduces this behavior and shows that the CID can identify the quantum critical point from a sequence of images, without ensemble averaging over disorder realizations. The same framework tracks fractional quantum Hall transitions and resolves isolated fractional puddles that coexist with a disordered background. By contrast, the trivial band-edge crossover in a MoSe2/WS2 moiré heterobilayer exhibits qualitatively different behavior, suggesting that the pronounced CID enhancement observed at the quantum Hall critical point is not simply a generic feature of a conductive-insulating crossover. We further show that the geometric information encoded in CID is not reducible to a correlation length, and it can even distinguish spatial textures with identical multifractal statistics. Together, these results establish lossless compression as a framework for quantifying emergent spatial organization in quantum materials, with portability across imaging modalities and physical systems.

Growth Phase Map of α and \b{eta} Tantalum as a Function of Growth Temperature and Oxygen Partial Pressure

Marius O. Eji, Tyler J. Kirkpatrick, Ty J. Cawein, Joseph P. Corbett

Tantalum thin films are used in low-loss superconducting quantum circuits, yet the growth conditions governing selection of $α$-Ta(110), $α$-Ta(111), and metastable $β$-Ta on c-plane sapphire remain uncertain. We deposited 100 nm Ta films by dc magnetron sputtering on Al$_2$O$_3$(0001) at 300-900 $^\circ$C while varying the O$_2$ partial pressure from a $5.5\times10^{-11}$ Torr ultrahigh-vacuum baseline to $2.23\times10^{-4}$ Torr. Absorption-corrected X-ray diffraction texture coefficients were used to construct a ternary phase-orientation growth map in terms of temperature and the oxygen-to-Ta impingement flux ratio ($2Φ_{O_2}/Φ_{Ta}\leq4.8\times10^{-6}$ to 19). Without added oxygen, $α$-Ta(110) dominated at every temperature. At an O$_2$ pressure of only $2.5\times10^{-9}$ Torr ($2Φ_{O_2}/Φ_{Ta}=2.2\times10^{-4}$), $α$-Ta(111) accounted for 98-99% of the normalized texture weight from 300 to 700 $^\circ$C, decreasing to 65% at 900 $^\circ$C. Substantial $β$-Ta formed only at the highest oxygen dose, dominating at 300 $^\circ$C and disappearing at 900 $^\circ$C. The apparent $β$(001)/$α$(110) selection energy of $1.07\pm0.16$ eV is comparable to the activation energy for oxygen diffusion in Ta, consistent with oxygen transport influencing phase selection. Oxygen also produced orientation-dependent changes in crystallite size and microstrain. These findings establish oxygen-controlled processing windows for phase- and orientation-selective growth of Ta films for superconducting devices.

Theoretical description of high-harmonic spectroscopy for uniaxial nematic and isotropic liquid crystals

Simon Vendelbo Bylling Jensen, Marta L. Murillo-Sánchez, Andrea Annunziata, Angel Rubio, Laura Cattaneo et al. (6 authors)

Enabled by their controllable optical anisotropy, liquid crystals form a cornerstone of linear optical and display technologies. Bringing the versatile platform into the nonlinear regime, we explore its nonlinear optical anisotropy by high-order harmonic generation spectroscopy. Through a comprehensive study targeting gas, liquid, nematic, and crystalline phases, we present a semi-empirical tight-binding approach that provides insight into the intermolecular character of ultrafast light-driven electron dynamics across soft-matter mesophases. We demonstrate how intermolecular electron hybridization enables spectroscopic access to structural properties, such as the nematic director and order parameter. Outlining such foundational mechanisms opens avenues towards nonlinear liquid crystal devices and ultrafast soft-matter spectroscopies.

Phonon-assisted shift-current from first-principles: application to AlN monolayer

Yuncheng Mao, Pierre Lechifflart, Elena Cannuccia, Claudio Attaccalite

In this work, we present a first-principles approach to investigate phonon-assisted shift current in extended systems. Our method combines the special displacement technique with real-time simulations based on the dynamical Berry phase. Merging these two frameworks enables direct access to non-linear optical responses at finite temperatures, while naturally incorporating phonon scattering and phonon-assisted electronic transitions into the time-dependent dynamics. This allows us to capture temperature-dependent features and lattice fluctuations that are essential for a realistic description of non-linear photocurrent generation. As a representative example, we apply our approach to the shift-current response of a monolayer of hexagonal AlN (h-AlN). This system provides an ideal testing ground because it features an indirect band gap and lacks inversion symmetry. Moreover, h-AlN layers of different thicknesses have been fabricated and characterized in a variety of experimental settings, opening the concrete possibility of testing our predictions. Our results demonstrate the relevance of phonon-mediated processes and the importance of temperature in determining the magnitude and spectral features of the shift current in layered materials.

Retention-constrained fine-tuning of a pre-trained aluminium MACE potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity

Yue Wu, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Adapting a pre-trained machine-learning interatomic potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity requires improving defect accuracy without degrading the near-equilibrium elastic and vibrational response. Here we achieve this balance for the aluminium MACE potential QP17 by integrating conditional readout uncertainty, iterative full-parameter updates and protocol-compatible relative-energy anchoring. Sensitivities over 144 linear readout weights guide acquisition, with within-batch covariance updates and evolving neural representations. After four active-learning rounds (32 acquired configurations and 400 gradient updates), force-uncertainty acquisition reduces mean development force error from 22.43 to 21.52 meV/Angstrom, outperforming random sampling (22.70), geometry-diversity selection (22.46) and uniform training across all 104 candidate labels (22.71) under the same update budget. All three force-uncertainty runs satisfy bulk-mechanical retention criteria: historical relative-energy error decreases from 0.375 to 0.207 meV/atom, while harmonic phonon root-mean-square drift remains below 0.05 THz. Matched objective ablations reveal that relative-energy anchoring suppresses harmonic drift. Out-of-sample evaluations across planar faults, twin boundaries and grain boundaries demonstrate transferable gains in defect energies and deviatoric stresses, even though virial stresses are never fitted. A closed-loop molecular dynamics and density functional theory (MD-DFT) pilot confirms that automated acceptance gating intercepts updates that violate historical elastic-group energy or vibrational tolerances. Together, these components establish a retention-constrained fine-tuning protocol for improving plastic-deformation observables while preserving the tested bulk mechanical response.

Lanthanide Crystal Field Theory in the J-Mixing Regime: $f^n$ Configurations

Arta A. Safari, Markus Reiher

The low-energy sector of the lanthanide $f^n$ shell is to first approximation governed by the interplay of three interactions: Coulomb repulsion, spin-same-orbit coupling and the ligand crystal field potential. To rationalize the energy landscape in different chemical environments, it is desirable to extract the phenomenological crystal field parameters from experimental data; however, in practice, high-dimensional fitting procedures complicate their routine determination. Here, we present a computational extraction procedure based on real tensorial sets and the $U(7)$ canonical chain that, unlike existing operator equivalent methods, takes all $J$ multiplets of the lowest-energy $LS$ term into account, ensuring meaningful results even in the presence of considerable $J$-mixing. Two series of lanthanide compounds, [Ln(III)(C$_8$H$_8$)$_2$]$^-$} and doped Ln(III):Y$_2$O$_3$} (Ln $\in$ {Ce, Pr, Nd, Sm, Eu}) serve as examples for high- ($D_{8h}$) and low-symmetry ($C_2$) scenarios. Consequences for the generalisation toward actinides are briefly discussed.

A low-volume cold-atom microwave clock with an additively manufactured cavity

Samuel A. Smith, Sean Dyer, Alan Bregazzi, Rajnandan Choudhury Das, Oliver S. Burrow et al. (9 authors)

We present the performance of a compact microwave interrogated atomic clock based on laser cooled $^{87}$$\rm{Rb}$ atoms. The clock utilizes a micro-fabricated grating magneto-optical trap (gMOT) to cool and trap atoms, and an additively manufactured microwave cavity for microwave interrogation. We demonstrate a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of $370$ at a 1 Hz bandwidth and mid-term instability of $6.7\times10^{-14}$ at an averaging time of $τ=3 \times 10^{4}$ s. In addition, a low cost mu-metal foil has been implemented on the vacuum chamber to reduce the quadratic Zeeman shift which was previously limiting stability beyond $τ= 10$ s.

Transverse detuned loading for modulation bandwidth enhancement in VCSELs

Giacomo Graziano, Richard Schatz, Antoine Pissis, Wilfried Maineult, Aurelio Rossinelli et al. (14 authors)

Directly modulated vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers (VCSELs) are central to short-reach optical interconnects, where modulation bandwidth and power efficiency are critical factors for increasing data transfer capacity. Conventional oxide VCSELs still require power-hungry signal processing to achieve high-speed data transmission above 100 Gb/s. Here we report an experimental implementation of detuned loading in a VCSEL to enhance the modulation bandwidth. The detuned loading is achieved by carrier-induced index variations that reshape an engineered transverse mode so its overlap with spatially selective optical losses decreases with increased carrier density. This produces a negative differential loss reminiscent of that in detuned-loaded DBR lasers lasing on the long-wavelength reflectivity flank. The resulting 850 nm single-mode VCSEL exhibits an enhanced carrier-photon relaxation frequency of 44 GHz, yielding a 3dB electrical modulation bandwidth exceeding 50 GHz. The enhancement persists from 25 °C to 75 °C showing the robustness of the mechanism introduced. The same device supports 106.25 Gb/s NRZ transmission without digital signal processing, transmitter pre-emphasis or receiver equalization. These results establish transverse detuned loading as a robust alternative approach for extending the modulation bandwidth of vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers.
